Two intersecting lines form four angles. After measuring one angle as $68^\circ$, the other three can be found without a protractor. Which relationships make that deduction possible?

## adjacent, supplementary and vertically opposite angles and angle bisectors

**A short exploration.**
Adjacent angles on a straight line sum to $180^\circ$. If one is $68^\circ$, its neighbour is $112^\circ$. The angle opposite the $68^\circ$ angle is equal to it because both are supplementary to the $112^\circ$ angle.

:::definition Angle pairs
Adjacent angles share a vertex and an arm without overlapping interiors. Complementary angles sum to $90^\circ$; supplementary angles sum to $180^\circ$. Vertically opposite angles are formed by intersecting lines and are equal.
:::

:::definition Angle bisector
An angle bisector starts at the vertex, lies inside the angle and divides it into two equal angles. If $Ot$ bisects $\angle AOB$, then $\angle AOt=\angle tOB=\dfrac12\angle AOB$.
:::

**Why does this work?**
Vertical angles are equal by supplementary reasoning: if $x+y=180^\circ$ and $z+y=180^\circ$, then $x=z$. An angle bisector needs all three conditions concerning endpoint, position and measure; equal angles alone are insufficient if the ray lies outside.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example A linear pair
Two angles form a linear pair and one is $73^\circ$. Find the other.

**Solution.**
$180^\circ-73^\circ=107^\circ$.

Therefore, $107^\circ$.
:::

:::example Complementary angles
Complementary angles measure $x$ and $2x$. Find $x$.

**Solution.**
$x+2x=90^\circ$, so $3x=90^\circ$ and $x=30^\circ$.

Therefore, $30^\circ$.
:::

:::example Vertical angles
Two lines intersect. One angle is $124^\circ$. Find its vertical opposite.

**Solution.**
Vertically opposite angles are equal, so the required angle is $124^\circ$.

Therefore, $124^\circ$.
:::

:::example Bisecting a turn
A sign forms an $86^\circ$ angle. A ray is placed on its bisector. Find the angle between the ray and each arm.

**Solution.**

*Understand.* Record the given data, the required result and any conditions.

*Plan.* Choose the relevant definition or rule, then work in a logical order.

*Solve.* $86^\circ\div2=43^\circ$, so each part is $43^\circ$.

*Look back.* Compare the result with the data and use an independent check.

Therefore, $43^\circ$.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Confusing adjacent and vertical angles.** Calling two angles sharing an arm vertically opposite. Vertical angles do not share an arm; corresponding arms are opposite rays. Check position: a shared arm indicates adjacency; opposite positions indicate vertical angles.
- **Declaring a bisector from appearance.** Seeing a ray near the middle and declaring it a bisector. A diagram does not guarantee equal measures. Use the data $\angle AOt=\angle tOB$ and that the ray lies inside.
:::

**Connections.** Angle relationships replace measurement with deduction. They are central tools for proving parallel lines, the triangle angle sum and polygon properties. When solving a problem, separate three layers: the data, the mathematical relationship and the conclusion. Before substituting values, state why the chosen rule applies; after each step, check signs, units and conditions. A diagram, small table or simple case often reveals a missing assumption. Estimate before calculating so that you know whether the answer should be large or small, positive or negative. After obtaining a result, use an independent check: substitute it back into the data, measure it on the diagram, or test a boundary case. If one item of data changes, predict which part of the method changes and which part remains valid. This approach turns a formula into a connected system of reasons and makes the idea usable in an unfamiliar situation instead of only in a memorised exercise. It also produces written work that another student can follow, question and verify line by line.

## Summary

- Complementary angles sum to $90^\circ$.
- Supplementary angles sum to $180^\circ$.
- Vertically opposite angles are equal.
- An angle bisector divides an angle into two equal parts.
- Do not conclude from appearance without given information.
