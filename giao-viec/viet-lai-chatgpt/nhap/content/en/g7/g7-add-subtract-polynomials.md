## polynomials, degree, addition and subtraction

**A short exploration.**
Add by degree: $(3x^2+x^2)+(2x-4x)+(5+7)=4x^2-2x+12$. To subtract $P_2$, change the sign of every term in it and then add.

:::definition Polynomial and degree
A polynomial in one variable is a finite sum of terms $ax^n$ with non-negative integer exponent $n$. The degree of a polynomial different from $0$ is the greatest exponent whose coefficient differs from $0$ after simplification.
:::

:::definition Addition and subtraction
To add polynomials, group like terms and add coefficients. To subtract a polynomial, add its opposite: change the sign of every term in the subtracted polynomial.
:::

**Why does this work?**
Each power $1,x,x^2,\ldots$ acts as an independent kind of unit. Polynomial addition combines coefficients of matching kinds. Subtraction distributes a negative sign across the entire bracket because $A-B=A+(-1)B$.

**How to check your own work.** For a question on this topic, first state in words what is given and what must be found. Next write the rule you will use, substitute values or reason one step at a time, and then check the result in a different way: put it back into the data, estimate its size, or compare it with a diagram or number line. If two methods disagree, locate the first step where they differ instead of changing only the final answer. This habit catches sign errors, missing units and overlooked cases.

**Systems view.** polynomials, degree, addition and subtraction is not an isolated trick. Follow the chain: the data determine a model, the model determines the calculation, and the result must return to the original situation. When one piece of data changes, predict whether the result should increase, decrease or stay unchanged before calculating again.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example Adding polynomials
Calculate $(3x^2+2x-1)+(x^2-5x+4)$.

**Solution.**
Combine by degree: $4x^2-3x+3$.

Therefore, $4x^2-3x+3$.
:::

:::example Subtracting polynomials
Calculate $(5x^2-x+6)-(2x^2+3x-4)$.

**Solution.**
Change every sign in the second polynomial: $5x^2-x+6-2x^2-3x+4=3x^2-4x+10$.

Therefore, $3x^2-4x+10$.
:::

:::example Degree after simplification
Find the degree of $4x^3-2x+x^3+7$.

**Solution.**
It simplifies to $5x^3-2x+7$; the greatest exponent is $3$, so degree $3$.

Therefore, $3$.
:::

:::example An algebraic area total
Two regions have areas $2x^2+3x$ and $x^2-x+5$. Write the total area.

**Solution.**

*Understand.* Record the given data, the required result and any conditions.

*Plan.* Choose the relevant definition or rule, then work in a logical order.

*Solve.* Add like terms: $3x^2+2x+5$.

*Look back.* Compare the result with the data and use an independent check.

Therefore, $3x^2+2x+5$.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Failing to change every sign.** In $P-(x^2-3x+4)$, changing only the first term sign. The subtraction sign multiplies the whole polynomial by $-1$. Write $P-x^2+3x-4$.
- **Combining different powers.** Adding $2x^2+3x$ as $5x^3$. The variable parts differ, so the terms are unlike. Order by degree and combine only within a column.
:::

**Connections.** Polynomial addition and subtraction combine or compare algebraic models. Aligning powers resembles aligning place values and prepares for polynomial multiplication, division and equations. When solving a problem, separate three layers: the data, the mathematical relationship and the conclusion. Before substituting values, state why the chosen rule applies; after each step, check signs, units and conditions. A diagram, small table or simple case often reveals a missing assumption. Estimate before calculating so that you know whether the answer should be large or small, positive or negative. After obtaining a result, use an independent check: substitute it back into the data, measure it on the diagram, or test a boundary case. If one item of data changes, predict which part of the method changes and which part remains valid. This approach turns a formula into a connected system of reasons and makes the idea usable in an unfamiliar situation instead of only in a memorised exercise. It also produces written work that another student can follow, question and verify line by line.

## Summary

- A polynomial uses non-negative integer exponents.
- Degree is the greatest exponent remaining after simplification.
- Add polynomials by adding coefficients of equal powers.
- Subtract by changing every term sign and then adding.
- Substitution is one way to check a result.
