A triangle has angles $48^\circ$ and $67^\circ$. Without measuring the third angle, it can still be found because every plane triangle has the same angle sum. Where does that fixed total come from?

## triangle angle sum, exterior angles and unknown angles

**A short exploration.**
Through one triangle vertex, draw a line parallel to the opposite side. The other two interior angles transfer as alternate interior angles at the new line; together with the vertex angle, the three form a straight angle of $180^\circ$.

:::definition Triangle angle-sum theorem
In every triangle $ABC$, $\angle A+\angle B+\angle C=180^\circ$. Therefore, knowing two angles determines the third.
:::

:::definition Exterior angle of a triangle
An exterior angle is formed by one side and the extension of an adjacent side. Its measure equals the sum of the two remote interior angles and is supplementary to the adjacent interior angle.
:::

**Why does this work?**
An auxiliary parallel line turns the three separate angles into adjacent angles on a straight line, so their sum is $180^\circ$. The exterior-angle theorem follows: if $e+C=180^\circ$ and $A+B+C=180^\circ$, then $e=A+B$.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example Finding a third angle
A triangle has angles $48^\circ,67^\circ$. Find the remaining angle.

**Solution.**
$180^\circ-48^\circ-67^\circ=65^\circ$.

Therefore, $65^\circ$.
:::

:::example An isosceles triangle
An isosceles triangle has vertex angle $40^\circ$. Find each base angle.

**Solution.**
The equal base angles total $180^\circ-40^\circ=140^\circ$, so each is $70^\circ$.

Therefore, $70^\circ$.
:::

:::example Using an exterior angle
A triangle exterior angle is $128^\circ$ and one remote interior angle is $53^\circ$. Find the other remote angle.

**Solution.**
$128^\circ=53^\circ+x$, so $x=75^\circ$.

Therefore, $75^\circ$.
:::

:::example A triangular roof
A roof cross-section is an isosceles triangle with vertex angle $36^\circ$. Find the angle between each slope and the base.

**Solution.**

*Understand.* Record the given data, the required result and any conditions.

*Plan.* Choose the relevant definition or rule, then work in a logical order.

*Solve.* Each base angle is $(180^\circ-36^\circ)\div2=72^\circ$.

*Look back.* Compare the result with the data and use an independent check.

Therefore, $72^\circ$.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Putting an exterior angle in the $180^\circ$ interior sum.** Adding two interior angles and an exterior angle and setting the sum to $180^\circ$. The angle-sum theorem applies to the three interior angles. Use either all three interior angles or the exterior angle equal to the two remote angles.
- **Halving without isosceles information.** Automatically halving the sum of two remaining angles. The angles are equal only when suitable information is given. Halve only for an isosceles triangle or explicitly equal angles.
:::

**Connections.** The triangle angle sum links parallel lines to polygons. It leads to exterior angles, polygon angle sums and many properties of special triangles. Estimate before calculating so that you know whether the answer should be large or small, positive or negative. After obtaining a result, use an independent check: substitute it back into the data, measure it on the diagram, or test a boundary case. If one item of data changes, predict which part of the method changes and which part remains valid. This approach turns a formula into a connected system of reasons and makes the idea usable in an unfamiliar situation instead of only in a memorised exercise. It also produces written work that another student can follow, question and verify line by line.

## Summary

- The interior angles of a triangle sum to $180^\circ$.
- An exterior angle equals the two remote interior angles.
- An exterior angle is supplementary to its adjacent interior angle.
- The acute angles of a right triangle are complementary.
- A triangle cannot have two right or two obtuse angles.
