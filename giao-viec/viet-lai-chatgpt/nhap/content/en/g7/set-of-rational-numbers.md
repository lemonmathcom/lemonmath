A temperature of $-2.5^\circ$C, a share $\dfrac34$ and a water rise of $1.2$ m can all be written as quotients of integers. Which set contains positive and negative numbers, integers and these fractions?

## rational numbers and their representation and comparison

**A short exploration.**
Write $-2.5=-\dfrac{25}{10}=-\dfrac52$, $1.2=\dfrac65$ and $3=\dfrac31$. Each has form $\dfrac ab$ with integers $a,b$ and $b\ne0$. On a number line, they are ordered from left to right.

:::definition Rational number
A rational number can be written as $\dfrac ab$, where $a,b\in\mathbb Z$ and $b\ne0$. The set is denoted by $\mathbb Q$. Every integer is rational because $n=\dfrac n1$.
:::

:::definition Representation and order
Every rational number corresponds to a point on the number line. Equivalent fractions represent the same point. To compare, use a common positive denominator, convert to decimals, or use positions on the number line.
:::

**Why does this work?**
Equivalent fractions such as $\dfrac12$ and $\dfrac24$ represent the same quotient and hence the same number. With equal positive denominators, the larger numerator gives the larger number. For negative numbers, the point nearer $0$ lies farther right and is therefore greater.

If two methods disagree, locate the first step where they differ instead of changing only the final answer. This habit catches sign errors, missing units and overlooked cases.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example Recognising a rational number
Explain why $-7$ is rational.

**Solution.**
$-7=\dfrac{-7}{1}$ has integer numerator and denominator, with denominator different from $0$.

Therefore, $-7\in\mathbb Q$.
:::

:::example Simplifying a representation
Simplify $\dfrac{-18}{24}$.

**Solution.**
Divide numerator and denominator by $6$: $\dfrac{-18}{24}=-\dfrac34$.

Therefore, $-\dfrac34$.
:::

:::example Comparing negative rationals
Compare $-\dfrac35$ and $-\dfrac7{10}$.

**Solution.**
$-\dfrac35=-\dfrac6{10}$. Since $-6>-7$, $-\dfrac35>-\dfrac7{10}$.

Therefore, $-\dfrac35>-\dfrac7{10}$.
:::

:::example Relative elevations
Three points have elevations $-1.5$ m, $\dfrac14$ m and $-\dfrac23$ m relative to a reference. Order them from lowest to highest.

**Solution.**

*Understand.* Record the given data, the required result and any conditions.

*Plan.* Choose the relevant definition or rule, then work in a logical order.

*Solve.* $-1.5=-\dfrac32$. Thus $-\dfrac32<-\dfrac23<\dfrac14$.

*Look back.* Compare the result with the data and use an independent check.

Therefore, $-\dfrac32,-\dfrac23,\dfrac14$.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Allowing a zero denominator.** Treating $\dfrac50$ as a rational number. Division by $0$ is undefined. In $\dfrac ab$, always require $b\ne0$.
- **Reversing negative order.** Concluding $-8>-3$ because $8>3$. On the number line, $-8$ lies to the left of $-3$. Compare positions or distances from $0$: $-8<-3$.
:::

**Connections.** Rational numbers extend integers to describe parts of a unit, ratios, measurements and continuous change. The number line unifies fractions, decimals and integers in one order. When solving a problem, separate three layers: the data, the mathematical relationship and the conclusion. Before substituting values, state why the chosen rule applies; after each step, check signs, units and conditions. A diagram, small table or simple case often reveals a missing assumption. Estimate before calculating so that you know whether the answer should be large or small, positive or negative. After obtaining a result, use an independent check: substitute it back into the data, measure it on the diagram, or test a boundary case. If one item of data changes, predict which part of the method changes and which part remains valid. This approach turns a formula into a connected system of reasons and makes the idea usable in an unfamiliar situation instead of only in a memorised exercise. It also produces written work that another student can follow, question and verify line by line.

## Summary

- $\mathbb Q=\left\{\dfrac ab:a,b\in\mathbb Z,b\ne0\right\}$.
- Every integer belongs to $\mathbb Q$.
- Equivalent fractions represent the same number.
- A point farther right on the number line represents a greater number.
- $|x|$ is the distance from $x$ to $0$.
