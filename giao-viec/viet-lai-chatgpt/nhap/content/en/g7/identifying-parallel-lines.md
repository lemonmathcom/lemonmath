Two road markings are crossed by a transversal. If a pair of alternate interior angles are both $72^\circ$, the markings can be proved parallel without extending them indefinitely. Why can a local angle relationship determine both entire lines?

## criteria for identifying parallel lines

**A short exploration.**
Draw lines $a,b$ and transversal $t$. Mark corresponding, alternate interior and co-interior pairs. When $a\parallel b$, the first two types are equal and co-interior angles sum to $180^\circ$. Their converses become criteria for parallelism.

:::definition Parallel lines and a transversal
Distinct coplanar lines with no common point are parallel. A line meeting both at distinct points is called a transversal.
:::

:::definition Angle criteria
If a transversal forms equal corresponding angles, equal alternate interior angles, or supplementary co-interior angles, then the two lines it crosses are parallel.
:::

**Why does this work?**
The criteria are converses of angle properties for parallel lines. One valid pair is enough because linear-pair and vertical-angle relationships transfer measures to the remaining angles, forcing the same direction.

**How to check your own work.** For a question on this topic, first state in words what is given and what must be found. If two methods disagree, locate the first step where they differ instead of changing only the final answer. This habit catches sign errors, missing units and overlooked cases.

Follow the chain: the data determine a model, the model determines the calculation, and the result must return to the original situation. When one piece of data changes, predict whether the result should increase, decrease or stay unchanged before calculating again.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example Equal alternate interior angles
Transversal $t$ makes alternate interior angles of $64^\circ$ on $a,b$. What follows?

**Solution.**
A pair of alternate interior angles is equal, so $a\parallel b$.

Therefore, $a\parallel b$.
:::

:::example Corresponding angles
Corresponding angles measure $3x+5$ and $5x-25$ degrees. Find $x$ for the lines to be parallel.

**Solution.**
Require $3x+5=5x-25$, so $2x=30$ and $x=15$.

Therefore, $15$.
:::

:::example Co-interior angles
Co-interior angles are $118^\circ$ and $62^\circ$. Are the lines parallel?

**Solution.**
$118^\circ+62^\circ=180^\circ$, so the criterion proves the lines parallel.

Therefore, yes.
:::

:::example Rails in a design
A crossbar meets two rails and makes corresponding angles of $88^\circ$. A designer must check that the rails have the same direction. What is the conclusion?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* Record the given data, the required result and any conditions.

*Plan.* Choose the relevant definition or rule, then work in a logical order.

*Solve.* The corresponding angles are equal, so the rails are parallel in the line model.

*Look back.* Compare the result with the data and use an independent check.

Therefore, parallel.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Pairing the wrong angles.** Choosing any two apparently equal angles and calling them corresponding. The pair type depends on position relative to the intersections and transversal. Mark the interior region and transversal sides before naming the pair.
- **Reversing a property without justification.** Inferring parallel lines from any two equal angles. Only correctly positioned corresponding or alternate interior pairs give a criterion. State the pair type and the converse being used.
:::

**Connections.** Parallel criteria turn local angle observations into a global conclusion about direction. They support constructions, quadrilateral proofs, technical drawings and alignment checks. When solving a problem, separate three layers: the data, the mathematical relationship and the conclusion. Before substituting values, state why the chosen rule applies; after each step, check signs, units and conditions. A diagram, small table or simple case often reveals a missing assumption. Estimate before calculating so that you know whether the answer should be large or small, positive or negative. After obtaining a result, use an independent check: substitute it back into the data, measure it on the diagram, or test a boundary case. If one item of data changes, predict which part of the method changes and which part remains valid. This approach turns a formula into a connected system of reasons and makes the idea usable in an unfamiliar situation instead of only in a memorised exercise. It also produces written work that another student can follow, question and verify line by line.

## Summary

- Equal corresponding angles imply parallel lines.
- Equal alternate interior angles imply parallel lines.
- Supplementary co-interior angles imply parallel lines.
- Two coplanar lines perpendicular to one line are parallel.
- Identify the angle pair position before applying a criterion.

a) Two coplanar lines perpendicular to one line are parallel.
b) Equal alternate interior angles are a criterion.
c) Co-interior angles must be equal.
d) Any equal angles prove parallelism.
::key T T F F

::key 48

::key 90
Each right angle measures $90^\circ$, which then proves the lines parallel.
