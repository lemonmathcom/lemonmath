A steady printer produces $18$ pages per minute. After $x$ minutes, pages printed are $y=18x$. Ratio $\dfrac yx$ is always $18$, and doubling time doubles pages. What relationship has these features?

## direct proportion, constant of proportionality and graph

**A short exploration.**
Make a table of $(x,y)$: $(1,18),(2,36),(3,54)$. Every quotient $y\div x$ is $18$. The points lie on a straight line through the origin because $x=0$ gives $y=0$.

:::definition Direct proportion
Quantities $x,y$ are directly proportional if a constant $k\ne0$ exists such that $y=kx$. Number $k$ is the constant of proportionality and $k=\dfrac yx$ when $x\ne0$.
:::

:::definition Property and graph
If $y$ is directly proportional to $x$, then $\dfrac{y_1}{y_2}=\dfrac{x_1}{x_2}$ when denominators are valid. The graph of $y=kx$ is a straight line through the origin.
:::

**Why does this work?**
$y=kx$ shows every $y$ value is produced by the same multiplication from $x$. Replacing $x$ by $cx$ therefore replaces $y$ by $cy$. Conversely, a constant ratio $y/x$ defines one common multiplier and hence the formula.

**How to check your own work.** For a question on this topic, first state in words what is given and what must be found. Next write the rule you will use, substitute values or reason one step at a time, and then check the result in a different way: put it back into the data, estimate its size, or compare it with a diagram or number line. If two methods disagree, locate the first step where they differ instead of changing only the final answer. This habit catches sign errors, missing units and overlooked cases.

**Systems view.** direct proportion, constant of proportionality and graph is not an isolated trick. Follow the chain: the data determine a model, the model determines the calculation, and the result must return to the original situation. When one piece of data changes, predict whether the result should increase, decrease or stay unchanged before calculating again.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example Finding the constant
Given $y$ is directly proportional to $x$ and $y=28$ when $x=4$, find $k$.

**Solution.**
$k=\dfrac yx=\dfrac{28}{4}=7$.

Therefore, $7$.
:::

:::example Forming a formula
With $k=7$, write $y$ in terms of $x$ and find $y$ when $x=9$.

**Solution.**
$y=7x$; when $x=9$, $y=7\cdot9=63$.

Therefore, $y=63$.
:::

:::example Checking a table
Are pairs $(2,6),(5,15),(8,24)$ directly proportional?

**Solution.**
Ratios $6\div2=15\div5=24\div8=3$, so yes, with $k=3$.

Therefore, yes; $k=3$.
:::

:::example Cost by mass
A hypothetical grain costs $48$ thousand dong per kg. Find the cost of $2.5$ kg when there is no fixed charge.

**Solution.**

*Understand.* Record the given data, the required result and any conditions.

*Plan.* Choose the relevant definition or rule, then work in a logical order.

*Solve.* $y=48x$, so $y=48\cdot2.5=120$ thousand dong.

*Look back.* Compare the result with the data and use an independent check.

Therefore, $120$ thousand dong.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Assuming any straight line is direct proportion.** Treating $y=3x+4$ as direct proportion because its graph is straight. A direct-proportion graph must also pass through the origin. Check whether $x=0$ gives $y=0$.
- **Using differences instead of ratios.** Checking whether $y-x$ is constant to identify direct proportion. Direct proportion requires constant quotient $y/x$. Calculate $k=\dfrac yx$ for several pairs.
:::

**Connections.** Direct proportion models cost without fixed fees, steady motion, unit conversion and scaling. Formula $y=kx$ is one of the simplest and most important function models. When solving a problem, separate three layers: the data, the mathematical relationship and the conclusion. Before substituting values, state why the chosen rule applies; after each step, check signs, units and conditions. A diagram, small table or simple case often reveals a missing assumption. Estimate before calculating so that you know whether the answer should be large or small, positive or negative. After obtaining a result, use an independent check: substitute it back into the data, measure it on the diagram, or test a boundary case. If one item of data changes, predict which part of the method changes and which part remains valid. This approach turns a formula into a connected system of reasons and makes the idea usable in an unfamiliar situation instead of only in a memorised exercise. It also produces written work that another student can follow, question and verify line by line.

## Summary

- Direct proportion has form $y=kx$ with $k\ne0$.
- $k=\dfrac yx$ when $x\ne0$.
- Multiplying $x$ by a factor multiplies $y$ by the same factor.
- The graph is a straight line through the origin.
- A non-constant ratio means no direct proportion.
