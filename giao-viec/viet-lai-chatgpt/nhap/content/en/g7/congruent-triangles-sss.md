## congruent triangles and side–side–side congruence

**A short exploration.**
Construct a triangle with base $AB=8$ cm, $AC=5$ cm and $BC=7$ cm. Point $C$ must be an intersection of circles centred at $A,B$ with radii $5,7$ cm; the two possible positions are reflections across $AB$. Thus the triangle is fixed up to reflection.

:::definition Congruent triangles
Two triangles are congruent if they can coincide exactly, so corresponding sides and angles are equal. The notation $\triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF$ records $A\leftrightarrow D$, $B\leftrightarrow E$, $C\leftrightarrow F$.
:::

:::definition Side–side–side congruence
If the three sides of one triangle equal the three corresponding sides of another, the triangles are congruent by side–side–side, abbreviated SSS.
:::

**Why does this work?**
Three sides fix the three distances between vertices. After matching one side, the third vertex must lie on two circles whose radii are the remaining sides; possible positions differ only by reflection. Reflection preserves lengths and angles.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example Finding correspondence
Given $AB=DE$, $BC=EF$, $CA=FD$, write the triangle congruence in corresponding order.

**Solution.**
$A$ is the common endpoint corresponding to $D$, so $A\leftrightarrow D$; likewise $B\leftrightarrow E$, $C\leftrightarrow F$. Thus $\triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF$.

Therefore, $\triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF$.
:::

:::example Applying SSS
Two triangles have corresponding sides $4$ cm, $6$ cm and $7$ cm. What follows?

**Solution.**
All three corresponding side pairs are equal, so the triangles are congruent by SSS.

Therefore, congruent by SSS.
:::

:::example Deducing an angle
Given $\triangle ABC\cong\triangle MNP$ and $\angle B=58^\circ$, find $\angle N$.

**Solution.**
The order gives $B\leftrightarrow N$, so corresponding angles are equal: $\angle N=58^\circ$.

Therefore, $58^\circ$.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Writing vertices in the wrong order.** Writing congruent triangles with non-corresponding vertices paired. Later deductions about sides and angles then become wrong. Make a table of the three side or vertex pairs before writing the notation.
- **Using SSS with only two sides.** Treating two equal side pairs as sufficient. The included angle or remaining side can vary. Check that all three corresponding side pairs are known.
:::

**Connections.** Congruence turns length data into conclusions about angles and positions. SSS supports compass constructions and proofs involving midpoints, bisectors and symmetry. When solving a problem, separate three layers: the data, the mathematical relationship and the conclusion. Estimate before calculating so that you know whether the answer should be large or small, positive or negative. After obtaining a result, use an independent check: substitute it back into the data, measure it on the diagram, or test a boundary case. If one item of data changes, predict which part of the method changes and which part remains valid. This approach turns a formula into a connected system of reasons and makes the idea usable in an unfamiliar situation instead of only in a memorised exercise. It also produces written work that another student can follow, question and verify line by line.

## Summary

- Congruent triangles have equal corresponding sides and angles.
- Letter order in the notation determines corresponding vertices.
- Three equal corresponding side pairs give SSS congruence.
- A common side counts as an equal side pair.
- Deduce corresponding parts only after proving congruence.
