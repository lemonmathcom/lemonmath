A rectangle has area $12x^3+18x^2$ and one side $6x$. The other side is found by division, giving $2x^2+3x$. Which coefficient and exponent rules support algebraic division?

## monomial division, division by a monomial and polynomial division

**A short exploration.**
Divide term by term: $\dfrac{12x^3}{6x}=2x^2$ and $\dfrac{18x^2}{6x}=3x$. Multiplying back, $(2x^2+3x)6x=12x^3+18x^2$, checks the quotient.

:::definition Dividing monomials
When dividing monomials different from $0$, divide coefficients and subtract exponents of the same variable: $\dfrac{ax^m}{bx^n}=\dfrac abx^{m-n}$ where the expression is defined.
:::

:::definition Dividing a polynomial
To divide a polynomial by a monomial, divide every term by the monomial and add the results. In general polynomial division, find quotient and remainder so that $A=BQ+R$, where degree of $R$ is less than degree of $B$.
:::

**Why does this work?**
The exponent rule comes from cancelling common factors: $x^m\div x^n=x^{m-n}$. Distribution gives $\dfrac{A+B}{M}=\dfrac AM+\dfrac BM$ when $M\ne0$. Multiplying back reconstructs the dividend and provides a reliable check.

**How to check your own work.** For a question on this topic, first state in words what is given and what must be found. Next write the rule you will use, substitute values or reason one step at a time, and then check the result in a different way: put it back into the data, estimate its size, or compare it with a diagram or number line. If two methods disagree, locate the first step where they differ instead of changing only the final answer. This habit catches sign errors, missing units and overlooked cases.

**Systems view.** monomial division, division by a monomial and polynomial division is not an isolated trick. Follow the chain: the data determine a model, the model determines the calculation, and the result must return to the original situation. When one piece of data changes, predict whether the result should increase, decrease or stay unchanged before calculating again.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example Dividing monomials
Calculate $18x^5\div6x^2$.

**Solution.**
Divide coefficients and subtract exponents: $18\div6=3$, $x^{5-2}=x^3$, so the quotient is $3x^3$.

Therefore, $3x^3$.
:::

:::example Polynomial by monomial
Calculate $(15x^3-10x^2+5x)\div5x$.

**Solution.**
Dividing each term gives $3x^2-2x+1$.

Therefore, $3x^2-2x+1$.
:::

:::example Checking by multiplication
Check $(8x^2+12x)\div4x=2x+3$.

**Solution.**
Multiply quotient by divisor: $(2x+3)4x=8x^2+12x$, equal to the dividend.

Therefore, true.
:::

:::example A rectangle dimension
A hypothetical rectangle has area $20x^2+30x$ m² and width $10x$ m. Find its length.

**Solution.**

*Understand.* Record the given data, the required result and any conditions.

*Plan.* Choose the relevant definition or rule, then work in a logical order.

*Solve.* Length is $(20x^2+30x)\div10x=2x+3$ m, with $x\ne0$.

*Look back.* Compare the result with the data and use an independent check.

Therefore, $2x+3$ m.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Dividing only the first term.** In $(6x^2+9x)\div3x$, dividing only $6x^2$. The divisor applies to every term in the dividend. Write $2x+3$.
- **Subtracting exponents of different variables.** Combining an exponent of $x$ with one of $y$. Only powers of the same base cancel. Handle each variable separately and state denominator restrictions.
:::

**Connections.** Polynomial division reverses multiplication and supports rational-expression simplification, factorisation, finding dimensions from area and studying polynomial roots. When solving a problem, separate three layers: the data, the mathematical relationship and the conclusion. Before substituting values, state why the chosen rule applies; after each step, check signs, units and conditions. A diagram, small table or simple case often reveals a missing assumption. Estimate before calculating so that you know whether the answer should be large or small, positive or negative. After obtaining a result, use an independent check: substitute it back into the data, measure it on the diagram, or test a boundary case. If one item of data changes, predict which part of the method changes and which part remains valid. This approach turns a formula into a connected system of reasons and makes the idea usable in an unfamiliar situation instead of only in a memorised exercise. It also produces written work that another student can follow, question and verify line by line.

## Summary

- Divide coefficients and subtract exponents of the same variable.
- Divide a polynomial by a monomial term by term.
- The divisor must differ from $0$.
- $A=BQ+R$ and $\deg R<\deg B$.
- Multiply quotient by divisor and add remainder to check.
