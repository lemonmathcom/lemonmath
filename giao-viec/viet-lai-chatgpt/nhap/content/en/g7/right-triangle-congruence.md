## special congruence cases for right triangles

**A short exploration.**
In a right triangle, the $90^\circ$ angle is fixed and the acute angles are complementary. Two legs give SAS with an included right angle. A hypotenuse and one leg determine the other leg by Pythagoras, then SSS applies.

:::definition Parts of a right triangle
The side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse and is the longest side. The two sides forming the right angle are the legs.
:::

:::definition Special congruence cases
Two right triangles are congruent if their corresponding legs are equal, or if their hypotenuse and one corresponding leg are equal. One corresponding side and one acute angle, together with the right angles, may also establish congruence.
:::

**Why does this work?**
The right angles supply one equal angle pair automatically. Two legs use SAS. For hypotenuse–leg, the other leg has square $c^2-a^2$, so its positive length is uniquely determined and SSS completes the proof.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example Two legs
Two right triangles have corresponding legs $6$ cm and $8$ cm. Prove congruence.

**Solution.**
Two sides and the included angles are equal because each included angle is $90^\circ$. Thus the triangles are congruent by SAS.

Therefore, congruent.
:::

:::example Hypotenuse–leg
Two right triangles have hypotenuse $10$ cm and one leg $6$ cm. Are they congruent?

**Solution.**
The other leg in each is $\sqrt{10^2-6^2}=8$ cm. All three sides correspond, so yes.

Therefore, yes.
:::

:::example A side and an acute angle
Two right triangles have an equal corresponding leg and the adjacent acute angle is $35^\circ$ in both. Which case applies?

**Solution.**
Each also has an equal right angle; the known side lies between those angles, so ASA applies.

Therefore, ASA.
:::

:::example A stair brace
Two braces form right triangles with the floor; each brace is $2.5$ m and each vertical leg is $1.5$ m. Are the frames the same size?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* Record the given data, the required result and any conditions.

*Plan.* Choose the relevant definition or rule, then work in a logical order.

*Solve.* The hypotenuse and a corresponding leg are equal, so the right triangles are congruent. Each base is $2$ m.

*Look back.* Compare the result with the data and use an independent check.

Therefore, yes; base $2$ m.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Misidentifying the hypotenuse.** Choosing the visually longest side without locating the right angle. The hypotenuse is defined by being opposite the right angle. Mark the right angle first, then choose the opposite side.
- **Using only a hypotenuse.** Declaring right triangles congruent because their hypotenuses are equal. A fixed hypotenuse allows many different right triangles. Add a leg or a suitable angle condition.
:::

**Connections.** Special right-triangle cases shorten proofs because the right angle is fixed. They appear in altitudes, perpendicular bisectors, distances and right-angled structures. When solving a problem, separate three layers: the data, the mathematical relationship and the conclusion. Before substituting values, state why the chosen rule applies; after each step, check signs, units and conditions. A diagram, small table or simple case often reveals a missing assumption. Estimate before calculating so that you know whether the answer should be large or small, positive or negative. After obtaining a result, use an independent check: substitute it back into the data, measure it on the diagram, or test a boundary case. If one item of data changes, predict which part of the method changes and which part remains valid. This approach turns a formula into a connected system of reasons and makes the idea usable in an unfamiliar situation instead of only in a memorised exercise. It also produces written work that another student can follow, question and verify line by line.

## Summary

- The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle.
- Two equal legs give SAS.
- An equal hypotenuse and one leg establish congruence.
- A suitable side and acute angle may use ASA.
- Equal hypotenuses alone are insufficient.
