A hypothetical cold store lowers its temperature by $3^\circ$C each hour. After $4$ hours, the change is $4\cdot(-3)=-12^\circ$C. Looking $4$ hours backwards, how should the sign of the product change? Integer multiplication records both size and direction.

## multiplication, exact division, factors and multiples of integers

**A short exploration.**
Repeated addition gives $3\cdot(-2)=(-2)+(-2)+(-2)=-6$. In the sequence $3\cdot2=6$, $2\cdot2=4$, $1\cdot2=2$, $0\cdot2=0$, lowering the first factor by $1$ lowers the product by $2$, so $(-1)\cdot2=-2$. The sign rule follows naturally.

:::definition Rules for multiplying and dividing integers
To multiply two integers, multiply their absolute values; equal signs give a positive product and different signs give a negative product. In exact division by an integer other than $0$, equal signs give a positive quotient and different signs a negative quotient. Any integer multiplied by $0$ gives $0$.
:::

:::definition Factors and multiples in $\mathbb Z$
For $a,b\in\mathbb Z$ with $b\ne0$, if $a=bq$ for some $q\in\mathbb Z$, then $a$ is divisible by $b$; $b$ is a factor of $a$, and $a$ is a multiple of $b$. If $d$ is a factor of $a$, then $-d$ is also a factor of $a$.
:::

**Why does this work?**
We preserve familiar properties, especially distribution. Since $0=(-3)\cdot0=(-3)[2+(-2)]=(-3)\cdot2+(-3)\cdot(-2)=-6+(-3)\cdot(-2)$, the product $(-3)\cdot(-2)$ must be $6$. Thus two negative signs give a positive product. Division reads multiplication backwards: if $a=bq$, then $a\div b=q$.

**How to check your own work.** For a question on this topic, first state in words what is given and what must be found. Next write the rule you will use, substitute values or reason one step at a time, and then check the result in a different way: put it back into the data, estimate its size, or compare it with a diagram or number line. If two methods disagree, locate the first step where they differ instead of changing only the final answer. This habit catches sign errors, missing units and overlooked cases.

**Systems view.** multiplication, exact division, factors and multiples of integers is not an isolated trick. Follow the chain: the data determine a model, the model determines the calculation, and the result must return to the original situation. When one piece of data changes, predict whether the result should increase, decrease or stay unchanged before calculating again.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example Multiplying two integers
Calculate $(-14)\cdot6$ and $(-9)\cdot(-8)$.

**Solution.**
Different signs give $(-14)\cdot6=-(14\cdot6)=-84$. Equal signs give $(-9)\cdot(-8)=9\cdot8=72$.

Therefore, $-84$ and $72$.
:::

:::example A product of several integers
Calculate $(-2)\cdot3\cdot(-5)\cdot(-4)$.

**Solution.**
There are three negative factors, so the product is negative. Its absolute value is $2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot4=120$, so the product is $-120$.

Therefore, $-120$.
:::

:::example Exact integer division
Calculate $(-96)\div12$ and $(-96)\div(-12)$.

**Solution.**
Different signs: $(-96)\div12=-8$. Equal signs: $(-96)\div(-12)=8$.

Therefore, $-8$ and $8$.
:::

:::example Finding integer factors
List the integer factors of $18$.

**Solution.**
The positive factors are $1,2,3,6,9,18$. Adding their opposites gives $\pm1,\pm2,\pm3,\pm6,\pm9,\pm18$.

Therefore, $\{\pm1,\pm2,\pm3,\pm6,\pm9,\pm18\}$.
:::

:::example A temperature change
A cold store cools by $3^\circ$C per hour for $4$ hours. If it begins at $5^\circ$C, what is the final temperature?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* We know the initial temperature, hourly decrease and number of hours.

*Plan.* Find the total change by multiplication, then add it to the starting temperature.

*Solve.* The hourly change is $-3^\circ$C, so the total change is $4\cdot(-3)=-12^\circ$C. The final temperature is $5+(-12)=-7^\circ$C.

*Look back.* A fall of $12^\circ$C from $5^\circ$C passes $0$ and stops at $-7^\circ$C. How would the decrease change if the time doubled?

Therefore, the final temperature is $-7^\circ$C.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Assuming negative times negative stays negative.** Wrong: $(-4)(-7)=-28$. Factors with equal signs give a positive product. Correct: $(-4)(-7)=28$.
- **Listing positive factors only.** In $\mathbb Z$, saying that the factors of $12$ are only $1,2,3,4,6,12$ is incomplete. If $d\mid12$, then $-d\mid12$. Include $\pm1,\pm2,\pm3,\pm4,\pm6,\pm12$.
:::

**Connections.** Integer multiplication combines magnitude with the direction of change. Distribution supports efficient calculation and later algebraic simplification. Divisibility, factors and multiples extend from natural numbers to integers by including opposites.

## Summary

- Equal signs give a positive product or quotient; different signs give a negative one.
- A product with an even number of negative factors is positive; with an odd number it is negative.
- $a=bq$ means that $a$ is divisible by $b$, where $b\ne0$.
- Integer factors occur in opposite pairs.
- Every multiple of $a$ has the form $ak$, $k\in\mathbb Z$.
