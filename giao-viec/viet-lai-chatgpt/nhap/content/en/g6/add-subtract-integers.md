A game awards $8$ points for a correct answer and subtracts $11$ for an incorrect one. You have $6$ points, then answer one question correctly and one incorrectly. Your final score is $6+8-11$. How can we calculate it quickly without a sign error?

## adding and subtracting integers and removing brackets

**A short exploration.**
On a number line, adding $4$ means moving $4$ units right, while adding $-4$ means moving $4$ units left. Thus $3+(-4)=-1$. The subtraction $3-4$ gives the same movement, suggesting that subtracting $4$ is adding its opposite, $-4$.

:::definition Adding and subtracting integers
To add integers with the same sign, add their absolute values and keep the common sign. With different signs, subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger and use the sign of the number with greater absolute value. For all $a,b\in\mathbb Z$, $a-b=a+(-b)$ to subtract integers.
:::

:::definition Rules for removing brackets
When removing brackets preceded by $+$, keep every sign inside. When removing brackets preceded by $-$, change every sign inside. Integer addition is commutative and associative.
:::

**Why does this work?**
The opposite of $b$ adds to $b$ to give $0$. Since $(a-b)+b=a$, the operation that cancels $b$ is addition of $-b$, so $a-b=a+(-b)$. A minus before brackets means taking the opposite of the whole sum: $-(b+c)=(-b)+(-c)$, so every sign inside must change.

**How to check your own work.** For a question on this topic, first state in words what is given and what must be found. Next write the rule you will use, substitute values or reason one step at a time, and then check the result in a different way: put it back into the data, estimate its size, or compare it with a diagram or number line. If two methods disagree, locate the first step where they differ instead of changing only the final answer. This habit catches sign errors, missing units and overlooked cases.

**Systems view.** adding and subtracting integers and removing brackets is not an isolated trick. Follow the chain: the data determine a model, the model determines the calculation, and the result must return to the original situation. When one piece of data changes, predict whether the result should increase, decrease or stay unchanged before calculating again.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example Adding integers with the same sign
Calculate $(-17)+(-9)$.

**Solution.**
Both numbers are negative, so add the absolute values and keep the negative sign: $(-17)+(-9)=-(17+9)=-26$.

Therefore, $-26$.
:::

:::example Adding integers with different signs
Calculate $(-23)+15$.

**Solution.**
$|-23|>|15|$, so the result is negative: $(-23)+15=-(23-15)=-8$.

Therefore, $-8$.
:::

:::example Changing subtraction into addition
Calculate $12-(-7)$ and $-5-9$.

**Solution.**
$12-(-7)=12+7=19$. Also, $-5-9=-5+(-9)=-14$.

Therefore, $19$ and $-14$.
:::

:::example Removing brackets
Calculate $18-[7-(-5)]+(-4)$.

**Solution.**
Remove brackets after the minus and change every sign: $18-[7-(-5)]+(-4)=18-7-5-4=2$.

Therefore, $2$.
:::

:::example A game score
You have $6$ points, gain $8$, then lose $11$. Find the final score.

**Solution.**

*Understand.* Points gained are positive and points lost are negative.

*Plan.* Form a signed sum and group conveniently.

*Solve.* The final score is $6+8-11=14-11=3$. Alternatively, group $8-11=-3$ and calculate $6+(-3)=3$.

*Look back.* $3$ is $3$ less than $6$ because the net change is $8-11=-3$. If the incorrect answer lost only $7$ points, would the final score rise or fall?

Therefore, the final score is $3$.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Keeping the minus when subtracting a negative.** Wrong: $5-(-3)=5-3$. Subtracting a number means adding its opposite; the opposite of $-3$ is $3$. Correct: $5-(-3)=5+3=8$.
- **Changing only the first sign in brackets.** Wrong: $-(4-7)=-4-7$. The minus applies to the entire expression in brackets. Correct: $-(4-7)=-4+7=3$.
:::

**Connections.** Adding and subtracting integers describe directed changes such as temperature rises and falls, income and spending, or upward and downward movement. Bracket rules prepare for simplifying algebraic expressions; their basis is still replacing subtraction by addition of the opposite.

## Summary

- Same signs: add absolute values and keep the sign.
- Different signs: subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger and use the sign of the larger.
- $a-b=a+(-b)$.
- A $+$ before brackets keeps signs; a $-$ changes every sign.
- Terms in a sum may be reordered and regrouped for convenience.
