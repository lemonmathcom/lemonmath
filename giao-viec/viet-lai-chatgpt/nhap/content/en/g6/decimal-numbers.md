A hypothetical shop labels two bags of fruit as $1.25$ kg and $0.8$ kg. Their total may be wrong if tenths and hundredths are not aligned. Why may $0.8$ be written as $0.80$, and how can we calculate efficiently while preserving every digit’s place value?

## decimals and calculations with decimals

**A short exploration.**
$1.25=1+\dfrac2{10}+\dfrac5{100}$, while $0.8=\dfrac8{10}=\dfrac{80}{100}=0.80$. Hence $1.25+0.80=2.05$. Digits in corresponding place-value columns are combined just as with natural numbers.

:::definition Decimal number
A decimal has a whole-number part and a decimal part separated by a decimal point. Places to the right are tenths, hundredths, thousandths, and so on. Adding or removing trailing $0$ digits on the right does not change the value.
:::

:::definition The four operations
For addition and subtraction, align decimal points. For multiplication, multiply as natural numbers and place the decimal point so that the product has the total number of decimal places in the factors. For division by a decimal, multiply dividend and divisor by the same power of $10$ to make the divisor a natural number.
:::

**Why does this work?**
Every decimal is a fraction with denominator $10,100,1000,\ldots$. Aligning columns for addition or subtraction puts quantities in the same unit. In multiplication, fraction denominators multiply, so decimal-place counts add. Multiplying dividend and divisor by the same number other than $0$ preserves the quotient, which justifies shifting both decimal points in division.

**How to check your own work.** For a question on this topic, first state in words what is given and what must be found. Next write the rule you will use, substitute values or reason one step at a time, and then check the result in a different way: put it back into the data, estimate its size, or compare it with a diagram or number line. If two methods disagree, locate the first step where they differ instead of changing only the final answer. This habit catches sign errors, missing units and overlooked cases.

**Systems view.** decimals and calculations with decimals is not an isolated trick. Follow the chain: the data determine a model, the model determines the calculation, and the result must return to the original situation. When one piece of data changes, predict whether the result should increase, decrease or stay unchanged before calculating again.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example Place value
Write $-12.305$ as a place-value sum and state the hundredths digit.

**Solution.**
$-12.305=-\left(10+2+\dfrac3{10}+\dfrac5{1000}\right)$. The hundredths digit is $0$.

Therefore, the hundredths digit is $0$.
:::

:::example Addition and subtraction
Calculate $7.48+2.6$ and $15-8.375$.

**Solution.**
Write $2.6=2.60$ and $15=15.000$. Then $7.48+2.60=10.08$ and $15.000-8.375=6.625$.

Therefore, $10.08$ and $6.625$.
:::

:::example Decimal multiplication
Calculate $3.24\cdot1.5$.

**Solution.**
$324\cdot15=4860$. The factors have $3$ decimal places in total, so the product is $4.860=4.86$.

Therefore, $4.86$.
:::

:::example Division by a decimal
Calculate $12.6\div0.35$.

**Solution.**
Multiply both numbers by $100$: $12.6\div0.35=1260\div35=36$.

Therefore, $36$.
:::

:::example Mass of two fruit bags
Two bags weigh $1.25$ kg and $0.8$ kg. An empty box weighs $0.35$ kg. Find the total mass of the box and fruit.

**Solution.**

*Understand.* Add three masses, all measured in kilograms.

*Plan.* Write two decimal places for each number and add by columns.

*Solve.* $1.25+0.80+0.35=2.40$ kg.

*Look back.* $2.4$ kg is greater than each component and less than $3$ kg, so it is reasonable. Removing the box reduces the total by exactly $0.35$ kg.

Therefore, the mass is $2.4$ kg.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Not aligning decimal points.** Adding $2.5$ and $0.37$ as if they were $25+37$. The digit $5$ is in the tenths place, while $7$ is in the hundredths place. $2.50+0.37=2.87$.
- **Shifting only the dividend.** $4.8\div0.6=48\div0.6$. Dividend and divisor must be multiplied by the same number. $4.8\div0.6=48\div6=8$.
:::

**Connections.** A decimal is a base-$10$ fraction notation, so every rule comes from place value. Before calculating, estimate with nearby numbers: $3.24\cdot1.5$ should be near $3\cdot1.5=4.5$, so an answer such as $48.6$ must have a misplaced decimal point. When dividing a positive number by a number below $1$, the quotient should be larger than the dividend; when multiplying by a number below $1$, the product should be smaller than the other positive factor. For money, mass and length, keep the unit in the conclusion and remove trailing $0$ digits only after the calculation is complete. An inverse calculation, such as multiplying the quotient by the divisor, provides an independent check. These skills prepare for rounding, estimation, percentages and statistical data. Clear column layout also makes regrouping visible and prevents digits from silently moving between places.

## Summary

- Adding or removing trailing $0$ digits does not change a decimal’s value.
- For addition and subtraction, align decimal points.
- For multiplication, product decimal places equal the total in the factors.
- For division by a decimal, shift both decimal points by the same number of places.
- Estimate and use inverse operations to check.
