A hypothetical library has $240$ storybooks, of which $\dfrac38$ are science stories. We find the science books as a fraction of $240$. Conversely, if $90$ books are $\dfrac38$ of the total, we must find the whole. The two questions use inverse operations.

## two key fraction problems

**A short exploration.**
$\dfrac38$ of $240$ is $240\cdot\dfrac38=90$. Reversing the statement, if $\dfrac38$ of a number is $90$, the number is $90\div\dfrac38=240$. Multiplication moves from the whole to a part; division moves from a known part to the whole.

:::definition Finding a fraction of a quantity
To find $\dfrac mn$ of a quantity $a$, calculate $a\cdot\dfrac mn$ ($n\ne0$).
:::

:::definition Finding the whole from a fractional part
If $\dfrac mn$ of $x$ is $b$ and $m,n\ne0$, then $x=b\div\dfrac mn=b\cdot\dfrac nm$.
:::

**Why does this work?**
Let the whole be $x$. The phrase “$\dfrac mn$ of $x$” means $x\cdot\dfrac mn$. If the whole is known, substitute for $x$ and multiply. If the part $b$ is known, solve $x\cdot\dfrac mn=b$ by division. Deciding whether the question asks for a part or the whole matters more than memorising a label.

**How to check your own work.** For a question on this topic, first state in words what is given and what must be found. Next write the rule you will use, substitute values or reason one step at a time, and then check the result in a different way: put it back into the data, estimate its size, or compare it with a diagram or number line. If two methods disagree, locate the first step where they differ instead of changing only the final answer. This habit catches sign errors, missing units and overlooked cases.

**Systems view.** two key fraction problems is not an isolated trick. Follow the chain: the data determine a model, the model determines the calculation, and the result must return to the original situation. When one piece of data changes, predict whether the result should increase, decrease or stay unchanged before calculating again.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example Finding a part
Find $\dfrac7{12}$ of $96$.

**Solution.**
$96\cdot\dfrac7{12}=8\cdot7=56$.

Therefore, $56$.
:::

:::example Finding the whole
$\dfrac58$ of a number is $45$. Find the number.

**Solution.**
The number is $45\div\dfrac58=45\cdot\dfrac85=72$.

Therefore, $72$.
:::

:::example Finding the remainder
A container holds $150$ kg of rice and $\dfrac25$ is sold. Find the mass remaining.

**Solution.**
The remaining fraction is $1-\dfrac25=\dfrac35$. The remaining mass is $150\cdot\dfrac35=90$ kg.

Therefore, $90$ kg.
:::

:::example Books in a library
$90$ science storybooks make up $\dfrac38$ of a library collection. How many storybooks are there?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* The part $90$ and fraction $\dfrac38$ are known; find the whole.

*Plan.* Divide the part value by its fraction.

*Solve.* Let the total be $x$. We have $\dfrac38x=90$, so $x=90\div\dfrac38=240$.

*Look back.* $\dfrac38$ of $240$ is $90$, so the result checks. If the part rises while the fraction stays fixed, the whole must also rise.

Therefore, the library has $240$ storybooks.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Dividing when finding a part.** Wrong: finding $\dfrac34$ of $20$ by $20\div\dfrac34$. A fraction below $1$ of a whole must be smaller than the whole. $20\cdot\dfrac34=15$.
- **Multiplying when finding the whole.** Wrong: $18$ is $\dfrac23$ of a number, so calculate $18\cdot\dfrac23$. The whole must be greater than the part $18$. $18\div\dfrac23=27$.
:::

**Connections.** The two problems are opposite directions of one relation: $\text{part}=\text{whole}\times\text{fraction}$. Before calculating, draw a bar for the whole, shade the known part and mark whether the question asks for a part or for the whole. When the fraction is below $1$, the part must be smaller than the whole; this is a powerful estimate. After finding an answer, always perform the inverse check: multiplying the proposed whole by the fraction must reproduce the known part. In a multi-stage problem, a fraction may refer either to the original whole or to what remains, so read phrases such as “of the original amount” and “of the remainder” carefully. The part–whole model later supports percentages, discounts, statistics and rates of work. It also helps organise units and shows why the two standard procedures are not unrelated tricks but inverse uses of one equation.

## Summary

- To find $\dfrac mn$ of $a$, calculate $a\cdot\dfrac mn$.
- If $\dfrac mn$ of a number is $b$, calculate $b\div\dfrac mn$.
- For a remainder, first find the remaining fraction.
- Distinguish a fraction of the whole from a fraction of the remainder.
- Check with the inverse calculation and include units.
