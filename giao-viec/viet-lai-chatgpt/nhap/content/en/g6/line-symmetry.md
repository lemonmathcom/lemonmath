Fold a square along the line joining the midpoints of two opposite sides and the halves coincide. Rotate it through $180^\circ$ about the intersection of its diagonals and the shape also coincides with itself. How are these two kinds of matching alike and different?

## line symmetry and centre symmetry

**A short exploration.**
Draw a point $A$ and its reflection $A\prime$ in a line $d$. The line $d$ is perpendicular to $AA\prime$ and passes through the midpoint of $AA\prime$. For centre symmetry about $O$, the points $A,O,A\prime$ are collinear and $OA=OA\prime$. One operation reflects in a line; the other is a half-turn about a point.

:::definition Line of symmetry
A line $d$ is a line of symmetry of a shape if folding along $d$ makes the two parts coincide; equivalently, reflecting the shape in $d$ gives the original shape.
:::

:::definition Centre of symmetry
A point $O$ is a centre of symmetry of a shape if a rotation of $180^\circ$ about $O$ maps the shape onto itself. Each point $A$ is paired with a point $A\prime$ such that $O$ is the midpoint of $AA\prime$.
:::

**Why does this work?**
For a fold along an axis, each point and its image are equally far from the line on one perpendicular, so the halves coincide. Under a half-turn, the direction from the centre to a point reverses while its length stays fixed; hence the centre is the midpoint of corresponding points. These conditions replace a vague judgement that a shape “looks balanced”.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example Lines of symmetry of a rectangle
How many lines of symmetry does a non-square rectangle have?

**Solution.**
The two lines through the midpoints of opposite sides make matching halves. The diagonals are not axes because they would interchange a long side with a short side.

Therefore, $2$ lines of symmetry.
:::

:::example Axes of an equilateral triangle
How many lines of symmetry does an equilateral triangle have?

**Solution.**
Each line through a vertex and the midpoint of the opposite side is an axis. There are three vertices, so there are three axes.

Therefore, $3$ lines of symmetry.
:::

:::example Centre of a parallelogram
Identify the centre of symmetry of parallelogram $ABCD$.

**Solution.**
The diagonals bisect each other. A rotation of $180^\circ$ about their intersection swaps $A$ with $C$ and $B$ with $D$, so the shape maps onto itself.

Therefore, the intersection of the diagonals is the centre.
:::

:::example Designing a symbol
A symbol consists of two equal arrows pointing in opposite directions, with midpoint $O$ between them. A rotation of $180^\circ$ swaps the arrows. What symmetry does the symbol have?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* The data directly describe the result of a half-turn.

*Plan.* Compare with the definition of centre symmetry without assuming a fold.

*Solve.* A $180^\circ$ rotation about $O$ leaves the whole symbol unchanged, so $O$ is a centre of symmetry. There is not enough information to conclude that it has a line of symmetry.

*Look back.* Each arrow maps exactly to the other. Would the half-turn still match if the arrows had different lengths?

Therefore, the symbol has centre of symmetry $O$.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Any line splitting area equally is an axis.** Equal areas need not coincide when folded. Symmetry requires every point to have a matching image. Check perpendicular distances to the line and exact coincidence.
- **Confusing centre and line symmetry.** Calling a point an axis of symmetry. An axis is a line; a centre is a point. Folding/reflection uses an axis; a $180^\circ$ rotation uses a centre.
:::

**Connections.** Symmetry describes patterns, design and geometry through transformations rather than sizes alone. A square has both line and centre symmetry; an equilateral triangle has line but not centre symmetry; a general parallelogram has centre but not line symmetry.

## Summary

- A line of symmetry is a fold line that makes the halves coincide.
- A point and its reflection are equally distant from the axis on a perpendicular.
- A centre of symmetry leaves a shape unchanged after a $180^\circ$ rotation.
- The centre is the midpoint of corresponding points.
- A shape may have both types, one type, or neither.
