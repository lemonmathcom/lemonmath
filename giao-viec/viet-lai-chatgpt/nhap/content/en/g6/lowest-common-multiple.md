A green light flashes every $18$ seconds and a red light every $24$ seconds. They have just flashed together. What is the least time until they flash together again? We need the lowest common multiple.

## common multiples and the lowest common multiple

**A short exploration.**
The first positive multiples of $6$ are $6,12,18,24,30,36,\ldots$; those of $8$ are $8,16,24,32,40,48,\ldots$. The least positive common multiple is $24$.

:::definition Common multiples and lowest common multiple
A common multiple of two or more numbers is divisible by each number. The least common multiple different from $0$ is the lowest common multiple, written $\operatorname{LCM}(a,b)$.
:::

:::definition Using prime factors
Prime-factorise the numbers, take every prime factor that occurs with its greatest exponent, then multiply.
:::

**Why does this work?**
A common multiple must contain all prime factors needed by each number. Each exponent must therefore be at least the greatest corresponding exponent; choosing exactly those exponents gives the lowest common multiple.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example Finding the LCM by listing
Find the LCM of $4$ and $10$.

**Solution.**
The positive multiples of $4$ are $4,8,12,16,20,\ldots$; those of $10$ are $10,20,30,\ldots$; the first common value is $20$.

Therefore, $20$.
:::

:::example Using prime factors
Find the LCM of $18$ and $24$.

**Solution.**
We have $18=2\cdot3^2$ and $24=2^3\cdot3$. Taking all factors with the greatest exponents gives $LCM=72$.

Therefore, $72$.
:::

:::example Three numbers
Find the LCM of $6,15,20$.

**Solution.**
$6=2\cdot3$, $15=3\cdot5$, $20=2^2\cdot5$. Taking $2^2\cdot3\cdot5$ gives 60.

Therefore, $60$.
:::

:::example Two flashing lights
One light flashes every $18$ seconds and another every $24$ seconds. If they flash together at hour $8$, when do they first flash together again?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* The waiting time must be the least positive common multiple of $18$ and $24$.

*Plan.* We find the LCM by prime factorisation.

*Solve.* $18=2\cdot3^2$ and $24=2^3\cdot3$, so $\operatorname{LCM}(18,24)=2^3\cdot3^2=72$ seconds. The $72$ seconds equal $1$ minute $12$ seconds.

*Look back.* $72$ is divisible by both $18$ and $24$; all positive common multiples are multiples of $72$. How would the meeting time change if one period changed?

Therefore, the lights flash together again at $8$ h $1$ min $12$ s.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Taking common factors only.** That gives a common factor, not necessarily a multiple of each number. The LCM uses every factor with its greatest exponent. Check the result by division against each original number.
:::

**Connections.** The ideas about common multiples and the lowest common multiple build on earlier learning and will be used in later topics. Notice the link between the data, the rule and the conclusion.

## Summary

- A common multiple is divisible by each given number.
- The LCM is the least positive common multiple.
- Use the greatest exponent of every prime factor present.
- Problems about repeating cycles meeting again usually use the LCM.
