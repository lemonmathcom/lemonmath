## perimeter and area of familiar quadrilaterals

**A short exploration.**
Arrange unit squares in a $4\times3$ rectangle. There are $4$ squares in each of $3$ rows, so the area is $4\cdot3=12$ square units. Walking around the boundary gives $4+3+4+3=14$ units. Area measures coverage; perimeter measures the boundary.

:::definition Perimeter
The perimeter of a polygon is the sum of its side lengths. A rectangle has $P=2(a+b)$; a square of side $a$ has $P=4a$.
:::

:::definition Area
Rectangle: $A=ab$; square: $A=a^2$; parallelogram or rhombus: $A=ah$, where $h$ is the perpendicular height to base $a$; trapezium: $A=\dfrac{(a+b)h}{2}$, where $a,b$ are the parallel sides.
:::

**Why does this work?**
Cutting a triangle from one end of a parallelogram and moving it to the other makes a rectangle with the same base and perpendicular height, so its area is $ah$. Two congruent trapeziums join to form a parallelogram with base $a+b$ and height $h$; one trapezium is half of it, giving $A=\dfrac{(a+b)h}{2}$.

**How to check your own work.** For a question on this topic, first state in words what is given and what must be found. Next write the rule you will use, substitute values or reason one step at a time, and then check the result in a different way: put it back into the data, estimate its size, or compare it with a diagram or number line. If two methods disagree, locate the first step where they differ instead of changing only the final answer. This habit catches sign errors, missing units and overlooked cases.

**Systems view.** perimeter and area of familiar quadrilaterals is not an isolated trick. Follow the chain: the data determine a model, the model determines the calculation, and the result must return to the original situation. When one piece of data changes, predict whether the result should increase, decrease or stay unchanged before calculating again.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example A rectangle
Find the perimeter and area of a rectangle $9$ cm long and $5$ cm wide.

**Solution.**
$P=2(9+5)=28$ cm; $A=9\cdot5=45$ cm².

Therefore, $P=28$ cm, $A=45$ cm².
:::

:::example A parallelogram
A parallelogram has base $12$ cm, other side $7$ cm and perpendicular height $5$ cm to the base. Find its perimeter and area.

**Solution.**
$P=2(12+7)=38$ cm. Area uses the perpendicular height, not the sloping side: $A=12\cdot5=60$ cm².

Therefore, $P=38$ cm, $A=60$ cm².
:::

:::example A trapezium
A trapezium has parallel sides $8$ cm and $14$ cm and height $6$ cm. Find its area.

**Solution.**
$A=\dfrac{(8+14)\cdot6}{2}=66$ cm².

Therefore, $66$ cm².
:::

:::example Fencing and turf
A rectangular garden is $18$ m by $12$ m and has a $3$ m gate. Find the fencing length and the turf area.

**Solution.**

*Understand.* The gate reduces the fencing length but not the garden area.

*Plan.* Find the perimeter and subtract the gate; find the area by length times width.

*Solve.* The perimeter is $2(18+12)=60$ m, so the fence is $60-3=57$ m long. The turf area is $18\cdot12=216$ m².

*Look back.* $57<60$ by exactly the gate width, and area is in m². Which quantity would change if a path were added inside?

Therefore, $57$ m of fencing and $216$ m² of turf.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Using a sloping side as the height.** For a parallelogram with base $12$ cm and side $7$ cm, writing $A=12\cdot7$. The height must be perpendicular to the base; the side may be sloping. Use $A=ah$, where $h$ is the perpendicular distance between the parallel sides.
- **Mixing units.** Writing an area as $45$ cm. Area counts unit squares. Write $45$ cm²; perimeter uses cm.
:::

**Connections.** Perimeter is one-dimensional, while area is two-dimensional. If every length is multiplied by $k$, perimeter is multiplied by $k$ but area by $k^2$. This difference is a useful check when a shape is enlarged or reduced.

## Summary

- Perimeter is the sum of side lengths.
- Rectangle: $P=2(a+b)$, $A=ab$.
- Square: $P=4a$, $A=a^2$.
- Parallelogram and rhombus: $A=ah$.
- Trapezium: $A=\dfrac{(a+b)h}{2}$.
- Convert all lengths to the same unit before calculating.
