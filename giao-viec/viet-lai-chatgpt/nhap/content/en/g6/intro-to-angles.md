## angles, angle measure and types of angles

**A short exploration.**
Draw rays $OA,OB$ with common endpoint $O$. Place the protractor centre at $O$, align the $0^\circ$ edge with $OA$, and read where $OB$ crosses the scale. As $OB$ turns farther from $OA$, the measure increases.

:::definition Angles and their parts
Two rays with a common endpoint form an angle. The common endpoint is the vertex and the rays are the arms. An angle with vertex $O$ and arms $OA,OB$ is written $\angle AOB$ or $\angle BOA$.
:::

:::definition Measure and classification
Angle measure uses degrees. An acute angle is between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$; a right angle is $90^\circ$; an obtuse angle is between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$; a straight angle is $180^\circ$.
:::

**Why does this work?**
Measure describes the turn from one arm to the other and is independent of how long the rays are drawn. The middle letter in a three-letter name is always the vertex, so reading the name correctly locates the protractor centre.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example Naming an angle
An angle has vertex $M$ and arms $MN,MP$. Give two three-letter names.

**Solution.**
The vertex must be in the middle, so the names are $\angle NMP$ and $\angle PMN$.

Therefore, $\angle NMP,\angle PMN$.
:::

:::example Classifying an angle
Classify an angle measuring $128^\circ$.

**Solution.**
Since $90^\circ<128^\circ<180^\circ$, it is obtuse.

Therefore, obtuse.
:::

:::example Finding a remaining angle
A right angle is divided by ray $OC$ into angles of $37^\circ$ and $x$. Find $x$.

**Solution.**
$x=90^\circ-37^\circ=53^\circ$.

Therefore, $53^\circ$.
:::

:::example A robot turn
A robot faces north and turns clockwise through $135^\circ$. What type is the turn, and how many more degrees make a straight angle?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* Record the given data, the required result and any conditions.

*Plan.* Choose the relevant definition or rule, then work in a logical order.

*Solve.* $135^\circ$ is obtuse. The missing turn is $180^\circ-135^\circ=45^\circ$.

*Look back.* Compare the result with the data and use an independent check.

Therefore, obtuse; $45^\circ$.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Misplacing the protractor centre.** Putting the protractor centre on an arm rather than at the vertex. The reading then does not represent the turn about the vertex. Place the centre at the vertex and one arm through $0^\circ$.
- **Choosing the wrong scale.** Reading $120^\circ$ for an angle that is clearly acute. The two protractor scales increase in opposite directions. Start at the $0^\circ$ mark aligned with the arm and check the angle type.
:::

**Connections.** Angles measure change of direction. The idea appears in triangles, parallel lines, maps, design, rotational motion and later trigonometry. Estimate before calculating so that you know whether the answer should be large or small, positive or negative. After obtaining a result, use an independent check: substitute it back into the data, measure it on the diagram, or test a boundary case. If one item of data changes, predict which part of the method changes and which part remains valid. This approach turns a formula into a connected system of reasons and makes the idea usable in an unfamiliar situation instead of only in a memorised exercise. It also produces written work that another student can follow, question and verify line by line.

## Summary

- Two rays with a common endpoint form an angle.
- In $\angle AOB$, letter $O$ is the vertex.
- Angle measure is independent of drawn arm length.
- A right angle is $90^\circ$ and a straight angle is $180^\circ$.
- Non-overlapping adjacent angles can have their measures added.
