A design team wants to arrange tiles for a schoolyard pattern. Equilateral triangles can form a regular hexagon, while rectangles cover a region in rows and columns. To choose the right tile, we must recognise a shape from its sides, angles and diagonals. Is a quadrilateral with four equal sides necessarily a square?

## familiar plane shapes

**A short exploration.**
Draw two perpendicular segments that bisect each other, then join their four endpoints in order. If the segments are equal, the result is a square; if they are unequal, it is a rhombus. Both have four equal sides, but their angles need not be the same.

:::definition Regular shapes
An equilateral triangle has three equal sides and three $60^\circ$ angles. A square has four equal sides and four right angles. A regular hexagon has six equal sides and six equal angles; joining its centre to the vertices divides it into six equilateral triangles.
:::

:::definition Familiar quadrilaterals
A rectangle has four right angles. A rhombus has four equal sides. A parallelogram has two pairs of opposite sides parallel. An isosceles trapezium has one pair of opposite sides parallel and equal non-parallel sides. A square is both a rectangle and a rhombus.
:::

**Why does this work?**
A shape is named by properties that must remain true, not by how it is rotated on the page. A rectangle remains a rectangle when tilted because its four angles are still right angles. One property may not be enough: four equal sides guarantee a rhombus, but a right angle is also needed to conclude that it is a square.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example Recognising a shape from properties
A quadrilateral has four equal sides and one right angle. What shape is it?

**Solution.**
Four equal sides give a rhombus. Adjacent angles of a rhombus are supplementary; if one is $90^\circ$, all four are $90^\circ$.

Therefore, it is a square.
:::

:::example Angles of a regular hexagon
Join the centre $O$ of regular hexagon $ABCDEF$ to its vertices. Find $\angle AOB$.

**Solution.**
The six equal angles at the centre make a full turn of $360^\circ$, so $\angle AOB=360^\circ\div6=60^\circ$.

Therefore, $60^\circ$.
:::

:::example Using diagonals
A parallelogram has equal diagonals. Identify the shape.

**Solution.**
In a parallelogram, the diagonals bisect each other. When they are also equal, the resulting congruent triangles show that the angles of the shape are right angles.

Therefore, the shape is a rectangle.
:::

:::example Choosing a tile for a frame
A frame needs quadrilateral tiles with exactly one pair of opposite sides parallel and equal non-parallel sides. Which shape should be chosen?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* We must combine the conditions about parallel sides and non-parallel sides.

*Plan.* Compare the conditions with each quadrilateral definition.

*Solve.* One pair of opposite sides parallel gives a trapezium; equal non-parallel sides make it isosceles.

*Look back.* A parallelogram has two pairs of opposite sides parallel, so it does not meet “exactly one pair”. What could we conclude if the equal-side condition were removed?

Therefore, choose an isosceles trapezium.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Renaming a tilted shape.** A tilted square is called a rhombus but “not a square”. Rotating a shape does not change side lengths or angles. It remains a square and is also a special rhombus.
- **Using an insufficient property.** Four equal sides must make a square. A non-square rhombus also has four equal sides. Add a right angle or an equivalent condition.
:::

**Connections.** These shapes form a hierarchy: a square is a special rectangle and a special rhombus, and both are special parallelograms. Recognising this structure lets us transfer known properties from a broad class to its special cases.

## Summary

- A regular shape has equal sides and equal angles.
- A rectangle has four right angles; a rhombus has four equal sides.
- A parallelogram has two pairs of opposite sides parallel.
- An isosceles trapezium has one pair of opposite sides parallel and equal non-parallel sides.
- A square is both a rectangle and a rhombus.
