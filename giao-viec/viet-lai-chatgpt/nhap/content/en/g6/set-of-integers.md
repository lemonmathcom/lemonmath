On a hypothetical morning, the temperature in Sa Pa is $3^\circ$C. By night it falls by $7^\circ$C, passing below $0^\circ$C. Natural numbers cannot describe the new temperature; we need numbers on the other side of $0$. What is the night-time temperature?

## the set of integers

**A short exploration.**
On a number line, move three units right from $0$ to $3$, or three units left to $-3$. The points are equally far from $0$ but on opposite sides. We call $3$ and $-3$ opposites.

:::definition Integers and the set of integers
The natural numbers other than $0$ are positive integers; the numbers $-1,-2,-3,\ldots$ are negative integers. Together with $0$, they form $\mathbb Z=\{\ldots,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,\ldots\}$. The number $0$ is neither positive nor negative.
:::

:::definition Opposites and absolute value
The opposite of $a$ is $-a$. The absolute value $|a|$ is the distance from $a$ to $0$ on the number line, so it is always non-negative: $|a|\ge0$.
:::

**Why does this work?**
On a number line, a number to the right is always greater than one to the left. Every positive number lies to the right of $0$, and every negative number to the left of $0$, so a positive integer is greater than $0$ and any negative integer. Of two negative integers, the one closer to $0$ lies farther right and is greater; for example, $-2>-5$.

**How to check your own work.** For a question on this topic, first state in words what is given and what must be found. Next write the rule you will use, substitute values or reason one step at a time, and then check the result in a different way: put it back into the data, estimate its size, or compare it with a diagram or number line. If two methods disagree, locate the first step where they differ instead of changing only the final answer. This habit catches sign errors, missing units and overlooked cases.

**Systems view.** the set of integers is not an isolated trick. Follow the chain: the data determine a model, the model determines the calculation, and the result must return to the original situation. When one piece of data changes, predict whether the result should increase, decrease or stay unchanged before calculating again.

## Worked examples and applications

:::example Recognising integers
Which of $-7,0,4,\dfrac32,-2.5$ are integers?

**Solution.**
$-7,0,4$ belong to $\mathbb Z$. The numbers $\dfrac32$ and $-2.5$ are not integers because they are not whole-number points on the number line.

Therefore, the integers are $-7,0,4$.
:::

:::example Opposites and absolute values
Find the opposite and absolute value of $-8$ and $5$.

**Solution.**
The opposite of $-8$ is $8$, and $|-8|=8$. The opposite of $5$ is $-5$, and $|5|=5$.

Therefore, $8,-5$; the absolute values are $8,5$, respectively.
:::

:::example Ordering integers
Arrange $-6,3,0,-1,5$ in ascending order.

**Solution.**
$-6,-1,0,3,5$ appear from left to right on the number line.

Therefore, $-6<-1<0<3<5$.
:::

:::example A temperature change
The morning temperature is $3^\circ$C and then falls by $7^\circ$C. Represent the new temperature by an integer.

**Solution.**

*Understand.* A fall in temperature means moving towards smaller numbers.

*Plan.* Use the number line, starting at $3$ and moving $7$ units left.

*Solve.* Start at $3$ and move $7$ units left on the number line: $3\to2\to1\to0\to-1\to-2\to-3\to-4$.

*Look back.* The distance from $3$ to $-4$ is $7$ units, so the result fits. A fall of only $3^\circ$C would stop at $0^\circ$C.

Therefore, the new temperature is $-4^\circ$C.
:::

## Common mistakes and connections

:::warning What can go wrong?
- **Calling $0$ a positive integer.** This is wrong because positive integers begin at $1$. $0$ is the boundary between positive and negative integers. Correct: $0\in\mathbb Z$ but is neither positive nor negative.
- **Comparing negatives by their natural-number parts.** Wrong: $-9>-4$ because $9>4$. $-9$ is left of $-4$ on the number line. Correct: $-9<-4$.
:::

**Connections.** Integers extend natural numbers to describe heights below a reference level, temperatures below $0$, debts and opposite directions. The number line turns comparison into position; absolute value measures distance only, so it has no sign.

## Summary

- $\mathbb Z$ contains negative integers, $0$ and positive integers.
- $0$ is neither positive nor negative.
- The opposite of $a$ is $-a$; the opposite of $0$ is $0$.
- $|a|$ is the distance from $a$ to $0$.
- On the number line, a point farther right represents a greater number.
