:::goals
- Find the ratio of two numbers or of two quantities of the same kind (in the same unit).
- Work out the percentage one number is of another.
- Find a percentage of a given amount, and find an amount when a percentage of it is known.
- Solve real-life problems about discounts, interest and map scales.
:::

Two shops near Lan's house both have a sale. The first shop's sign says: "Jacket $240$ thousand dong, $25\%$ off". The second shop's sign says: "Jacket $280$ thousand dong, $60$ thousand dong off".

Lan works it out in her head: $25\%$ off $240$ thousand is also $60$ thousand off. So are the two shops giving the same discount? In money, yes. But if you ask "which shop is more **generous** compared with its original price", the answer is different. To compare like that, we need ratios and percentages.

## The ratio of two numbers

:::definition Ratio
The quotient of the number $a$ divided by the number $b$ ($b\ne0$) is called the **ratio** of $a$ to $b$, written $a:b$ or $\dfrac{a}{b}$.
:::

A ratio tells us how many times as large $a$ is as $b$, or what part of $b$ the number $a$ is. For example, if a class has $15$ boys and $20$ girls, the ratio of boys to girls is $\dfrac{15}{20}=\dfrac34$: for every $3$ boys there are $4$ girls.

Unlike a fraction, in the ratio $\dfrac{a}{b}$ the numbers $a$ and $b$ can be integers, fractions or decimals. For example, $\dfrac{1.5}{2}$ is a ratio, even though it is not a fraction.

:::warning The two quantities must be in the same unit
To find the ratio of two quantities of the same kind (two lengths, two masses, two times…), first **change them to the same unit**. The ratio of $3$ m to $75$ cm is not $3:75$ but $300:75=4$.
:::

## Ratios as percentages

In primary school you met the sign $\%$: $25\%$ means $\dfrac{25}{100}$. Writing a ratio as a percentage makes comparing easier, because everything is brought to "out of $100$".

:::theorem Percentage
The percentage that $a$ is of $b$ ($b\ne0$) is
$$\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot100\%.$$
:::

For example, $3$ as a percentage of $8$ is $\dfrac{3}{8}\cdot100\%=\dfrac{300}{8}\%=37.5\%$.

## Two problems about percentages

These two problems are exactly the two problems about fractions you have just learnt, with a fraction whose denominator is $100$.

:::theorem Finding a percentage of an amount
To find $m\%$ of the number $b$, work out
$$b\cdot\dfrac{m}{100}.$$
:::

:::theorem Finding the whole from a percentage of it
To find a number when $m\%$ of it equals $a$, work out
$$a:\dfrac{m}{100}.$$
:::

The question "multiply or divide?" is answered just as in the last lesson: if you know the whole (which matches $100\%$), multiply; if you do not know the whole, divide.

## Map scales

On a map, every real distance is shrunk by the same ratio. That ratio is called the **map scale**:
$$T=\dfrac{a}{b},$$
where $a$ is the distance on the map and $b$ is the matching real distance, **in the same unit**. A map with scale $1:2\,000\,000$ means that $1$ cm on the map stands for $2\,000\,000$ cm, that is, $20$ km, in real life.

## Examples

We start with ratios, remembering to use the same unit.

:::example Finding a ratio
Find the ratio of:

a) $3$ m to $75$ cm;

b) $\dfrac23$ of an hour to $20$ minutes.

**Solution.**

a) Change $3$ m $=300$ cm. The ratio of $3$ m to $75$ cm is
$$300:75=4.$$

b) Change $\dfrac23$ of an hour $=\dfrac23\cdot60=40$ minutes. The ratio of $\dfrac23$ of an hour to $20$ minutes is
$$40:20=2.$$

So a) $4$; b) $2$.
:::

Next, percentages.

:::example Finding a percentage
Work out:

a) $3$ as a percentage of $8$;

b) $1.2$ kg as a percentage of $300$ g.

**Solution.**

a) $3$ as a percentage of $8$ is
$$\dfrac{3}{8}\cdot100\%=\dfrac{300}{8}\%=37.5\%.$$

b) Change $1.2$ kg $=1200$ g. $1.2$ kg as a percentage of $300$ g is
$$\dfrac{1200}{300}\cdot100\%=4\cdot100\%=400\%.$$

So a) $37.5\%$; b) $400\%$.

**Remark.** A percentage can be more than $100\%$, when the first number is larger than the second.
:::

The first problem: we know the whole and want a part.

:::example Finding a percentage of an amount
Work out:

a) $15\%$ of $240$;

b) $40\%$ of $2.5$ kg.

**Solution.**

a) $15\%$ of $240$ is
$$240\cdot\dfrac{15}{100}=\dfrac{3600}{100}=36.$$

b) $40\%$ of $2.5$ kg is
$$2.5\cdot\dfrac{40}{100}=2.5\cdot0.4=1\ (\text{kg}).$$

So a) $36$; b) $1$ kg.
:::

The second problem: we know a part and want the whole.

:::example Finding the whole from a percentage of it
In a survey, $14$ students chose swimming, which is $35\%$ of the students asked. How many students were asked?

**Solution.**

The number of students asked is the whole, which we do not know; $35\%$ of it is $14$. The number of students asked is
$$14:\dfrac{35}{100}=14\cdot\dfrac{100}{35}=\dfrac{1400}{35}=40\ (\text{students}).$$

Check: $40\cdot\dfrac{35}{100}=14$, which is correct.

So $40$ students were asked.
:::

Finally, we answer Lan's question.

:::example Which shop is more generous?
The first shop sells a jacket for $240$ thousand dong with $25\%$ off. The second shop sells a jacket for $280$ thousand dong with $60$ thousand dong off.

a) How much does each shop take off? After the discount, which jacket is cheaper?

b) Compared with the original price, which shop takes off a larger percentage?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* Part a) compares amounts of money; part b) compares the discount with the original price, that is, a percentage.

*Plan.* Find $25\%$ of $240$ to get the first shop's discount. Find $60$ as a percentage of $280$ to get the second shop's discount rate.

*Solve.*

a) The first shop takes off
$$240\cdot\dfrac{25}{100}=60\ (\text{thousand dong}),$$
so the price after the discount is $240-60=180$ (thousand dong).

The second shop takes off $60$ thousand dong, so the price after the discount is $280-60=220$ (thousand dong).

Both shops take off the same amount of money, but the jacket at the first shop is cheaper ($180<220$).

b) The second shop takes off
$$\dfrac{60}{280}\cdot100\%=\dfrac{6000}{280}\%\approx21.4\%$$
of the original price, while the first shop takes off $25\%$. Since $25\%>21.4\%$, the first shop is more generous.

So a) each shop takes off $60$ thousand dong, and the first shop's jacket is cheaper; b) the first shop takes off a larger percentage.

*Look back.* The same $60$ thousand dong is a larger percentage of a smaller original price. That is why sellers like to write "$25\%$ off" on cheaper items and "$60$ thousand off" on dearer ones: they show whichever number looks bigger.
:::

:::warning Common mistakes
- **Not using the same unit.** Wrong: the ratio of $3$ m to $75$ cm is $\dfrac{3}{75}=\dfrac{1}{25}$. Why it is wrong: metres and centimetres are not the same unit. Right: $\dfrac{300}{75}=4$.
- **Forgetting to multiply by $100\%$.** Wrong: $3$ as a percentage of $8$ is $0.375\%$. Why it is wrong: $\dfrac38=0.375$ is the ratio; to get a percentage, multiply by $100\%$. Right: $37.5\%$.
- **Multiplying when you should divide.** Wrong: "$35\%$ of the students is $14$, so there are $14\cdot\dfrac{35}{100}=4.9$ students." Why it is wrong: the whole is unknown, so we must divide; and $4.9$ students makes no sense. Right: $14:\dfrac{35}{100}=40$.
- **Thinking that going up and then down by the same percentage gets you back.** Wrong: up $10\%$ then down $10\%$ returns to the original price. Why it is wrong: the decrease is worked out on the higher price, which is more than the original. Right: the final price is $99\%$ of the original (see the Challenge exercise).
:::

## Summary

- The ratio of $a$ to $b$ ($b\ne0$) is $a:b=\dfrac{a}{b}$; two quantities of the same kind must be in the same unit.
- $a$ as a percentage of $b$ is $\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot100\%$.
- $m\%$ of $b$ is $b\cdot\dfrac{m}{100}$ (know the whole: multiply).
- If $m\%$ of a number is $a$, the number is $a:\dfrac{m}{100}$ (whole unknown: divide).
- Map scale $T=\dfrac{a}{b}$, where $a$ is the distance on the map and $b$ the real distance, in the same unit.

The two sale signs from the opening take off the same amount of money, but one takes off $25\%$ and the other only about $21.4\%$. Percentages give us a common yardstick for comparing things of different sizes. Next time you go shopping, try working out which sign is really the better deal. If you need to revise the two basic problems, see [Two problems about fractions](lesson:two-fraction-problems).

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Find the ratio of:

a) $1.2$ m to $40$ cm;

b) $\dfrac34$ kg to $250$ g;

c) $45$ minutes to $1.5$ hours.

::hint
**What you need:** the ratio of $a$ to $b$ is $a:b$; both quantities must be in the same unit.

**Steps:**
1. Change both quantities to the same unit.
2. Write the ratio and simplify.

::solution
a) Change $1.2$ m $=120$ cm. The ratio is $120:40=3$.

b) Change $\dfrac34$ kg $=\dfrac34\cdot1000=750$ g. The ratio is $750:250=3$.

c) Change $1.5$ hours $=90$ minutes. The ratio is $45:90=\dfrac{1}{2}$.

So the ratios are $3$; $3$; $\dfrac12$.

**Answer.** a) $3$; b) $3$; c) $\dfrac12$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $7$ as a percentage of $20$;

b) $1.5$ as a percentage of $6$;

c) $2$ hours as a percentage of $40$ minutes.

::hint
**What you need:** $a$ as a percentage of $b$ is $\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot100\%$.

**Steps:**
1. Change to the same unit if necessary.
2. Write the ratio and multiply by $100\%$.

::solution
a) $7$ as a percentage of $20$ is $\dfrac{7}{20}\cdot100\%=35\%$.

b) $1.5$ as a percentage of $6$ is $\dfrac{1.5}{6}\cdot100\%=0.25\cdot100\%=25\%$.

c) Change $2$ hours $=120$ minutes. The percentage is $\dfrac{120}{40}\cdot100\%=300\%$.

So the results are $35\%$; $25\%$; $300\%$.

**Answer.** a) $35\%$; b) $25\%$; c) $300\%$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $12\%$ of $350$;

b) $2.5\%$ of $800$;

c) $120\%$ of $45$.

::hint
**What you need:** $m\%$ of $b$ is $b\cdot\dfrac{m}{100}$.

**Steps:**
1. Write the multiplication.
2. Calculate.

::solution
a) $12\%$ of $350$ is $350\cdot\dfrac{12}{100}=\dfrac{4200}{100}=42$.

b) $2.5\%$ of $800$ is $800\cdot\dfrac{2.5}{100}=8\cdot2.5=20$.

c) $120\%$ of $45$ is $45\cdot\dfrac{120}{100}=45\cdot1.2=54$.

So the results are $42$; $20$; $54$.

**Answer.** a) $42$; b) $20$; c) $54$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the number, given that:

a) $30\%$ of the number is $21$;

b) $1.5\%$ of the number is $3$.

::hint
**What you need:** if $m\%$ of a number is $a$, the number is $a:\dfrac{m}{100}$.

**Steps:**
1. Write the division.
2. Turn it into multiplying by the reciprocal, then calculate.

::solution
a) The number is
$$21:\dfrac{30}{100}=21\cdot\dfrac{100}{30}=70.$$

b) The number is
$$3:\dfrac{1.5}{100}=3\cdot\dfrac{100}{1.5}=\dfrac{300}{1.5}=200.$$

Check: $70\cdot\dfrac{30}{100}=21$ and $200\cdot\dfrac{1.5}{100}=3$, both correct.

So a) $70$; b) $200$.

**Answer.** a) $70$; b) $200$.
:::

:::exercise
Class 6A has $40$ students, and $45\%$ of them like football. How many students in the class like football?

::hint
**What you need:** find $45\%$ of $40$.

**Steps:**
1. Write the multiplication $40\cdot\dfrac{45}{100}$.
2. Calculate.

::solution
The number of students who like football is
$$40\cdot\dfrac{45}{100}=\dfrac{1800}{100}=18\ (\text{students}).$$

So $18$ students in class 6A like football.

**Answer.** $18$ students.
:::

:::exercise
A rice cooker costs $500$ thousand dong. Because materials have become dearer, the shop raises the price by $8\%$. Find the new price of the rice cooker.

::hint
**What you need:** the new price is the old price plus $8\%$ of the old price, that is, $108\%$ of the old price.

**Steps:**
1. Find the increase.
2. Add it to the old price.

::solution
The increase is
$$500\cdot\dfrac{8}{100}=40\ (\text{thousand dong}).$$

The new price of the rice cooker is
$$500+40=540\ (\text{thousand dong}).$$

So the new price of the rice cooker is $540$ thousand dong.

**Answer.** $540$ thousand dong.
:::

:::exercise
Mr Hùng puts $20$ million dong into a savings account at an interest rate of $6\%$ a year. After one year, how much interest does he receive, and what is his total, savings plus interest?

::hint
**What you need:** the interest for one year is $6\%$ of the money saved.

**Steps:**
1. Find $6\%$ of $20$ million dong.
2. Add it to the money saved.

::solution
The interest after one year is
$$20\cdot\dfrac{6}{100}=1.2\ (\text{million dong}).$$

The total, savings plus interest, is
$$20+1.2=21.2\ (\text{million dong}).$$

So after one year Mr Hùng receives $1.2$ million dong in interest, and his total is $21.2$ million dong.

**Answer.** Interest $1.2$ million dong; total $21.2$ million dong.
:::

:::exercise
On a map with scale $1:2\,000\,000$, the distance between two cities measures $4.5$ cm. Find the real distance between the two cities in kilometres.

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ cm on the map stands for $2\,000\,000$ cm in real life.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply $4.5$ by $2\,000\,000$ to get the real distance in centimetres.
2. Change to kilometres ($1$ km $=100\,000$ cm).

::solution
The real distance between the two cities is
$$4.5\cdot2\,000\,000=9\,000\,000\ (\text{cm}).$$

Since $1$ km $=100\,000$ cm,
$$9\,000\,000\ \text{cm}=90\ \text{km}.$$

So the real distance between the two cities is $90$ km.

**Answer.** $90$ km.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
The price of a bicycle goes up by $10\%$, then comes down by $10\%$ of the raised price. Is the final price higher or lower than the original price, and by what percentage?

::hint
**What you need:** the decrease is worked out on the raised price, not on the original price.

**Steps:**
1. Suppose the original price is $100$ (in any unit of money).
2. Work out the price after the rise, then after the fall, and compare with $100$.

::solution
Suppose the original price is $100$ (units of money).

After a $10\%$ rise, the price is
$$100+100\cdot\dfrac{10}{100}=100+10=110.$$

After a $10\%$ fall from $110$, the decrease is $110\cdot\dfrac{10}{100}=11$, so the final price is
$$110-11=99.$$

The final price is $100-99=1$ lower than the original, that is, $\dfrac{1}{100}\cdot100\%=1\%$ of the original price.

So the final price is $1\%$ lower than the original price.

**Answer.** $1\%$ lower.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
The ratio of $60$ cm to $2$ m is
A. $30$.
B. $\dfrac{3}{10}$.
C. $\dfrac{10}{3}$.
D. $\dfrac{3}{100}$.
::key B
$2$ m $=200$ cm, so the ratio is $\dfrac{60}{200}=\dfrac{3}{10}$. A does not change the unit; C reverses the order; D wrongly changes $2$ m into $2000$ cm.

::mc
$9$ as a percentage of $12$ is
A. $75\%$.
B. $0.75\%$.
C. $133\%$.
D. $25\%$.
::key A
$\dfrac{9}{12}\cdot100\%=75\%$. B forgets to multiply by $100\%$; C reverses the two numbers; D is the missing part.

::mc
$20\%$ of $150$ is
A. $75$.
B. $750$.
C. $3$.
D. $30$.
::key D
$150\cdot\dfrac{20}{100}=30$. A takes half of $150$; B divides by $\dfrac{20}{100}$ instead of multiplying; C divides by $1000$ instead of $100$.

::mc
$25\%$ of a number is $40$. The number is
A. $10$.
B. $160$.
C. $65$.
D. $1000$.
::key B
$40:\dfrac{25}{100}=160$. A multiplies instead of dividing; C adds $40+25$; D multiplies $40\cdot25$.

::tf
A shirt costing $300$ thousand dong is sold at $20\%$ off. Decide whether each statement is true or false.
a) The discount is $60$ thousand dong.
b) The price after the discount is $240$ thousand dong.
c) The price after the discount is $80\%$ of the original price.
d) If the shop then raises the price by $20\%$, the price goes back to $300$ thousand dong.
::key T T T F
a) True: $300\cdot\dfrac{20}{100}=60$. b) True: $300-60=240$. c) True: $\dfrac{240}{300}\cdot100\%=80\%$. d) False: $240\cdot1.2=288$ thousand dong.

::sa
Work out $1.5\%$ of $600$.
::key 9
$600\cdot\dfrac{1.5}{100}=6\cdot1.5=9$.

::sa
A class has $24$ students, and $18$ of them cycle to school. What percentage of the class cycles to school? (Write the number only, without the $\%$ sign.)
::key 75
$\dfrac{18}{24}\cdot100\%=75\%$.
