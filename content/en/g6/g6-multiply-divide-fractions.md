Lan's father wants to fit a small pane of glass into a door. The pane is a rectangle $\dfrac34$ m long and $\dfrac23$ m wide. What is the area of the pane in square metres?

We know that the area of a rectangle is length times width, so the answer is $\dfrac34\cdot\dfrac23$. But how do we multiply two fractions? This time, a picture will give us the rule by itself.

## Multiplying two fractions

The pane covers $3$ columns and $2$ rows, that is, $3\cdot2=6$ small boxes. So the area of the pane is $\dfrac{6}{12}=\dfrac12\ \text{m}^2$. Notice: the numerator $6=3\cdot2$ is the product of the two numerators, and the denominator $12=4\cdot3$ is the product of the two denominators.

:::theorem Rule for multiplying fractions
To multiply two fractions, multiply the numerators together and multiply the denominators together:
$$\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{a\cdot c}{b\cdot d}.$$
:::

In particular, multiplying a fraction by an integer: $\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot m=\dfrac{a\cdot m}{b}$. The sign of the product follows the sign rule for integers: same signs give a positive product, different signs give a negative product.

:::tip Simplify before multiplying
Before multiplying, if a numerator and a denominator have a common factor, divide both by it. The numbers get smaller and the result comes out in its simplest form straight away:
$$\dfrac{-3}{5}\cdot\dfrac{10}{9}=\dfrac{(-3)\cdot10}{5\cdot9}=\dfrac{(-1)\cdot2}{1\cdot3}=\dfrac{-2}{3}.$$
:::

## Properties of multiplying fractions

Multiplying fractions has all the familiar properties: it is **commutative**, **associative**, **multiplying by $1$** changes nothing, and it is **distributive over addition**:
$$\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot\left(\dfrac{c}{d}+\dfrac{p}{q}\right)=\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot\dfrac{c}{d}+\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot\dfrac{p}{q}.$$

As with integers, the distributive property read backwards lets us take out a common factor, and that is often the shortest route.

## Reciprocals

:::definition Reciprocal
Two numbers are called **reciprocals** of each other if their product is $1$. For a non-zero fraction $\dfrac{a}{b}$ (that is, $a\ne0$, $b\ne0$), its reciprocal is $\dfrac{b}{a}$, because
$$\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot\dfrac{b}{a}=\dfrac{a\cdot b}{b\cdot a}=1.$$
:::

To find the reciprocal, just swap the numerator and the denominator. The reciprocal of $\dfrac{-3}{7}$ is $\dfrac{7}{-3}=\dfrac{-7}{3}$; the reciprocal of $5=\dfrac51$ is $\dfrac15$. The number $0$ has no reciprocal, because $0$ times any number is $0$, never $1$.

Many students mix these up: the **opposite** makes the sum $0$ (change the sign), while the **reciprocal** makes the product $1$ (swap numerator and denominator).

## Dividing fractions

There is $\dfrac34$ of a litre of water on the table. It is poured into small cups holding $\dfrac18$ of a litre each. How many cups can be filled? We need to work out $\dfrac34:\dfrac18$.

Think of it like this: $1$ litre fills $8$ cups, so $\dfrac34$ of a litre fills $\dfrac34\cdot8=6$ cups. So dividing by $\dfrac18$ is the same as multiplying by $8$, the reciprocal of $\dfrac18$.

:::theorem Rule for dividing fractions
To divide a fraction by a non-zero fraction, multiply the first fraction by the **reciprocal** of the divisor:
$$\dfrac{a}{b}:\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot\dfrac{d}{c}\qquad\left(\dfrac{c}{d}\ne0\right).$$
:::

With this rule, division comes back to multiplication. You do not need to learn anything new; just remember to turn over the **divisor**, not the number being divided.

## Examples

We start with multiplication, including multiplying by an integer.

:::example Multiplying fractions
Calculate:

a) $\dfrac{-3}{5}\cdot\dfrac{10}{9}$;

b) $\dfrac{4}{-7}\cdot\dfrac{-21}{8}$;

c) $\dfrac{-5}{12}\cdot8$.

**Solution.**

a) Simplify $3$ with $9$ and $5$ with $10$, then multiply:
$$\dfrac{-3}{5}\cdot\dfrac{10}{9}=\dfrac{(-3)\cdot10}{5\cdot9}=\dfrac{(-1)\cdot2}{1\cdot3}=\dfrac{-2}{3}.$$

b) Make the denominator positive: $\dfrac{4}{-7}=\dfrac{-4}{7}$. Both factors are negative, so the product is positive:
$$\dfrac{-4}{7}\cdot\dfrac{-21}{8}=\dfrac{4\cdot21}{7\cdot8}=\dfrac{1\cdot3}{1\cdot2}=\dfrac{3}{2}.$$

c) Multiply the numerator by the integer and keep the denominator:
$$\dfrac{-5}{12}\cdot8=\dfrac{(-5)\cdot8}{12}=\dfrac{-40}{12}=\dfrac{-10}{3}.$$

So the results are $\dfrac{-2}{3}$; $\dfrac{3}{2}$; $\dfrac{-10}{3}$.
:::

The properties of multiplication help us avoid heavy calculations.

:::example Calculating in a sensible order
Calculate:

a) $\dfrac{-7}{11}\cdot\dfrac{5}{9}+\dfrac{-7}{11}\cdot\dfrac{4}{9}$;

b) $\dfrac{-4}{5}\cdot\dfrac{13}{17}\cdot\dfrac{-5}{4}$.

**Solution.**

a) The two products share the factor $\dfrac{-7}{11}$. Using the distributive property:
$$\dfrac{-7}{11}\cdot\dfrac{5}{9}+\dfrac{-7}{11}\cdot\dfrac{4}{9}=\dfrac{-7}{11}\cdot\left(\dfrac{5}{9}+\dfrac{4}{9}\right)=\dfrac{-7}{11}\cdot1=\dfrac{-7}{11}.$$

b) The fractions $\dfrac{-4}{5}$ and $\dfrac{-5}{4}$ are reciprocals of each other. Using the commutative and associative properties:
$$\dfrac{-4}{5}\cdot\dfrac{13}{17}\cdot\dfrac{-5}{4}=\left(\dfrac{-4}{5}\cdot\dfrac{-5}{4}\right)\cdot\dfrac{13}{17}=1\cdot\dfrac{13}{17}=\dfrac{13}{17}.$$

So a) $\dfrac{-7}{11}$; b) $\dfrac{13}{17}$.
:::

Now for reciprocals and division.

:::example Reciprocals and division
a) Find the reciprocal of $\dfrac{-3}{7}$ and of $-6$.

b) Work out $\dfrac{2}{3}:\dfrac{-4}{9}$ and $-6:\dfrac{3}{5}$.

**Solution.**

a) The reciprocal of $\dfrac{-3}{7}$ is $\dfrac{7}{-3}=\dfrac{-7}{3}$.

We have $-6=\dfrac{-6}{1}$, so the reciprocal of $-6$ is $\dfrac{1}{-6}=\dfrac{-1}{6}$.

b) Multiply the first number by the reciprocal of the divisor:
$$\dfrac{2}{3}:\dfrac{-4}{9}=\dfrac{2}{3}\cdot\dfrac{9}{-4}=\dfrac{2}{3}\cdot\dfrac{-9}{4}=\dfrac{1\cdot(-3)}{1\cdot2}=\dfrac{-3}{2};$$
$$-6:\dfrac{3}{5}=-6\cdot\dfrac{5}{3}=\dfrac{(-6)\cdot5}{3}=-10.$$

So a) $\dfrac{-7}{3}$ and $\dfrac{-1}{6}$; b) $\dfrac{-3}{2}$ and $-10$.
:::

Dividing fractions answers questions of the kind "how many times does it fit".

:::example Cutting a wire
A coil of wire is $7\dfrac12$ m long. It is cut into pieces $\dfrac34$ m long. How many pieces are there?

**Solution.**

Change the mixed number into a fraction: $7\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{15}{2}$.

The number of pieces is
$$\dfrac{15}{2}:\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{15}{2}\cdot\dfrac{4}{3}=\dfrac{15\cdot4}{2\cdot3}=\dfrac{60}{6}=10\ (\text{pieces}).$$

So there are $10$ pieces of wire.
:::

Finally, a journey problem with a mixed number.

:::example How far the car goes
A car travels at $42$ km/h for $2$ hours $20$ minutes. Find the distance the car travels.

**Solution.**

*Understand.* We know the speed and the time, and we need the distance. The time is given in hours and minutes.

*Plan.* Change the time into hours as a mixed number, then use distance equals speed times time.

*Solve.* We have $20$ minutes $=\dfrac{20}{60}$ hour $=\dfrac13$ hour, so the travelling time is
$$2\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{7}{3}\ (\text{hours}).$$

The distance the car travels is
$$42\cdot\dfrac{7}{3}=\dfrac{42\cdot7}{3}=14\cdot7=98\ (\text{km}).$$

So the car travels $98$ km.

*Look back.* Is the result reasonable? In $2$ hours the car goes $84$ km, and in $3$ hours it goes $126$ km. The time is between $2$ and $3$ hours, so a distance of $98$ km, between $84$ and $126$ km, makes sense.
:::

:::warning Common mistakes
- **Finding a common denominator when multiplying.** Wrong: $\dfrac34\cdot\dfrac23=\dfrac{9}{12}\cdot\dfrac{8}{12}=\dfrac{72}{12}$. Why it is wrong: a common denominator is only needed for adding and subtracting; to multiply, multiply numerator by numerator and denominator by denominator. Right: $\dfrac34\cdot\dfrac23=\dfrac{6}{12}=\dfrac12$.
- **Turning over the wrong fraction.** Wrong: $\dfrac23:\dfrac{-4}{9}=\dfrac32\cdot\dfrac{-4}{9}$. Why it is wrong: only the divisor is turned over; the number being divided stays as it is. Right: $\dfrac23\cdot\dfrac{9}{-4}=\dfrac{-3}{2}$.
- **Mixing up the reciprocal and the opposite.** Wrong: "The reciprocal of $\dfrac{-3}{7}$ is $\dfrac37$." Why it is wrong: $\dfrac{-3}{7}\cdot\dfrac37=\dfrac{-9}{49}\ne1$; $\dfrac37$ is the opposite. Right: the reciprocal is $\dfrac{-7}{3}$.
- **Multiplying both numerator and denominator by the integer.** Wrong: $\dfrac{-5}{12}\cdot8=\dfrac{-40}{96}$. Why it is wrong: $8=\dfrac81$, so the denominator is only multiplied by $1$. Right: $\dfrac{-5}{12}\cdot8=\dfrac{-40}{12}=\dfrac{-10}{3}$.
:::

## Summary

- Multiplying: $\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{a\cdot c}{b\cdot d}$; by an integer: $\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot m=\dfrac{a\cdot m}{b}$. Simplify before multiplying.
- Multiplying fractions is commutative and associative, multiplying by $1$ changes nothing, and it is distributive over addition.
- The reciprocal of $\dfrac{a}{b}$ ($a\ne0$) is $\dfrac{b}{a}$; the product of two reciprocals is $1$; $0$ has no reciprocal.
- Dividing means multiplying by the reciprocal of the divisor: $\dfrac{a}{b}:\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot\dfrac{d}{c}$.

The pane from the opening has area $\dfrac34\cdot\dfrac23=\dfrac12\ \text{m}^2$, exactly $6$ of the $12$ boxes of the square. Keep that picture in mind: it explains why multiplying two fractions less than $1$ gives a result smaller than both. In the next lesson we use multiplication and division to solve [two problems about fractions](lesson:two-fraction-problems).

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Calculate:

a) $\dfrac{2}{7}\cdot\dfrac{-14}{5}$;

b) $\dfrac{-9}{10}\cdot\dfrac{-5}{6}$;

c) $\dfrac{-3}{8}\cdot16$.

::hint
**What you need:** $\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{a\cdot c}{b\cdot d}$; same signs give a positive product, different signs a negative one.

**Steps:**
1. Decide the sign of the product.
2. Simplify across, then multiply.

::solution
a) The factors have different signs, so the product is negative. Simplify $7$ with $14$:
$$\dfrac{2}{7}\cdot\dfrac{-14}{5}=\dfrac{2\cdot(-14)}{7\cdot5}=\dfrac{2\cdot(-2)}{1\cdot5}=\dfrac{-4}{5}.$$

b) Both factors are negative, so the product is positive. Simplify $9$ with $6$ and $10$ with $5$:
$$\dfrac{-9}{10}\cdot\dfrac{-5}{6}=\dfrac{9\cdot5}{10\cdot6}=\dfrac{3\cdot1}{2\cdot2}=\dfrac{3}{4}.$$

c) We have
$$\dfrac{-3}{8}\cdot16=\dfrac{(-3)\cdot16}{8}=(-3)\cdot2=-6.$$

So the results are $\dfrac{-4}{5}$; $\dfrac{3}{4}$; $-6$.

**Answer.** a) $\dfrac{-4}{5}$; b) $\dfrac{3}{4}$; c) $-6$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the reciprocal of each number: $\dfrac{3}{5}$; $\dfrac{-7}{2}$; $-4$; $1$.

::hint
**What you need:** the reciprocal of $\dfrac{a}{b}$ is $\dfrac{b}{a}$; the product of two reciprocals is $1$.

**Steps:**
1. Write an integer as a fraction with denominator $1$.
2. Swap numerator and denominator, then make the denominator positive.

::solution
The reciprocal of $\dfrac{3}{5}$ is $\dfrac{5}{3}$.

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{-7}{2}$ is $\dfrac{2}{-7}=\dfrac{-2}{7}$.

We have $-4=\dfrac{-4}{1}$, so the reciprocal of $-4$ is $\dfrac{1}{-4}=\dfrac{-1}{4}$.

Since $1\cdot1=1$, the reciprocal of $1$ is $1$.

So the reciprocals are $\dfrac{5}{3}$; $\dfrac{-2}{7}$; $\dfrac{-1}{4}$; $1$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{5}{3}$; $\dfrac{-2}{7}$; $\dfrac{-1}{4}$; $1$.
:::

:::exercise
Calculate:

a) $\dfrac{5}{6}:\dfrac{10}{9}$;

b) $\dfrac{-8}{15}:\dfrac{4}{5}$;

c) $\dfrac{3}{4}:(-6)$.

::hint
**What you need:** $\dfrac{a}{b}:\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot\dfrac{d}{c}$.

**Steps:**
1. Turn the division into multiplying by the reciprocal of the divisor.
2. Simplify across, then multiply.

::solution
a) We have
$$\dfrac{5}{6}:\dfrac{10}{9}=\dfrac{5}{6}\cdot\dfrac{9}{10}=\dfrac{5\cdot9}{6\cdot10}=\dfrac{1\cdot3}{2\cdot2}=\dfrac{3}{4}.$$

b) We have
$$\dfrac{-8}{15}:\dfrac{4}{5}=\dfrac{-8}{15}\cdot\dfrac{5}{4}=\dfrac{(-8)\cdot5}{15\cdot4}=\dfrac{(-2)\cdot1}{3\cdot1}=\dfrac{-2}{3}.$$

c) The reciprocal of $-6$ is $\dfrac{-1}{6}$, so
$$\dfrac{3}{4}:(-6)=\dfrac{3}{4}\cdot\dfrac{-1}{6}=\dfrac{3\cdot(-1)}{4\cdot6}=\dfrac{-3}{24}=\dfrac{-1}{8}.$$

So the results are $\dfrac{3}{4}$; $\dfrac{-2}{3}$; $\dfrac{-1}{8}$.

**Answer.** a) $\dfrac{3}{4}$; b) $\dfrac{-2}{3}$; c) $\dfrac{-1}{8}$.
:::

:::exercise
Calculate in a sensible order:

a) $\dfrac{5}{9}\cdot\dfrac{7}{13}+\dfrac{5}{9}\cdot\dfrac{6}{13}$;

b) $\dfrac{-2}{3}\cdot\dfrac{9}{14}\cdot\dfrac{-7}{3}$.

::hint
**What you need:** the distributive property; the commutative and associative properties.

**Steps:**
1. Part a): take out the common factor $\dfrac59$.
2. Part b): decide the sign, then simplify across all three fractions.

::solution
a) The two products share the factor $\dfrac{5}{9}$. Using the distributive property:
$$\dfrac{5}{9}\cdot\dfrac{7}{13}+\dfrac{5}{9}\cdot\dfrac{6}{13}=\dfrac{5}{9}\cdot\left(\dfrac{7}{13}+\dfrac{6}{13}\right)=\dfrac{5}{9}\cdot1=\dfrac{5}{9}.$$

b) The product has two negative factors, so it is positive. We have
$$\dfrac{-2}{3}\cdot\dfrac{9}{14}\cdot\dfrac{-7}{3}=\dfrac{2\cdot9\cdot7}{3\cdot14\cdot3}=\dfrac{126}{126}=1.$$

So a) $\dfrac{5}{9}$; b) $1$.

**Answer.** a) $\dfrac{5}{9}$; b) $1$.
:::

:::exercise
Find $x$, given that:

a) $x\cdot\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{-9}{10}$;

b) $\dfrac{2}{3}:x=\dfrac{-4}{9}$.

::hint
**What you need:** an unknown factor equals the product divided by the known factor; the divisor equals the number being divided divided by the quotient.

**Steps:**
1. Write the calculation that gives $x$.
2. Turn the division into multiplying by the reciprocal.

::solution
a) We have $x\cdot\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{-9}{10}$, so
$$x=\dfrac{-9}{10}:\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{-9}{10}\cdot\dfrac{4}{3}=\dfrac{(-3)\cdot2}{5\cdot1}=\dfrac{-6}{5}.$$

b) We have $\dfrac{2}{3}:x=\dfrac{-4}{9}$. The divisor equals the number being divided divided by the quotient, so
$$x=\dfrac{2}{3}:\dfrac{-4}{9}=\dfrac{2}{3}\cdot\dfrac{-9}{4}=\dfrac{1\cdot(-3)}{1\cdot2}=\dfrac{-3}{2}.$$

Check: $\dfrac{-6}{5}\cdot\dfrac34=\dfrac{-18}{20}=\dfrac{-9}{10}$; $\dfrac23:\dfrac{-3}{2}=\dfrac23\cdot\dfrac{-2}{3}=\dfrac{-4}{9}$. Both are correct.

So a) $x=\dfrac{-6}{5}$; b) $x=\dfrac{-3}{2}$.

**Answer.** a) $x=\dfrac{-6}{5}$; b) $x=\dfrac{-3}{2}$.
:::

:::exercise
A rectangular garden is $12\dfrac{1}{2}$ m long, and its width is $\dfrac{4}{5}$ of its length. Find the area of the garden.

::hint
**What you need:** the width equals the length times $\dfrac45$; the area equals length times width.

**Steps:**
1. Change the mixed number into a fraction and find the width.
2. Find the area.

::solution
Change the mixed number into a fraction: $12\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{25}{2}$.

The width of the garden is
$$\dfrac{25}{2}\cdot\dfrac{4}{5}=\dfrac{25\cdot4}{2\cdot5}=\dfrac{5\cdot2}{1\cdot1}=10\ (\text{m}).$$

The area of the garden is
$$\dfrac{25}{2}\cdot10=125\ (\text{m}^2).$$

So the garden has an area of $125\ \text{m}^2$.

**Answer.** $125\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
Mum buys $2\dfrac{1}{4}$ kg of meat at $120$ thousand dong per kilogram. How much does she pay?

::hint
**What you need:** the cost equals the mass times the price per kilogram.

**Steps:**
1. Change the mixed number into a fraction.
2. Multiply by the price.

::solution
Change the mixed number into a fraction: $2\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{9}{4}$.

The amount Mum pays is
$$\dfrac{9}{4}\cdot120=\dfrac{9\cdot120}{4}=9\cdot30=270\ (\text{thousand dong}).$$

So Mum pays $270$ thousand dong.

**Answer.** $270$ thousand dong.
:::

:::exercise
A motorbike travels $90$ km in $1\dfrac{1}{2}$ hours. Find its average speed.

::hint
**What you need:** speed equals distance divided by time.

**Steps:**
1. Change $1\dfrac12$ hours into a fraction.
2. Divide $90$ by that fraction.

::solution
Change the mixed number into a fraction: $1\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{3}{2}$ (hours).

The average speed of the motorbike is
$$90:\dfrac{3}{2}=90\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{90\cdot2}{3}=60\ (\text{km/h}).$$

So the average speed of the motorbike is $60$ km/h.

**Answer.** $60$ km/h.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Work out the product
$$P=\left(1-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\cdot\left(1-\dfrac{1}{3}\right)\cdot\left(1-\dfrac{1}{4}\right)\cdots\left(1-\dfrac{1}{10}\right).$$

::hint
**What you need:** $1-\dfrac{1}{n}=\dfrac{n-1}{n}$; when multiplying, the numerator of one fraction cancels with the denominator of another.

**Steps:**
1. Work out each bracket.
2. Write out the product and see which numbers cancel.

::solution
We have $1-\dfrac{1}{n}=\dfrac{n-1}{n}$, so
$$P=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}\cdot\dfrac{3}{4}\cdots\dfrac{9}{10}=\dfrac{1\cdot2\cdot3\cdots9}{2\cdot3\cdot4\cdots10}.$$

The factors $2$; $3$; …; $9$ appear in both the numerator and the denominator, so they cancel. Only $1$ is left on top and only $10$ underneath. Hence
$$P=\dfrac{1}{10}.$$

So $P=\dfrac{1}{10}$.

**Answer.** $P=\dfrac{1}{10}$.
:::
