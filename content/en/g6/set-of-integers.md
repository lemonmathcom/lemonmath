:::goals
- Recognise negative integers and use integers to describe quantities that go in two opposite directions.
- Know the set $\mathbb{Z}$ of integers and show integers on a number line.
- Find the opposite of an integer.
- Compare two integers and put a list of integers in order.
:::

One winter morning the weather forecast says: "The temperature on the mountain top is minus $2$ degrees Celsius, while in the town at the foot of the mountain it is $12$ degrees." In a lift, the buttons read $3$, $2$, $1$, $\text{G}$, then $\text{B1}$, $\text{B2}$ for the basement floors. A diver is $15$ m below the surface of the sea.

Each situation has a reference point in the middle (zero degrees, the ground floor, sea level) and two opposite sides. The natural numbers can only count one side. To talk about the other side, we need new numbers: the negative integers.

## Negative integers and the set of integers

We write minus $2$ degrees Celsius as $-2^\circ\text{C}$, read "minus two degrees Celsius". The sign "$-$" in front of a non-zero natural number tells us the number is on the side "below the reference point".

:::definition Negative integers, the set of integers
- The numbers $-1$; $-2$; $-3$; … are the **negative integers**.
- The numbers $1$; $2$; $3$; … are the **positive integers** (sometimes written $+1$; $+2$; $+3$; …).
- The set made of the negative integers, the number $0$ and the positive integers is called the **set of integers**, written $\mathbb{Z}$:
$$\mathbb{Z}=\{\dots;-3;-2;-1;0;1;2;3;\dots\}.$$
:::

:::note The number $0$
The number $0$ is neither negative nor positive. It is the reference point in the middle.
:::

Integers let us record quantities with two opposite directions neatly. For example: a height of $+800$ m (above sea level) and a depth of $-15$ m (below sea level); having $50$ thousand dong is written $+50$, owing $50$ thousand dong is written $-50$; the year $1000$ AD and the year $200$ BC can be written $1000$ and $-200$.

## Integers on the number line

We draw a horizontal line, choose a point as the **origin** marked $0$, and choose a unit of length. The positive integers are to the right of the origin and the negative integers to the left, equally spaced by the chosen unit. Going from left to right is the **positive direction**.

The point that shows the integer $a$ on the number line is called **the point $a$**. A number line can also stand upright, like the scale on a thermometer: then the positive direction is upwards.

## Opposite numbers

On the number line, the points $3$ and $-3$ are on opposite sides of $0$ and at the same distance from $0$: each is three units away.

:::definition Opposite numbers
Two numbers on opposite sides of $0$ and at the same distance from $0$ are called **opposite numbers**. The opposite of $a$ is written $-a$. The opposite of $0$ is $0$.
:::

So the opposite of $5$ is $-5$, and the opposite of $-7$ is $7$. We write $-(-7)=7$: taking the opposite twice brings you back to where you started.

## Comparing two integers

On a horizontal number line, the number further **to the left** is smaller. So $-3<1$ (the point $-3$ is to the left of the point $1$) and $-5<-2$ (the point $-5$ is to the left of the point $-2$). This matches everyday experience: $-5^\circ\text{C}$ is colder than $-2^\circ\text{C}$.

:::theorem Rules for comparing
- Every positive integer is greater than $0$; every negative integer is less than $0$.
- Every negative integer is less than every positive integer.
- For two negative integers, the one whose number part (without the "$-$") is larger is the **smaller** one. For example, $8>3$, so $-8<-3$.
:::

Many students slip here: they see that $8$ is bigger than $3$ and think $-8$ is bigger than $-3$. Remember the thermometer: minus $8$ degrees is colder than minus $3$ degrees, so $-8$ is smaller.

## Examples

We start by using integers to describe situations.

:::example Recording with integers
Use integers to describe these quantities:

a) A mountain top is $1250$ m above sea level;

b) A submarine is $120$ m below sea level;

c) An owes Binh $20$ thousand dong.

**Solution.**

We take sea level as the reference point $0$: above it we use positive numbers, below it negative numbers.

a) The height of the mountain top is $+1250$ m.

b) The height of the submarine is $-120$ m.

c) Take money An has as positive and money An owes as negative. An's money with respect to Binh is $-20$ thousand dong.

So the quantities are recorded as $+1250$ m; $-120$ m; $-20$ thousand dong.
:::

Next we get used to set notation.

:::example In $\mathbb{Z}$ or in $\mathbb{N}$?
Write $\in$ or $\notin$ in each box: $-4\ \square\ \mathbb{N}$; $\ -4\ \square\ \mathbb{Z}$; $\ 0\ \square\ \mathbb{Z}$; $\ 9\ \square\ \mathbb{Z}$.

**Solution.**

The number $-4$ is a negative integer, not a natural number, so $-4\notin\mathbb{N}$ and $-4\in\mathbb{Z}$.

The numbers $0$ and $9$ are both integers, so $0\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $9\in\mathbb{Z}$.

So $-4\notin\mathbb{N}$; $-4\in\mathbb{Z}$; $0\in\mathbb{Z}$; $9\in\mathbb{Z}$.
:::

Opposite numbers are a very simple idea, but signs are easy to get wrong.

:::example Finding opposites
Find the opposite of each number: $6$; $-11$; $0$; $-(-4)$.

**Solution.**

The opposite of $6$ is $-6$.

The opposite of $-11$ is $11$.

The opposite of $0$ is $0$.

We have $-(-4)=4$, so the opposite of $-(-4)$ is $-4$.

So the opposites are $-6$; $11$; $0$; $-4$.
:::

Now we use the comparing rules to put numbers in order.

:::example Increasing order
Put these integers in increasing order: $5$; $-7$; $0$; $-2$; $12$; $-15$.

**Solution.**

The negative integers are $-7$; $-2$; $-15$. Since $15>7>2$, we have $-15<-7<-2$.

The negative integers are all less than $0$, and $0$ is less than the positive integers $5$ and $12$.

Since $5<12$, we get the order
$$-15<-7<-2<0<5<12.$$

So in increasing order: $-15$; $-7$; $-2$; $0$; $5$; $12$.
:::

Finally, a problem about reading real-looking data.

:::example Where is it coldest?
The table shows the temperature at $6$ a.m. in four places on the same day (made-up data):

| Place | A | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature ($^\circ\text{C}$) | $3$ | $-4$ | $-1$ | $0$ |

Which place is the coldest, and which is the warmest?

**Solution.**

The coldest place has the smallest temperature, and the warmest place has the largest.

We compare: the two negative numbers are $-4$ and $-1$; since $4>1$, we have $-4<-1$. The negative numbers are less than $0$, and $0<3$.

So $-4<-1<0<3$.

So place B is the coldest ($-4^\circ\text{C}$) and place A is the warmest ($3^\circ\text{C}$).
:::

:::warning Common mistakes
- **Comparing negative numbers like natural numbers.** Wrong: $-8>-3$ because $8>3$. Why it is wrong: the point $-8$ is to the left of the point $-3$. Right: $-8<-3$.
- **Thinking $0$ is positive.** Wrong: "$0$ is the smallest positive integer." Why it is wrong: $0$ is neither positive nor negative. Right: the smallest positive integer is $1$.
- **Thinking the opposite is always negative.** Wrong: "The opposite of $-6$ is $-6$." Why it is wrong: $-6$ and $-6$ are on the same side of $0$. Right: the opposite of $-6$ is $6$.
- **Drawing an uneven number line.** Wrong: the gap from $0$ to $1$ is longer than the gap from $-1$ to $0$. Why it is wrong: consecutive integers must be exactly one unit apart. Right: space both sides evenly.
:::

## Summary

- $\mathbb{Z}=\{\dots;-2;-1;0;1;2;\dots\}$ is made of the negative integers, $0$ and the positive integers; $0$ is neither negative nor positive.
- Integers describe quantities going in two opposite directions from a reference point.
- On a horizontal number line, positive numbers are to the right of $0$ and negative numbers to the left.
- The opposite of $a$ is $-a$; $-(-a)=a$; the opposite of $0$ is $0$.
- The number further left is smaller: negative $<0<$ positive; for two negative numbers, the one that is "bigger without the sign" is smaller.

Next time you hear "minus $2$ degrees" on the forecast, you will know at once where it sits on the number line, and how much colder it is than "minus $1$ degree". In the next lesson we learn how to add and subtract these numbers.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Use integers to describe:

a) The second basement floor of a building (the ground floor is $0$);

b) A temperature $5$ degrees below $0^\circ\text{C}$;

c) A shop makes a profit of $2$ million dong.

::hint
**What you need:** choose a reference point $0$; one side gets positive numbers, the opposite side negative numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Find the reference point in each situation.
2. Decide whether the quantity is on the positive or the negative side.

::solution
a) Take the ground floor as $0$, with floors above positive and basement floors negative. The second basement floor is $-2$.

b) Take $0^\circ\text{C}$ as the reference point. Temperatures below $0^\circ\text{C}$ are negative, so it is $-5^\circ\text{C}$.

c) Take profit as positive and loss as negative. A profit of $2$ million dong is $+2$ million dong.

So we have $-2$; $-5^\circ\text{C}$; $+2$ million dong.

**Answer.** a) $-2$; b) $-5^\circ\text{C}$; c) $+2$ million dong.
:::

:::exercise
Which of the numbers $-6$; $0$; $15$; $-1$; $8$; $-20$ are negative integers, and which are positive integers?

::hint
**What you need:** a negative integer has a "$-$" sign in front of a non-zero natural number; $0$ is neither negative nor positive.

**Steps:**
1. Pick out the numbers with a "$-$" sign.
2. Do not put $0$ in either group.

::solution
The numbers with a "$-$" sign in front are $-6$; $-1$; $-20$, so they are negative integers.

The numbers $15$ and $8$ are positive integers.

The number $0$ is neither negative nor positive.

So the negative integers are $-6$; $-1$; $-20$, and the positive integers are $15$; $8$.

**Answer.** Negative: $-6$; $-1$; $-20$. Positive: $15$; $8$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the opposite of each number: $9$; $-14$; $0$; $-(-3)$.

::hint
**What you need:** the opposite of $a$ is $-a$; $-(-a)=a$.

**Steps:**
1. Change the sign of each number.
2. For $-(-3)$, simplify first, then find the opposite.

::solution
The opposite of $9$ is $-9$.

The opposite of $-14$ is $14$.

The opposite of $0$ is $0$.

We have $-(-3)=3$, so the opposite of $-(-3)$ is $-3$.

So the opposites are $-9$; $14$; $0$; $-3$.

**Answer.** $-9$; $14$; $0$; $-3$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$ or $<$ in each box:

a) $-5\ \square\ 2$;

b) $-9\ \square\ -4$;

c) $0\ \square\ -11$;

d) $-100\ \square\ -101$.

::hint
**What you need:** negative numbers are less than $0$ and less than positive numbers; for two negative numbers, the one with the larger number part is smaller.

**Steps:**
1. Find the sign of each number.
2. Use the comparing rules.

::solution
a) A negative number is less than a positive number, so $-5<2$.

b) Since $9>4$, we have $-9<-4$.

c) A negative number is less than $0$, so $0>-11$.

d) Since $100<101$, we have $-100>-101$.

So a) $<$; b) $<$; c) $>$; d) $>$.

**Answer.** a) $<$; b) $<$; c) $>$; d) $>$.
:::

:::exercise
Put these numbers in decreasing order: $-3$; $7$; $-12$; $0$; $-1$; $4$.

::hint
**What you need:** decreasing order goes from largest to smallest.

**Steps:**
1. Order the positive numbers from largest to smallest, then put $0$.
2. Order the negative numbers: the one "closer to $0$" is larger.

::solution
The positive numbers are $7$ and $4$, with $7>4$.

The number $0$ is less than every positive number and greater than every negative number.

The negative numbers are $-1$; $-3$; $-12$. Since $1<3<12$, we have $-1>-3>-12$.

So we get
$$7>4>0>-1>-3>-12.$$

So in decreasing order: $7$; $4$; $0$; $-1$; $-3$; $-12$.

**Answer.** $7$; $4$; $0$; $-1$; $-3$; $-12$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the integers $x$ such that $-4<x<3$.

::hint
**What you need:** the integers lying between two numbers on the number line.

**Steps:**
1. Draw (or picture) the number line from $-4$ to $3$.
2. List the integers in between, without the two ends.

::solution
The integers greater than $-4$ and less than $3$ are those between the points $-4$ and $3$ on the number line, not counting those two points.

They are $-3$; $-2$; $-1$; $0$; $1$; $2$.

So $x\in\{-3;-2;-1;0;1;2\}$.

**Answer.** $x\in\{-3;-2;-1;0;1;2\}$.
:::

:::exercise
The table gives the height above sea level of some places (made-up data):

| Place | Hilltop | Lake surface | Lake bed | Mine |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Height (m) | $350$ | $12$ | $-8$ | $-150$ |

a) Which place is the lowest?

b) Which places are below sea level?

::hint
**What you need:** a negative height means below sea level; the lowest place has the smallest height.

**Steps:**
1. Put the four numbers in increasing order.
2. Pick out the negative numbers.

::solution
a) The two negative numbers are $-8$ and $-150$; since $150>8$, we have $-150<-8$. The negative numbers are less than the positive numbers $12$ and $350$.

So $-150<-8<12<350$, and the mine is the lowest place.

b) The places with negative heights are the lake bed ($-8$ m) and the mine ($-150$ m).

So the mine is the lowest; the lake bed and the mine are below sea level.

**Answer.** a) The mine; b) The lake bed and the mine.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
On the number line, the point $A$ shows the number $-2$. The point $B$ is exactly $5$ units from $A$. Which number does $B$ show?

::hint
**What you need:** moving right makes numbers bigger; moving left makes them smaller.

**Steps:**
1. Look at the case where $B$ is to the right of $A$.
2. Look at the case where $B$ is to the left of $A$.

::solution
There are two cases.

If $B$ is to the right of $A$: from $-2$ we move $5$ units to the right, passing $-1$; $0$; $1$; $2$; $3$. So $B$ shows $3$.

If $B$ is to the left of $A$: from $-2$ we move $5$ units to the left, passing $-3$; $-4$; $-5$; $-6$; $-7$. So $B$ shows $-7$.

So the point $B$ shows $3$ or $-7$.

**Answer.** $3$ or $-7$.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
Which of these is a negative integer?
A. $0$.
B. $-(-5)$.
C. $-9$.
D. $7$.
::key C
$-9$ is a negative integer. A: $0$ is not negative; B: $-(-5)=5$ is positive; D is positive.

::mc
The opposite of $-15$ is
A. $15$.
B. $-15$.
C. $0$.
D. $\dfrac{1}{15}$.
::key A
The opposite of $-15$ is $15$. B is the number itself; C is unrelated; D is the reciprocal (later topic), not the opposite.

::mc
Which statement is true?
A. $-6>-2$.
B. $-1<-10$.
C. $0<-3$.
D. $-7<-4$.
::key D
Since $7>4$, $-7<-4$. A and B compare negative numbers like natural numbers; C is false because negative numbers are less than $0$.

::mc
How many integers $x$ satisfy $-3\le x\le2$?
A. $4$.
B. $5$.
C. $6$.
D. $7$.
::key C
They are $-3$; $-2$; $-1$; $0$; $1$; $2$, which is $6$ numbers. A leaves out both ends; B forgets $0$; D counts one too many.

::tf
Decide whether each statement is true or false.
a) Every natural number is an integer.
b) The number $0$ is the smallest positive integer.
c) The opposite of a negative integer is a positive integer.
d) Of two negative integers, the one with the smaller number part is smaller.
::key T F T F
a) True: $\mathbb{N}\subset\mathbb{Z}$. b) False: $0$ is not positive; the smallest positive integer is $1$. c) True: for example, the opposite of $-4$ is $4$. d) False: for example, $3<8$ but $-3>-8$.

::sa
What is the largest negative integer?
::key -1
The negative integers are $-1$; $-2$; $-3$; …, and $-1$ is closest to $0$, so it is the largest.

::sa
At $6$ a.m. the temperature is $-3^\circ\text{C}$; by noon it has risen by $8^\circ\text{C}$. What is the temperature at noon, in degrees Celsius?
::key 5
On a thermometer, going up $8$ marks from $-3$: the first $3$ marks reach $0$, and $5$ more reach $5$. The noon temperature is $5^\circ\text{C}$.
:::
