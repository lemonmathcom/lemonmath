:::goals
- Read data from bar charts and dual bar charts.
- Draw a bar chart from a statistical table.
- Draw conclusions: largest and smallest values, totals, differences, percentages.
- Use a dual bar chart to compare two data sets, and recognise charts that can mislead.
:::

In the last lesson, class 6A made a pictogram of their favourite sports. Pictograms are very easy on the eye, but they have a drawback: when a value is $13$ or $17$, we have to draw awkward bits of symbols that are hard to read accurately. And when we want to compare class 6A with class 6B, two pictograms side by side do not make the differences easy to see.

Bar charts solve both problems. Each value is a bar, and how tall or short the bar is can be read accurately from a scale on an axis. And when we put two bars side by side for two classes, we get a dual bar chart: one look tells us which class is ahead in which sport.

## Bar charts

A bar chart has:

- A **horizontal axis**: the names of the categories (the clubs, the classes, the months…).
- A **vertical axis**: evenly spaced marks showing the values, starting from $0$, with the unit.
- **One bar** for each category; the bars are rectangles of equal width, evenly spaced; the **height** of a bar shows its value.

:::tip Steps for drawing a bar chart
1. Draw a horizontal axis and a vertical axis at right angles.
2. On the horizontal axis, write the names of the categories, evenly spaced.
3. On the vertical axis, choose a scale that suits the data (starting from $0$) and write the unit.
4. Draw bars whose heights are the values and whose widths are equal; write each value on top of its bar.
5. Give the chart a title.
:::

## Examples

We start by reading a bar chart.

:::example Reading a bar chart
Look at the chart "Number of students in the school clubs".

a) How many students are in each club?

b) Which club has the most students, and which has the fewest?

c) How many club places are there altogether in the five clubs?

**Solution.**

a) Read the value on top of each bar: Maths $12$; English $18$; Football $24$; Art $9$; Music $15$ students.

b) The Football bar is the tallest ($24$) and the Art bar the shortest ($9$). So the Football club has the most students and the Art club the fewest.

c) The total number of club places is
$$12+18+24+9+15=78.$$

So a) as above; b) Football has the most, Art the fewest; c) $78$ club places.
:::

From a chart we can also make other comparisons.

:::example Comparing on a bar chart
Using the chart "Number of students in the school clubs":

a) How many more students does the Football club have than the Art club?

b) How many times as many students does the Football club have as the Maths club?

**Solution.**

a) The Football club has
$$24-9=15\ (\text{students})$$
more than the Art club.

b) We have $24:12=2$, so the Football club has $2$ times as many students as the Maths club.

So a) $15$ students; b) $2$ times as many.

**Remark.** On the chart, the Football bar is also exactly twice as tall as the Maths bar. When a chart is drawn correctly, the ratio of the bar heights shows the ratio of the values.
:::

Reading a dual bar chart means reading pair by pair.

:::example Reading a dual bar chart
Look at the chart "Favourite sports of the students in classes 6A and 6B".

a) In which sports do more students in class 6B than in class 6A like the sport?

b) How many students are there in each class (each student chose exactly one sport)?

**Solution.**

a) Compare each pair of bars:

Football: 6A has $15$, 6B has $12$, so 6A has more.

Badminton: 6A has $10$, 6B has $14$, so 6B has more.

Swimming: 6A has $5$, 6B has $8$, so 6B has more.

Basketball: 6A has $10$, 6B has $6$, so 6A has more.

b) Class 6A has $15+10+5+10=40$ students. Class 6B has $12+14+8+6=40$ students.

So a) badminton and swimming; b) each class has $40$ students.
:::

Drawing a bar chart means choosing a sensible scale.

:::example Drawing a bar chart
The numbers of books An read in five months are: January $4$ books, February $2$, March $5$, April $3$, May $6$. Describe how to draw a bar chart of these data.

**Solution.**

Draw a horizontal axis and write the five months, evenly spaced: January, February, March, April, May.

Draw a vertical axis at right angles to it. The largest value is $6$, so mark the vertical axis from $0$ to $6$, each mark standing for $1$ book; write the unit "Number of books".

Draw five bars of equal width with heights $4$; $2$; $5$; $3$; $6$ marks; write each value on top of its bar.

Give the chart a title: "Books An read in the first five months of the year".

So the chart has five bars of heights $4$; $2$; $5$; $3$; $6$ units on a vertical axis marked from $0$ to $6$.
:::

Finally, we use the dual bar chart to answer a question that needs some thought.

:::example Which class loves football more?
Using the chart "Favourite sports of the students in classes 6A and 6B", work out the percentage of each class that likes football. Which class likes football more?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* We need to compare how much the two classes like football. Both classes have $40$ students, but to compare reliably we use percentages.

*Plan.* Divide the number who like football by the size of the class and multiply by $100\%$.

*Solve.* In class 6A, $15$ of the $40$ students like football, that is,
$$\dfrac{15}{40}\cdot100\%=37.5\%.$$

In class 6B, $12$ of the $40$ students like football, that is,
$$\dfrac{12}{40}\cdot100\%=30\%.$$

Since $37.5\%>30\%$, class 6A likes football more.

So class 6A likes football more ($37.5\%$ against $30\%$).

*Look back.* Here the two classes are the same size, so comparing the numbers ($15>12$) gives the same conclusion. But if the classes were different sizes, only the percentages would allow a fair comparison.
:::

:::warning Common mistakes
- **A vertical axis that does not start at $0$.** Wrong: starting the vertical axis at $100$ to make the bars "look more different". Why it is wrong: the heights of the bars are no longer in proportion to the values, so the reader is misled. Right: the vertical axis of a bar chart must start at $0$ (see exercise 8).
- **Uneven scale.** Wrong: marking $0$; $5$; $10$; $20$; $50$ at equal spacing on the vertical axis. Why it is wrong: each gap must stand for the same amount. Right: $0$; $10$; $20$; $30$; …
- **Forgetting the key on a dual bar chart.** Wrong: drawing two bars side by side without saying which class each belongs to. Why it is wrong: the reader cannot tell what to compare. Right: always include a key for the two kinds of bar.
- **Reading the wrong bar in a pair.** Wrong: reading the white bar as class 6A's value. Why it is wrong: you must check the key. Right: look at the key first, then read.
:::

## Summary

- Bar chart: one bar per category; the height of a bar shows its value, read from a vertical axis with an even scale starting at $0$.
- Drawing a bar chart: draw two axes, write the categories, choose a scale, draw bars of equal width, write the values and the title.
- Dual bar chart: each category has two bars side by side for two data sets; there must be a key.
- From a chart we find the largest and smallest values, totals, differences and percentages, and draw conclusions.

The dual bar chart in this lesson shows at once something two pictograms could hardly say clearly: class 6A is keener on football and basketball, while class 6B prefers badminton and swimming. A good chart lets the data speak for itself. In the next lesson we move on to games of chance with [possible outcomes and events](lesson:outcomes-and-events). If you need to revise how to make a statistical table, see [Data and data collection](lesson:data-collection).

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Using the chart "Number of students in the school clubs":

a) How many students are in the English club?

b) Which clubs have more than $12$ students?

::hint
**What you need:** read the value on top of each bar.

**Steps:**
1. Find the English bar.
2. Compare each value with $12$.

::solution
a) The English bar shows $18$, so the English club has $18$ students.

b) The values greater than $12$ are: English ($18$), Football ($24$), Music ($15$). The Maths club has exactly $12$ students, not more than $12$.

So a) $18$ students; b) English, Football, Music.

**Answer.** a) $18$; b) English, Football, Music.
:::

:::exercise
Using the chart "Number of students in the school clubs", what percentage of all the club places does the Football club have? (Round to the nearest whole number.)

::hint
**What you need:** the total number of places is $78$; $a$ as a percentage of $b$ is $\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot100\%$.

**Steps:**
1. Write the ratio $\dfrac{24}{78}$.
2. Multiply by $100\%$ and round.

::solution
The total number of places in the five clubs is $78$.

The Football club has
$$\dfrac{24}{78}\cdot100\%\approx30.77\%\approx31\%$$
of the places.

So the Football club has about $31\%$ of all the places.

**Answer.** About $31\%$.
:::

:::exercise
Using the chart "Favourite sports of the students in classes 6A and 6B", work out the total number of students in both classes who like each sport. Which sport is the most popular across both classes?

::hint
**What you need:** add the two values in each pair of bars.

**Steps:**
1. For each sport, add the values for 6A and 6B.
2. Compare the totals.

::solution
The total number of students in both classes who like each sport is:

Football: $15+12=27$ students.

Badminton: $10+14=24$ students.

Swimming: $5+8=13$ students.

Basketball: $10+6=16$ students.

Since $27$ is the largest, football is the most popular across both classes.

So football $27$, badminton $24$, swimming $13$, basketball $16$; football is the most popular.

**Answer.** Football ($27$ students).
:::

:::exercise
Using the chart "Favourite sports of the students in classes 6A and 6B", in which sport is the difference between the two classes largest?

::hint
**What you need:** the difference is the larger number minus the smaller.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the difference for each sport.
2. Find the largest difference (there may be more than one sport).

::solution
The difference between the two classes for each sport is:

Football: $15-12=3$.

Badminton: $14-10=4$.

Swimming: $8-5=3$.

Basketball: $10-6=4$.

The largest difference is $4$, in badminton and basketball.

So badminton and basketball have the largest difference ($4$ students).

**Answer.** Badminton and basketball ($4$ students).
:::

:::exercise
The numbers of hours five students spend on homework in a week are: An $8$ hours, Bình $6$ hours, Chi $10$ hours, Dũng $7$ hours, Hà $9$ hours. Describe how to draw a bar chart of these data (the scale on the vertical axis, the height of each bar).

::hint
**What you need:** the steps for drawing a bar chart.

**Steps:**
1. The largest value is $10$: choose a suitable scale.
2. The height of each bar is the number of hours.

::solution
Draw a horizontal axis and write the five names, evenly spaced: An, Bình, Chi, Dũng, Hà.

Draw a vertical axis at right angles to it, marked from $0$ to $10$, each mark standing for $1$ hour (or each mark for $2$ hours, to keep it neat); write the unit "Hours".

Draw five bars of equal width with heights $8$; $6$; $10$; $7$; $9$ hours; write each value on top of its bar.

Give the chart a title: "Weekly homework hours of five students".

So the chart has five bars of heights $8$; $6$; $10$; $7$; $9$ hours on a vertical axis marked from $0$ to $10$.

**Answer.** Vertical axis from $0$ to $10$; bars of heights $8$; $6$; $10$; $7$; $9$.
:::

:::exercise
In the last lesson, the four grade 6 classes planted these numbers of trees: 6A $30$, 6B $25$, 6C $40$, 6D $35$. If we draw a bar chart with each mark on the vertical axis standing for $5$ trees, how many marks tall is each bar? At least how many marks does the vertical axis need?

::hint
**What you need:** the number of marks is the number of trees divided by $5$.

**Steps:**
1. Divide each number of trees by $5$.
2. The vertical axis needs at least as many marks as the tallest bar.

::solution
The height of each bar (in marks) is:

6A: $30:5=6$ marks; 6B: $25:5=5$ marks; 6C: $40:5=8$ marks; 6D: $35:5=7$ marks.

The tallest bar is $8$ marks (class 6C), so the vertical axis needs at least $8$ marks, that is, from $0$ to $40$ trees.

So the bars are $6$; $5$; $8$; $7$ marks tall, and the vertical axis needs at least $8$ marks.

**Answer.** $6$; $5$; $8$; $7$ marks; at least $8$ marks.
:::

:::exercise
The table shows the rainfall (mm) in the four rainy-season months in two cities $A$ and $B$ (made-up data):

| Month | June | July | August | September |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| City $A$ | $180$ | $220$ | $260$ | $200$ |
| City $B$ | $150$ | $240$ | $230$ | $210$ |

If we draw a dual bar chart from this table:

a) In which months is city $A$'s bar taller than city $B$'s?

b) Find the total rainfall over the four months for each city. Which city had more rain?

::hint
**What you need:** compare each pair of values; add up each city's values.

**Steps:**
1. Compare month by month.
2. Add up the four months for each city, then compare.

::solution
a) Compare month by month:

June: $180>150$, $A$ is taller.

July: $220<240$, $B$ is taller.

August: $260>230$, $A$ is taller.

September: $200<210$, $B$ is taller.

So city $A$'s bar is taller in June and August.

b) The total rainfall in city $A$ is $180+220+260+200=860$ (mm).

The total rainfall in city $B$ is $150+240+230+210=830$ (mm).

Since $860>830$, city $A$ had more rain.

So a) June and August; b) $A$: $860$ mm, $B$: $830$ mm, so city $A$ had more rain.

**Answer.** a) June, August. b) $A$: $860$ mm; $B$: $830$ mm; $A$ had more rain.
:::

:::exercise
A shop draws a bar chart comparing its takings in two months: last month $110$ million dong, this month $120$ million dong. They start the vertical axis at $100$ million dong. On the chart, how many times as tall as last month's bar does this month's bar look? In reality, by what percentage did the takings go up? (Round to the nearest whole number.) What do you notice?

::hint
**What you need:** when the axis starts at $100$, a bar's height shows only the part above $100$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the visible parts of the bars: $110-100$ and $120-100$.
2. Work out the real percentage rise: $\dfrac{120-110}{110}\cdot100\%$.

::solution
The vertical axis starts at $100$, so the visible part of last month's bar stands for $110-100=10$ and this month's for $120-100=20$. So this month's bar looks $20:10=2$ times as tall as last month's.

In reality, the takings rose by $120-110=10$ million dong, that is, by
$$\dfrac{10}{110}\cdot100\%\approx9.09\%\approx9\%.$$

So on the chart the takings seem to have doubled, but in reality they rose by only about $9\%$. A chart whose vertical axis does not start at $0$ can easily mislead; when reading a chart, always look at the vertical axis.

**Answer.** Looks $2$ times as tall; really up about $9\%$; the chart is misleading.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Using the chart "Number of students in the school clubs", what is the mean number of students per club? (Round to the nearest whole number.) Which club's size is closest to that mean?

::hint
**What you need:** the mean is the total divided by the number of clubs.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $78:5$ and round.
2. Compare how far each value is from the mean.

::solution
The total number of places is $78$, and there are $5$ clubs. The mean number per club is
$$78:5=15.6\approx16\ (\text{students}).$$

The distances from each value to $15.6$ are: Maths $3.6$; English $2.4$; Football $8.4$; Art $6.6$; Music $0.6$.

The smallest distance is $0.6$, for the Music club ($15$ students).

So the mean is about $16$ students per club, and the Music club is closest to it.

**Answer.** About $16$ students; the Music club.
:::
