- Give two or more fractions a common denominator by using the LCM of the denominators.
- Compare two fractions with the same denominator or with different denominators, including negative fractions.
- Put a list of fractions in increasing or decreasing order.
- Write a fraction greater than $1$ as a positive mixed number, and change a positive mixed number into a fraction.
:::

An and Binh take two different tests. An gets $\dfrac{7}{10}$ of the questions on his test right, and Binh gets $\dfrac34$ of the questions on his test right. Who did better?

Looking at the two fractions, it is hard to say straight away. $7$ is bigger than $3$, but $10$ is also bigger than $4$. One splits into ten parts and takes seven; the other splits into four parts and takes three. The two ways of splitting are different, so the "parts" have different sizes and cannot be counted together.

To compare, we must bring the two fractions to the same way of splitting. This is called finding a common denominator, and it is the key to the whole lesson.

## Giving fractions a common denominator

By the basic property of fractions, we may multiply the numerator and the denominator by the same non-zero number. So we multiply in such a way that the two fractions end up with the same denominator:

$$\dfrac{7}{10}=\dfrac{7\cdot2}{10\cdot2}=\dfrac{14}{20},\qquad \dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{3\cdot5}{4\cdot5}=\dfrac{15}{20}.$$

Now both are split into $20$ parts. We chose $20$ because it is divisible by both $10$ and $4$, and it is the smallest such number: $20=\text{LCM}(10, 4)$.

:::theorem Rule for finding a common denominator
To give two or more fractions with **positive denominators** a common denominator:
1. Find a common multiple of the denominators (usually the LCM) to use as the **common denominator**.
2. Find the **multiplying factor** for each denominator by dividing the common denominator by that denominator.
3. Multiply the numerator and the denominator of each fraction by its multiplying factor.
:::

If a fraction has a negative denominator, change it to a positive one first: $\dfrac{5}{-6}=\dfrac{-5}{6}$. Using the LCM as the common denominator keeps the numbers as small as possible, which makes the calculation lighter.

## Comparing two fractions with the same denominator

Once the denominators are the same, the parts are the same size, so all that remains is to see which fraction takes more parts.

:::theorem Comparing fractions with the same denominator
Of two fractions with the same **positive denominator**, the one with the larger numerator is the larger fraction.
:::

For $\dfrac{14}{20}$ and $\dfrac{15}{20}$: since $14<15$, we have $\dfrac{14}{20}<\dfrac{15}{20}$, that is, $\dfrac{7}{10}<\dfrac{3}{4}$. Binh did slightly better than An.

The rule also works when the numerators are negative; just compare the numerators as integers. For example, $-7<-4$, so $\dfrac{-7}{9}<\dfrac{-4}{9}$. The number line below shows this: the fraction further to the left is the smaller one.

:::note Negative fractions, positive fractions
A fraction greater than $0$ is called a positive fraction, and a fraction less than $0$ is called a negative fraction. A negative fraction is always less than a positive fraction. So when you compare a negative fraction with a positive one, you do not need a common denominator.
:::

## Comparing two fractions with different denominators

:::theorem Comparing fractions with different denominators
To compare two fractions with different denominators, write them as two fractions with the **same positive denominator**, then compare the numerators: the fraction with the larger numerator is the larger fraction.
:::

In short, three steps: positive denominators, common denominator, compare numerators. The place that needs most care is negative numerators: $-15<-14$, even though $15>14$.

## Positive mixed numbers

Four friends share $7$ cakes equally. Each gets $\dfrac74$ of a cake. But in real life people would share like this: each friend first takes $1$ whole cake, and the remaining $3$ cakes are cut into quarters, so each gets another $\dfrac34$ of a cake. We write

$$\dfrac74=1+\dfrac34=1\dfrac34.$$

:::definition Positive mixed number
The form $1\dfrac34$ is called a **positive mixed number**, read "one and three quarters"; $1$ is the **whole-number part** and $\dfrac34$ is the **fractional part**. The fractional part of a mixed number is always less than $1$.
:::

**Changing a fraction greater than $1$ into a mixed number.** Divide the numerator by the denominator: the quotient is the whole-number part, the remainder is the numerator of the fractional part, and the denominator stays the same. For example, $17:5=3$ remainder $2$, so $\dfrac{17}{5}=3\dfrac25$.

**Changing a mixed number into a fraction.** Multiply the whole-number part by the denominator and add the numerator; the denominator stays the same:
$$2\dfrac37=\dfrac{2\cdot7+3}{7}=\dfrac{17}{7}.$$

A mixed number lets us picture the size quickly: looking at $3\dfrac25$ we see at once that the number is a little more than $3$, whereas $\dfrac{17}{5}$ takes a moment's thought.

## Examples

We start with common denominators, the preparation for every comparison.

:::example Giving two fractions a common denominator
Write the fractions $\dfrac{-3}{4}$ and $\dfrac{5}{6}$ with a common denominator.

**Solution.**

We have $\text{LCM}(4, 6)=12$, so we choose $12$ as the common denominator.

The multiplying factor for the denominator $4$ is $12:4=3$; the multiplying factor for the denominator $6$ is $12:6=2$.

Multiplying the numerator and the denominator of each fraction by its multiplying factor, we get
$$\dfrac{-3}{4}=\dfrac{(-3)\cdot3}{4\cdot3}=\dfrac{-9}{12},\qquad \dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{5\cdot2}{6\cdot2}=\dfrac{10}{12}.$$

So with a common denominator the two fractions are $\dfrac{-9}{12}$ and $\dfrac{10}{12}$.
:::

With the same denominator, just remember to make the denominator positive.

:::example Comparing fractions with the same denominator
Compare:

a) $\dfrac{-7}{9}$ and $\dfrac{-4}{9}$;

b) $\dfrac{5}{-11}$ and $\dfrac{-3}{11}$.

**Solution.**

a) The two fractions have the same positive denominator, $9$. Since $-7<-4$,
$$\dfrac{-7}{9}<\dfrac{-4}{9}.$$

b) First write $\dfrac{5}{-11}$ with a positive denominator: $\dfrac{5}{-11}=\dfrac{-5}{11}$.

The fractions $\dfrac{-5}{11}$ and $\dfrac{-3}{11}$ have the same positive denominator, $11$. Since $-5<-3$, we get $\dfrac{-5}{11}<\dfrac{-3}{11}$.

So a) $\dfrac{-7}{9}<\dfrac{-4}{9}$; b) $\dfrac{5}{-11}<\dfrac{-3}{11}$.
:::

Now different denominators, including a pair of negative fractions that is very easy to get wrong.

:::example Comparing fractions with different denominators
Compare:

a) $\dfrac{7}{10}$ and $\dfrac{3}{4}$;

b) $\dfrac{-5}{8}$ and $\dfrac{-7}{12}$.

**Solution.**

a) We have $\text{LCM}(10, 4)=20$. With a common denominator:
$$\dfrac{7}{10}=\dfrac{7\cdot2}{10\cdot2}=\dfrac{14}{20},\qquad \dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{3\cdot5}{4\cdot5}=\dfrac{15}{20}.$$

Since $14<15$, we have $\dfrac{14}{20}<\dfrac{15}{20}$. Hence $\dfrac{7}{10}<\dfrac{3}{4}$.

b) We have $\text{LCM}(8, 12)=24$. With a common denominator:
$$\dfrac{-5}{8}=\dfrac{(-5)\cdot3}{8\cdot3}=\dfrac{-15}{24},\qquad \dfrac{-7}{12}=\dfrac{(-7)\cdot2}{12\cdot2}=\dfrac{-14}{24}.$$

Since $-15<-14$, we have $\dfrac{-15}{24}<\dfrac{-14}{24}$. Hence $\dfrac{-5}{8}<\dfrac{-7}{12}$.

So a) $\dfrac{7}{10}<\dfrac{3}{4}$; b) $\dfrac{-5}{8}<\dfrac{-7}{12}$.
:::

When several fractions have to be ordered, we give them all a common denominator in one go.

:::example Increasing order
Put these numbers in increasing order: $\dfrac{-1}{2}$; $\dfrac{2}{3}$; $\dfrac{-3}{4}$; $0$; $\dfrac{5}{6}$.

**Solution.**

We have $\text{LCM}(2, 3, 4, 6)=12$. With a common denominator:
$$\dfrac{-1}{2}=\dfrac{-6}{12},\quad \dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{8}{12},\quad \dfrac{-3}{4}=\dfrac{-9}{12},\quad 0=\dfrac{0}{12},\quad \dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{10}{12}.$$

Comparing the numerators, we have $-9<-6<0<8<10$. Hence
$$\dfrac{-9}{12}<\dfrac{-6}{12}<\dfrac{0}{12}<\dfrac{8}{12}<\dfrac{10}{12}.$$

So in increasing order the numbers are $\dfrac{-3}{4}$; $\dfrac{-1}{2}$; $0$; $\dfrac{2}{3}$; $\dfrac{5}{6}$.
:::

Next come mixed numbers: converting both ways and comparing.

:::example Positive mixed numbers
a) Write the fraction $\dfrac{17}{5}$ as a mixed number.

b) Write the mixed number $2\dfrac{3}{7}$ as a fraction.

c) Compare $2\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\dfrac{9}{4}$.

**Solution.**

a) We have $17:5=3$ (remainder $2$). Hence $\dfrac{17}{5}=3\dfrac{2}{5}$.

b) We have $2\dfrac{3}{7}=\dfrac{2\cdot7+3}{7}=\dfrac{17}{7}$.

c) Change the mixed number into a fraction: $2\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{2\cdot3+1}{3}=\dfrac{7}{3}$.

Write $\dfrac{7}{3}$ and $\dfrac{9}{4}$ with the common denominator $12$:
$$\dfrac{7}{3}=\dfrac{28}{12},\qquad \dfrac{9}{4}=\dfrac{27}{12}.$$

Since $28>27$, we have $\dfrac{7}{3}>\dfrac{9}{4}$, that is, $2\dfrac{1}{3}>\dfrac{9}{4}$.

So a) $3\dfrac25$; b) $\dfrac{17}{7}$; c) $2\dfrac13>\dfrac94$.

**Remark.** There is a quicker way for part c): $\dfrac94=2\dfrac14$. The two mixed numbers have the same whole-number part $2$, and $\dfrac13>\dfrac14$, so $2\dfrac13>2\dfrac14$.
:::

Finally, a problem that compares three people at once.

:::example Who has read the most?
Three friends, An, Binh and Chi, are reading the same book. After a week, An has read $\dfrac{5}{8}$ of the book, Binh has read $\dfrac{7}{12}$ and Chi has read $\dfrac{2}{3}$. Who has read the most, and who has read the least?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* The three friends are reading the same book, so the parts they have read can be compared. We need to order the three fractions $\dfrac58$; $\dfrac{7}{12}$; $\dfrac23$.

*Plan.* The three fractions have different denominators. We give them a common denominator and compare the numerators.

*Solve.* We have $\text{LCM}(8, 12, 3)=24$. With a common denominator:
$$\dfrac{5}{8}=\dfrac{5\cdot3}{8\cdot3}=\dfrac{15}{24},\qquad \dfrac{7}{12}=\dfrac{7\cdot2}{12\cdot2}=\dfrac{14}{24},\qquad \dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{2\cdot8}{3\cdot8}=\dfrac{16}{24}.$$

Since $14<15<16$, we have $\dfrac{14}{24}<\dfrac{15}{24}<\dfrac{16}{24}$, that is,
$$\dfrac{7}{12}<\dfrac{5}{8}<\dfrac{2}{3}.$$

So Chi has read the most and Binh has read the least.

*Look back.* Is the result reasonable? All three fractions are a little more than $\dfrac12$ ($\dfrac{12}{24}$), and they differ by only $\dfrac{1}{24}$ of the book. The three friends have read almost the same amount, and without a common denominator it is very hard to see who is ahead.
:::

:::warning Common mistakes
- **Comparing numerators and denominators separately when the denominators differ.** Wrong: $\dfrac{7}{10}>\dfrac{3}{4}$ "because $7>3$ and $10>4$". Why it is wrong: with different denominators the parts have different sizes, so the numerators cannot be compared directly. Right: with a common denominator, $\dfrac{14}{20}<\dfrac{15}{20}$, so $\dfrac{7}{10}<\dfrac{3}{4}$.
- **Comparing negative numerators like natural numbers.** Wrong: $\dfrac{-15}{24}>\dfrac{-14}{24}$ "because $15>14$". Why it is wrong: we must compare the integers $-15$ and $-14$, and $-15<-14$. Right: $\dfrac{-15}{24}<\dfrac{-14}{24}$.
- **Comparing while a denominator is still negative.** Wrong: $\dfrac{5}{-11}>\dfrac{-3}{11}$ "because $5>-3$". Why it is wrong: the rule for comparing numerators only applies when the fractions have the same **positive** denominator. Right: $\dfrac{5}{-11}=\dfrac{-5}{11}<\dfrac{-3}{11}$.
- **Converting a mixed number wrongly.** Wrong: $2\dfrac{3}{7}=\dfrac{2\cdot3}{7}=\dfrac{6}{7}$. Why it is wrong: $2\dfrac37$ means $2+\dfrac37$, not $2\cdot\dfrac37$. Right: $2\dfrac{3}{7}=\dfrac{2\cdot7+3}{7}=\dfrac{17}{7}$.
:::

## Summary

- Common denominator: choose one (usually the LCM of the positive denominators), find the multiplying factors, and multiply each numerator and denominator by its factor.
- Same positive denominator: the larger numerator gives the larger fraction (compare numerators as integers).
- Different denominators: positive denominators first, then a common denominator, then compare numerators.
- A negative fraction is less than $0$ and less than every positive fraction.
- Positive mixed numbers: $\dfrac{17}{5}=3\dfrac25$ ($17$ divided by $5$ is $3$ remainder $2$); the other way, $2\dfrac37=\dfrac{2\cdot7+3}{7}=\dfrac{17}{7}$.

Back to An and Binh: $\dfrac{7}{10}=\dfrac{14}{20}$ and $\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{15}{20}$, so Binh got a larger part right. Two fractions can only be compared when they speak the same "language", that is, when they have the same denominator. You will meet exactly this idea again in the lesson on adding and subtracting fractions. If you need to revise how to find an LCM, see [Common multiples. Lowest common multiple](lesson:lowest-common-multiple).

:::exercise
Write the fractions in each part with a common denominator:

a) $\dfrac{2}{5}$ and $\dfrac{-3}{7}$;

b) $\dfrac{-5}{6}$ and $\dfrac{7}{9}$;

c) $\dfrac{1}{4}$; $\dfrac{-2}{3}$ and $\dfrac{5}{8}$.

::hint
**What you need:** the common denominator is the LCM of the denominators; each multiplying factor is the common denominator divided by that denominator.

**Steps:**
1. Find the LCM of the denominators.
2. Multiply the numerator and the denominator of each fraction by its multiplying factor.

::solution
a) We have $\text{LCM}(5, 7)=35$. The multiplying factors are $7$ and $5$. Hence
$$\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{2\cdot7}{5\cdot7}=\dfrac{14}{35},\qquad \dfrac{-3}{7}=\dfrac{(-3)\cdot5}{7\cdot5}=\dfrac{-15}{35}.$$

b) We have $\text{LCM}(6, 9)=18$. The multiplying factors are $3$ and $2$. Hence
$$\dfrac{-5}{6}=\dfrac{(-5)\cdot3}{6\cdot3}=\dfrac{-15}{18},\qquad \dfrac{7}{9}=\dfrac{7\cdot2}{9\cdot2}=\dfrac{14}{18}.$$

c) We have $\text{LCM}(4, 3, 8)=24$. The multiplying factors are $6$; $8$; $3$. Hence
$$\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{6}{24},\qquad \dfrac{-2}{3}=\dfrac{-16}{24},\qquad \dfrac{5}{8}=\dfrac{15}{24}.$$

So a) $\dfrac{14}{35}$ and $\dfrac{-15}{35}$; b) $\dfrac{-15}{18}$ and $\dfrac{14}{18}$; c) $\dfrac{6}{24}$; $\dfrac{-16}{24}$ and $\dfrac{15}{24}$.

**Answer.** a) $\dfrac{14}{35}$; $\dfrac{-15}{35}$. b) $\dfrac{-15}{18}$; $\dfrac{14}{18}$. c) $\dfrac{6}{24}$; $\dfrac{-16}{24}$; $\dfrac{15}{24}$.
:::

:::exercise
Compare:

a) $\dfrac{-5}{13}$ and $\dfrac{-8}{13}$;

b) $\dfrac{4}{-7}$ and $\dfrac{-6}{7}$;

c) $0$ and $\dfrac{-2}{9}$.

::hint
**What you need:** with the same positive denominator, compare the numerators; a negative fraction is less than $0$.

**Steps:**
1. Make the denominator positive if necessary.
2. Compare the two numerators as integers.

::solution
a) The two fractions have the same positive denominator, $13$. Since $-5>-8$, we get $\dfrac{-5}{13}>\dfrac{-8}{13}$.

b) We have $\dfrac{4}{-7}=\dfrac{-4}{7}$. The fractions $\dfrac{-4}{7}$ and $\dfrac{-6}{7}$ have the same positive denominator, $7$. Since $-4>-6$, we get $\dfrac{-4}{7}>\dfrac{-6}{7}$, that is, $\dfrac{4}{-7}>\dfrac{-6}{7}$.

c) We have $0=\dfrac{0}{9}$. Since $0>-2$, we get $\dfrac{0}{9}>\dfrac{-2}{9}$, that is, $0>\dfrac{-2}{9}$.

So a) $\dfrac{-5}{13}>\dfrac{-8}{13}$; b) $\dfrac{4}{-7}>\dfrac{-6}{7}$; c) $0>\dfrac{-2}{9}$.

**Answer.** a) $>$; b) $>$; c) $>$.
:::

:::exercise
Compare:

a) $\dfrac{3}{8}$ and $\dfrac{5}{12}$;

b) $\dfrac{-4}{9}$ and $\dfrac{-5}{12}$;

c) $\dfrac{7}{-15}$ and $\dfrac{-2}{5}$.

::hint
**What you need:** with different denominators, write the fractions with the same positive denominator, then compare numerators.

**Steps:**
1. Make the denominators positive and find their LCM.
2. Write with the common denominator, then compare the two numerators.

::solution
a) We have $\text{LCM}(8, 12)=24$. With a common denominator:
$$\dfrac{3}{8}=\dfrac{9}{24},\qquad \dfrac{5}{12}=\dfrac{10}{24}.$$
Since $9<10$, we get $\dfrac{3}{8}<\dfrac{5}{12}$.

b) We have $\text{LCM}(9, 12)=36$. With a common denominator:
$$\dfrac{-4}{9}=\dfrac{-16}{36},\qquad \dfrac{-5}{12}=\dfrac{-15}{36}.$$
Since $-16<-15$, we get $\dfrac{-4}{9}<\dfrac{-5}{12}$.

c) We have $\dfrac{7}{-15}=\dfrac{-7}{15}$ and $\text{LCM}(15, 5)=15$. With a common denominator:
$$\dfrac{-2}{5}=\dfrac{(-2)\cdot3}{5\cdot3}=\dfrac{-6}{15}.$$
Since $-7<-6$, we get $\dfrac{-7}{15}<\dfrac{-6}{15}$, that is, $\dfrac{7}{-15}<\dfrac{-2}{5}$.

So a) $\dfrac{3}{8}<\dfrac{5}{12}$; b) $\dfrac{-4}{9}<\dfrac{-5}{12}$; c) $\dfrac{7}{-15}<\dfrac{-2}{5}$.

**Answer.** a) $<$; b) $<$; c) $<$.
:::

:::exercise
Put these fractions in decreasing order: $\dfrac{3}{4}$; $\dfrac{-2}{3}$; $\dfrac{5}{6}$; $\dfrac{-1}{2}$.

::hint
**What you need:** write them all with the same positive denominator, then order the numerators from largest to smallest.

**Steps:**
1. Find the LCM of $4$; $3$; $6$; $2$.
2. Write with the common denominator and compare the numerators.

::solution
We have $\text{LCM}(4, 3, 6, 2)=12$. With a common denominator:
$$\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{9}{12},\quad \dfrac{-2}{3}=\dfrac{-8}{12},\quad \dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{10}{12},\quad \dfrac{-1}{2}=\dfrac{-6}{12}.$$

Comparing the numerators, we have $10>9>-6>-8$. Hence
$$\dfrac{10}{12}>\dfrac{9}{12}>\dfrac{-6}{12}>\dfrac{-8}{12}.$$

So in decreasing order the fractions are $\dfrac{5}{6}$; $\dfrac{3}{4}$; $\dfrac{-1}{2}$; $\dfrac{-2}{3}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{5}{6}$; $\dfrac{3}{4}$; $\dfrac{-1}{2}$; $\dfrac{-2}{3}$.
:::

:::exercise
a) Write the fractions $\dfrac{23}{6}$ and $\dfrac{41}{8}$ as mixed numbers.

b) Write the mixed numbers $4\dfrac{2}{9}$ and $1\dfrac{7}{10}$ as fractions.

::hint
**What you need:** divide the numerator by the denominator to get the whole-number part and the remainder; the other way, multiply the whole-number part by the denominator and add the numerator.

**Steps:**
1. Part a): carry out the division with remainder.
2. Part b): work out the new numerator and keep the denominator.

::solution
a) We have $23:6=3$ (remainder $5$), so $\dfrac{23}{6}=3\dfrac{5}{6}$.

We have $41:8=5$ (remainder $1$), so $\dfrac{41}{8}=5\dfrac{1}{8}$.

b) We have $4\dfrac{2}{9}=\dfrac{4\cdot9+2}{9}=\dfrac{38}{9}$.

We have $1\dfrac{7}{10}=\dfrac{1\cdot10+7}{10}=\dfrac{17}{10}$.

So a) $3\dfrac56$ and $5\dfrac18$; b) $\dfrac{38}{9}$ and $\dfrac{17}{10}$.

**Answer.** a) $3\dfrac56$; $5\dfrac18$. b) $\dfrac{38}{9}$; $\dfrac{17}{10}$.
:::

:::exercise
Compare $3\dfrac{1}{4}$ and $\dfrac{10}{3}$.

::hint
**What you need:** change the mixed number into a fraction, then use a common denominator.

**Steps:**
1. Write $3\dfrac14$ as a fraction.
2. Give it and $\dfrac{10}{3}$ a common denominator and compare the numerators.

::solution
We have $3\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{3\cdot4+1}{4}=\dfrac{13}{4}$.

Write $\dfrac{13}{4}$ and $\dfrac{10}{3}$ with the common denominator $\text{LCM}(4, 3)=12$:
$$\dfrac{13}{4}=\dfrac{39}{12},\qquad \dfrac{10}{3}=\dfrac{40}{12}.$$

Since $39<40$, we get $\dfrac{13}{4}<\dfrac{10}{3}$.

So $3\dfrac{1}{4}<\dfrac{10}{3}$.

**Answer.** $3\dfrac{1}{4}<\dfrac{10}{3}$.
:::

:::exercise
An's house and Binh's house are the same distance from school. This morning An took $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of an hour to get to school and Binh took $\dfrac{5}{12}$ of an hour. Who travelled faster?

::hint
**What you need:** over the same distance, whoever takes less time travels faster.

**Steps:**
1. Give $\dfrac25$ and $\dfrac{5}{12}$ a common denominator.
2. Compare the two times.

::solution
We have $\text{LCM}(5, 12)=60$. With a common denominator:
$$\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{2\cdot12}{5\cdot12}=\dfrac{24}{60},\qquad \dfrac{5}{12}=\dfrac{5\cdot5}{12\cdot5}=\dfrac{25}{60}.$$

Since $24<25$, we get $\dfrac{2}{5}<\dfrac{5}{12}$, that is, An took less time than Binh.

The two travel the same distance, so the one who takes less time travels faster.

So An travelled faster than Binh.

**Answer.** An travelled faster.
:::

:::exercise
A shop sells a whole batch of rice in three days. On the first day it sells $\dfrac{3}{10}$ of the batch, on the second day $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of the batch and on the third day $\dfrac{9}{20}$ of the batch. On which day did the shop sell the most rice, and on which day the least?

::hint
**What you need:** write the three fractions with the same denominator, then compare numerators.

**Steps:**
1. Find the LCM of $10$; $4$; $20$.
2. Write with the common denominator and order them.

::solution
We have $\text{LCM}(10, 4, 20)=20$. With a common denominator:
$$\dfrac{3}{10}=\dfrac{6}{20},\qquad \dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{5}{20},\qquad \dfrac{9}{20}=\dfrac{9}{20}.$$

Since $5<6<9$, we have $\dfrac{5}{20}<\dfrac{6}{20}<\dfrac{9}{20}$, that is,
$$\dfrac{1}{4}<\dfrac{3}{10}<\dfrac{9}{20}.$$

So the shop sold the most rice on the third day and the least on the second day.

**Answer.** Most: the third day. Least: the second day.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find all the integers $x$ such that $\dfrac{-1}{2}<\dfrac{x}{12}<\dfrac{-1}{4}$.

::hint
**What you need:** write all three fractions with denominator $12$; with the same positive denominator, comparing fractions means comparing numerators.

**Steps:**
1. Write $\dfrac{-1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{-1}{4}$ with denominator $12$.
2. Find the integers $x$ between the two numerators.

::solution
With the common denominator $12$:
$$\dfrac{-1}{2}=\dfrac{-6}{12},\qquad \dfrac{-1}{4}=\dfrac{-3}{12}.$$

The condition becomes
$$\dfrac{-6}{12}<\dfrac{x}{12}<\dfrac{-3}{12}.$$

The three fractions have the same positive denominator $12$, so this holds when $-6<x<-3$.

The integers greater than $-6$ and less than $-3$ are $-5$ and $-4$.

So $x\in\{-5;-4\}$.

**Answer.** $x\in\{-5;-4\}$.
:::

Given that $5\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{x}{3}$, find the natural number $x$.
::key 17
$5\dfrac23=\dfrac{5\cdot3+2}{3}=\dfrac{17}{3}$, so $x=17$.

How many integers $x$ satisfy $\dfrac{1}{3}<\dfrac{x}{6}<\dfrac{3}{2}$?
::key 6
With denominator $6$: $\dfrac26<\dfrac{x}{6}<\dfrac96$, so $2<x<9$. The integers are $3$; $4$; $5$; $6$; $7$; $8$, six in all.
