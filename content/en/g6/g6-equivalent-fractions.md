:::goals
- Recognise a fraction whose numerator and denominator are integers, with a non-zero denominator.

Share $3$ oranges equally among $4$ friends, and each gets $\dfrac34$ of an orange. You have known that fraction since primary school: it is the result of the division $3:4$.

Now for a different story. Over $4$ hours, the temperature outside falls steadily by $3^\circ\text{C}$ in total. How much does the temperature change each hour? The total change is $-3$, shared equally over $4$ hours, that is, $(-3):4$. This division does not give an integer. To write the result, we need a fraction with a negative numerator: $\dfrac{-3}{4}$.

Now that we have negative integers, fractions have to "grow" too. The sections below show you that these new fractions behave exactly like the old ones.

## Extending the idea of a fraction

:::definition Fraction
For integers $a$, $b$ with $b\ne0$, we call $\dfrac{a}{b}$ a **fraction**; $a$ is the **numerator** and $b$ is the **denominator** of the fraction. The fraction $\dfrac{a}{b}$ is read "$a$ over $b$".
:::

So $\dfrac{-3}{4}$; $\dfrac{5}{-7}$; $\dfrac{-2}{-9}$; $\dfrac{0}{6}$ are all fractions. The fraction $\dfrac{a}{b}$ keeps its old meaning: it is the result of dividing the integer $a$ by the non-zero integer $b$. For example, $(-8):11=\dfrac{-8}{11}$.

Every integer can also be written as a fraction with denominator $1$: $6=\dfrac61$; $-4=\dfrac{-4}{1}$.

Two things are **not** fractions. One is $\dfrac{3}{0}$, because the denominator is $0$ (there is no division by $0$). The other is $\dfrac{1.5}{2}$, because the numerator is not an integer.

## Equal fractions

In primary school you learnt that $\dfrac12=\dfrac24$: half a cake is also two quarters of a cake. Notice something about those four numbers: $1\cdot4=2\cdot2$. Try another pair, $\dfrac23=\dfrac{10}{15}$: we have $2\cdot15=3\cdot10$. Each time, the "cross" products are equal.

We take exactly this as the test for equal fractions, even when numerators and denominators are negative.

:::definition Equal fractions
Two fractions $\dfrac{a}{b}$ and $\dfrac{c}{d}$ are called **equal**, written $\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{c}{d}$, if
$$a\cdot d=b\cdot c.$$
:::

This is usually called **cross-multiplication**: the numerator of one fraction times the denominator of the other. For example, $\dfrac{-3}{4}=\dfrac{6}{-8}$ because $(-3)\cdot(-8)=24$ and $4\cdot6=24$.

## The basic property of fractions

Going from $\dfrac12$ to $\dfrac24$, we multiplied both the numerator and the denominator by $2$. That works for every fraction and every non-zero integer.

:::theorem The basic property of fractions
- If we multiply both the numerator and the denominator of a fraction by the same **non-zero** integer, we get a fraction equal to the given one:
$$\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{a\cdot m}{b\cdot m}\quad\text{where } m \text{ is an integer},\ m\ne0.$$
- If we divide both the numerator and the denominator of a fraction by the same **common factor**, we get a fraction equal to the given one:
$$\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{a:n}{b:n}\quad\text{where } n \text{ is a common factor of } a \text{ and } b.$$
:::

Why is it true? Check by cross-multiplying: $a\cdot(b\cdot m)$ and $b\cdot(a\cdot m)$ both equal $a\cdot b\cdot m$, so the two fractions are equal.

This property gives us two very useful moves.

**Writing a fraction with a positive denominator.** Multiply both the numerator and the denominator by $-1$:
$$\dfrac{5}{-7}=\dfrac{5\cdot(-1)}{(-7)\cdot(-1)}=\dfrac{-5}{7},\qquad \dfrac{-2}{-9}=\dfrac{2}{9}.$$
From now on, when we calculate or compare, we always write fractions with a positive denominator, because they are easier to read.

**Simplifying a fraction.** Divide both the numerator and the denominator by a common factor other than $1$ and $-1$, and we get an equal fraction with a "smaller" numerator and denominator.

:::definition Simplest form
A fraction is in its **simplest form** when the only common factors of its numerator and denominator are $1$ and $-1$. To bring a fraction to its simplest form, divide both the numerator and the denominator by the highest common factor of their number parts.
:::

For example, HCF$(18, 24)=6$, so $\dfrac{-18}{24}=\dfrac{-18:6}{24:6}=\dfrac{-3}{4}$, and $\dfrac{-3}{4}$ is in its simplest form.

A fraction can be written in infinitely many ways: $\dfrac{-3}{4}=\dfrac{-6}{8}=\dfrac{-9}{12}=\dfrac{6}{-8}=\dots$ They are all the same number. The simplest form with a positive denominator is the neatest way to write it, and it is the one usually chosen for an answer.

## Examples

We start by learning to recognise a fraction.

:::example Recognising fractions
a) Which of the following are fractions: $\dfrac{-5}{7}$; $\dfrac{4}{-9}$; $\dfrac{3}{0}$; $\dfrac{0}{3}$; $\dfrac{1.5}{2}$?

b) Write these divisions as fractions: $(-8):11$; $6:(-13)$.

**Solution.**

a) A fraction must have an integer numerator and an integer denominator, and the denominator must not be $0$.

$\dfrac{-5}{7}$; $\dfrac{4}{-9}$; $\dfrac{0}{3}$ satisfy this, so they are fractions.

$\dfrac{3}{0}$ has denominator $0$, so it is not a fraction.

$\dfrac{1.5}{2}$ has a numerator that is not an integer, so it is not a fraction.

b) We have $(-8):11=\dfrac{-8}{11}$ and $6:(-13)=\dfrac{6}{-13}$.

So a) the fractions are $\dfrac{-5}{7}$; $\dfrac{4}{-9}$; $\dfrac{0}{3}$; b) $\dfrac{-8}{11}$ and $\dfrac{6}{-13}$.
:::

Cross-multiplication quickly answers the question "are they equal?".

:::example Checking whether two fractions are equal
Are the fractions in each pair equal?

a) $\dfrac{-2}{5}$ and $\dfrac{6}{-15}$;

b) $\dfrac{3}{-4}$ and $\dfrac{-9}{-12}$.

**Solution.**

a) We have $(-2)\cdot(-15)=30$ and $5\cdot6=30$.

Since $(-2)\cdot(-15)=5\cdot6$, we get $\dfrac{-2}{5}=\dfrac{6}{-15}$.

b) We have $3\cdot(-12)=-36$ and $(-4)\cdot(-9)=36$.

Since $-36\ne36$, we get $\dfrac{3}{-4}\ne\dfrac{-9}{-12}$.

So the pair in part a) is equal and the pair in part b) is not.

**Remark.** In part b), $\dfrac{3}{-4}$ is negative while $\dfrac{-9}{-12}$ is positive, so they cannot be equal. Looking at the signs first is also a quick check.
:::

Cross-multiplication also helps us find an unknown number.

:::example Finding an unknown number
Find the integer $x$, given that $\dfrac{x}{-6}=\dfrac{10}{15}$.

**Solution.**

Since $\dfrac{x}{-6}=\dfrac{10}{15}$, by the definition of equal fractions we have
$$x\cdot15=(-6)\cdot10.$$

So $15x=-60$, hence
$$x=(-60):15=-4.$$

Check: $\dfrac{-4}{-6}=\dfrac{4}{6}=\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $\dfrac{10}{15}=\dfrac{2}{3}$, so the two fractions are equal.

So $x=-4$.
:::

Next, we use the basic property in the "multiply" direction.

:::example Positive denominators and missing numbers
a) Write the fractions $\dfrac{5}{-8}$ and $\dfrac{-7}{-12}$ with positive denominators.

b) Find the number that replaces $?$ in $\dfrac{-3}{4}=\dfrac{?}{20}$.

**Solution.**

a) Multiplying both the numerator and the denominator of each fraction by $-1$, we get
$$\dfrac{5}{-8}=\dfrac{5\cdot(-1)}{(-8)\cdot(-1)}=\dfrac{-5}{8},\qquad \dfrac{-7}{-12}=\dfrac{(-7)\cdot(-1)}{(-12)\cdot(-1)}=\dfrac{7}{12}.$$

b) The denominator $4$ has been multiplied by $5$ to make $20$ (because $20:4=5$). So the numerator must also be multiplied by $5$:
$$\dfrac{-3}{4}=\dfrac{(-3)\cdot5}{4\cdot5}=\dfrac{-15}{20}.$$

So a) $\dfrac{5}{-8}=\dfrac{-5}{8}$; $\dfrac{-7}{-12}=\dfrac{7}{12}$; b) the missing number is $-15$.
:::

And now the "divide" direction, that is, simplifying.

:::example Simplifying to the simplest form
Write each fraction in its simplest form:

a) $\dfrac{-18}{24}$;

b) $\dfrac{45}{-60}$;

c) $\dfrac{28}{42}$.

**Solution.**

a) We have HCF$(18, 24)=6$. Dividing both the numerator and the denominator by $6$:
$$\dfrac{-18}{24}=\dfrac{-18:6}{24:6}=\dfrac{-3}{4}.$$

b) First write the fraction with a positive denominator: $\dfrac{45}{-60}=\dfrac{-45}{60}$. We have HCF$(45, 60)=15$, so
$$\dfrac{-45}{60}=\dfrac{-45:15}{60:15}=\dfrac{-3}{4}.$$

c) We have HCF$(28, 42)=14$, so
$$\dfrac{28}{42}=\dfrac{28:14}{42:14}=\dfrac{2}{3}.$$

So the results are $\dfrac{-3}{4}$; $\dfrac{-3}{4}$; $\dfrac{2}{3}$.

**Remark.** The fractions in parts a) and b) look different, but after simplifying we see that they are equal. Bringing fractions to their simplest form is a reliable way to recognise "the same number".
:::

Finally, a question about two classes that is hard to answer fairly without fractions.

:::example Which class has more cyclists?
Class 6A has $40$ students, of whom $24$ cycle to school. Class 6B has $45$ students, of whom $27$ cycle to school. Hà says: "Class 6B has more students who cycle, so cycling is more popular in class 6B." Is Hà right?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* Class 6B has more cyclists ($27>24$), but class 6B is also bigger. To compare fairly, we must see what part of each class cycles.

*Plan.* Write the fraction "number of cyclists over number of students in the class" for each class, bring it to its simplest form, then compare.

*Solve.* In class 6A, the cyclists make up $\dfrac{24}{40}$ of the students. Since HCF$(24, 40)=8$,
$$\dfrac{24}{40}=\dfrac{24:8}{40:8}=\dfrac{3}{5}.$$

In class 6B, the cyclists make up $\dfrac{27}{45}$ of the students. Since HCF$(27, 45)=9$,
$$\dfrac{27}{45}=\dfrac{27:9}{45:9}=\dfrac{3}{5}.$$

The two fractions are equal: in both classes, the cyclists make up $\dfrac35$ of the students.

So Hà is not right: cycling is equally popular in the two classes.

*Look back.* We can check by cross-multiplying: $24\cdot45=1080$ and $40\cdot27=1080$, so $\dfrac{24}{40}=\dfrac{27}{45}$. When two groups have different sizes, compare them by "parts", not by counts.
:::

:::warning Common mistakes
- **Treating $\dfrac{a}{0}$ as a fraction.** Wrong: "$\dfrac{3}{0}$ is a fraction with numerator $3$ and denominator $0$." Why it is wrong: there is no division by $0$. Right: the denominator of a fraction must not be $0$; the numerator may be $0$, for example $\dfrac{0}{3}=0$.
- **Cross-multiplying with the wrong signs.** Wrong: $\dfrac{3}{-4}=\dfrac{-9}{-12}$ "because $3\cdot12=4\cdot9$". Why it is wrong: the signs of the negative numbers have been dropped. Right: $3\cdot(-12)=-36$, while $(-4)\cdot(-9)=36$, so the fractions are not equal.
- **Adding instead of multiplying.** Wrong: $\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{2+2}{3+2}=\dfrac{4}{5}$. Why it is wrong: the basic property is only about **multiplying** or **dividing** the numerator and the denominator by the same number; $2\cdot5\ne3\cdot4$. Right: $\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{2\cdot2}{3\cdot2}=\dfrac{4}{6}$.
- **Not simplifying fully.** Wrong: "$\dfrac{-18}{24}=\dfrac{-9}{12}$ is in its simplest form." Why it is wrong: $9$ and $12$ still have the common factor $3$. Right: $\dfrac{-18}{24}=\dfrac{-3}{4}$.
:::

## Summary

- $\dfrac{a}{b}$ is a fraction when $a$, $b$ are integers and $b\ne0$; it is the result of the division $a:b$. An integer $a$ can be written as $\dfrac{a}{1}$.
- $\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{c}{d}$ if $a\cdot d=b\cdot c$ (cross-multiplication).
- Multiplying the numerator and the denominator by the same non-zero integer, or dividing them by the same common factor, gives a fraction equal to the given one.
- Change the signs of both the numerator and the denominator to get a positive denominator: $\dfrac{5}{-7}=\dfrac{-5}{7}$.
- Simplest form: divide the numerator and the denominator by the HCF of their number parts.

Each hour the temperature changes by $\dfrac{-3}{4}$ of a degree: the answer to the opening situation now has a proper name, and you know it is also $\dfrac{-6}{8}$ or $\dfrac{3}{-4}$. One number, many ways of writing it. In the next lesson we use this to [compare two fractions](lesson:comparing-fractions-mixed-numbers). If you need to revise how to find an HCF, look back at [Common factors. Highest common factor](lesson:highest-common-factor).

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Write each division as a fraction and state the numerator and the denominator of each fraction:

a) $5:(-9)$;

b) $(-4):7$;

c) $(-11):(-3)$.

::hint
**What you need:** $a:b=\dfrac{a}{b}$ for integers $a$, $b$ with $b\ne0$.

**Steps:**
1. The number being divided is the numerator, and the divisor is the denominator.
2. Keep the sign of each number.

::solution
a) We have $5:(-9)=\dfrac{5}{-9}$, with numerator $5$ and denominator $-9$.

b) We have $(-4):7=\dfrac{-4}{7}$, with numerator $-4$ and denominator $7$.

c) We have $(-11):(-3)=\dfrac{-11}{-3}$, with numerator $-11$ and denominator $-3$.

So the fractions are $\dfrac{5}{-9}$; $\dfrac{-4}{7}$; $\dfrac{-11}{-3}$.

**Answer.** a) $\dfrac{5}{-9}$; b) $\dfrac{-4}{7}$; c) $\dfrac{-11}{-3}$.
:::

:::exercise
Are the fractions in each pair equal? Why?

a) $\dfrac{4}{-10}$ and $\dfrac{-6}{15}$;

b) $\dfrac{-5}{8}$ and $\dfrac{15}{-24}$;

c) $\dfrac{-3}{7}$ and $\dfrac{9}{21}$.

::hint
**What you need:** $\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{c}{d}$ if $a\cdot d=b\cdot c$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the two cross products, watching the signs.
2. Compare the two products.

::solution
a) We have $4\cdot15=60$ and $(-10)\cdot(-6)=60$. Since the products are equal, $\dfrac{4}{-10}=\dfrac{-6}{15}$.

b) We have $(-5)\cdot(-24)=120$ and $8\cdot15=120$. Since the products are equal, $\dfrac{-5}{8}=\dfrac{15}{-24}$.

c) We have $(-3)\cdot21=-63$ and $7\cdot9=63$. Since $-63\ne63$, we get $\dfrac{-3}{7}\ne\dfrac{9}{21}$.

So the pairs in parts a) and b) are equal, and the pair in part c) is not.

**Answer.** a) Equal; b) equal; c) not equal.
:::

:::exercise
Find the integer $x$, given that:

a) $\dfrac{x}{12}=\dfrac{-5}{6}$;

b) $\dfrac{-14}{x}=\dfrac{2}{3}$.

::hint
**What you need:** cross-multiplication: if $\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{c}{d}$, then $a\cdot d=b\cdot c$.

**Steps:**
1. Write the equality of the two cross products.
2. Find the unknown factor by dividing.

::solution
a) Since $\dfrac{x}{12}=\dfrac{-5}{6}$, we have $x\cdot6=12\cdot(-5)$.

So $6x=-60$, hence $x=(-60):6=-10$.

b) Since $\dfrac{-14}{x}=\dfrac{2}{3}$, we have $(-14)\cdot3=x\cdot2$.

So $2x=-42$, hence $x=(-42):2=-21$.

Check: $\dfrac{-10}{12}=\dfrac{-5}{6}$ and $\dfrac{-14}{-21}=\dfrac{2}{3}$, both correct.

So a) $x=-10$; b) $x=-21$.

**Answer.** a) $x=-10$; b) $x=-21$.
:::

:::exercise
Write each fraction as an equal fraction with a positive denominator: $\dfrac{3}{-5}$; $\dfrac{-9}{-14}$; $\dfrac{1}{-2}$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply both the numerator and the denominator by $-1$.

**Steps:**
1. Change the sign of the numerator.
2. Change the sign of the denominator.

::solution
Multiplying both the numerator and the denominator of each fraction by $-1$, we get:

$\dfrac{3}{-5}=\dfrac{3\cdot(-1)}{(-5)\cdot(-1)}=\dfrac{-3}{5}$.

$\dfrac{-9}{-14}=\dfrac{(-9)\cdot(-1)}{(-14)\cdot(-1)}=\dfrac{9}{14}$.

$\dfrac{1}{-2}=\dfrac{1\cdot(-1)}{(-2)\cdot(-1)}=\dfrac{-1}{2}$.

So the required fractions are $\dfrac{-3}{5}$; $\dfrac{9}{14}$; $\dfrac{-1}{2}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{-3}{5}$; $\dfrac{9}{14}$; $\dfrac{-1}{2}$.
:::

:::exercise
Write each fraction in its simplest form:

a) $\dfrac{-24}{36}$;

b) $\dfrac{35}{-49}$;

c) $\dfrac{-60}{-84}$;

d) $\dfrac{121}{-33}$.

::hint
**What you need:** divide the numerator and the denominator by the HCF of their number parts; it helps to get a positive denominator first.

**Steps:**
1. Write the fraction with a positive denominator.
2. Find the HCF, then divide the numerator and the denominator by it.

::solution
a) We have HCF$(24, 36)=12$, so $\dfrac{-24}{36}=\dfrac{-24:12}{36:12}=\dfrac{-2}{3}$.

b) We have $\dfrac{35}{-49}=\dfrac{-35}{49}$. Since HCF$(35, 49)=7$, we get $\dfrac{-35}{49}=\dfrac{-35:7}{49:7}=\dfrac{-5}{7}$.

c) We have $\dfrac{-60}{-84}=\dfrac{60}{84}$. Since HCF$(60, 84)=12$, we get $\dfrac{60}{84}=\dfrac{60:12}{84:12}=\dfrac{5}{7}$.

d) We have $\dfrac{121}{-33}=\dfrac{-121}{33}$. Since HCF$(121, 33)=11$, we get $\dfrac{-121}{33}=\dfrac{-121:11}{33:11}=\dfrac{-11}{3}$.

So the results are $\dfrac{-2}{3}$; $\dfrac{-5}{7}$; $\dfrac{5}{7}$; $\dfrac{-11}{3}$.

**Answer.** a) $\dfrac{-2}{3}$; b) $\dfrac{-5}{7}$; c) $\dfrac{5}{7}$; d) $\dfrac{-11}{3}$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the integers $x$, $y$, given that $\dfrac{-2}{3}=\dfrac{x}{12}=\dfrac{10}{y}$.

::hint
**What you need:** the basic property of fractions: multiply the numerator and the denominator by the same non-zero integer.

**Steps:**
1. From denominator $3$ to denominator $12$: multiplied by what?
2. From numerator $-2$ to numerator $10$: multiplied by what?

::solution
We have $12=3\cdot4$, so we multiply both the numerator and the denominator of $\dfrac{-2}{3}$ by $4$:
$$\dfrac{-2}{3}=\dfrac{(-2)\cdot4}{3\cdot4}=\dfrac{-8}{12}.$$
Hence $x=-8$.

We have $10=(-2)\cdot(-5)$, so we multiply both the numerator and the denominator of $\dfrac{-2}{3}$ by $-5$:
$$\dfrac{-2}{3}=\dfrac{(-2)\cdot(-5)}{3\cdot(-5)}=\dfrac{10}{-15}.$$
Hence $y=-15$.

So $x=-8$ and $y=-15$.

**Answer.** $x=-8$; $y=-15$.
:::

:::exercise
Write each length of time as a fraction of an hour in its simplest form:

a) $15$ minutes;

b) $40$ minutes;

c) $54$ minutes.

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ hour $=60$ minutes, so $n$ minutes is $\dfrac{n}{60}$ of an hour.

**Steps:**
1. Write a fraction with denominator $60$.
2. Bring it to its simplest form.

::solution
Since $1$ hour is $60$ minutes, $n$ minutes is $\dfrac{n}{60}$ of an hour.

a) $15$ minutes is $\dfrac{15}{60}$ of an hour. Since HCF$(15, 60)=15$, we get $\dfrac{15}{60}=\dfrac{1}{4}$.

b) $40$ minutes is $\dfrac{40}{60}$ of an hour. Since HCF$(40, 60)=20$, we get $\dfrac{40}{60}=\dfrac{2}{3}$.

c) $54$ minutes is $\dfrac{54}{60}$ of an hour. Since HCF$(54, 60)=6$, we get $\dfrac{54}{60}=\dfrac{9}{10}$.

So $15$ minutes $=\dfrac14$ hour; $40$ minutes $=\dfrac23$ hour; $54$ minutes $=\dfrac{9}{10}$ hour.

**Answer.** a) $\dfrac14$ hour; b) $\dfrac23$ hour; c) $\dfrac{9}{10}$ hour.
:::

:::exercise
A school asks $150$ grade 6 students about their favourite sport, and each student chooses exactly one. $90$ choose football, $36$ choose badminton and the rest choose swimming. What part of the students asked chose each sport? (Give fractions in their simplest form.)

::hint
**What you need:** the part taken by a group equals the number in the group divided by the total; simplify using the HCF.

**Steps:**
1. Find the number who chose swimming.
2. Write three fractions with denominator $150$, then simplify.

::solution
The number of students who chose swimming is
$$150-90-36=24\ (\text{students}).$$

The students who chose football make up $\dfrac{90}{150}$. Since HCF$(90, 150)=30$, we get $\dfrac{90}{150}=\dfrac{3}{5}$.

The students who chose badminton make up $\dfrac{36}{150}$. Since HCF$(36, 150)=6$, we get $\dfrac{36}{150}=\dfrac{6}{25}$.

The students who chose swimming make up $\dfrac{24}{150}$. Since HCF$(24, 150)=6$, we get $\dfrac{24}{150}=\dfrac{4}{25}$.

So football makes up $\dfrac35$, badminton $\dfrac{6}{25}$ and swimming $\dfrac{4}{25}$ of the students asked.

**Answer.** Football $\dfrac35$; badminton $\dfrac{6}{25}$; swimming $\dfrac{4}{25}$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find all the fractions equal to $\dfrac{-3}{4}$ whose denominator is a positive integer less than $20$.

::hint
**What you need:** $\dfrac{-3}{4}$ is in its simplest form, so every fraction equal to it has the form $\dfrac{-3\cdot m}{4\cdot m}$ with $m$ a non-zero integer.

**Steps:**
1. The denominator is $4\cdot m$, which must be positive and less than $20$: find the values of $m$.
2. Write the fraction for each of those values.

::solution
The fraction $\dfrac{-3}{4}$ is in its simplest form, so the fractions equal to it have the form $\dfrac{(-3)\cdot m}{4\cdot m}$ with $m$ a non-zero integer.

The denominator $4\cdot m$ is a positive integer less than $20$, so $m$ is a positive integer and $4\cdot m<20$. Hence $m\in\{1;2;3;4\}$.

For $m=1$, we get $\dfrac{-3}{4}$.

For $m=2$, we get $\dfrac{-6}{8}$.

For $m=3$, we get $\dfrac{-9}{12}$.

For $m=4$, we get $\dfrac{-12}{16}$.

So the required fractions are $\dfrac{-3}{4}$; $\dfrac{-6}{8}$; $\dfrac{-9}{12}$; $\dfrac{-12}{16}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{-3}{4}$; $\dfrac{-6}{8}$; $\dfrac{-9}{12}$; $\dfrac{-12}{16}$.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
Which of the following is not a fraction?
A. $\dfrac{-3}{5}$.
B. $\dfrac{0}{7}$.
C. $\dfrac{4}{0}$.
D. $\dfrac{6}{-1}$.
::key C
The denominator of a fraction must not be $0$. A numerator of $0$ (B) and a negative denominator (D) are both allowed.

::mc
Which of these fractions is equal to $\dfrac{-2}{3}$?
A. $\dfrac{2}{3}$.
B. $\dfrac{-4}{9}$.
C. $\dfrac{6}{9}$.
D. $\dfrac{4}{-6}$.
::key D
$(-2)\cdot(-6)=12=3\cdot4$. A and C are positive; B multiplies the numerator by $2$ but the denominator by $3$.

::mc
Written in its simplest form, the fraction $\dfrac{-36}{48}$ is
A. $\dfrac{-3}{4}$.
B. $\dfrac{-6}{8}$.
C. $\dfrac{3}{4}$.
D. $\dfrac{-9}{12}$.
::key A
HCF$(36, 48)=12$, so $\dfrac{-36}{48}=\dfrac{-3}{4}$. B and D are not fully simplified; C loses the sign.

::mc
Given that $\dfrac{x}{8}=\dfrac{-3}{4}$, the value of $x$ is
A. $6$.
B. $-6$.
C. $-12$.
D. $-24$.
::key B
$4x=8\cdot(-3)=-24$, so $x=-6$. A has the wrong sign; D forgets to divide by $4$; C divides by $2$ by mistake.

::tf
Decide whether each statement is true or false.
a) Every integer can be written as a fraction.
b) $\dfrac{-5}{-9}=\dfrac{5}{9}$.
c) Multiplying both the numerator and the denominator of a fraction by $0$ gives a fraction equal to the given one.
d) $\dfrac{-7}{21}$ is in its simplest form.
::key T T F F
a) True: $a=\dfrac{a}{1}$. b) True: multiply the numerator and the denominator by $-1$. c) False: the denominator would be $0$, so it is no longer a fraction. d) False: $7$ and $21$ have the common factor $7$; $\dfrac{-7}{21}=\dfrac{-1}{3}$.

::sa
Find the integer $x$, given that $\dfrac{-4}{5}=\dfrac{x}{-35}$.
::key 28
$5x=(-4)\cdot(-35)=140$, so $x=28$.

::sa
Given that $36$ minutes is $\dfrac{a}{5}$ of an hour, find the natural number $a$.
::key 3
$36$ minutes $=\dfrac{36}{60}$ hour $=\dfrac{3}{5}$ hour (dividing the numerator and the denominator by $12$), so $a=3$.
:::
