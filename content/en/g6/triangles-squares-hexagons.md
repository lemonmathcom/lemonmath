:::goals
- Recognise an equilateral triangle, a square, a regular hexagon, a rectangle, a rhombus, a parallelogram and an isosceles trapezium.
- Describe the parts of each shape: vertices, sides, angles, diagonals.
- Use facts about sides, angles and diagonals to find missing lengths and angles.
- Tell the shapes apart by the properties they share and the ones they do not.
:::

Look around for a moment. A floor tile is a square. A warning sign by the road is a triangle with three equal sides. Each cell of a honeycomb is a neat six-sided shape. A door is a rectangle, the pattern on a jumper is made of rhombuses, the side of an escalator leans into a parallelogram, and the side of a handbag widens into an isosceles trapezium.

You have been able to name these shapes for a long time. But if someone asks "why is this a rhombus and not a square?", what do you say? To answer, we have to be able to talk about the sides, angles and diagonals of each shape. That is the job of this lesson.

## Three "regular" shapes: equilateral triangle, square, regular hexagon

"Regular" has a very precise meaning here: all the sides are equal and all the angles are equal.

### The equilateral triangle

:::definition Equilateral triangle
An equilateral triangle $ABC$ has:
- three equal sides: $AB=BC=CA$;
- three equal angles, each of $60^\circ$.
:::

You can check this yourself: cut an equilateral triangle out of paper and fold it in half in three different ways. Each time the two halves match exactly.

### The square

:::definition Square
A square $ABCD$ has:
- four equal sides: $AB=BC=CD=DA$;
- four equal angles, all right angles;
- two equal diagonals: $AC=BD$.
:::

A **diagonal** of a quadrilateral is a line segment joining two vertices that are not next to each other. Every quadrilateral has exactly two diagonals.

### The regular hexagon

Take six identical equilateral triangles and put them together with one vertex of each at a common point $O$. They fit exactly and make a six-sided shape. That is a regular hexagon.

:::diagram Regular hexagon $ABCDEF$ made of six equilateral triangles

:::

:::definition Regular hexagon
A regular hexagon $ABCDEF$ has:
- six equal sides: $AB=BC=CD=DE=EF=FA$;
- six equal angles, each of $120^\circ$;
- three **main diagonals** $AD$, $BE$, $CF$, which are equal and meet at one point $O$.
:::

Why is each angle $120^\circ$? Because at each vertex of the hexagon, two angles of two equilateral triangles are joined: $60^\circ+60^\circ=120^\circ$. From the same picture we see that $OA=OB=AB$, so each main diagonal is twice as long as a side of the hexagon.

## Rectangles and rhombuses

A square has two special features at once: four right angles and four equal sides. Keep one and drop the other, and we get two new shapes.

:::definition Rectangle
A rectangle $ABCD$ has:
- four equal angles, all right angles;
- equal opposite sides: $AB=CD$, $BC=DA$;
- two pairs of parallel opposite sides;
- two equal diagonals, $AC=BD$, which cut each other at their midpoints.
:::

Keep the four equal sides: a rhombus.

:::definition Rhombus
A rhombus $ABCD$ has:
- four equal sides: $AB=BC=CD=DA$;
- two pairs of parallel opposite sides;
- equal opposite angles;
- two diagonals that are perpendicular to each other.
:::

Looking back, a square is both a rectangle (because it has four right angles) and a rhombus (because it has four equal sides). It inherits the properties of both: its diagonals are equal and also perpendicular.

## Parallelograms and isosceles trapeziums

Push a rectangle over to one side, keeping the lengths of its sides. The angles are no longer right angles, but the opposite sides are still parallel and equal. We get a parallelogram.

:::definition Parallelogram
A parallelogram $ABCD$ has:
- two pairs of parallel opposite sides: $AB$ is parallel to $CD$, and $BC$ is parallel to $DA$;
- equal opposite sides: $AB=CD$, $BC=DA$;
- equal opposite angles: angle $A$ equals angle $C$, and angle $B$ equals angle $D$;
- two diagonals that cut each other at their midpoints.
:::

An isosceles trapezium is different: it has only **one** pair of parallel opposite sides, called the two bases. The other two sides, called the legs, slope evenly to either side.

:::diagram Isosceles trapezium $ABCD$

:::definition Isosceles trapezium
An isosceles trapezium $ABCD$ (with bases $AB$, $CD$) has:
- two parallel bases: $AB$ is parallel to $CD$;
- two equal legs: $AD=BC$;
- two equal diagonals: $AC=BD$;
- equal angles on each base: angle $C$ equals angle $D$, and angle $A$ equals angle $B$.
:::

## The big picture

The seven shapes are not separate facts. The table below shows where the quadrilaterals are alike and where they differ.

| Quadrilateral | Four equal sides | Four right angles | Equal diagonals | Perpendicular diagonals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Square | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| Rectangle | not necessarily | yes | yes | not necessarily |
| Rhombus | yes | not necessarily | not necessarily | yes |
| Parallelogram | not necessarily | not necessarily | not necessarily | not necessarily |
| Isosceles trapezium | not necessarily | not necessarily | yes | not necessarily |

"Not necessarily" means: in general no, but in a special case yes. For instance, a square is also a rectangle, and it does have four equal sides. In grade 8 you will prove all of these properties. For now, practise looking, measuring and describing accurately.

## Examples

We start with the shape that has the fewest sides.

:::example Sides and angles of an equilateral triangle
An equilateral triangle $ABC$ has $AB=5$ cm.

a) Find the lengths of the sides $BC$ and $CA$.

b) State the sizes of the three angles of the triangle.

**Solution.**

a) Triangle $ABC$ is equilateral, so its three sides are equal:
$$BC=CA=AB=5\ \text{cm}.$$

b) Triangle $ABC$ is equilateral, so its three angles are equal and each is $60^\circ$.

So $BC=CA=5$ cm, and the angles $A$, $B$, $C$ are all $60^\circ$.
:::

With a regular hexagon, the key is to remember that it is made of six equilateral triangles.

:::example Main diagonals of a regular hexagon
A regular hexagon $ABCDEF$ has side $3$ cm, and its three main diagonals meet at $O$.

a) Name the three main diagonals.

b) Find the length of $OA$ and the length of the main diagonal $AD$.

**Solution.**

a) The three main diagonals are $AD$, $BE$, $CF$.

b) The three main diagonals split the regular hexagon into six equilateral triangles. In the equilateral triangle $OAB$, we have
$$OA=AB=3\ \text{cm}.$$

In the same way, triangle $ODE$ is equilateral, so $OD=DE=3$ cm.

The point $O$ lies on the diagonal $AD$, so
$$AD=OA+OD=3+3=6\ (\text{cm}).$$

So $OA=3$ cm and $AD=6$ cm.

**Remark.** In a regular hexagon, a main diagonal is always twice as long as a side.
:::

On to the rectangle, where opposite sides and the two diagonals both come in pairs.

:::example A rectangle and its diagonals
A rectangle $ABCD$ has $AB=8$ cm, $BC=6$ cm and diagonal $AC=10$ cm. Let $O$ be the point where the diagonals meet. Find $CD$, $DA$, $BD$ and $OA$.

**Solution.**

The opposite sides of the rectangle $ABCD$ are equal, so
$$CD=AB=8\ \text{cm},\qquad DA=BC=6\ \text{cm}.$$

The two diagonals of a rectangle are equal, so
$$BD=AC=10\ \text{cm}.$$

The diagonals cut each other at their midpoints, so $O$ is the midpoint of $AC$. Hence
$$OA=AC:2=10:2=5\ (\text{cm}).$$

So $CD=8$ cm, $DA=6$ cm, $BD=10$ cm and $OA=5$ cm.
:::

A parallelogram has no right angles, but its opposite angles still help us.

:::example Sides and angles of a parallelogram
A parallelogram $ABCD$ has $AB=7$ cm, $BC=4$ cm and angle $A$ equal to $65^\circ$. Find $CD$, $DA$ and the size of angle $C$.

**Solution.**

The opposite sides of the parallelogram $ABCD$ are equal, so
$$CD=AB=7\ \text{cm},\qquad DA=BC=4\ \text{cm}.$$

Angle $C$ and angle $A$ are opposite angles of the parallelogram, so they are equal. Hence angle $C$ is $65^\circ$.

So $CD=7$ cm, $DA=4$ cm and angle $C$ is $65^\circ$.
:::

Now we try to recognise a shape from a description.

:::example Which shape is it?
Each sentence describes one of the quadrilaterals in this lesson. Say which shape it is.

a) A quadrilateral with four equal sides, whose diagonals are perpendicular but not equal.

b) A quadrilateral with only one pair of parallel opposite sides, whose other two sides are equal and whose diagonals are equal.

c) A quadrilateral with four right angles, in which two adjacent sides are $5$ cm and $3$ cm long.

**Solution.**

a) Four equal sides means a rhombus or a square. The diagonals are not equal, so it is not a square. It is a rhombus.

b) Only one pair of parallel opposite sides, two equal legs and two equal diagonals: it is an isosceles trapezium.

c) Four right angles means a rectangle or a square. Two adjacent sides are not equal ($5\ne3$), so it is not a square. It is a rectangle.

So a) a rhombus; b) an isosceles trapezium; c) a rectangle.
:::

Finally, a problem for someone who builds windows.

:::example A window frame shaped like an isosceles trapezium
A builder makes an attic window frame in the shape of an isosceles trapezium from aluminium bars. The long base is $120$ cm, the short base is $80$ cm and each leg is $50$ cm. To make the frame stronger, the builder adds two aluminium bars along the two diagonals. One diagonal measures $110$ cm. How many centimetres of aluminium are needed altogether?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* The frame is made of the four sides of an isosceles trapezium and its two diagonals. We know the two bases, the length of a leg and one diagonal.

*Plan.* The two legs of an isosceles trapezium are equal, and so are the two diagonals. So measuring one diagonal is enough. We add up the lengths of the six bars.

*Solve.* The total length of the four sides of the frame is
$$120+80+50+50=300\ (\text{cm}).$$

An isosceles trapezium has equal diagonals, so the other diagonal is also $110$ cm long. The total length of the two diagonal bars is
$$110+110=220\ (\text{cm}).$$

The total length of aluminium needed is
$$300+220=520\ (\text{cm}).$$

So the builder needs $520$ cm of aluminium altogether.

*Look back.* What if the frame were an ordinary trapezium (not isosceles)? Then the two diagonals could have different lengths, and the builder would have to measure both. A property of the shape saves us one measurement.
:::

:::warning Common mistakes
- **Thinking every rhombus has right angles.** Wrong: "A rhombus has four equal sides, so it has four right angles." Why it is wrong: four equal sides do not force the angles to be right angles; a rhombus can be "squashed". Right: only a square has both four equal sides and four right angles.
- **Thinking a parallelogram has equal diagonals.** Wrong: "$ABCD$ is a parallelogram, so $AC=BD$." Why it is wrong: when a rectangle is pushed over, one diagonal gets longer and the other shorter. Right: the diagonals of a parallelogram cut each other at their midpoints, but in general they are not equal.
- **Mixing up the legs and the bases of an isosceles trapezium.** Wrong: "An isosceles trapezium has two equal bases." Why it is wrong: the bases are parallel but of different lengths; it is the **legs** that are equal. Right: $AD=BC$ (legs), while $AB$ is parallel to $CD$ (bases).
- **Taking any diagonal of a regular hexagon as a main diagonal.** Wrong: "$AC$ is a main diagonal of the regular hexagon $ABCDEF$." Why it is wrong: a main diagonal joins two opposite vertices and passes through the centre $O$. Right: the three main diagonals are $AD$, $BE$, $CF$.
:::

## Summary

- Equilateral triangle: three equal sides, three angles of $60^\circ$.
- Square: four equal sides, four right angles, two equal diagonals.
- Regular hexagon: six equal sides, six angles of $120^\circ$, three equal main diagonals, each twice as long as a side.
- Rectangle: four right angles, equal opposite sides, equal diagonals that cut each other at their midpoints.
- Rhombus: four equal sides, equal opposite angles, perpendicular diagonals.
- Parallelogram: opposite sides parallel and equal, equal opposite angles, diagonals that cut each other at their midpoints.
- Isosceles trapezium: two parallel bases, two equal legs, two equal diagonals, equal angles on each base.

So why is a shape a rhombus and not a square? Now you can answer: it has four equal sides, but its angles are not right angles and its diagonals are not equal. Naming a shape is one thing; being able to say why is what understanding it means. In the next lesson we use these shapes to work out [perimeter and area](lesson:quadrilateral-perimeter-area).

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
An equilateral triangle $MNP$ has $MN=7$ cm.

a) Find the lengths of $NP$ and $PM$.

b) Find the total length of the three sides of the triangle.

c) State the size of angle $M$.

::hint
**What you need:** an equilateral triangle has three equal sides and three angles of $60^\circ$.

**Steps:**
1. Use the fact that the three sides are equal.
2. Add the three sides, or multiply one side by $3$.

::solution
a) Triangle $MNP$ is equilateral, so its three sides are equal:
$$NP=PM=MN=7\ \text{cm}.$$

b) The total length of the three sides is
$$7\cdot3=21\ (\text{cm}).$$

c) Triangle $MNP$ is equilateral, so each angle is $60^\circ$. Hence angle $M$ is $60^\circ$.

So $NP=PM=7$ cm; the three sides total $21$ cm; angle $M$ is $60^\circ$.

**Answer.** a) $7$ cm; $7$ cm. b) $21$ cm. c) $60^\circ$.
:::

:::exercise
A square $ABCD$ has $AB=4$ cm. Lan measures the diagonal $AC$ and gets about $5.7$ cm.

a) Find the lengths of the sides $BC$, $CD$, $DA$.

b) Without measuring, say how long the diagonal $BD$ is.

::hint
**What you need:** a square has four equal sides and two equal diagonals.

**Steps:**
1. Use the fact that the four sides are equal.
2. Use the fact that the two diagonals are equal.

::solution
a) The square $ABCD$ has four equal sides, so
$$BC=CD=DA=AB=4\ \text{cm}.$$

b) A square has equal diagonals, so $BD=AC$. Hence $BD$ is about $5.7$ cm long.

So $BC=CD=DA=4$ cm and $BD$ is about $5.7$ cm long.

**Answer.** a) $4$ cm. b) About $5.7$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
A regular hexagon $ABCDEF$ has main diagonal $AD=10$ cm.

a) Find the length of a side of the hexagon.

b) Find the total length of the six sides of the hexagon.

::hint
**What you need:** a main diagonal of a regular hexagon is twice as long as a side.

**Steps:**
1. Divide the length of the main diagonal by $2$.
2. Multiply the side by $6$.

::solution
a) The three main diagonals split the regular hexagon into six equilateral triangles, so a main diagonal is twice as long as a side. Hence a side of the hexagon is
$$10:2=5\ (\text{cm}).$$

b) A regular hexagon has six equal sides, so the total length of the six sides is
$$5\cdot6=30\ (\text{cm}).$$

So a side of the hexagon is $5$ cm long and the six sides total $30$ cm.

**Answer.** a) $5$ cm. b) $30$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
A rectangle $MNPQ$ has $MN=12$ cm, $NP=5$ cm and diagonal $MP=13$ cm. Let $O$ be the point where the diagonals meet. Find $PQ$, $QM$, $NQ$ and $OM$.

::hint
**What you need:** a rectangle has equal opposite sides; its diagonals are equal and cut each other at their midpoints.

**Steps:**
1. Find the side opposite $MN$ and the side opposite $NP$.
2. Use $NQ=MP$ and the fact that $O$ is the midpoint of $MP$.

::solution
The opposite sides of the rectangle $MNPQ$ are equal, so
$$PQ=MN=12\ \text{cm},\qquad QM=NP=5\ \text{cm}.$$

The two diagonals of a rectangle are equal, so
$$NQ=MP=13\ \text{cm}.$$

The point $O$ is the midpoint of the diagonal $MP$, so
$$OM=MP:2=13:2=6.5\ (\text{cm}).$$

So $PQ=12$ cm, $QM=5$ cm, $NQ=13$ cm and $OM=6.5$ cm.

**Answer.** $PQ=12$ cm; $QM=5$ cm; $NQ=13$ cm; $OM=6.5$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
A parallelogram $ABCD$ has $AB=9$ cm, $BC=6$ cm and angle $A$ equal to $70^\circ$. Find $CD$, $DA$ and the size of angle $C$.

::hint
**What you need:** a parallelogram has equal opposite sides and equal opposite angles.

**Steps:**
1. Find the side opposite $AB$ and the side opposite $BC$.
2. Find the angle opposite angle $A$.

::solution
The opposite sides of the parallelogram $ABCD$ are equal, so
$$CD=AB=9\ \text{cm},\qquad DA=BC=6\ \text{cm}.$$

Angle $C$ is opposite angle $A$, so the two angles are equal. Hence angle $C$ is $70^\circ$.

So $CD=9$ cm, $DA=6$ cm and angle $C$ is $70^\circ$.

**Answer.** $CD=9$ cm; $DA=6$ cm; angle $C$ is $70^\circ$.
:::

:::exercise
Among the five quadrilaterals square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram and isosceles trapezium, which ones always have:

a) four equal sides;

b) two equal diagonals;

c) two pairs of parallel opposite sides?

::hint
**What you need:** the comparison table in "The big picture" and the definition boxes.

**Steps:**
1. For each part, go through the shapes one by one.
2. Choose only the shapes for which the property is always true.

::solution
a) The square and the rhombus have four equal sides. The rectangle, the parallelogram and the isosceles trapezium do not have this property in general.

b) The square, the rectangle and the isosceles trapezium have two equal diagonals.

c) The square, the rectangle, the rhombus and the parallelogram have two pairs of parallel opposite sides. The isosceles trapezium has only one pair of parallel opposite sides (the bases).

So a) square, rhombus; b) square, rectangle, isosceles trapezium; c) square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram.

**Answer.** a) Square, rhombus. b) Square, rectangle, isosceles trapezium. c) Square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram.
:::

:::exercise
Minh has a piece of wire $48$ cm long. He wants to bend the whole piece into a closed shape. Find the length of each side if Minh bends it into:

a) an equilateral triangle;

b) a square;

c) a regular hexagon.

::hint
**What you need:** an equilateral triangle has $3$ equal sides, a square has $4$, a regular hexagon has $6$.

**Steps:**
1. The length of the wire equals the total length of the sides.
2. Divide $48$ by the number of sides of each shape.

::solution
The $48$ cm of wire is shared equally among the sides of the shape.

a) An equilateral triangle has $3$ equal sides, so each side is $48:3=16$ (cm).

b) A square has $4$ equal sides, so each side is $48:4=12$ (cm).

c) A regular hexagon has $6$ equal sides, so each side is $48:6=8$ (cm).

So the sides of the equilateral triangle, the square and the regular hexagon are $16$ cm; $12$ cm; $8$ cm long.

**Answer.** a) $16$ cm; b) $12$ cm; c) $8$ cm.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A desk top is an isosceles trapezium with short base $60$ cm, long base $120$ cm and each leg $60$ cm.

a) A plastic strip is glued all the way round the edge of the desk top. Find the length of strip needed.

b) Two such desk tops are pushed together along their long bases to make one big table top. What shape is the big table top? Find the total length of its sides.

::hint
**What you need:** a main diagonal of a regular hexagon is twice as long as a side; a main diagonal splits a regular hexagon into two identical isosceles trapeziums.

**Steps:**
1. Part a): add the four sides of the isosceles trapezium.
2. Part b): when the long bases are joined, they end up inside; count and measure the sides left on the outside.

::solution
a) The length of the strip equals the total length of the four sides of the isosceles trapezium:
$$60+120+60+60=300\ (\text{cm}).$$

b) When the two desk tops are joined along their long bases, the two long bases coincide and lie inside. The sides on the outside are the two short bases and the four legs, and all six are $60$ cm long.

The combined shape has six equal sides, and the segment joining two opposite vertices (the old long base) is $120$ cm, twice a side. This is exactly a regular hexagon of side $60$ cm, with the old long base as a main diagonal.

The total length of the sides of the big table top is
$$60\cdot6=360\ (\text{cm}).$$

So one desk top needs $300$ cm of strip; the big table top is a regular hexagon, and its sides total $360$ cm.

**Answer.** a) $300$ cm. b) A regular hexagon; $360$ cm.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
Each angle of an equilateral triangle measures
A. $45^\circ$.
B. $60^\circ$.
C. $90^\circ$.
D. $120^\circ$.
::key B
An equilateral triangle has three equal angles of $60^\circ$. C is the angle of a square; D is the angle of a regular hexagon.

::mc
How many main diagonals does a regular hexagon have?
A. $3$.
B. $6$.
C. $2$.
D. $9$.
::key A
Each main diagonal joins two opposite vertices, and the six vertices make $3$ pairs. B is the number of sides; C is the number of diagonals of a quadrilateral; D is the total number of diagonals of a hexagon.

::mc
Which of these shapes always has perpendicular diagonals?
A. A rectangle.
B. A parallelogram.
C. An isosceles trapezium.
D. A rhombus.
::key D
A rhombus has perpendicular diagonals. A rectangle and an isosceles trapezium have equal diagonals, which are not necessarily perpendicular; a parallelogram has neither property.

::mc
A parallelogram $ABCD$ has $AB=7$ cm and $BC=4$ cm. The length of the side $CD$ is
A. $4$ cm.
B. $11$ cm.
C. $7$ cm.
D. $3$ cm.
::key C
$CD$ is opposite $AB$, so $CD=AB=7$ cm. A confuses it with the adjacent side $BC$; B adds the two sides; D subtracts them.

::tf
Decide whether each statement is true or false.
a) A rhombus always has four right angles.
b) A rectangle has two equal diagonals.
c) An isosceles trapezium has two equal legs.
d) Each angle of a regular hexagon is $120^\circ$.
::key F T T T
a) False: a rhombus has four equal sides, but its angles are not necessarily right angles. b) True. c) True. d) True: each angle is made of two $60^\circ$ angles of two equilateral triangles.

::sa
A regular hexagon has sides $7$ cm long. How many centimetres long is its main diagonal?
::key 14
A main diagonal is twice as long as a side: $7\cdot2=14$ (cm).

::sa
A piece of wire $60$ cm long is bent exactly into a rhombus. How many centimetres long is each side of the rhombus?
::key 15
A rhombus has four equal sides, so each side is $60:4=15$ (cm).
:::
