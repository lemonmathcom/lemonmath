On a winter night in the mountains, the temperature drops by $3^\circ\text{C}$ every hour. Right now it is $0^\circ\text{C}$. What will the temperature be $4$ hours from now? You can do this in your head: four drops of $3$ degrees each, so $-12^\circ\text{C}$.

What about $4$ hours **ago**? It was $12$ degrees warmer than now, that is, $12^\circ\text{C}$. If we write "$4$ hours ago" as $-4$ and "dropping $3$ degrees each hour" as $-3$, then we have just worked out $(-4)\cdot(-3)=12$. Two negative numbers multiplied together give a positive number. That sounds strange, but it is quite natural, and the sections below show you why.

## Multiplying two integers with different signs

Multiplying by a natural number is repeated addition. That is still true when the number being repeated is negative:

$$4\cdot(-3)=(-3)+(-3)+(-3)+(-3)=-12.$$

Four drops of $3$ degrees make a drop of $12$ degrees. We see that $4\cdot(-3)=-(4\cdot3)$.

:::theorem Multiplying two integers with different signs
To multiply two integers with different signs, multiply their number parts and put a "$-$" sign in front of the result:
$$m\cdot(-n)=(-n)\cdot m=-(m\cdot n)\quad\text{where } m,\ n \text{ are non-zero natural numbers.}$$
:::

In short: positive times negative, or negative times positive, gives negative.

## Multiplying two integers with the same sign

Two positive integers are multiplied like natural numbers. What about two negative integers? Let the pattern speak for itself:

$$\begin{aligned}
(-3)\cdot 3&=-9\\
(-3)\cdot 2&=-6\\
(-3)\cdot 1&=-3\\
(-3)\cdot 0&=0\\
(-3)\cdot(-1)&=\ ?\\
(-3)\cdot(-2)&=\ ?
\end{aligned}$$

Going down the list, the second factor goes down by $1$ and the product goes **up** by $3$: $-9$; $-6$; $-3$; $0$. If the pattern carries on, the last two lines must be $3$ and $6$. So $(-3)\cdot(-1)=3$ and $(-3)\cdot(-2)=6$.

:::theorem Multiplying two negative integers
To multiply two negative integers, multiply their number parts:
$$(-m)\cdot(-n)=m\cdot n\quad\text{where } m,\ n \text{ are non-zero natural numbers.}$$
:::

:::note The sign rule for products
- $(+)\cdot(+)=(+)$ and $(-)\cdot(-)=(+)$: two numbers with the **same sign** give a **positive** product.
- $(+)\cdot(-)=(-)$ and $(-)\cdot(+)=(-)$: two numbers with **different signs** give a **negative** product.
- The product of any integer and $0$ is always $0$.
:::

When you multiply several numbers, you only need to count the negative factors. Every two negative factors pair up into a positive product. So if the number of negative factors is even, the product is positive, and if it is odd, the product is negative (provided no factor is $0$).

## Properties of multiplication

:::theorem Properties
For all integers $a$, $b$, $c$:
- Commutative: $a\cdot b=b\cdot a$.
- Associative: $(a\cdot b)\cdot c=a\cdot(b\cdot c)$.
- Multiplying by $1$: $a\cdot1=a$.
- Distributive over addition: $a\cdot(b+c)=a\cdot b+a\cdot c$.
:::

The distributive property also holds for subtraction: $a\cdot(b-c)=a\cdot b-a\cdot c$. Read from right to left, it lets us **take out a common factor**: $a\cdot b+a\cdot c=a\cdot(b+c)$. This is a quick way of calculating that you will use a great deal.

## Exact division

We know that $(-3)\cdot4=-12$. Just as with natural numbers, one multiplication gives us two divisions: $(-12):4=-3$ and $(-12):(-3)=4$.

:::definition Exact division
Let $a$, $b$ be integers with $b\ne0$. If there is an integer $q$ such that $a=b\cdot q$, we say that $a$ is **divisible by** $b$, written $a\ \vdots\ b$. We write $a:b=q$ and call $q$ the **quotient** of $a$ divided by $b$.
:::

Since division comes from multiplication, the sign of the quotient follows the sign rule for products: same signs give a positive quotient, different signs give a negative quotient. The method: divide the number parts, then put in the sign.

## Factors and multiples of an integer

:::definition Factors and multiples
Let $a$, $b$ be integers with $b\ne0$. If $a$ is divisible by $b$, we say that $a$ is a **multiple** of $b$ and $b$ is a **factor** of $a$.
:::

For example, $-12=4\cdot(-3)$, so $-12$ is a multiple of $4$ and of $-3$, while $4$ and $-3$ are factors of $-12$.

Compared with natural numbers, one thing is new: factors and multiples now come in opposite pairs. If $b$ is a factor of $a$, then $-b$ is also a factor of $a$. Why? Because if $a=b\cdot q$, then $a=(-b)\cdot(-q)$. So to find all the factors of an integer, we find the positive factors and then add their opposites. The same goes for multiples.

## Examples

We start by practising the sign rule.

:::example Multiplying two integers with different signs
Calculate:

a) $(-6)\cdot9$;

b) $12\cdot(-5)$;

c) $(-25)\cdot4$.

**Solution.**

In all three parts the two factors have different signs, so the product is negative. We multiply the number parts and put a "$-$" sign in front of the result.

a) $(-6)\cdot9=-(6\cdot9)=-54$.

b) $12\cdot(-5)=-(12\cdot5)=-60$.

c) $(-25)\cdot4=-(25\cdot4)=-100$.

So the results are $-54$; $-60$; $-100$.
:::

Now two numbers with the same sign. With three factors, we multiply them two at a time.

:::example Multiplying negative integers
Calculate:

a) $(-7)\cdot(-8)$;

b) $(-15)\cdot(-6)$;

c) $(-2)\cdot(-5)\cdot(-9)$.

**Solution.**

a) Both factors are negative, so the product is positive: $(-7)\cdot(-8)=7\cdot8=56$.

b) In the same way, $(-15)\cdot(-6)=15\cdot6=90$.

c) We multiply the first two factors first:
$$(-2)\cdot(-5)\cdot(-9)=[(-2)\cdot(-5)]\cdot(-9)=10\cdot(-9)=-90.$$

So the results are $56$; $90$; $-90$.

**Remark.** Part c) has three negative factors, an odd number, so the product is negative.
:::

The properties of multiplication help us avoid heavy calculations.

:::example Calculating in a sensible order
Calculate:

a) $(-4)\cdot37\cdot(-25)$;

b) $(-17)\cdot46+(-17)\cdot54$.

**Solution.**

a) We see that $4\cdot25=100$. Using the commutative and associative properties, we group $(-4)$ with $(-25)$:
$$(-4)\cdot37\cdot(-25)=[(-4)\cdot(-25)]\cdot37=100\cdot37=3700.$$

b) The two products share the factor $-17$. Using the distributive property, we take $-17$ out:
$$\begin{aligned}
(-17)\cdot46+(-17)\cdot54&=(-17)\cdot(46+54)\\
&=(-17)\cdot100\\
&=-1700.
\end{aligned}$$

So the results are $3700$ and $-1700$.
:::

Division follows the same sign rule.

:::example Dividing integers
Calculate:

a) $(-48):6$;

b) $63:(-7)$;

c) $(-72):(-9)$.

**Solution.**

a) The signs are different, so the quotient is negative. We have $48:6=8$, hence $(-48):6=-8$.

b) The signs are different, so the quotient is negative. We have $63:7=9$, hence $63:(-7)=-9$.

c) The signs are the same, so the quotient is positive. We have $72:9=8$, hence $(-72):(-9)=8$.

Check by multiplying: $6\cdot(-8)=-48$; $(-7)\cdot(-9)=63$; $(-9)\cdot8=-72$.

So the results are $-8$; $-9$; $8$.
:::

With factors and multiples, remember to include the negative ones.

:::example Finding factors and multiples
a) Find all the factors of $-10$.

b) Find the multiples of $-4$ that are greater than $-10$ and less than $10$.

**Solution.**

a) The positive factors of $10$ are $1$; $2$; $5$; $10$.

The opposite of each of these is also a factor of $-10$.

So the factors of $-10$ are $1$; $-1$; $2$; $-2$; $5$; $-5$; $10$; $-10$.

b) The multiples of $-4$ have the form $(-4)\cdot q$ with $q$ an integer; they are the numbers
$$0;\ 4;\ -4;\ 8;\ -8;\ 12;\ -12;\ \dots$$

Among these, the ones greater than $-10$ and less than $10$ are $-8$; $-4$; $0$; $4$; $8$.

So the required multiples are $-8$; $-4$; $0$; $4$; $8$.
:::

Finally, a profit-and-loss problem, where negative numbers turn up very naturally.

:::example Profit or loss?
During a sale, a shop sells $12$ shirts, making a profit of $35$ thousand dong on each, and clears $8$ pairs of trousers, making a loss of $40$ thousand dong on each. After the sale, how many thousand dong of profit or loss has the shop made?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* There are two amounts: a profit from the shirts and a loss from the trousers. We need to combine them to see whether the shop ends up with a profit or a loss.

*Plan.* Take profit as positive and loss as negative. Each pair of trousers makes a "profit" of $-40$ thousand dong. We work out the total profit from both items.

*Solve.* The profit from $12$ shirts is $12\cdot35=420$ (thousand dong).

The profit from $8$ pairs of trousers is $8\cdot(-40)=-320$ (thousand dong).

The shop's total profit is
$$420+(-320)=100\ (\text{thousand dong}).$$

Since $100>0$, the shop has made a profit.

So after the sale the shop has made a profit of $100$ thousand dong.

*Look back.* What if the shop had cleared $11$ pairs of trousers instead of $8$? Then the total would be $420+11\cdot(-40)=420-440=-20$, a loss of $20$ thousand dong. The sign of the result answers the question "profit or loss" straight away.
:::

:::warning Common mistakes
- **Negative times negative is still negative.** Wrong: $(-7)\cdot(-8)=-56$. Why it is wrong: two factors with the same sign give a positive product. Right: $(-7)\cdot(-8)=56$.
- **Carrying the sign rule of multiplication over to addition.** Wrong: $(-7)+(-8)=15$ "because negative and negative make positive". Why it is wrong: "same signs give positive" is only for multiplication and division. Right: $(-7)+(-8)=-15$.
- **Forgetting the negative factors.** Wrong: "The factors of $6$ are $1$; $2$; $3$; $6$." Why it is wrong: in the set of integers, the opposite of each factor is also a factor. Right: the factors of $6$ are $\pm1$; $\pm2$; $\pm3$; $\pm6$.
- **Thinking $0$ is a factor.** Wrong: "$0$ is a factor of $5$." Why it is wrong: there is no division by $0$. Right: $0$ is a multiple of every non-zero integer, but it is not a factor of any number.
:::

## Summary

- Different signs: multiply the number parts and put a "$-$" sign. For example, $4\cdot(-3)=-12$.
- Same signs: multiply the number parts; the product is positive. For example, $(-4)\cdot(-3)=12$.
- A product of several non-zero factors: an even number of negative factors gives a positive product, an odd number gives a negative product.
- Multiplication is commutative, associative and distributive: $a\cdot b+a\cdot c=a\cdot(b+c)$.
- $a$ is divisible by $b$ ($b\ne0$) if $a=b\cdot q$ with $q$ an integer; the sign of the quotient follows the sign rule for products.
- If $a\ \vdots\ b$, then $a$ is a multiple of $b$ and $b$ is a factor of $a$. Factors and multiples come in opposite pairs; $0$ is a multiple of every non-zero integer but a factor of none.

Now the winter night from the opening holds no mystery: $4$ hours later it is $4\cdot(-3)=-12$ degrees, and $4$ hours earlier it was $(-4)\cdot(-3)=12$ degrees. The rule "negative times negative is positive" is just a short way of saying what happens when you go back in time while the temperature is falling. If you need to revise addition and subtraction, see [Adding and subtracting integers](lesson:add-subtract-integers).

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Calculate:

a) $(-8)\cdot7$;

b) $9\cdot(-11)$;

c) $(-13)\cdot(-4)$;

d) $(-20)\cdot(-5)\cdot(-3)$.

::hint
**What you need:** different signs give a negative product, same signs give a positive product.

**Steps:**
1. Decide the sign of the product.
2. Multiply the number parts, then put in the sign.

::solution
a) The factors have different signs, so $(-8)\cdot7=-(8\cdot7)=-56$.

b) The factors have different signs, so $9\cdot(-11)=-(9\cdot11)=-99$.

c) The factors have the same sign, so $(-13)\cdot(-4)=13\cdot4=52$.

d) We multiply the first two factors first:
$$(-20)\cdot(-5)\cdot(-3)=[(-20)\cdot(-5)]\cdot(-3)=100\cdot(-3)=-300.$$

So the results are $-56$; $-99$; $52$; $-300$.

**Answer.** a) $-56$; b) $-99$; c) $52$; d) $-300$.
:::

:::exercise
Calculate:

a) $(-56):8$;

b) $81:(-9)$;

c) $(-120):(-12)$;

d) $0:(-7)$.

::hint
**What you need:** the sign of a quotient follows the sign of a product; $0$ divided by a non-zero integer is $0$.

**Steps:**
1. Decide the sign of the quotient.
2. Divide the number parts, then put in the sign.

::solution
a) The signs are different, so the quotient is negative. Since $56:8=7$, we get $(-56):8=-7$.

b) The signs are different, so the quotient is negative. Since $81:9=9$, we get $81:(-9)=-9$.

c) The signs are the same, so the quotient is positive. Since $120:12=10$, we get $(-120):(-12)=10$.

d) Since $0=(-7)\cdot0$, we get $0:(-7)=0$.

So the results are $-7$; $-9$; $10$; $0$.

**Answer.** a) $-7$; b) $-9$; c) $10$; d) $0$.
:::

:::exercise
Calculate in a sensible order:

a) $(-8)\cdot19\cdot(-125)$;

b) $43\cdot(-28)+43\cdot(-72)$.

::hint
**What you need:** $8\cdot125=1000$; the distributive property $a\cdot b+a\cdot c=a\cdot(b+c)$.

**Steps:**
1. Part a): group the two factors whose product is a round thousand.
2. Part b): take out the common factor.

::solution
a) Using the commutative and associative properties, we group $(-8)$ with $(-125)$:
$$(-8)\cdot19\cdot(-125)=[(-8)\cdot(-125)]\cdot19=1000\cdot19=19\,000.$$

b) The two products share the factor $43$. Using the distributive property, we have
$$\begin{aligned}
43\cdot(-28)+43\cdot(-72)&=43\cdot[(-28)+(-72)]\\
&=43\cdot(-100)\\
&=-4300.
\end{aligned}$$

So the results are $19\,000$ and $-4300$.

**Answer.** a) $19\,000$; b) $-4300$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the integer $x$, given that:

a) $(-6)\cdot x=42$;

b) $x:(-4)=-9$.

::hint
**What you need:** an unknown factor equals the product divided by the known factor; the number being divided equals the quotient times the divisor.

**Steps:**
1. Decide what role $x$ plays in the calculation.
2. Write the calculation that gives $x$, watching the signs.

::solution
a) We have $(-6)\cdot x=42$.

The unknown factor equals the product divided by the known factor, so
$$x=42:(-6)=-7.$$

b) We have $x:(-4)=-9$.

The number being divided equals the quotient times the divisor, so
$$x=(-9)\cdot(-4)=36.$$

Check: $(-6)\cdot(-7)=42$ and $36:(-4)=-9$, both correct.

So a) $x=-7$; b) $x=36$.

**Answer.** a) $x=-7$; b) $x=36$.
:::

:::exercise
a) Find all the factors of $-12$.

b) Find the multiples of $6$ that are greater than $-20$ and less than $15$.

::hint
**What you need:** if $b$ is a factor of $a$, then $-b$ is also a factor of $a$; multiples of $6$ have the form $6\cdot q$ with $q$ an integer.

**Steps:**
1. Find the positive factors of $12$, then add their opposites.
2. List the multiples of $6$ (negative ones and $0$ included), then pick those in the given range.

::solution
a) The positive factors of $12$ are $1$; $2$; $3$; $4$; $6$; $12$.

The opposite of each of these is also a factor of $-12$.

So the factors of $-12$ are $\pm1$; $\pm2$; $\pm3$; $\pm4$; $\pm6$; $\pm12$.

b) The multiples of $6$ are $0$; $\pm6$; $\pm12$; $\pm18$; $\pm24$; …

Among these, the ones greater than $-20$ and less than $15$ are $-18$; $-12$; $-6$; $0$; $6$; $12$.

So the required multiples are $-18$; $-12$; $-6$; $0$; $6$; $12$.

**Answer.** a) $\pm1$; $\pm2$; $\pm3$; $\pm4$; $\pm6$; $\pm12$. b) $-18$; $-12$; $-6$; $0$; $6$; $12$.
:::

:::exercise
Without calculating, compare each product with $0$:

a) $(-3)\cdot(-5)\cdot(-7)\cdot(-9)$;

b) $(-1)\cdot(-2)\cdot(-3)\cdot(-4)\cdot(-5)$.

::hint
**What you need:** in a product of non-zero numbers, an even number of negative factors gives a positive product, an odd number gives a negative one.

**Steps:**
1. Count the negative factors.
2. Decide the sign of the product.

::solution
a) The product has $4$ negative factors. Every two negative factors give a positive product, so the product of $4$ negative factors is positive.

Hence $(-3)\cdot(-5)\cdot(-7)\cdot(-9)>0$.

b) The product has $5$ negative factors. The first four give a positive product, and multiplying by the fifth negative factor gives a negative number.

Hence $(-1)\cdot(-2)\cdot(-3)\cdot(-4)\cdot(-5)<0$.

So the product in part a) is greater than $0$ and the product in part b) is less than $0$.

**Answer.** a) Greater than $0$; b) less than $0$.
:::

:::exercise
A blast freezer is at $6^\circ\text{C}$. Once it is switched on, the temperature inside drops by $4^\circ\text{C}$ every minute. What is the temperature inside after $5$ minutes, in degrees Celsius?

::hint
**What you need:** each minute the temperature changes by $-4^\circ\text{C}$; after $5$ minutes it changes by $5\cdot(-4)$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the total change in temperature after $5$ minutes.
2. Add it to the starting temperature.

::solution
Each minute the temperature drops by $4^\circ\text{C}$, that is, it changes by $-4^\circ\text{C}$.

After $5$ minutes the temperature has changed by
$$5\cdot(-4)=-20\ (^\circ\text{C}).$$

The temperature inside is then
$$6+(-20)=-14\ (^\circ\text{C}).$$

So after $5$ minutes the temperature inside is $-14^\circ\text{C}$.

**Answer.** $-14^\circ\text{C}$.
:::

:::exercise
In a quiz with $20$ questions, each correct answer scores $5$ points, each wrong answer loses $2$ points, and a question left blank scores $0$ points. An answers $14$ questions correctly, gets $5$ wrong and leaves $1$ blank. How many points does An score?

::hint
**What you need:** losing $2$ points means scoring $-2$ points.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the points for the correct answers and for the wrong answers.
2. Add the results.

::solution
The $14$ correct answers score $14\cdot5=70$ (points).

Each wrong answer scores $-2$ points, so the $5$ wrong answers score $5\cdot(-2)=-10$ (points).

The blank question scores $0$ points.

An's total score is
$$70+(-10)+0=60\ (\text{points}).$$

So An scores $60$ points.

**Answer.** $60$ points.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find all the integers $x$ such that $x+1$ is a factor of $5$.

::hint
**What you need:** the factors of $5$ include the negative ones: $\pm1$; $\pm5$.

**Steps:**
1. List all the factors of $5$.
2. For each factor, find $x$ from $x+1$ equal to that factor.

::solution
The factors of $5$ are $1$; $-1$; $5$; $-5$.

Since $x+1$ is a factor of $5$, there are four cases.

If $x+1=1$, then $x=1-1=0$.

If $x+1=-1$, then $x=-1-1=-2$.

If $x+1=5$, then $x=5-1=4$.

If $x+1=-5$, then $x=-5-1=-6$.

So $x\in\{-6;-2;0;4\}$.

**Answer.** $x\in\{-6;-2;0;4\}$.
:::
