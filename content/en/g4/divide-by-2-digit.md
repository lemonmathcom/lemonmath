title: Dividing by a two-digit number
description: Learn to divide larger numbers by a two-digit number: estimate the quotient, divide exactly or with a remainder, check, and solve word problems.
minutes: 20
---

:::goals
- Set out and divide a number with several digits by a two-digit number (exact division and division with a remainder).
- Estimate each digit of the quotient, then test it and adjust when needed.
- Know that the remainder is always less than the divisor, and check by multiplying.
- Solve word problems using division, and know when a remainder means one more unit.
:::

A drinks shop has $1\,560$ bottles of water to pack into crates, each crate holding $24$ bottles. The shop wants every crate to hold exactly $24$ bottles.

How many crates are needed to pack all the bottles? You must divide $1\,560$ by $24$. This lesson shows you how to divide by a two-digit number with confidence, by estimating and then testing.

## Let's explore

Try it by "taking away step by step". Each crate holds $24$ bottles.

- Take $60$ crates: $24\times60=1\,440$ bottles. There are $1\,560-1\,440=120$ bottles left.
- $120\div24=5$, so $5$ more crates.
- Altogether $60+5=65$ crates.

So $1\,560\div24=65$. When we set out the division, we work from the highest place in the steps **divide, multiply, subtract, bring down**, but with a two-digit divisor we must **estimate** the quotient first.

For $1\,560\div24$: the digit $1$ and the two digits $15$ are both less than $24$, so we take three digits, $156$.

- Estimate: $15\div2$ is about $7$. Try $24\times7=168$, which is more than $156$, so it is too big. Try $24\times6=144$, which works. Write $6$.
- $156-144=12$. Bring down $0$ to make $120$.
- $120\div24=5$, because $24\times5=120$. Write $5$. The remainder is $0$.

The quotient is $65$ and the remainder is $0$. So $65$ crates are needed.

:::theorem Dividing by a two-digit number
To divide a number by a two-digit number:
1. Divide from left to right. Take the first part of the dividend that is big enough to divide by the divisor.
2. **Estimate** the quotient: divide the first digit (or first two digits) of that part by the first digit of the divisor, then test by multiplying by the divisor.
3. If the product is too big, decrease the quotient. If, after subtracting, the remainder is not less than the divisor, increase the quotient.
4. Divide, multiply, subtract, then bring down the next digit; continue to the end.

The remainder is always **less than the divisor**. To check: $\text{quotient}\times\text{divisor}+\text{remainder}=\text{dividend}$.
:::

Why check the remainder? If the remainder is not less than the divisor, we can divide one more time, which means the quotient we chose was too small.

**Connection.** This lesson uses multiplying by a two-digit number to test and to estimate the quotient, and the method for dividing by a one-digit number that you already know. You also use the rule for dividing by $10$ and $100$ to divide quickly by multiples of ten.

## Examples

We start with an exact division.

:::example Exact division
Set out and work out $936\div12$.

**Solution.** The digit $9$ is less than $12$, so we take $93$.
- Estimate $9\div1=9$; try $12\times9=108>93$, so decrease. $12\times7=84$ works. Write $7$; $93-84=9$.
- Bring down $6$ to make $96$. $96\div12=8$, because $12\times8=96$. Write $8$; $96-96=0$.

The quotient is $78$ and the remainder is $0$.

**Check.** $12\times78=936$. Correct.

So $936\div12=78$.
:::

Next, an example where we must adjust the estimated quotient.

:::example Estimate, then adjust
Set out and work out $2\,592\div36$.

**Solution.** The two digits $25$ are less than $36$, so we take $259$.
- Estimate $25\div3$ is about $8$. Try $36\times8=288>259$, too big. Try $36\times7=252$, which works. Write $7$; $259-252=7$.
- Bring down $2$ to make $72$. $72\div36=2$, because $36\times2=72$. Write $2$; $72-72=0$.

The quotient is $72$ and the remainder is $0$.

**Check.** $36\times72=2\,592$. Correct.

So $2\,592\div36=72$.
:::

When a zero appears in the quotient and there is a remainder, take care.

:::example A zero in the quotient, with a remainder
Set out and work out $4\,205\div35$.

**Solution.** The two digits $42$ are greater than $35$, so we take $42$.
- $42\div35=1$, write $1$; $42-35=7$.
- Bring down $0$ to make $70$. $70\div35=2$, write $2$; $70-70=0$.
- Bring down $5$ to make $5$. $5\div35=0$, write $0$; the remainder is $5$.

The quotient is $120$ and the remainder is $5$ (less than the divisor $35$).

**Check.** $120\times35+5=4\,200+5=4\,205$. Correct.

So $4\,205\div35=120$ (remainder $5$).
:::

When the divisor is a multiple of ten, you can divide quickly.

:::example Dividing by a multiple of ten
Work out $5\,400\div60$ in your head.

**Solution.** Dividing both the dividend and the divisor by $10$ does not change the quotient:
$$5\,400\div60=540\div6=90.$$
So $5\,400\div60=90$.
:::

Now back to the drinks shop.

:::example Packing bottles into crates
A shop has $1\,560$ bottles of water to pack into crates, each crate holding $24$ bottles. How many crates are needed to pack all of them?

**Understand.** There are $1\,560$ bottles and each crate holds $24$. We need the number of crates.

**Plan.** The number of crates is the number of bottles divided by the bottles in each crate: $1\,560\div24$.

**Solve.** The number of crates needed is $1\,560\div24=65$ (crates).

**Answer:** $65$ crates.

**Look back.** Check by multiplying: $65\times24=1\,560$. Correct. Let's also estimate: $1\,560$ is close to $1\,600$ and $24$ is close to $25$, and $1\,600\div25=64$, which is close to $65$. If the shop had $1\,570$ bottles, how many crates would be needed?
:::

Finally, a problem where the remainder matters.

:::example Cutting a rope
A rope $1\,000$ cm long is cut into pieces, each $35$ cm long. What is the greatest number of such pieces that can be cut, and how many centimetres of rope are left over?

**Solution.** We have $1\,000\div35=28$ (remainder $20$), because $35\times28=980$ and $1\,000-980=20$.

So at most $28$ pieces can be cut, with $20$ cm of rope left over. The remainder $20$ cm is less than $35$ cm, so it is not enough for another piece.

**Answer:** $28$ pieces; $20$ cm left over.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **A remainder that is not less than the divisor.** Wrong: $156\div24=5$ (remainder $36$). Why it is wrong: the remainder $36$ is greater than the divisor $24$, so we can divide further. Right: $156\div24=6$ (remainder $12$).
- **Leaving out the digit 0 in the quotient.** Wrong: $4\,205\div35=12$ (remainder $5$). Why it is wrong: after bringing down $0$ and $5$ there are two more places, so the quotient has three digits; $12\times35+5=425$, not $4\,205$. Right: $4\,205\div35=120$ (remainder $5$).
- **Choosing a quotient digit that is too big without testing.** Wrong: $259\div36=8$. Why it is wrong: $36\times8=288$ is more than $259$, so it cannot be subtracted. Right: decrease to $7$, because $36\times7=252$.
:::

## Summary

- Divide from left to right; estimate the quotient digit using the first digit, then multiply to test.
- If the product is too big, decrease the quotient; if the remainder is not less than the divisor, increase the quotient.
- The remainder is always less than the divisor. Check: $\text{quotient}\times\text{divisor}+\text{remainder}=\text{dividend}$.
- When a division in a real problem has a remainder, think whether the leftover needs one more unit.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Set out and work out: $884\div13$.

::hint
**What you need:** estimate the quotient digit, then test by multiplying by $13$.

**Steps:**
1. What is $88\div13$? Try $13\times6$ and $13\times7$.
2. Bring down $4$ and continue.

::solution
- $88\div13=6$, because $13\times6=78$; $88-78=10$.
- Bring down $4$ to make $104$. $104\div13=8$, because $13\times8=104$; remainder $0$.

**Check.** $13\times68=884$. Correct.

**Answer.** $884\div13=68$.
:::

:::exercise
Set out and work out: $1\,728\div24$.

::hint
**What you need:** $17$ is less than $24$, so take the first three digits.

**Steps:**
1. What is $172\div24$? Try $24\times7$.
2. Bring down $8$ and continue.

::solution
- $172\div24=7$, because $24\times7=168$; $172-168=4$.
- Bring down $8$ to make $48$. $48\div24=2$; remainder $0$.

**Check.** $24\times72=1\,728$. Correct.

**Answer.** $1\,728\div24=72$.

Set out and work out: $3\,570\div42$.

::hint
**What you need:** take the first three digits $357$; try quotients near $8$.

**Steps:**
1. $357\div42$: try $42\times8$.
2. Bring down $0$ and continue.

::solution
- $357\div42=8$, because $42\times8=336$; $357-336=21$.
- Bring down $0$ to make $210$. $210\div42=5$, because $42\times5=210$; remainder $0$.

**Check.** $42\times85=3\,570$. Correct.

**Answer.** $3\,570\div42=85$.

:::exercise
Set out and work out: $2\,345\div52$.

::hint
**What you need:** the remainder must be less than $52$.

**Steps:**
1. What is $234\div52$? Try $52\times4$.
2. Bring down $5$, continue, then check.

::solution
- $234\div52=4$, because $52\times4=208$; $234-208=26$.
- Bring down $5$ to make $265$. $265\div52=5$, because $52\times5=260$; remainder $5$.

**Check.** $45\times52+5=2\,340+5=2\,345$. Correct.

**Answer.** $2\,345\div52=45$ (remainder $5$).

:::exercise
A shop has $1\,080$ pens to pack into boxes, each box holding $12$ pens. How many boxes can be filled?

::hint
**What you need:** the number of boxes is the total pens divided by the pens in each box.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Set out $1\,080\div12$ and check by multiplying.

::solution
The number of boxes filled is:
$$1\,080\div12=90\ \text{(boxes)}.$$

**Check.** $90\times12=1\,080$. Correct.

**Answer.** $90$ boxes.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a group of $250$ visitors hires coaches, each coach carrying at most $45$ people. What is the smallest number of coaches needed to carry the whole group?

::hint
**What you need:** when a division has a remainder, the leftover still needs one more coach.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Work out $250\div45$, then look at the remainder.

::solution
We have $250\div45=5$ (remainder $25$), because $45\times5=225$ and $250-225=25$.

$5$ coaches carry $225$ people, and the other $25$ people need $1$ more coach. The number of coaches to hire is $5+1=6$ (coaches).

**Answer.** At least $6$ coaches.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on multiplying by a two-digit number. A division has divisor $25$, quotient $48$ and remainder $13$. Find the dividend.

::hint
**What you need:** $\text{dividend}=\text{quotient}\times\text{divisor}+\text{remainder}$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $48\times25$.
2. Add the remainder.

::solution
The dividend is $48\times25+13=1\,200+13=1\,213$.

**Check.** $1\,213\div25=48$ (remainder $13$). Correct.

**Answer.** $1\,213$.
:::

:::exercise
Set out and work out: $7\,805\div41$.

::hint
**What you need:** a zero in the quotient, and the remainder less than the divisor.

**Steps:**
1. Take $78$: what is $78\div41$?
2. Bring down $0$, then bring down $5$.

::solution
- $78\div41=1$; $78-41=37$.
- Bring down $0$ to make $370$. $370\div41=9$, because $41\times9=369$; remainder $1$.
- Bring down $5$ to make $15$. $15\div41=0$; remainder $15$.

**Check.** $190\times41+15=7\,790+15=7\,805$. Correct.

**Answer.** $7\,805\div41=190$ (remainder $15$).
:::

:::exercise
Suppose Mum pays $1\,260\,000$ dong for $18$ identical chairs. How much does each chair cost?

::hint
**What you need:** $1\,260\,000=1\,260\times1\,000$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $1\,260\div18$.
2. Multiply the result by $1\,000$.

::solution
We have $1\,260\div18=70$.

The price of each chair is $70\times1\,000=70\,000$ (dong).

**Check.** $70\,000\times18=1\,260\,000$. Correct.

**Answer.** $70\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find the smallest three-digit natural number that leaves remainder $23$ when divided by $24$.

::hint
**What you need:** such a number equals $24\times\text{quotient}+23$.

**Steps:**
1. Write some numbers of the form $24\times\text{quotient}+23$ with quotient $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$.
2. Which is the first three-digit number?

::solution
The numbers that leave remainder $23$ when divided by $24$ have the form $24\times k+23$:
- $k=3$: $24\times3+23=95$ (two digits).
- $k=4$: $24\times4+23=119$ (three digits).

**Check.** $119\div24=4$ (remainder $23$). Correct.

**Answer.** $119$.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
$936\div12$ equals
A. $78$.
B. $780$.
C. $77$.
D. $68$.
::key A
$12\times78=936$, so $936\div12=78$. For $68$ we get $12\times68=816$, which is wrong; option B adds an extra zero.

::mc
$4\,205\div35$ equals
A. $12$ (remainder $5$).
B. $120$ (remainder $5$).
C. $120$ (remainder $35$).
D. $121$ (remainder $0$).
::key B
$120\times35+5=4\,205$. Option A is missing the zero in the quotient, and option C has a remainder that is not less than the divisor.

::mc
In a division by $25$, the largest possible remainder is
A. $26$.
B. $25$.
C. $24$.
D. $1$.
::key C
The remainder is always less than the divisor $25$, so the largest remainder is $24$.

::mc
$150$ exercise books are packed into boxes, each box holding $12$ books. What is the smallest number of boxes needed to pack all the books?
A. $12$ boxes.
B. $6$ boxes.
C. $14$ boxes.
D. $13$ boxes.
::key D
$150\div12=12$ (remainder $6$). $12$ boxes hold $144$ books, and the other $6$ books need $1$ more box: $12+1=13$ boxes.

::tf
Look at the division $2\,592\div36$.
a) The quotient of the division is $62$.
b) The remainder of the division is $12$.
c) $36\times72=2\,592$.
d) $36\times8=288$ is greater than $259$, so the first digit of the quotient cannot be $8$.

$123\div21=5$ (remainder $18$); bring down $4$ to make $184$, $184\div21=8$ (remainder $16$). So $1\,234\div21=58$ (remainder $16$).
