A rice shop (made-up numbers) has $3$ tonnes of rice. Today the shop sells $7$ tạ $5$ yến of rice. How many kilograms of rice does the shop have left?

To work this out, you need to know how many kilograms a tonne, a tạ and a yến are. After that you will learn two more units of time: the second and the century.

## Yến, tạ and tonnes

### Let's explore

Besides the kilogram, you use three larger units of mass. The units **yến** and **tạ** are used in Vietnam, and the **tonne** is used in many countries:

- $1$ yến $=10$ kg (one yến is $10$ kilograms).
- $1$ tạ $=10$ yến $=100$ kg.
- $1$ tonne $=10$ tạ $=1\,000$ kg.

Each larger unit is $10$ times the next smaller one in the list: tonne, tạ, yến, kilogram. You can remember them with this table:

| tonne | tạ | yến | kilogram |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| $1$ tonne $=10$ tạ | $1$ tạ $=10$ yến | $1$ yến $=10$ kg | $1$ kg $=1\,000$ g |

Use this table to convert units.

### Converting units of mass

:::theorem Converting units of mass
- $1$ yến $=10$ kg; $1$ tạ $=10$ yến $=100$ kg; $1$ tonne $=10$ tạ $=1\,000$ kg.
- To change from a larger unit to a smaller unit, multiply by $10$, $100$ or $1\,000$.
- To change from a smaller unit to a larger unit, divide by $10$, $100$ or $1\,000$.
:::

Why multiply when changing to a smaller unit? Because you need more small units to make one large unit: $1$ tạ is $100$ kg, so $5$ tạ is $5\times100=500$ kg.

We start by converting units.

:::example Converting units
a) Change $3$ tonnes, $5$ tạ and $4$ yến to kilograms.
b) Change $4\,500$ kg to tạ, $6\,000$ kg to tonnes and $90$ kg to yến.

**Solution.** a) $3$ tonnes $=3\times1\,000=3\,000$ kg; $5$ tạ $=5\times100=500$ kg; $4$ yến $=4\times10=40$ kg.

b) $4\,500$ kg $=4\,500\div100=45$ tạ; $6\,000$ kg $=6\,000\div1\,000=6$ tonnes; $90$ kg $=90\div10=9$ yến.
:::

Sometimes a measurement is given in two units.

:::example Measurements in two units
Change to kilograms: $2$ tonnes $5$ tạ; $3$ tạ $4$ yến; $1$ tonne $2$ tạ $5$ yến.

**Solution.** We change each unit and then add:

$2$ tonnes $5$ tạ $=2\,000+500=2\,500$ kg.

$3$ tạ $4$ yến $=300+40=340$ kg.

$1$ tonne $2$ tạ $5$ yến $=1\,000+200+50=1\,250$ kg.
:::

Add two measurements in the same units.

:::example Adding measurements of mass
One lorry carries $2$ tonnes $3$ tạ of rice and another lorry carries $1$ tonne $8$ tạ of rice. How much rice do the two lorries carry altogether?

**Solution.** The rice carried by the two lorries is:
$$2\text{ tonnes }3\text{ tạ}+1\text{ tonne }8\text{ tạ}=3\text{ tonnes }11\text{ tạ}.$$
Since $10$ tạ $=1$ tonne, $11$ tạ $=1$ tonne $1$ tạ. So $3$ tonnes $11$ tạ $=4$ tonnes $1$ tạ.

**Answer.** $4$ tonnes $1$ tạ.
:::

Finally, the problem at the start. We work in four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example The rice shop
A rice shop (made-up numbers) has $3$ tonnes of rice. Today the shop sells $7$ tạ $5$ yến of rice. How many kilograms of rice does the shop have left? Change the result to tonnes, tạ and yến.

**Understand.** We are given the starting amount of rice, $3$ tonnes, and the amount sold, $7$ tạ $5$ yến. We are asked for the amount left, in kilograms, and then in tonnes, tạ and yến.

**Plan.** The two measurements are in different units, so we change both to kilograms, then subtract the rice sold from the starting amount.

**Solve.**

Solution

The starting amount of rice is $3$ tonnes $=3\,000$ kg.

The amount sold is $7$ tạ $5$ yến $=700+50=750$ kg.

The amount left is:
$$3\,000-750=2\,250\ \text{(kg)}.$$
We have $2\,250$ kg $=2\,000+200+50$ kg $=2$ tonnes $2$ tạ $5$ yến.

Answer: $2\,250$ kg, which is $2$ tonnes $2$ tạ $5$ yến.

**Look back.** Check: $2\,250+750=3\,000$ kg. Correct. You can also work in yến: $3$ tonnes $=300$ yến and $7$ tạ $5$ yến $=75$ yến, so $300-75=225$ yến is left, which is $2\,250$ kg. Both ways give the same result.
:::

## Seconds

A unit of time smaller than the minute is the **second**. The second hand of a clock makes one full turn in $1$ minute.

- $1$ minute $=60$ seconds.
- $1$ hour $=60$ minutes $=3\,600$ seconds.

Why is $1$ hour $3\,600$ seconds? Because $1$ hour $=60$ minutes and each minute is $60$ seconds, so $1$ hour $=60\times60=3\,600$ seconds.

:::example Converting and calculating with minutes and seconds
a) Change $2$ minutes $15$ seconds to seconds.
b) Change $195$ seconds to minutes and seconds.
c) Work out $1$ minute $48$ seconds $-1$ minute $12$ seconds.

**Solution.** a) $2$ minutes $=2\times60=120$ seconds, so $2$ minutes $15$ seconds $=120+15=135$ seconds.

b) We have $195\div60=3$ (remainder $15$), so $195$ seconds $=3$ minutes $15$ seconds.

c) Both measurements have $1$ minute, so we only subtract the seconds: $48-12=36$ seconds.
:::

## Centuries

A **century** is a large unit of time: $1$ century $=100$ years. Centuries are numbered from the start: the first century is the years from year $1$ to year $100$, the second century is the years from year $101$ to year $200$, and so on.

:::note Roman numerals
In Vietnam, centuries are named with Roman numerals: $\text{I}=1$, $\text{V}=5$, $\text{X}=10$, $\text{XX}=20$, $\text{XXI}=21$. For example, the $20$th century is written XX and the $21$st century is written XXI.
:::

:::theorem Finding the century of a year
To find which century the year $y$ is in, divide $y$ by $100$:
1. If it divides exactly (the remainder is $0$), the century is the quotient.
2. If there is a remainder, the century is the quotient plus $1$.
:::

Why add $1$ when there is a remainder? Because the remainder tells you the year has moved into a new century. For example, the year $1945$: $1945\div100=19$ (remainder $45$), which means $19$ whole centuries have passed and we are in the $20$th century.

Find the century of some years from history (according to Vietnamese history books).

:::example Which year is in which century?
Find the century of these years: the year $938$ (the victory at Bạch Đằng), the year $1010$ (King Lý Thái Tổ moved the capital to Thăng Long), the year $1789$ (Quang Trung defeated the Qing army), the year $1945$ (the Declaration of Independence), the year $2000$ and the year $2001$.

**Solution.** We divide each year by $100$:

| Year | Division | Century |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| $938$ | $938\div100=9$ (remainder $38$) | $9+1=10$, the 10th century (X) |
| $1010$ | $1010\div100=10$ (remainder $10$) | $10+1=11$, the 11th century (XI) |
| $1789$ | $1789\div100=17$ (remainder $89$) | $17+1=18$, the 18th century (XVIII) |
| $1945$ | $1945\div100=19$ (remainder $45$) | $19+1=20$, the 20th century (XX) |
| $2000$ | $2000\div100=20$ (remainder $0$) | $20$, the 20th century (XX) |
| $2001$ | $2001\div100=20$ (remainder $1$) | $20+1=21$, the 21st century (XXI) |
:::

**Connection.** You use multiplication, division and division with a remainder from earlier lessons to convert units and to find centuries. In later lessons you will meet other units, such as units of area.

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Mixing up tạ and tonnes.** Wrong: $1$ tạ $=1\,000$ kg. Why it is wrong: tạ was mixed up with tonnes. Right: $1$ tạ $=100$ kg and $1$ tonne $=1\,000$ kg.
- **Finding the wrong century for a round hundred year.** Wrong: the year $2000$ is in the 21st century. Why it is wrong: the 20th century is the years from $1901$ to $2000$, so the year $2000$ is the last year of the 20th century. Right: the year $2000$ is in the 20th century.
- **Mixing up the number of seconds in a minute.** Wrong: $1$ minute $=100$ seconds. Why it is wrong: $100$ is a familiar number and it was used by mistake. Right: $1$ minute $=60$ seconds.
:::

## Summary

- $1$ yến $=10$ kg; $1$ tạ $=10$ yến $=100$ kg; $1$ tonne $=10$ tạ $=1\,000$ kg. Multiply to change to a smaller unit; divide to change to a larger unit.
- When you add or subtract measurements in two units, change to one unit, then calculate.
- $1$ minute $=60$ seconds; $1$ hour $=3\,600$ seconds.
- $1$ century $=100$ years. Divide the year by $100$: if it divides exactly, the century is the quotient; if there is a remainder, the century is the quotient plus $1$.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Change: $7$ tạ $=\ ?$ kg and $6\,000$ kg $=\ ?$ tonnes.

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ tạ $=100$ kg; $1$ tonne $=1\,000$ kg.

**Steps:**
1. Change $7$ tạ to kilograms by multiplying.
2. Change $6\,000$ kg to tonnes by dividing.

::solution
$7$ tạ $=7\times100=700$ kg. $6\,000$ kg $=6\,000\div1\,000=6$ tonnes.

**Answer.** $700$ kg and $6$ tonnes.
:::

:::exercise
Change $2$ tạ $5$ yến to kilograms.

::hint
**What you need:** change each unit and then add.

**Steps:**
1. Change $2$ tạ to kilograms.
2. Change $5$ yến to kilograms and add.

::solution
$2$ tạ $=200$ kg and $5$ yến $=50$ kg. So $2$ tạ $5$ yến $=200+50=250$ kg.

**Answer.** $250$ kg.
:::

:::exercise
Work out: $3$ tonnes $4$ tạ $+2$ tonnes $9$ tạ.

::hint
**What you need:** add the tonnes together and the tạ together; $10$ tạ $=1$ tonne.

**Steps:**
1. Add the tonnes and add the tạ.
2. Change $10$ tạ or more into tonnes.

::solution
$3$ tonnes $4$ tạ $+2$ tonnes $9$ tạ $=5$ tonnes $13$ tạ. Since $13$ tạ $=1$ tonne $3$ tạ, the result is $6$ tonnes $3$ tạ.

**Answer.** $6$ tonnes $3$ tạ.
:::

:::exercise
Work out: $5$ tonnes $-2$ tonnes $7$ tạ.

::hint
**What you need:** change $5$ tonnes to tạ so both have the same unit.

**Steps:**
1. Change $5$ tonnes to tạ.
2. Change $2$ tonnes $7$ tạ to tạ, then subtract.

::solution
$5$ tonnes $=50$ tạ and $2$ tonnes $7$ tạ $=27$ tạ. We have $50-27=23$ tạ, which is $2$ tonnes $3$ tạ.

**Answer.** $2$ tonnes $3$ tạ.
:::

:::exercise
A lorry (made-up numbers) carries $4$ tonnes of goods, and $2$ tonnes $5$ tạ have been unloaded. How many kilograms of goods are left on the lorry?

::hint
**What you need:** change both measurements to kilograms, then subtract.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Change $4$ tonnes and $2$ tonnes $5$ tạ to kilograms.
3. Subtract the goods unloaded from the goods carried.

::solution
Solution

The goods at the start are $4$ tonnes $=4\,000$ kg. The goods unloaded are $2$ tonnes $5$ tạ $=2\,500$ kg.

The goods left are:
$$4\,000-2\,500=1\,500\ \text{(kg)}.$$

**Answer.** $1\,500$ kg of goods.
:::

:::exercise
Change $3$ minutes $20$ seconds to seconds and $255$ seconds to minutes and seconds.

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ minute $=60$ seconds.

**Steps:**
1. Change $3$ minutes to seconds, then add $20$ seconds.
2. Divide $255$ by $60$, and write the quotient and the remainder.

::solution
$3$ minutes $20$ seconds $=3\times60+20=200$ seconds. We have $255\div60=4$ (remainder $15$), so $255$ seconds $=4$ minutes $15$ seconds.

**Answer.** $200$ seconds and $4$ minutes $15$ seconds.
:::

:::exercise
Which centuries are the years $1010$ and $1945$ in? The 21st century is the years from which year to which year?

::hint
**What you need:** divide the year by $100$; if there is a remainder, add $1$ to the quotient.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $1010\div100$ and $1945\div100$.
2. The $21$st century starts after the $20$th century ends at the year $2000$.

::solution
$1010\div100=10$ (remainder $10$), so the year $1010$ is in the $11$th century, which is XI. $1945\div100=19$ (remainder $45$), so the year $1945$ is in the $20$th century, which is XX. The XX century ends at the year $2000$, so the XXI century is the years from $2001$ to $2100$.

**Answer.** The 11th century; the 20th century; from the year $2001$ to the year $2100$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on three-step word problems. A store (made-up numbers) has $2$ tonnes of animal feed. Every day the store uses $4$ yến of feed. After $10$ days the store receives $3$ tạ more feed. How many kilograms of feed does the store have now?

::hint
**What you need:** change the measurements to kilograms and do three steps.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the feed used in $10$ days.
2. Work out the feed left after $10$ days.
3. Add the feed received.

::solution
Solution

The feed used in $10$ days is $4\times10=40$ yến $=400$ kg.

The feed left is:
$$2\,000-400=1\,600\ \text{(kg)}.$$
The feed now is:
$$1\,600+300=1\,900\ \text{(kg)}.$$

**Answer.** $1\,900$ kg of feed.
:::

:::exercise
Lan changes $2$ tạ $3$ yến $=5$ yến. Check Lan's result. If it is wrong, correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ tạ $=10$ yến.

**Steps:**
1. Change $2$ tạ to yến.
2. Add $3$ yến.

::solution
We have $2$ tạ $=2\times10=20$ yến. So $2$ tạ $3$ yến $=20+3=23$ yến. Lan added $2+3$ but forgot to change tạ to yến.

**Check.** $23$ yến $=230$ kg and $2$ tạ $3$ yến $=200+30=230$ kg. Correct.

**Answer.** Lan is wrong; the correct result is $23$ yến.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A lorry can carry at most $5$ tonnes of goods. It has already loaded $28$ sacks of rice, each weighing $1$ tạ $5$ yến. At most how many sacks of maize can it carry as well, if each sack of maize weighs $5$ yến?

::hint
**What you need:** change everything to kilograms and work step by step.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the mass of the $28$ sacks of rice.
2. Work out the mass of goods it can still carry.
3. Divide by the mass of one sack of maize.

::solution
One sack of rice weighs $1$ tạ $5$ yến $=150$ kg. The mass of $28$ sacks of rice is $28\times150=4\,200$ kg. The lorry can still carry $5\,000-4\,200=800$ kg. One sack of maize weighs $5$ yến $=50$ kg, so the number of sacks of maize is $800\div50=16$.

**Answer.** $16$ sacks of maize.
:::
