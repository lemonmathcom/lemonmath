:::goals
- Read, write and break down numbers with up to nine digits, for example $123\,456\,789$.
- Name the places and periods (units, thousands, millions) and find the value of a digit.
- Compare numbers with many digits and put them in order.
- Solve word problems that need comparing large numbers.
:::

A country (made-up numbers) has $98\,765\,432$ people. Another country has $98\,756\,432$ people. How do you read these numbers? Which country has more people?

Eight-digit numbers are hard to read and compare if you look at the digits one by one. This lesson shows you how to split a number into periods, read large numbers and compare them.

## Numbers in the millions

### Let's explore

Remember: $999\,999+1=1\,000\,000$, read "one million". After the hundred-thousands place there are three more places: the millions, ten-millions and hundred-millions places. These three places make the millions period.

Put the number $123\,456\,789$ into a table of places and periods:

| Millions period | | | Thousands period | | | Units period | | |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Hundred-millions | Ten-millions | Millions | Hundred-thousands | Ten-thousands | Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones |
| $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $5$ | $6$ | $7$ | $8$ | $9$ |

Read the table: the number $123\,456\,789$ has $1$ hundred-million, $2$ ten-millions, $3$ millions, $4$ hundred-thousands, $5$ ten-thousands, $6$ thousands, $7$ hundreds, $8$ tens and $9$ ones. We write:
$$123\,456\,789=100\,000\,000+20\,000\,000+3\,000\,000+400\,000+50\,000+6\,000+700+80+9.$$

You read this number from left to right, one period at a time: "one hundred and twenty-three million, four hundred and fifty-six thousand, seven hundred and eighty-nine".

### Reading and writing large numbers

:::theorem Places and periods
Each period has three places:
- The units period has the ones, tens and hundreds places.
- The thousands period has the thousands, ten-thousands and hundred-thousands places.
- The millions period has the millions, ten-millions and hundred-millions places.

Every $10$ units in one place make $1$ unit in the place next to it on the left. When reading, we read each period from left to right: read the three digits of the period like a three-digit number, then add the name of the period.
:::

Why split into periods? Because you already know how to read three digits, and you only need to add "million" or "thousand" after each group of three digits.

:::note Reading numbers
If a whole period is $0$, we do not read that period: $305\,000\,042$ is "three hundred and five million and forty-two".
:::

We start by reading and writing numbers.

:::example Reading and writing numbers
a) Read the number $70\,205\,060$.
b) Write the number "nine hundred and two million, four hundred thousand".

**Solution.** a) The millions period is $70$, the thousands period is $205$, the units period is $060$. We read it as "seventy million, two hundred and five thousand and sixty".

b) "Nine hundred and two million" is $902\,000\,000$ and "four hundred thousand" is $400\,000$. We write $902\,000\,000+400\,000=902\,400\,000$.

So the number is $902\,400\,000$.
:::

Next, break a number down by places.

:::example Breaking down the number $480\,305\,026$
Write $480\,305\,026$ as a sum. Which place is the digit $8$ in, and what is its value?

**Solution.** The number $480\,305\,026$ has $4$ hundred-millions, $8$ ten-millions, $0$ millions, $3$ hundred-thousands, $0$ ten-thousands, $5$ thousands, $0$ hundreds, $2$ tens and $6$ ones. We have:
$$480\,305\,026=400\,000\,000+80\,000\,000+300\,000+5\,000+20+6.$$
The digit $8$ is in the ten-millions place, in the millions period, so its value is $80\,000\,000$.
:::

Find the value of digits in different periods.

:::example The value of a digit
In the number $53\,842\,710$, which place are the digits $8$ and $2$ in, and what are their values?

**Solution.** The number $53\,842\,710$ has $5$ ten-millions, $3$ millions, $8$ hundred-thousands, $4$ ten-thousands, $2$ thousands, $7$ hundreds, $1$ ten and $0$ ones.

The digit $8$ is in the hundred-thousands place, in the thousands period, and its value is $800\,000$. The digit $2$ is in the thousands place, in the thousands period, and its value is $2\,000$.
:::

## Comparing numbers with many digits

:::theorem Comparing two numbers with many digits
To compare two numbers with many digits:
1. The number with more digits is greater.
2. If both numbers have the same number of digits, compare pairs of digits in the same place, from left to right. The first pair that differs decides: the larger digit gives the greater number.
3. If every pair of digits is equal, the numbers are equal.
:::

Why compare from the left? Because a digit in a higher place is worth much more: a digit in the ten-millions place is worth $10$ million, more than all the digits to its right added together.

We answer the question at the start.

:::example Comparing two numbers
Compare: a) $9\,876\,543$ and $10\,000\,000$; b) $98\,765\,432$ and $98\,756\,432$.

**Solution.** a) $9\,876\,543$ has seven digits and $10\,000\,000$ has eight digits. So $9\,876\,543<10\,000\,000$.

b) Both numbers have eight digits. Ten-millions: $9=9$. Millions: $8=8$. Hundred-thousands: $7=7$. Ten-thousands: $6>5$. So $98\,765\,432>98\,756\,432$.

So the country with $98\,765\,432$ people has more people.
:::

When there are many numbers, compare them two at a time and then put them in order.

:::example Putting numbers in order
Put the numbers $345\,210\,000$, $345\,120\,000$, $99\,999\,999$, $345\,210\,500$ in order from smallest to largest.

**Solution.** The number $99\,999\,999$ has eight digits and the other three have nine digits, so $99\,999\,999$ is the smallest. The other three all have $345$ million; we compare the hundred-thousands: $2$, $1$ and $2$. The number $345\,120\,000$ has the smallest hundred-thousands digit. The numbers $345\,210\,000$ and $345\,210\,500$ differ only in the hundreds: $0<5$.

We have $99\,999\,999<345\,120\,000<345\,210\,000<345\,210\,500$.
:::

Use this way of comparing to solve a real problem.

:::example The prices of three cars
The prices of three cars (made-up numbers) are $320\,000\,000$ dong, $285\,000\,000$ dong and $318\,500\,000$ dong. Put the prices in order from lowest to highest. Which car is the cheapest?

**Solution.** All three numbers have nine digits. Hundred-millions: $3$, $2$, $3$. The number $285\,000\,000$ has the smallest hundred-millions digit. The other two have the same hundred-millions digit, $3$; ten-millions: $2$ and $1$. The number $318\,500\,000$ has the smaller ten-millions digit.

We have $285\,000\,000<318\,500\,000<320\,000\,000$.

**Answer.** The car priced at $285\,000\,000$ dong is the cheapest.
:::

Finally, a problem that needs some thinking. We work in four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example Counting whole millions
How many whole numbers of millions are greater than $95\,000\,000$ and less than $100\,000\,000$?

**Understand.** Whole numbers of millions are numbers like $1\,000\,000$, $2\,000\,000$, ... We need the ones that lie between $95\,000\,000$ and $100\,000\,000$.

**Plan.** The numbers must be greater than $95\,000\,000$ and less than $100\,000\,000$, so we do not count these two end points. We list the whole numbers of millions that follow $95\,000\,000$.

**Solve.**

Solution

The whole numbers of millions greater than $95\,000\,000$ and less than $100\,000\,000$ are $96\,000\,000$; $97\,000\,000$; $98\,000\,000$; $99\,000\,000$.

There are $4$ such numbers.

Answer: $4$ numbers.

**Look back.** $95\,000\,000$ is not counted because the question says "greater than", and $100\,000\,000$ is not counted because the question says "less than". Check: $95\,000\,000<96\,000\,000<99\,000\,000<100\,000\,000$. Correct.
:::

**Connection.** Comparing pairs of digits from left to right is exactly how you compare numbers with fewer digits. In the next lesson you will use these large numbers to round numbers and to meet the sequence of natural numbers.

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Leaving out a period when writing.** Wrong: "three hundred and five million and forty-two" written as $305\,042$. Why it is wrong: the thousands period is all $0$, which is not read but must still be written as three $0$s. Right: $305\,000\,042$.
- **Comparing by the large digits.** Wrong: $99\,999\,999>100\,000\,000$ because $9>1$. Why it is wrong: $100\,000\,000$ has nine digits, more than the eight digits of $99\,999\,999$. Right: $99\,999\,999<100\,000\,000$.
- **Comparing from right to left.** Wrong: $98\,756\,432>98\,765\,432$ because in the thousands place $6>5$. Why it is wrong: we must compare from left to right, and the first pair that differs is in the ten-thousands place, where $5<6$. Right: $98\,765\,432>98\,756\,432$.
:::

## Summary

- Each period has three places: units, thousands, millions. Read a number from left to right, one period at a time.
- When a whole period is $0$ we do not read it, but when writing the number we must still write three $0$s.
- Comparing two numbers: more digits means greater; with the same number of digits, compare from left to right, and the first pair of digits that differs decides.
- Putting many numbers in order: compare them two at a time, starting with the number of digits and then the highest places.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Write the number: four hundred and twenty million, three hundred thousand.

::hint
**What you need:** write each period from left to right; a period that is not read is written as three $0$s.

**Steps:**
1. What number is "four hundred and twenty million"?
2. What number is "three hundred thousand"? What is the units period?

::solution
"Four hundred and twenty million" is $420\,000\,000$ and "three hundred thousand" is $300\,000$. We have $420\,000\,000+300\,000=420\,300\,000$.

**Answer.** $420\,300\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
Read the number $508\,060\,000$.

::hint
**What you need:** a units period that is all $0$ is not read.

**Steps:**
1. Read the millions period: $508$.
2. Read the thousands period: $060$.

::solution
The number $508\,060\,000$ has millions period $508$, thousands period $060$ and units period $000$.

**Answer.** Five hundred and eight million, sixty thousand.
:::

:::exercise
Write $26\,804\,109$ as a sum.

::hint
**What you need:** each digit has a value that depends on its place.

**Steps:**
1. Make a table of places for $26\,804\,109$.
2. Leave out the place with digit $0$.

::solution
The number $26\,804\,109$ has $2$ ten-millions, $6$ millions, $8$ hundred-thousands, $0$ ten-thousands, $4$ thousands, $1$ hundred, $0$ tens and $9$ ones. We have:
$$26\,804\,109=20\,000\,000+6\,000\,000+800\,000+4\,000+100+9.$$

**Answer.** $20\,000\,000+6\,000\,000+800\,000+4\,000+100+9$.
:::

:::exercise
In the number $372\,915\,048$, which place and which period are the digits $7$ and $9$ in, and what are their values?

::hint
**What you need:** make a table of places and periods for the number.

**Steps:**
1. Write the name of the place of each digit from left to right.
2. Find the period and the value of the digits $7$ and $9$.

::solution
The number $372\,915\,048$ has $3$ hundred-millions, $7$ ten-millions, $2$ millions, $9$ hundred-thousands, $1$ ten-thousand, $5$ thousands, $0$ hundreds, $4$ tens and $8$ ones.

The digit $7$ is in the ten-millions place, in the millions period, with value $70\,000\,000$. The digit $9$ is in the hundred-thousands place, in the thousands period, with value $900\,000$.

**Answer.** $7$: ten-millions place, millions period, $70\,000\,000$; $9$: hundred-thousands place, thousands period, $900\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the gap: $487\,215\,000\ \ldots\ 487\,125\,000$.

::hint
**What you need:** compare pairs of digits from left to right.

**Steps:**
1. Do the numbers have the same number of digits?
2. Find the first pair of digits that differs.

::solution
Both numbers have nine digits. The hundred-millions, ten-millions and millions are equal. Hundred-thousands: $2>1$. So $487\,215\,000>487\,125\,000$.

**Answer.** The sign $>$.
:::

:::exercise
Put the numbers $508\,000\,000$, $58\,000\,000$, $580\,000\,000$, $85\,000\,000$ in order from smallest to largest.

::hint
**What you need:** the number with more digits is greater.

**Steps:**
1. Count the digits of each number.
2. Compare the numbers with the same number of digits from left to right.

::solution
The numbers $58\,000\,000$ and $85\,000\,000$ have eight digits, and the other two have nine digits. Among the eight-digit numbers: $5<8$, so $58\,000\,000<85\,000\,000$. Among the nine-digit numbers: the ten-millions digit $0<8$, so $508\,000\,000<580\,000\,000$.

We have $58\,000\,000<85\,000\,000<508\,000\,000<580\,000\,000$.

**Answer.** $58\,000\,000$; $85\,000\,000$; $508\,000\,000$; $580\,000\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
The prices of three motorbikes (made-up numbers) are $45\,000\,000$ dong, $52\,500\,000$ dong and $49\,800\,000$ dong. Put the prices in order from lowest to highest.

::hint
**What you need:** all three numbers have eight digits; compare from the ten-millions.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Compare the ten-millions digits of the three numbers.
3. For the two numbers with equal ten-millions digits, compare the millions.

::solution
Solution

The ten-millions digits of the three numbers are $4$, $5$ and $4$. The number $52\,500\,000$ has the largest ten-millions digit. The other two have the same ten-millions digit, $4$; millions: $5$ and $9$. The number $45\,000\,000$ is smaller.

We have $45\,000\,000<49\,800\,000<52\,500\,000$.

**Answer.** $45\,000\,000$ dong; $49\,800\,000$ dong; $52\,500\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on addition and subtraction within $100\,000$. Work out $1\,000\,000-99\,999$. How many digits does the result have? Which place and which period is the digit $9$ of the result in?

::hint
**What you need:** set out the subtraction and subtract from right to left, regrouping when needed.

**Steps:**
1. Set out $1\,000\,000-99\,999$.
2. Count the digits of the result and find the place and period of the digit $9$.

::solution
We have $1\,000\,000-99\,999=900\,001$. The result has six digits. The digit $9$ is in the hundred-thousands place, in the thousands period.

**Answer.** $900\,001$; six digits; hundred-thousands place, thousands period.
:::

:::exercise
Nam says: "$99\,999\,999$ is greater than $100\,000\,000$ because the digits $9$ are greater than the digit $1$." Check Nam's statement. If it is wrong, correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** the number with more digits is greater.

**Steps:**
1. Count the digits of each number.
2. Compare the two numbers using the rule.

::solution
The number $99\,999\,999$ has eight digits and $100\,000\,000$ has nine digits. The number with more digits is greater, so $99\,999\,999<100\,000\,000$. Nam only compared the first digit.

**Answer.** Nam is wrong; $99\,999\,999<100\,000\,000$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
How many whole numbers of millions are there from $1\,000\,000$ to $99\,000\,000$ (including both of these)?

::hint
**What you need:** whole numbers of millions are $1$ million, $2$ million, $3$ million, ...

**Steps:**
1. How many millions is $99\,000\,000$?
2. Count from $1$ million to that number of millions.

::solution
The whole numbers of millions from $1\,000\,000$ to $99\,000\,000$ are $1$ million, $2$ million, ..., $99$ million. The number of them is $99-1+1=99$.

**Answer.** $99$ numbers.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
The number "five hundred and three million, four hundred thousand" is written
A. $53\,400\,000$.
B. $503\,004\,000$.
C. $503\,400\,000$.
D. $530\,400\,000$.
::key C
"Five hundred and three million" is $503\,000\,000$ and "four hundred thousand" is $400\,000$, so the number is $503\,400\,000$. Option A is missing a $0$, option B puts the $4$ in the thousands place, and option D reads "and three" as "thirty".

::mc
The digit $8$ in the number $38\,645\,012$ belongs to
A. the thousands place, in the thousands period.
B. the ten-millions place, in the millions period.
C. the hundred-thousands place, in the thousands period.
D. the millions place, in the millions period.
::key D
The number $38\,645\,012$ has the digit $3$ in the ten-millions place and the digit $8$ in the millions place, in the millions period. Option B mixes it up with the digit $3$, option C with the digit $6$, and option A with the digit $5$.

::mc
Which of these numbers is the greatest?
A. $100\,000\,000$.
B. $98\,765\,432$.
C. $99\,999\,999$.
D. $99\,000\,000$.
::key A
The number $100\,000\,000$ has nine digits, and the others have eight digits. The number with more digits is greater.

::mc
The order from smallest to largest of $60\,500\,000$, $6\,500\,000$ and $65\,000\,000$ is
A. $60\,500\,000$; $6\,500\,000$; $65\,000\,000$.

C. $6\,500\,000$; $65\,000\,000$; $60\,500\,000$.
D. $65\,000\,000$; $60\,500\,000$; $6\,500\,000$.
::key B
The number $6\,500\,000$ has seven digits, so it is the smallest. The other two have eight digits: the millions digit $0<5$, so $60\,500\,000<65\,000\,000$. Option D is in order from largest to smallest, and options A and C have the wrong order.
