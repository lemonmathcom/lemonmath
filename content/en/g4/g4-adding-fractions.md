:::goals
- Add and subtract two fractions with the same denominator.
- Add and subtract two fractions with different denominators by writing them over a common denominator.
- Subtract a fraction from $1$ by writing $1$ as a fraction.
- Simplify the answer and solve word problems by adding and subtracting fractions.
:::

Suppose a roll of ribbon is $1$ m long. Lan cuts $\dfrac{1}{2}$ m to make a bow and cuts $\dfrac{1}{3}$ m to tie a present.

How much ribbon does Lan use altogether? How much of the roll is left? The fractions $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{1}{3}$ have different denominators, so you cannot add them straight away. This lesson shows you how to add and subtract fractions.

## Adding fractions

### Let's explore

Divide a strip of paper into $7$ equal parts. Colour $2$ parts, then colour $3$ more parts. The number of coloured parts is $2+3=5$, which is $\dfrac{5}{7}$ of the strip. So:
$$\dfrac{2}{7}+\dfrac{3}{7}=\dfrac{2+3}{7}=\dfrac{5}{7}.$$

These two fractions have the same denominator, so the parts are the same size and we only add the number of parts: add the numerators and keep the denominator.

With $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{1}{3}$ the parts are different sizes, so we cannot add yet. Write them over the common denominator $6$ and then add:
$$\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{3}{6}+\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{5}{6}.$$

:::theorem Adding two fractions
- **Same denominator:** add the two numerators and keep the denominator: $\dfrac{a}{c}+\dfrac{b}{c}=\dfrac{a+b}{c}$.
- **Different denominators:** write the two fractions over a common denominator, then add the two fractions that have the same denominator.

If possible, simplify the answer to its simplest form.
:::

Why do we need a common denominator? Only when the parts are the same size (the same denominator) can we add the numbers of parts, just as we can add $2$ oranges and $3$ oranges, while half of something and a third of something must first be changed to the same size of part.

**Connection.** This lesson uses the common denominator, simplifying and comparing fractions that you have already learned. It prepares you for multiplying and dividing fractions.

We start with fractions with the same denominator.

:::example Adding fractions with the same denominator
Work out:
a) $\dfrac{2}{7}+\dfrac{3}{7}$
b) $\dfrac{3}{8}+\dfrac{1}{8}$

**Solution.** a) $\dfrac{2}{7}+\dfrac{3}{7}=\dfrac{2+3}{7}=\dfrac{5}{7}$.

b) $\dfrac{3}{8}+\dfrac{1}{8}=\dfrac{3+1}{8}=\dfrac{4}{8}$. Both $4$ and $8$ are divisible by $4$, so we simplify: $\dfrac{4}{8}=\dfrac{1}{2}$.

So a) $\dfrac{5}{7}$; b) $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
:::

Now fractions with different denominators.

:::example Adding fractions with different denominators
Work out $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}$.

**Solution.** We write them over the common denominator $2\times3=6$:
$$\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{1\times3}{2\times3}=\dfrac{3}{6},\qquad \dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{1\times2}{3\times2}=\dfrac{2}{6}.$$
Then add: $\dfrac{3}{6}+\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{5}{6}$.

So $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{5}{6}$.
:::

The sum of two fractions can be greater than $1$.

:::example A sum greater than 1
Work out $\dfrac{3}{4}+\dfrac{5}{6}$.

**Solution.** The smaller common denominator is $12$:
$$\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{9}{12},\qquad \dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{10}{12}.$$
Then add: $\dfrac{9}{12}+\dfrac{10}{12}=\dfrac{19}{12}$.

The fraction $\dfrac{19}{12}$ has numerator greater than denominator, so it is greater than $1$. It cannot be simplified because $19$ and $12$ have no common divisor greater than $1$.

So $\dfrac{3}{4}+\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{19}{12}$.
:::

## Subtracting fractions

### Let's explore

Divide the strip of paper into $7$ equal parts. $5$ parts are coloured, and you rub out $2$ of them. There are $5-2=3$ parts left, which is $\dfrac{3}{7}$ of the strip:
$$\dfrac{5}{7}-\dfrac{2}{7}=\dfrac{5-2}{7}=\dfrac{3}{7}.$$

Back to the roll of ribbon, $1$ m long. You have used $\dfrac{5}{6}$ m, so $1-\dfrac{5}{6}$ m is left. Write $1=\dfrac{6}{6}$ and subtract:
$$1-\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{6}{6}-\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{1}{6}.$$

:::theorem Subtracting two fractions
- **Same denominator:** subtract the numerators and keep the denominator: $\dfrac{a}{c}-\dfrac{b}{c}=\dfrac{a-b}{c}$.
- **Different denominators:** write the two fractions over a common denominator, then subtract the two fractions that have the same denominator.
- **$1$ minus a fraction:** write $1=\dfrac{n}{n}$, where $n$ is the denominator of that fraction, then subtract.
:::

Why write $1=\dfrac{6}{6}$? A whole roll of ribbon is made of $6$ sixths, so $1=\dfrac{6}{6}$, and taking away $\dfrac{5}{6}$ is just subtracting numbers of parts of the same size.

:::example Subtracting fractions with the same denominator
Work out $\dfrac{7}{9}-\dfrac{4}{9}$.

**Solution.** $\dfrac{7}{9}-\dfrac{4}{9}=\dfrac{7-4}{9}=\dfrac{3}{9}$.

Both $3$ and $9$ are divisible by $3$, so we simplify: $\dfrac{3}{9}=\dfrac{1}{3}$.

So $\dfrac{7}{9}-\dfrac{4}{9}=\dfrac{1}{3}$.
:::

:::example Subtracting fractions with different denominators
Work out $\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{1}{4}$.

**Solution.** The smaller common denominator is $12$:
$$\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{10}{12},\qquad \dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{3}{12}.$$
Then subtract: $\dfrac{10}{12}-\dfrac{3}{12}=\dfrac{7}{12}$.

So $\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{7}{12}$.
:::

:::example 1 minus a fraction
Work out $1-\dfrac{3}{8}$.

**Solution.** We write $1=\dfrac{8}{8}$ and subtract:
$$1-\dfrac{3}{8}=\dfrac{8}{8}-\dfrac{3}{8}=\dfrac{5}{8}.$$
So $1-\dfrac{3}{8}=\dfrac{5}{8}$.
:::

Now we answer the question about the roll of ribbon at the start.

:::example The roll of ribbon
A roll of ribbon is $1$ m long. Lan cuts $\dfrac{1}{2}$ m to make a bow and cuts $\dfrac{1}{3}$ m to tie a present. How much ribbon does Lan use altogether, and how much of the roll is left?

**Understand.** The roll is $1$ m long; Lan uses $\dfrac{1}{2}$ m and $\dfrac{1}{3}$ m. We need the total length used and the length left.

**Plan.** The length used is the sum $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}$. The length left is $1$ minus the length used.

**Solve.** The length of ribbon Lan uses is $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{3}{6}+\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{5}{6}$ (m).

The length of ribbon left is $1-\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{6}{6}-\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{1}{6}$ (m).

**Answer:** $\dfrac{5}{6}$ m used; $\dfrac{1}{6}$ m left.

**Look back.** We check: $\dfrac{5}{6}+\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{6}{6}=1$ (m), exactly the length of the roll. It also makes sense because $\dfrac{5}{6}<1$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Adding the numerators and the denominators.** Wrong: $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{2}{5}$. Why it is wrong: the result $\dfrac{2}{5}$ is even smaller than $\dfrac{1}{2}$, but a sum must be greater than each addend. Right: $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{5}{6}$.
- **Forgetting to simplify the answer.** Wrong: $\dfrac{3}{8}+\dfrac{1}{8}=\dfrac{4}{8}$ and stop. Why it is wrong: $\dfrac{4}{8}$ is not in its simplest form. Right: $\dfrac{4}{8}=\dfrac{1}{2}$.
- **Subtracting both the numerators and the denominators.** Wrong: $\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{4}{2}$. Why it is wrong: a difference must be less than the starting fraction $\dfrac{5}{6}$, but $\dfrac{4}{2}=2$ is greater than $1$. Right: $\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{7}{12}$.
:::

## Summary

- To add or subtract two fractions with the same denominator: add or subtract the numerators and keep the denominator.
- Different denominators: write over a common denominator, then add or subtract.
- $1$ minus a fraction: write $1=\dfrac{n}{n}$, then subtract.
- Simplify the answer to its simplest form when you can.
- Check a subtraction by adding: $\dfrac{5}{6}+\dfrac{1}{6}=1$.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out $\dfrac{3}{10}+\dfrac{4}{10}$.

::hint
**What you need:** with the same denominator, add the numerators and keep the denominator.

**Steps:**
1. Which denominator do the fractions share?
2. Add the two numerators.

::solution
$$\dfrac{3}{10}+\dfrac{4}{10}=\dfrac{3+4}{10}=\dfrac{7}{10}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{7}{10}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $\dfrac{5}{12}+\dfrac{1}{12}$ and simplify the answer.

::hint
**What you need:** after adding, simplify if the numerator and denominator are both divisible by a number greater than $1$.

**Steps:**
1. Add the two numerators.
2. Simplify the fraction.

::solution
$$\dfrac{5}{12}+\dfrac{1}{12}=\dfrac{6}{12}=\dfrac{6\div6}{12\div6}=\dfrac{1}{2}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $\dfrac{2}{5}+\dfrac{1}{3}$.

::hint
**What you need:** with different denominators, first write over a common denominator.

**Steps:**
1. The common denominator is $5\times3$.
2. Rewrite, then add the two numerators.

::solution
$$\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{6}{15},\qquad \dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{5}{15}.$$
So $\dfrac{2}{5}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{6}{15}+\dfrac{5}{15}=\dfrac{11}{15}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{11}{15}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $\dfrac{7}{12}+\dfrac{5}{6}$.

::hint
**What you need:** $12$ is divisible by $6$, so only one fraction needs changing.

**Steps:**
1. Change $\dfrac{5}{6}$ to a fraction with denominator $12$.
2. Add the two fractions with the same denominator.

::solution
Since $12$ is divisible by $6$, $\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{5\times2}{6\times2}=\dfrac{10}{12}$.

So $\dfrac{7}{12}+\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{7}{12}+\dfrac{10}{12}=\dfrac{17}{12}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{17}{12}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $\dfrac{11}{15}-\dfrac{4}{15}$.

::hint
**What you need:** with the same denominator, subtract the numerators and keep the denominator.

**Steps:**
1. Subtract the two numerators.
2. See whether the answer can be simplified.

::solution
$$\dfrac{11}{15}-\dfrac{4}{15}=\dfrac{11-4}{15}=\dfrac{7}{15}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{7}{15}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $\dfrac{3}{4}-\dfrac{2}{5}$.

::hint
**What you need:** with different denominators, write over a common denominator and then subtract.

**Steps:**
1. The common denominator is $4\times5$.
2. Rewrite, then subtract the two numerators.

::solution
$$\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{15}{20},\qquad \dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{8}{20}.$$
So $\dfrac{3}{4}-\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{15}{20}-\dfrac{8}{20}=\dfrac{7}{20}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{7}{20}$.
:::

:::exercise
Uncle Hai grows vegetables on $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of his garden and flowers on $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of it. What fraction of the garden do the two crops cover? What fraction of the garden is left?

::hint
**What you need:** the whole garden is $1$; the part left is $1$ minus the part used.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Add $\dfrac{2}{5}+\dfrac{1}{4}$, then subtract the result from $1$.

::solution
The fraction of the garden with vegetables and flowers is:
$$\dfrac{2}{5}+\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{8}{20}+\dfrac{5}{20}=\dfrac{13}{20}.$$
The fraction left is $1-\dfrac{13}{20}=\dfrac{20}{20}-\dfrac{13}{20}=\dfrac{7}{20}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{13}{20}$ of the garden; $\dfrac{7}{20}$ is left.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a jug holds $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a litre of water. $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of a litre is poured out. How many litres of water are left in the jug?

::hint
**What you need:** the water left is the starting amount minus the amount poured out.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Subtract $\dfrac{3}{4}-\dfrac{1}{3}$ after writing over a common denominator.

::solution
The number of litres of water left is:
$$\dfrac{3}{4}-\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{9}{12}-\dfrac{4}{12}=\dfrac{5}{12}\ \text{(litres)}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{5}{12}$ litre.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on comparing fractions. Work out $\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{1}{6}$ and compare the result with $\dfrac{5}{6}$.

::hint
**What you need:** write over a common denominator to add, then compare the two fractions.

**Steps:**
1. $6$ is divisible by $3$; change $\dfrac{2}{3}$ to a fraction with denominator $6$.
2. Add, then compare with $\dfrac{5}{6}$.

::solution
$\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{4}{6}$, so $\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{4}{6}+\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{5}{6}$.

The two fractions have the same numerator and the same denominator, so they are equal.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{5}{6}$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Work out the sum $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{1}{8}+\dfrac{1}{16}$. How much is it short of $1$?

::hint
**What you need:** write all the fractions over the denominator $16$.

**Steps:**
1. Write each fraction with denominator $16$.
2. Add the numerators, then compare with $\dfrac{16}{16}$.

::solution
Over the denominator $16$: $\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{8}{16}$, $\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{4}{16}$, $\dfrac{1}{8}=\dfrac{2}{16}$.

The sum is $\dfrac{8}{16}+\dfrac{4}{16}+\dfrac{2}{16}+\dfrac{1}{16}=\dfrac{15}{16}$.

It is short of $1$ by $1-\dfrac{15}{16}=\dfrac{16}{16}-\dfrac{15}{16}=\dfrac{1}{16}$.

**Answer.** The sum is $\dfrac{15}{16}$; it is short of $1$ by $\dfrac{1}{16}$.
:::
