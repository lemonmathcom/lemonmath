:::goals
- Understand that equal fractions can have different numerators and denominators.
- Multiply or divide both the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number to get an equal fraction.
- Find a missing number in two equal fractions.
:::

Lan eats $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a cake. Nam eats $\dfrac{2}{4}$ of the same kind of cake. Who eats more? Have a guess.

At first glance the two fractions look quite different: the numerators differ and so do the denominators. Yet the two children may have eaten exactly the same amount. Once you can tell when two fractions are equal, you can spot at once which fractions are really the same.

## Let's explore

We cut the cake step by step and watch whether Lan's share changes.

- The cake is cut in half: Lan eats $1$ part out of $2$, which is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
- Cut each half into $2$ equal smaller pieces. The cake has $4$ pieces, and Lan's part is $2$ small pieces, which is $\dfrac{2}{4}$.
- Cut each small piece into $2$ again: the cake has $8$ pieces and Lan's part is $4$ pieces, which is $\dfrac{4}{8}$.
Notice that with every cut, the number of pieces in the whole cake and in Lan's part both grow.

The cake is the same cake and Lan's share is the same share; only the cutting is different. So $\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{2}{4}=\dfrac{4}{8}$. The two children ate the same amount.

Look closely at the numbers: the numerator and the denominator are both multiplied by $2$, since $1\times2=2$ and $2\times2=4$.

:::theorem The basic property of fractions
If we multiply both the numerator and the denominator of a fraction by the same non-zero whole number, we get a fraction equal to the one we started with.

If we divide both the numerator and the denominator by the same non-zero whole number (dividing both exactly), we also get an equal fraction.
:::

Why does the fraction stay the same? Multiplying both numbers by $2$ means cutting every part into $2$ equal smaller parts: the number of parts in the whole doubles, the number of parts taken doubles too, and the amount taken does not change.

## Examples

We start with multiplying: we know the new denominator and look for the new numerator.

:::example Multiplying the numerator and denominator
Find the missing number: $\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{?}{6}$.

**Solution.** The denominator $3$ became $6$, so it was multiplied by $2$, because $3\times2=6$. We multiply the numerator by $2$ as well:
$$\frac{2}{3}=\frac{2\times2}{3\times2}=\frac{4}{6}.$$
So the missing number is $4$.
:::

This time the multiplier is not so easy to spot. Ask yourself: what do we multiply the old denominator by to get the new one?

:::example Finding the multiplier
Find the missing number: $\dfrac{3}{5}=\dfrac{?}{20}$.

**Solution.** To find the multiplier, ask: $5$ times what makes $20$? That is $20\div5=4$. Multiply the numerator by $4$:
$$\frac{3}{5}=\frac{3\times4}{5\times4}=\frac{12}{20}.$$
So the missing number is $12$.

**Check.** $12\div4=3$ and $20\div4=5$, which gives $\dfrac{3}{5}$. Correct.
:::

Dividing works too, on one condition: both the numerator and the denominator must divide exactly.

:::example Dividing the numerator and denominator
Find the missing number: $\dfrac{6}{8}=\dfrac{?}{4}$.

**Solution.** The denominator $8$ became $4$, so it was divided by $2$, because $8\div2=4$. Divide the numerator by $2$ as well:
$$\frac{6}{8}=\frac{6\div2}{8\div2}=\frac{3}{4}.$$
So the missing number is $3$.
:::

Now we use the property on a question like the one at the start: who eats more?

:::example Who eats more?
Lan eats $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a cake and Ha eats $\dfrac{3}{6}$ of the same kind of cake. Do they eat the same amount?

**Solution.** We bring $\dfrac{3}{6}$ to a smaller denominator by dividing both the numerator and the denominator by $3$:
$$\frac{3}{6}=\frac{3\div3}{6\div3}=\frac{1}{2}.$$
So $\dfrac{3}{6}=\dfrac{1}{2}$, and the two children eat the same amount.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Multiplying only the numerator.** Writing $\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{4}{3}$ is wrong, because both the numerator and the denominator must be multiplied by the same number. The correct statement is $\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{4}{6}$.
- **Adding instead of multiplying.** Many students mix this up. Writing $\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{2}{3}$ (adding $1$ to both) is wrong, because the basic property is only about multiplying and dividing. The correct statement is $\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{2}{4}$.
- **Dividing when it does not divide exactly.** Dividing both $5$ and $8$ by $2$ is wrong, because $5$ does not divide exactly by $2$. Divide only when both numbers divide exactly by that number.
:::

## Summary

- Multiplying both the numerator and the denominator by the same non-zero number gives an equal fraction.
- Dividing both by the same non-zero number (exactly) also does.
- Example: $\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{2}{4}=\dfrac{4}{8}$.
- To find a missing number, find the multiplier or divisor from the two denominators.
- Equal fractions show the same point on a number line.
Back to the cake: one half and two quarters are two names for the same share, so Lan and Nam ate the same amount. Always do the same thing to the numerator and the denominator, and practise a few questions to make it stick.

## Practice

Every exercise has the key idea and a hint to get you started. Have a go on your own first, use the hint if you get stuck, and open the solution last to check.

:::exercise
Find the missing number: $\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{?}{6}$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply both the numerator and the denominator by the same number.

**Steps:**
1. $3$ times what makes $6$?
2. Multiply the numerator by that number.

::solution
When we multiply both the numerator and the denominator of a fraction by the same whole number other than $0$, we get a fraction equal to the original one.

The denominator has gone from $3$ to $6$. Since $3\times2=6$, we multiply both the numerator and the denominator by $2$:
$$\frac{1}{3}=\frac{1\times2}{3\times2}=\frac{2}{6}$$

So the missing number is $2$.

**Answer.** $2$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the missing number: $\dfrac{4}{5}=\dfrac{12}{?}$.

::hint
**What you need:** the numerator $4$ became $12$, so what was it multiplied by?

**Steps:**
1. Find the multiplier: $12\div4$.
2. Multiply the denominator by it.

::solution
When we multiply both the numerator and the denominator of a fraction by the same whole number other than $0$, we get a fraction equal to the original one.

The numerator has gone from $4$ to $12$. Since $12\div4=3$, we multiply both the numerator and the denominator by $3$:
$$\frac{4}{5}=\frac{4\times3}{5\times3}=\frac{12}{15}$$

So the missing number is $15$.

**Answer.** $15$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson: what is $3\div4$ written as a fraction? Then write that fraction with denominator $8$.

::hint
**What you need:** $a\div b=\dfrac{a}{b}$; multiply both the numerator and the denominator by the same number.

**Steps:**
1. Write $3\div4$ as a fraction.
2. Multiply the numerator and the denominator by $2$.

::solution
The quotient of a whole number divided by a whole number other than $0$ can be written as a fraction, with the dividend as the numerator and the divisor as the denominator:
$$3\div4=\frac{3}{4}$$

To write $\dfrac{3}{4}$ with denominator $8$: since $4\times2=8$, we multiply both the numerator and the denominator by $2$:
$$\frac{3}{4}=\frac{3\times2}{4\times2}=\frac{6}{8}$$

So $3\div4$ written as a fraction is $\dfrac{3}{4}$, and that fraction with denominator $8$ is $\dfrac{6}{8}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{3}{4}$; $\dfrac{6}{8}$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the missing number: $\dfrac{8}{12}=\dfrac{?}{3}$.

::hint
**What you need:** divide both the numerator and the denominator by the same number.

**Steps:**
1. $12$ divided by what gives $3$?
2. Divide the numerator by that number.

::solution
When we divide both the numerator and the denominator of a fraction by the same whole number other than $0$ (a common factor of both), we get a fraction equal to the original one.

The denominator has gone from $12$ to $3$. Since $12\div4=3$, we divide both the numerator and the denominator by $4$:
$$\frac{8}{12}=\frac{8\div4}{12\div4}=\frac{2}{3}$$

So the missing number is $2$.

**Answer.** $2$.
:::

:::exercise
Can we write $\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{2}{3}$ by adding $1$ to both the numerator and the denominator? Why?

::hint
**What you need:** the basic property uses only multiplying and dividing.

**Steps:**
1. Add $1$ to the numerator and denominator of $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
2. Compare with multiplying or dividing.

::solution
Adding $1$ to both the numerator and the denominator of $\dfrac{1}{2}$ gives $\dfrac{1+1}{2+1}=\dfrac{2}{3}$.

By the basic property of fractions, to get a fraction equal to $\dfrac{1}{2}$ we must multiply (or divide) both the numerator and the denominator by the same number, not add the same number. The numerator $1$ times $2$ gives $2$, but the denominator $2$ times $2$ does not give $3$.

The fraction equal to $\dfrac{1}{2}$ when both numbers are multiplied by $2$ is:
$$\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1\times2}{2\times2}=\frac{2}{4}$$

Check: with the common denominator $6$, $\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{3}{6}$ and $\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{4}{6}$; since $3\neq4$, $\dfrac{1}{2}\neq\dfrac{2}{3}$.

So we cannot write $\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{2}{3}$ by adding $1$ to both the numerator and the denominator; the fraction equal to $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is $\dfrac{2}{4}$.

**Answer.** No, because both numbers must be multiplied by the same number.
:::

:::exercise
Mai wrote: "$\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{6}{10}$ because I multiplied the numerator by $3$ and the denominator by $2$." Where did Mai go wrong? Write it correctly.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply the numerator and denominator by the same number.

**Steps:**
1. Did Mai multiply both numbers by the same number?
2. Multiply both numbers by $3$.

::solution
Mai multiplied the numerator $2$ by $3$ but the denominator $5$ by $2$. The two numbers used are different, so $\dfrac{2}{5}$ is not equal to $\dfrac{6}{10}$.

By the basic property of fractions, we must multiply both the numerator and the denominator by the same number. Multiplying both by $3$:
$$\frac{2}{5}=\frac{2\times3}{5\times3}=\frac{6}{15}$$

So Mai went wrong by multiplying the numerator and the denominator by two different numbers; the correct working is $\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{6}{15}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{6}{15}$.
:::

:::exercise
Nam eats $\dfrac{2}{4}$ of a cake, Ha eats $\dfrac{4}{8}$ and Lan eats $\dfrac{3}{6}$ (the cakes are all the same). Divide the numerator and denominator to write each fraction with numerator $1$, then say whether the three children ate the same amount.

::hint
**What you need:** divide both the numerator and the denominator by the same number.

**Steps:**
1. Divide $\dfrac{2}{4}$ by $2$, $\dfrac{4}{8}$ by $4$ and $\dfrac{3}{6}$ by $3$.
2. Compare the results.

::solution
Dividing both the numerator and the denominator by the same whole number other than $0$ gives a fraction equal to the original one.

Nam's share:
$$\frac{2}{4}=\frac{2\div2}{4\div2}=\frac{1}{2}$$

Ha's share:
$$\frac{4}{8}=\frac{4\div4}{8\div4}=\frac{1}{2}$$

Lan's share:
$$\frac{3}{6}=\frac{3\div3}{6\div3}=\frac{1}{2}$$

All three fractions equal $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and the cakes are all the same, so the three children ate the same amount.

So the three children ate the same amount, $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a cake each.

**Answer.** The three children ate the same amount, $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a cake each.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find the whole numbers $a$ and $b$ such that $\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{a}{20}=\dfrac{27}{b}$.

::hint
**What you need:** find the multiplier for each fraction from the denominator or numerator we know.

**Steps:**
1. $4\times5=20$: find $a$.
2. $3\times9=27$: find $b$.

::solution
We have $\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{a}{20}=\dfrac{27}{b}$, so we find $a$ and $b$ using the basic property of fractions.

Finding $a$: the denominator has gone from $4$ to $20$. Since $20\div4=5$, we multiply both the numerator and the denominator by $5$:
$$\frac{3}{4}=\frac{3\times5}{4\times5}=\frac{15}{20}$$

So $a=15$.

Finding $b$: the numerator has gone from $3$ to $27$. Since $27\div3=9$, we multiply both the numerator and the denominator by $9$:
$$\frac{3}{4}=\frac{3\times9}{4\times9}=\frac{27}{36}$$

So $b=36$.

Check: $15\div5=3$, $20\div5=4$; $27\div9=3$, $36\div9=4$. Both give $\dfrac{3}{4}$. Correct.

So $a=15$ and $b=36$.

**Answer.** $a=15$, $b=36$.
:::
