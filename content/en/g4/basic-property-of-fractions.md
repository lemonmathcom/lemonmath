:::goals
- Understand that equal fractions can have different numerators and denominators.
- Multiply or divide both the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number to get an equal fraction.
- Find a missing number in two equal fractions.
:::

Lan eats $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a cake. Nam eats $\dfrac{2}{4}$ of the same kind of cake. Who eats more?

The two fractions look different, because both the numerators and the denominators are different. But the two children may have eaten the same amount. This lesson helps you see when two fractions are equal.

## Let's explore

We cut the cake step by step.

- The cake is cut in half: Lan eats $1$ part out of $2$, which is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
- Cut each half into $2$ equal smaller pieces. The cake has $4$ pieces, and Lan's part is $2$ small pieces, which is $\dfrac{2}{4}$.
- Cut each small piece into $2$ again: the cake has $8$ pieces and Lan's part is $4$ pieces, which is $\dfrac{4}{8}$.

The cake and Lan's part have not changed, only the way we cut it. So $\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{2}{4}=\dfrac{4}{8}$. The two children ate the same amount.

We can see that the numerator and denominator are both multiplied by $2$: $1\times2=2$ and $2\times2=4$.

:::theorem The basic property of fractions
If we multiply both the numerator and the denominator of a fraction by the same non-zero whole number, we get a fraction equal to the one we started with.

If we divide both the numerator and the denominator by the same non-zero whole number (dividing both exactly), we also get an equal fraction.
:::

This is true because multiplying both numbers by $2$ means cutting every part into $2$ equal smaller parts: the number of parts in the whole doubles, the number of parts taken doubles too, and the amount taken does not change.

## Examples

We start with multiplying.

:::example Multiplying the numerator and denominator
Find the missing number: $\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{?}{6}$.

**Solution.** The denominator $3$ became $6$, so it was multiplied by $2$, because $3\times2=6$. We multiply the numerator by $2$ as well:
$$\frac{2}{3}=\frac{2\times2}{3\times2}=\frac{4}{6}.$$
So the missing number is $4$.
:::

Now the multiplier is harder to see.

:::example Finding the multiplier
Find the missing number: $\dfrac{3}{5}=\dfrac{?}{20}$.

**Solution.** To find the multiplier, ask: $5$ times what makes $20$? That is $20\div5=4$. Multiply the numerator by $4$:
$$\frac{3}{5}=\frac{3\times4}{5\times4}=\frac{12}{20}.$$
So the missing number is $12$.

**Check.** $12\div4=3$ and $20\div4=5$, which gives $\dfrac{3}{5}$. Correct.
:::

We can also divide, but both numbers must divide exactly.

:::example Dividing the numerator and denominator
Find the missing number: $\dfrac{6}{8}=\dfrac{?}{4}$.

**Solution.** The denominator $8$ became $4$, so it was divided by $2$, because $8\div2=4$. Divide the numerator by $2$ as well:
$$\frac{6}{8}=\frac{6\div2}{8\div2}=\frac{3}{4}.$$
So the missing number is $3$.
:::

Now we use the property to answer a question from everyday life.

:::example Who eats more?
Lan eats $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a cake and Ha eats $\dfrac{3}{6}$ of the same kind of cake. Do they eat the same amount?

**Solution.** We bring $\dfrac{3}{6}$ to a smaller denominator by dividing both the numerator and the denominator by $3$:
$$\frac{3}{6}=\frac{3\div3}{6\div3}=\frac{1}{2}.$$
So $\dfrac{3}{6}=\dfrac{1}{2}$, and the two children eat the same amount.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Multiplying only the numerator.** Wrong: $\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{4}{3}$. Why it is wrong: both the numerator and the denominator must be multiplied by the same number. Right: $\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{4}{6}$.
- **Adding instead of multiplying.** Wrong: $\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{2}{3}$ (adding $1$ to both). Why it is wrong: the basic property is only about multiplying and dividing. Right: $\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{2}{4}$.
- **Dividing when it does not divide exactly.** Wrong: dividing both $5$ and $8$ by $2$. Why it is wrong: $5$ does not divide exactly by $2$. Right: divide only when both numbers divide exactly by that number.
:::

## Summary

- Multiplying both the numerator and the denominator by the same non-zero number gives an equal fraction.
- Dividing both by the same non-zero number (exactly) also does.
- Example: $\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{2}{4}=\dfrac{4}{8}$.
- To find a missing number, find the multiplier or divisor from the two denominators.
- Equal fractions show the same point on a number line.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Find the missing number: $\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{?}{6}$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply both the numerator and the denominator by the same number.

**Steps:**
1. $3$ times what makes $6$?
2. Multiply the numerator by that number.

::solution
$3\times2=6$, so $\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{1\times2}{3\times2}=\dfrac{2}{6}$.

**Answer.** $2$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the missing number: $\dfrac{4}{5}=\dfrac{12}{?}$.

::hint
**What you need:** the numerator $4$ became $12$, so what was it multiplied by?

**Steps:**
1. Find the multiplier: $12\div4$.
2. Multiply the denominator by it.

::solution
$12\div4=3$, so $\dfrac{4}{5}=\dfrac{4\times3}{5\times3}=\dfrac{12}{15}$.

**Answer.** $15$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson: what is $3\div4$ written as a fraction? Then write that fraction with denominator $8$.

::hint
**What you need:** $a\div b=\dfrac{a}{b}$; multiply both the numerator and the denominator by the same number.

**Steps:**
1. Write $3\div4$ as a fraction.
2. Multiply the numerator and the denominator by $2$.

::solution
$3\div4=\dfrac{3}{4}$. Since $4\times2=8$, we get $\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{3\times2}{4\times2}=\dfrac{6}{8}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{3}{4}$; $\dfrac{6}{8}$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the missing number: $\dfrac{8}{12}=\dfrac{?}{3}$.

::hint
**What you need:** divide both the numerator and the denominator by the same number.

**Steps:**
1. $12$ divided by what gives $3$?
2. Divide the numerator by that number.

::solution
$12\div4=3$, so $\dfrac{8}{12}=\dfrac{8\div4}{12\div4}=\dfrac{2}{3}$.

**Answer.** $2$.
:::

:::exercise
Can we write $\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{2}{3}$ by adding $1$ to both the numerator and the denominator? Why?

::hint
**What you need:** the basic property uses only multiplying and dividing.

**Steps:**
1. Add $1$ to the numerator and denominator of $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
2. Compare with multiplying or dividing.

::solution
No. The numerator $1$ times $2$ gives $2$, but the denominator $2$ times $2$ does not give $3$. The two fractions are not equal; the fraction equal to $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is $\dfrac{2}{4}$.

**Answer.** No, because both numbers must be multiplied by the same number.
:::

:::exercise
Mai wrote: "$\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{6}{10}$ because I multiplied the numerator by $3$ and the denominator by $2$." Where did Mai go wrong? Write it correctly.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply the numerator and denominator by the same number.

**Steps:**
1. Did Mai multiply both numbers by the same number?
2. Multiply both numbers by $3$.

::solution
Mai multiplied the numerator by $3$ but the denominator by $2$: two different numbers. The correct working is $\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{2\times3}{5\times3}=\dfrac{6}{15}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{6}{15}$.
:::

:::exercise
Nam eats $\dfrac{2}{4}$ of a cake, Ha eats $\dfrac{4}{8}$ and Lan eats $\dfrac{3}{6}$ (the cakes are all the same). Divide the numerator and denominator to write each fraction with numerator $1$, then say whether the three children ate the same amount.

::hint
**What you need:** divide both the numerator and the denominator by the same number.

**Steps:**
1. Divide $\dfrac{2}{4}$ by $2$, $\dfrac{4}{8}$ by $4$ and $\dfrac{3}{6}$ by $3$.
2. Compare the results.

::solution
$\dfrac{2}{4}=\dfrac{2\div2}{4\div2}=\dfrac{1}{2}$; $\dfrac{4}{8}=\dfrac{4\div4}{8\div4}=\dfrac{1}{2}$; $\dfrac{3}{6}=\dfrac{3\div3}{6\div3}=\dfrac{1}{2}$. All three equal $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

**Answer.** The three children ate the same amount, $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a cake each.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find the whole numbers $a$ and $b$ such that $\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{a}{20}=\dfrac{27}{b}$.

::hint
**What you need:** find the multiplier for each fraction from the denominator or numerator we know.

**Steps:**
1. $4\times5=20$: find $a$.
2. $3\times9=27$: find $b$.

::solution
$\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{3\times5}{4\times5}=\dfrac{15}{20}$, so $a=15$. $\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{3\times9}{4\times9}=\dfrac{27}{36}$, so $b=36$.

**Check.** $15\div5=3$, $20\div5=4$; $27\div9=3$, $36\div9=4$. Correct.

**Answer.** $a=15$, $b=36$.
:::
