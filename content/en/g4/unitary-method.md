:::goals
- Use the unitary method: find the value of one unit first.
- Solve problems where we know the value of several units and need the value of a different number.
- Solve problems that ask how many units we can get for a given amount.
:::

Mum buys $4$ exercise books for $24\,000$ dong. How much do $7$ books of the same kind cost?

You cannot see the price of $7$ books straight away. But if you know the price of one book, the problem becomes much easier.

## Let's explore

We go step by step.

- Step 1: $4$ books cost $24\,000$ dong. Shared equally, how much is one book?

| Number of books | Cost |
|---|---|
| $4$ | $24\,000$ dong |
| $1$ | $24\,000\div4=6\,000$ dong |
| $7$ | $6\,000\times7=42\,000$ dong |

- Step 2: one book costs $6\,000$ dong, so $7$ books cost $7$ times as much.

So $7$ books cost $42\,000$ dong. This is called the **unitary method**: we go down to the cost of one book first.

:::theorem The unitary method
We know the value of several units and want the value of a different number of units:
1. Find the value of one unit: divide the given value by the number of units.
2. Find the value asked for: multiply the value of one unit by the number of units needed.
:::

This works because every book costs the same. So $7$ books cost $7$ times as much as one book.

## Examples

We start with a problem about amounts.

:::example Packets of sweets
$6$ identical packets hold $48$ sweets. How many sweets are in $9$ packets like these?

**Solution.** First find the sweets in one packet, then multiply by $9$:
$$48\div6=8\ \text{(sweets)},\qquad 8\times9=72\ \text{(sweets)}.$$
So $9$ packets hold $72$ sweets.
:::

Problems about travelling work in the same way.

:::example Cycling
A child cycles at a steady speed and goes $36$ km in $4$ hours. How far does the child go in $7$ hours?

**Solution.** Find the distance in $1$ hour, then multiply by $7$:
$$36\div4=9\ \text{(km)},\qquad 9\times7=63\ \text{(km)}.$$
So in $7$ hours the child goes $63$ km.
:::

Now a problem with bigger sums of money.

:::example Buying rice
$5$ kg of rice cost $75\,000$ dong. How much do $8$ kg of the same rice cost?

**Solution.** Find the cost of $1$ kg, then multiply by $8$:
$$75\,000\div5=15\,000\ \text{(dong)},\qquad 15\,000\times8=120\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
So $8$ kg of rice cost $120\,000$ dong.
:::

There is another kind: we know the money and ask how many we can buy. Then step 2 is a division.

:::example Buying oranges
$12$ oranges cost $60\,000$ dong. How many oranges of the same kind can we buy with $45\,000$ dong?

**Solution.** Find the cost of one orange. Then see how many times that fits into $45\,000$:
$$60\,000\div12=5\,000\ \text{(dong)},\qquad 45\,000\div5\,000=9\ \text{(oranges)}.$$
So $45\,000$ dong buys $9$ oranges.

**Check.** $9$ oranges cost $5\,000\times9=45\,000$ dong. Correct.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Multiplying straight away.** Wrong: $24\,000\times7$. Why it is wrong: $24\,000$ is the cost of $4$ books, not $1$. Right: divide by $4$ first.
- **Dividing by the wrong number.** Wrong: $24\,000\div7$. Why it is wrong: divide by the number of books we know the cost of, which is $4$. Right: $24\,000\div4=6\,000$.
- **Forgetting the unit.** Wrong: "Answer: $42\,000$". Right: "Answer: $42\,000$ dong".
:::

## Summary

- The unitary method has two steps: find the value of one unit (divide), then find the value asked for.
- To find the value of a different number of units, step 2 is a multiplication.
- To find how many we can get for some money, step 2 is a division.
- Example: $24\,000\div4=6\,000$, then $6\,000\times7=42\,000$.
- Write the unit in every calculation and in the answer.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
$3$ exercise books cost $18\,000$ dong. How much does one book cost?

::hint
**What you need:** the cost of one unit is the total cost divided by the number of units.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Divide $18\,000$ by $3$.

::solution
$18\,000\div3=6\,000$ (dong).

**Answer.** $6\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
$4$ identical cans of oil hold $28$ litres. How many litres do $6$ such cans hold?

::hint
**What you need:** go down to one can, then multiply.

**Steps:**
1. Find the litres in one can.
2. Multiply by $6$.

::solution
$28\div4=7$ (litres), $7\times6=42$ (litres).

**Answer.** $42$ litres.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson: find the mean of $12$, $18$ and $15$. A child reads that many pages each day. How many pages does the child read in $4$ days?

::hint
**What you need:** the mean is the sum divided by how many numbers there are; then multiply by the number of days.

**Steps:**
1. Find the mean of the three numbers.
2. Multiply by $4$.

::solution
$(12+18+15)\div3=45\div3=15$ (pages a day). $15\times4=60$ (pages).

**Answer.** $60$ pages.
:::

:::exercise
A worker makes $40$ items in $5$ days. How many items does the worker make in $9$ days at the same rate?

::hint
**What you need:** find the number of items made in one day.

**Steps:**
1. Divide $40$ by $5$.
2. Multiply the result by $9$.

::solution
$40\div5=8$ (items), $8\times9=72$ (items).

**Answer.** $72$ items.
:::

:::exercise
$6$ kg of sugar cost $90\,000$ dong. How much do $4$ kg of sugar cost? Why do we find the cost of $1$ kg first?

::hint
**What you need:** once we know the cost of one unit, we find the cost of many units by multiplying.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the cost of $1$ kg of sugar.
2. Multiply by $4$.

::solution
$90\,000\div6=15\,000$ (dong), $15\,000\times4=60\,000$ (dong). We find the cost of $1$ kg first because every kilogram costs the same, so from it we can find the cost of any number of kilograms.

**Answer.** $60\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
Lan worked: "$8$ pens cost $32\,000$ dong. So $5$ pens cost $32\,000\times5=160\,000$ dong." Where did Lan go wrong? Do it correctly.

::hint
**What you need:** $32\,000$ dong is the cost of $8$ pens, not of one pen.

**Steps:**
1. Find the cost of one pen.
2. Multiply by $5$.

::solution
Lan multiplied without finding the cost of one pen. The correct working is $32\,000\div8=4\,000$ (dong), $4\,000\times5=20\,000$ (dong).

**Answer.** $20\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
A tap runs at a steady rate and fills $45$ litres in $3$ minutes.
a) How many litres does the tap fill in one minute?
b) How many litres does it fill in $8$ minutes?
c) How many minutes does it take to fill a $150$-litre tank?

::hint
**What you need:** go down to one minute. In part c) step 2 is a division.

**Steps:**
1. Divide $45$ by $3$.
2. Multiply by $8$ in part b).
3. Divide $150$ by the litres in one minute in part c).

::solution
a) $45\div3=15$ (litres). b) $15\times8=120$ (litres). c) $150\div15=10$ (minutes).

**Answer.** a) $15$ litres; b) $120$ litres; c) $10$ minutes.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
$6$ identical boxes of biscuits cost $90\,000$ dong. Mai has $130\,000$ dong. What is the greatest number of such boxes Mai can buy, and how much money is left over?

::hint
**What you need:** find the cost of one box; then see how many boxes $130\,000$ dong pays for.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the cost of one box.
2. Try multiplying the cost of one box by $8$ and $9$ to see if the money is enough.
3. Work out the money left over.

::solution
One box costs $90\,000\div6=15\,000$ (dong). $15\,000\times8=120\,000$ (dong) is less than $130\,000$; $15\,000\times9=135\,000$ (dong) is more than $130\,000$. So Mai can buy $8$ boxes, with $130\,000-120\,000=10\,000$ (dong) left over.

**Answer.** $8$ boxes, with $10\,000$ dong left over.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
$5$ exercise books cost $30\,000$ dong. The cost of one book is
A. $6\,000$ dong.
B. $150\,000$ dong.
C. $25\,000$ dong.
D. $6$ dong.
::key A
$30\,000\div5=6\,000$. Option B multiplies instead of dividing, and C subtracts.

::mc
$4$ pens cost $12\,000$ dong. To find the cost of $8$ pens, the first step is
A. $12\,000\times4$.
B. $12\,000\div4$.
C. $12\,000\times8$.
D. $12\,000+8$.
::key B
We must find the cost of one pen first: $12\,000\div4=3\,000$. Option C multiplies straight away by $8$, a common mistake.

::mc
A bus travels at a steady speed and goes $90$ km in $3$ hours. How far does it go in $5$ hours?
A. $30$ km.
B. $450$ km.
C. $150$ km.
D. $95$ km.
::key C
$90\div3=30$ km an hour, then $30\times5=150$ km. Option A is only the distance for $1$ hour, and B multiplies without dividing.

::mc
$6$ packets of biscuits hold $48$ biscuits. How many biscuits are in $4$ such packets?
A. $8$ biscuits.
B. $288$ biscuits.
C. $12$ biscuits.
D. $32$ biscuits.
::key D
$48\div6=8$ biscuits in one packet, then $8\times4=32$. Option A is the number in one packet, and B is $48\times6$.

::tf
$4$ bottles of water cost $20\,000$ dong.
a) One bottle costs $5\,000$ dong.
b) $6$ bottles cost $30\,000$ dong.
c) $6$ bottles cost $20\,000\times6$ dong.
d) With $10\,000$ dong we can buy $2$ bottles.
::key T T F T
a) True: $20\,000\div4=5\,000$. b) True: $5\,000\times6=30\,000$. c) False: $20\,000$ is the cost of $4$ bottles. d) True: $10\,000\div5\,000=2$.

::sa
$5$ boxes hold $40$ pens. How many pens are in $9$ such boxes?
::key 72
$40\div5=8$ pens in one box, then $8\times9=72$ pens.

::sa
$4$ exercise books cost $24\,000$ dong. How many such books can we buy with $42\,000$ dong?
::key 7
One book costs $24\,000\div4=6\,000$ dong. $42\,000\div6\,000=7$ books.
:::
