:::goals
- Recognise a parallelogram and describe its opposite sides.
- Recognise a rhombus and describe its sides.
- Know that a rectangle and a rhombus are both parallelograms.
- Find the perimeter of a parallelogram and of a rhombus, and solve word problems about them.
:::

A shop makes folding iron gates. Each cell of the gate has four iron bars, every bar $25$ cm long, joined by hinges. When the gate is pulled open, the cells lean over and change shape.

The shape of a cell changes, but do the lengths of the bars change? What is the total length of the four bars of one cell? This lesson helps you find out.

## Let's explore

Draw on squared paper. Draw a horizontal segment $AB$, $4$ squares long. From $A$ go $1$ square right and $2$ squares up to reach $D$. From $B$ do exactly the same to reach $C$. Join $D$ to $C$.

:::diagram Drawing on squared paper

Look at the shape: $DC$ is also horizontal and $4$ squares long, like $AB$, so $AB\parallel DC$ and $AB=DC$. The sides $AD$ and $BC$ both go "$1$ square right, $2$ squares up", so they are parallel and equal in length.

The quadrilateral $ABCD$ has two pairs of parallel opposite sides. We call it a parallelogram.

:::definition Parallelogram
A **parallelogram** is a quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel opposite sides.

In a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal.
:::

:::diagram Parallelogram $ABCD$

Why are opposite sides equal? As in the exploration, $AD$ and $BC$ go in the same direction by the same number of squares, so sliding one side onto the other makes them fit exactly. You can check by cutting a parallelogram out of paper and measuring.

:::definition Rhombus
A **rhombus** is a quadrilateral with four equal sides.
:::

:::diagram Rhombus $ABCD$

Check with a set square: the opposite sides of a rhombus are also parallel. So **a rhombus is a special parallelogram**: it has four equal sides as well. A rectangle is a parallelogram too, because its two pairs of opposite sides are parallel.

:::theorem Perimeter of a parallelogram and of a rhombus
- A parallelogram with adjacent sides $a$ and $b$: $P=(a+b)\times2$.
- A rhombus with side $a$: $P=a\times4$.
:::

**Connection.** You already know parallel lines and rectangles. Parallelograms and rhombuses extend that idea: the sides are still parallel, but they no longer meet at right angles. In a later lesson you will use them to find areas.

## Examples

We start with the parallelogram.

:::example Perimeter of a parallelogram
In the parallelogram $ABCD$, $AB=6$ cm and $AD=5$ cm. Find $DC$, $BC$ and the perimeter of $ABCD$.

**Solution.** In a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal.

Hence $DC=AB=6$ cm and $BC=AD=5$ cm.

The perimeter of $ABCD$ is $(6+5)\times2=22$ (cm).

So $DC=6$ cm, $BC=5$ cm and the perimeter is $22$ cm.
:::

Next, the rhombus.

:::example Perimeter of a rhombus
The rhombus $MNPQ$ has side $7$ cm. Find the perimeter of the rhombus $MNPQ$.

**Solution.** A rhombus has four equal sides, each $7$ cm long.

The perimeter of $MNPQ$ is $7\times4=28$ (cm).

So the perimeter of the rhombus $MNPQ$ is $28$ cm.
:::

Now let's see how the two shapes are related.

:::example Is a rhombus a parallelogram?
Is a rhombus a parallelogram? Why?

**Solution.** A parallelogram is a quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel opposite sides.

A rhombus is a quadrilateral and its two pairs of opposite sides are parallel.

So a rhombus is a parallelogram.
:::

If we know the perimeter, we can find a side.

:::example Finding a side from the perimeter
a) A rhombus has perimeter $44$ cm. Find its side.

b) The parallelogram $ABCD$ has perimeter $30$ cm and $AB=9$ cm. Find $AD$.

**Solution.** a) The perimeter of a rhombus is its side times $4$, so the side is $44\div4=11$ (cm).

b) Half the perimeter is $30\div2=15$ (cm). Half the perimeter equals $AB+AD$, so $AD=15-9=6$ (cm).

So the side of the rhombus is $11$ cm and $AD=6$ cm.
:::

Finally, the gate from the start.

:::example A folding gate
Each cell of a folding gate is a rhombus with four iron bars, each $25$ cm long. Find the total length of the four bars of one cell. Does the total change when the gate is pulled open?

**Understand.** One cell is a rhombus with side $25$ cm. We need its perimeter, and then whether the perimeter changes when the cell leans over.

**Plan.** The total length of the four bars is the perimeter of the rhombus, $a\times4$.

**Solve.** The total length of the four bars of one cell is $25\times4=100$ (cm).

So the four bars of one cell are $100$ cm long altogether.

**Look back.** When the gate is pulled open, each bar is still $25$ cm, the cell is still a rhombus and the perimeter is still $100$ cm. The perimeter depends only on the lengths of the sides, not on how much the shape leans. If one bar were longer, would the cell still be a rhombus?
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Thinking a parallelogram has four equal sides.** Wrong: "In the parallelogram $ABCD$, $AB=6$ cm, so $AD=6$ cm." Why it is wrong: only opposite sides are equal; adjacent sides can differ. Right: $DC=AB=6$ cm, but $AD$ must be given in the problem.
- **Forgetting to multiply by $2$.** Wrong: the perimeter of a parallelogram with sides $6$ cm and $5$ cm is $6+5=11$ (cm). Why it is wrong: $6+5$ is only half the perimeter. Right: $(6+5)\times2=22$ (cm).
- **Thinking a rhombus must stand upright like a diamond.** Wrong: "This shape lies flat, so it is not a rhombus." Why it is wrong: a rhombus is recognised by its four equal sides, however it is placed. Right: measure the four sides; if they are equal, it is a rhombus.
:::

## Summary

- A **parallelogram** is a quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel opposite sides; opposite sides are equal.
- A **rhombus** is a quadrilateral with four equal sides; its opposite sides are parallel.
- Rhombuses and rectangles are both parallelograms.
- Perimeter of a parallelogram: $(a+b)\times2$. Perimeter of a rhombus: $a\times4$.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
In the parallelogram $EFGH$, $EF=8$ cm and $FG=3$ cm. Find $GH$ and $HE$.

::hint
**What you need:** in a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal.

**Steps:**
1. Which side is opposite to $EF$?
2. Which side is opposite to $FG$?

::solution
In the parallelogram $EFGH$, opposite sides are equal. Hence $GH=EF=8$ cm and $HE=FG=3$ cm.

**Answer.** $GH=8$ cm; $HE=3$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
The rhombus $ABCD$ has side $9$ cm. Find the perimeter of the rhombus $ABCD$.

::hint
**What you need:** a rhombus has four equal sides; $P=a\times4$.

**Steps:**
1. How long is each side in centimetres?
2. Multiply the length of one side by $4$.

::solution
The perimeter of the rhombus $ABCD$ is $9\times4=36$ (cm).

**Answer.** $36$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
Is a rectangle a parallelogram? Why?

::hint
**What you need:** a parallelogram is a quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel opposite sides.

**Steps:**
1. What are the two pairs of opposite sides of a rectangle like?
2. Compare with the definition of a parallelogram.

::solution
A rectangle is a quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel opposite sides. So a rectangle is a parallelogram.

**Answer.** Yes.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on parallel lines. On squared paper, draw a horizontal segment $AB$, $5$ squares long. From $A$ go $2$ squares right and $3$ squares up to reach $D$. From $B$ do the same to reach $C$. Join $D$ to $C$. What shape is $ABCD$? Why?

::hint
**What you need:** two segments that are both horizontal are parallel.

**Steps:**
1. Are $AB$ and $DC$ both horizontal?
2. Do $AD$ and $BC$ go the same way?

::solution
$DC$ is horizontal and $5$ squares long like $AB$, so $AB\parallel DC$. The sides $AD$ and $BC$ both go "$2$ squares right, $3$ squares up", so $AD\parallel BC$.

The quadrilateral $ABCD$ has two pairs of parallel opposite sides. So $ABCD$ is a parallelogram.

**Answer.** A parallelogram.
:::

:::exercise
A garden is a parallelogram with adjacent sides $18$ m and $12$ m. A fence is built all the way round the garden, leaving a gap of $3$ m for a gate. How many metres of fence are needed?

::hint
**What you need:** perimeter of a parallelogram $=(a+b)\times2$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? Adjacent sides $18$ m and $12$ m, gate $3$ m. What is asked? The length of the fence.
2. Work out the perimeter of the garden.
3. Subtract the gap for the gate.

::solution
The perimeter of the garden is $(18+12)\times2=60$ (m).

The length of the fence is $60-3=57$ (m).

**Answer.** $57$ m.
:::

:::exercise
A rhombus-shaped tile has side $20$ cm. A strip of trim goes round the edge of each tile. How many metres of trim are needed for $5$ such tiles?

::hint
**What you need:** $P=a\times4$; $1\ \text{m}=100\ \text{cm}$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the perimeter of one tile.
2. Do it for $5$ tiles, then change to metres.

::solution
The perimeter of one tile is $20\times4=80$ (cm).

The trim for $5$ tiles is $80\times5=400$ (cm), which is $4$ m.

**Answer.** $4$ m.
:::

:::exercise
The rhombus $ABCD$ has $AB=6$ cm. The parallelogram $MNPQ$ has $MN=8$ cm and the same perimeter as the rhombus $ABCD$. Find $NP$.

::hint
**What you need:** perimeter of a rhombus $=a\times4$; half the perimeter of the parallelogram $=MN+NP$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the perimeter of the rhombus $ABCD$.
2. Find half the perimeter of the parallelogram, then subtract $MN$.

::solution
The perimeter of the rhombus $ABCD$ is $6\times4=24$ (cm).

Half the perimeter of the parallelogram $MNPQ$ is $24\div2=12$ (cm). Hence $NP=12-8=4$ (cm).

**Answer.** $NP=4$ cm.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A parallelogram has perimeter $36$ cm, and its two adjacent sides differ by $4$ cm. Find the lengths of the two adjacent sides.

::hint
**What you need:** half the perimeter is the sum of the two adjacent sides; this is a "sum and difference" problem.

**Steps:**
1. Find the sum of the two adjacent sides.
2. Longer side $=$ (sum $+$ difference) $\div\,2$.
3. Check by working out the perimeter.

::solution
The sum of the two adjacent sides is $36\div2=18$ (cm).

The longer side is $(18+4)\div2=11$ (cm). The shorter side is $11-4=7$ (cm).

**Check.** $(11+7)\times2=36$. Correct.

**Answer.** $11$ cm and $7$ cm.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
A parallelogram is a quadrilateral with
A. two pairs of parallel opposite sides.
B. four right angles.
C. four equal sides.
D. one pair of perpendicular sides.
::key A
This is the definition of a parallelogram. Option C describes a rhombus and option B a rectangle.

::mc
The parallelogram $ABCD$ has $AB=7$ cm and $BC=4$ cm. Its perimeter is
A. $11$ cm.
B. $22$ cm.
C. $28$ cm.
D. $14$ cm.
::key B
$(7+4)\times2=22$ (cm). Option A forgets to multiply by $2$; option C is $7\times4$; option D is $7\times2$.

::mc
A rhombus has side $12$ cm. Its perimeter is
A. $24$ cm.
B. $36$ cm.
C. $48$ cm.
D. $144$ cm.
::key C
$12\times4=48$ (cm). Option A is $12\times2$, option B is $12\times3$ and option D is $12\times12$.

::mc
Which statement is true?
A. A parallelogram has four equal sides.
B. The opposite sides of a rhombus are not parallel.
C. A parallelogram has four right angles.
D. A rhombus is a parallelogram.
::key D
The opposite sides of a rhombus are parallel, so a rhombus is a parallelogram. The other statements are not true for every parallelogram or rhombus.

::tf
Look at the rhombus $ABCD$ with side $8$ cm.
a) $BC=5$ cm.
b) $AB\parallel DC$.
c) The perimeter of the rhombus is $32$ cm.
d) $AD\parallel BC$.
::key F T T T
a) False: a rhombus has four equal sides, so $BC=8$ cm. b) True: they are opposite sides, which are parallel. c) True: $8\times4=32$ (cm). d) True: $AD$ and $BC$ are opposite sides.

::sa
A rhombus has perimeter $52$ cm. How long is each side in centimetres?
::key 13
$52\div4=13$ (cm).

::sa
A parallelogram has perimeter $38$ cm and one side $11$ cm long. How long is the adjacent side in centimetres?
::key 8
Half the perimeter is $38\div2=19$ (cm). The adjacent side is $19-11=8$ (cm).
:::
