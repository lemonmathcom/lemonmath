:::goals
- Understand that the mean is the amount each number would have if the total were shared equally.
- Find the mean of two, three or four numbers: add them up, then divide by how many there are.
- Solve word problems about the mean, including finding a missing number.
:::

Nam has $6$ marbles, Lan has $8$ marbles and Ha has $10$ marbles. They want to share them out so that each child has the same number. How many marbles will each child have?

You already know division from earlier lessons. Today you use addition and division together to solve this.

## Let's explore

We can share equally by moving marbles from the child with more to the child with fewer.

- Ha has $10$ and Nam has $6$. Ha gives Nam $2$ marbles.
- Now Ha has $8$, Nam has $8$ and Lan still has $8$.

So each child has $8$ marbles. We say $8$ is the **mean** of $6$, $8$ and $10$.

There is a quicker way, with no moving. Put all the marbles together: $6+8+10=24$. Share them equally among $3$ children: $24\div3=8$.

:::theorem How to find the mean
To find the mean of several numbers, do two steps:
1. Add up all the numbers to get the sum.
2. Divide the sum by how many numbers there are.
:::

This works because putting everything together and dividing gives each number an equal share. That is exactly sharing equally.

## Examples

We start with three small numbers.

:::example Find the mean of $4$, $6$ and $8$
**Solution.** There are $3$ numbers, so we divide the sum by $3$:
$$(4+6+8)\div3=18\div3=6.$$
So the mean of $4$, $6$ and $8$ is $6$.
:::

Now try four numbers. Notice that what we divide by changes.

:::example Find the mean of $12$, $15$, $9$ and $16$
**Solution.** There are $4$ numbers, so we divide the sum by $4$:
$$(12+15+9+16)\div4=52\div4=13.$$
So the mean is $13$.
:::

Now use the same idea for a problem from everyday life.

:::example Planting trees
Four groups planted $7$, $9$, $5$ and $7$ trees. On average, how many trees did each group plant?

**Solution.** "On average each group" means sharing equally among $4$ groups. Add the trees of all four groups, then divide by $4$:
$$(7+9+5+7)\div4=28\div4=7\ \text{(trees)}.$$
So each group planted $7$ trees on average.
:::

Sometimes we know the mean and need a missing number. Then we find the sum first.

:::example Finding a missing number
The mean of three numbers is $9$. Two of the numbers are $7$ and $12$. Find the third number.

**Solution.** Three numbers with mean $9$ have sum $9\times3=27$. The two known numbers add up to $7+12=19$. The third number is:
$$27-19=8.$$
So the third number is $8$.

**Check.** $(7+12+8)\div3=27\div3=9$. Correct.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting to divide.** Wrong: the mean of $6$, $8$, $10$ is $24$. Why it is wrong: $24$ is only the sum. Right: $24\div3=8$.
- **Dividing by the wrong number.** Wrong: $(4+6+8)\div2=9$. Why it is wrong: there are $3$ numbers, so divide by $3$. Right: $18\div3=6$.
- **No unit in a word problem.** Wrong: "Answer: $7$". Right: "Answer: $7$ trees".
:::

## Summary

- The mean is the number each value would be if the total were shared equally.
- To find it, divide the sum by how many numbers there are.
- Example: $(4+6+8)\div3=18\div3=6$.
- If you know the mean and how many numbers there are, the sum is the mean times that number.
- In word problems, remember the unit.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Find the mean of $5$, $7$ and $9$.

::hint
**What you need:** the mean is the sum divided by how many numbers there are.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the sum of the three numbers.
2. Divide the sum by $3$.

::solution
$(5+7+9)\div3=21\div3=7$.

**Answer.** $7$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the mean of $10$, $20$, $30$ and $40$.

::hint
**What you need:** divide the sum by how many numbers there are.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the sum of the four numbers.
2. Divide the sum by $4$.

::solution
$(10+20+30+40)\div4=100\div4=25$.

**Answer.** $25$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember dividing by a two-digit number: work out $156\div12$. Then say what the mean is of $12$ numbers whose sum is $156$.

::hint
**What you need:** the mean is the sum divided by how many numbers there are.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $156\div12$ (try $12\times13$).
2. For $12$ numbers with sum $156$, the mean is $156\div12$.

::solution
$12\times13=156$, so $156\div12=13$. The mean of those $12$ numbers is $13$.

**Answer.** $13$.
:::

:::exercise
Three numbers have a sum of $45$. Find their mean.

::hint
**What you need:** the sum is already given, so just divide by how many numbers there are.

**Steps:**
1. How many numbers are there?
2. Divide $45$ by that number.

::solution
$45\div3=15$.

**Answer.** $15$.
:::

:::exercise
Two children put together $20$ marbles and $36$ marbles, then share them equally. How many marbles does each child get? Why do we divide by $2$?

::hint
**What you need:** to share among some children, divide the total by the number of children.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Work out the total, then divide by the number of children.

::solution
The total is $20+36=56$ (marbles). There are $2$ children, so $56\div2=28$ (marbles).

**Answer.** $28$ marbles.
:::

:::exercise
Mai wrote: "The mean of $4$, $8$, $6$, $10$: the sum is $28$, and $28\div3$ does not work out exactly, so there is no mean." Where did Mai go wrong? Do it correctly.

::hint
**What you need:** divide the sum by how many numbers there are.

**Steps:**
1. Count how many numbers there are.
2. Divide the sum by that number.

::solution
There are $4$ numbers, not $3$, so Mai divided by the wrong number. The correct working is $(4+8+6+10)\div4=28\div4=7$.

**Answer.** $7$.
:::

:::exercise
The midday temperature on five days in a row: Monday $22$ degrees, Tuesday $24$ degrees, Wednesday $25$ degrees, Thursday $23$ degrees, Friday $26$ degrees.
a) Find the mean temperature over the five days.
b) Which days were warmer than the mean?

::hint
**What you need:** the mean temperature is the mean of the five numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Add them up and divide by $5$.
2. Compare each day with your answer.

::solution
a) $(22+24+25+23+26)\div5=120\div5=24$ (degrees).
b) The days above $24$ degrees are Wednesday ($25$) and Friday ($26$).

**Answer.** a) $24$ degrees; b) Wednesday and Friday.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
The mean of four numbers is $15$. Three of the numbers are $12$, $18$ and $20$. Find the fourth number.

::hint
**What you need:** the sum is the mean times how many numbers there are.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the sum of all four numbers: $15\times4$.
2. Subtract the sum of the three known numbers.

::solution
The sum of the four numbers is $15\times4=60$. The three known numbers add up to $12+18+20=50$. The fourth number is $60-50=10$.

**Check.** $(12+18+20+10)\div4=60\div4=15$. Correct.

**Answer.** $10$.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
The mean of $6$, $8$ and $10$ is
A. $24$.
B. $12$.
C. $8$.
D. $6$.
::key C
$(6+8+10)\div3=24\div3=8$. Option A forgets to divide, and B divides by $2$.

::mc
The correct way to find the mean of $5$, $7$, $9$ and $11$ is
A. $(5+7+9+11)\div3$.
B. $(5+7+9+11)\div4$.
C. $5+7+9+11$.
D. $(5+11)\div4$.
::key B
There are $4$ numbers, so divide the sum by $4$. Option A divides by the wrong number, and C forgets to divide.

::mc
The mean of three numbers is $9$. Their sum is
A. $3$.
B. $12$.
C. $27$.
D. $9$.
::key C
The sum is $9\times3=27$. Option B adds $9+3$ instead of multiplying.

::mc
A shop sold $18$, $24$ and $21$ boxes of biscuits on three days. On average, how many boxes did it sell each day?
A. $63$ boxes.
B. $21$ boxes.
C. $7$ boxes.
D. $24$ boxes.
::key B
$(18+24+21)\div3=63\div3=21$. Option A forgets to divide, and C divides one extra time.

::tf
Look at the three numbers $4$, $6$, $8$.
a) Their sum is $18$.
b) Their mean is $6$.
c) The mean equals the sum divided by $2$.
d) The numbers $6$, $6$, $6$ have the same mean as the numbers above.
::key T T F T
a) True: $4+6+8=18$. b) True: $18\div3=6$. c) False: we must divide by $3$. d) True: $(6+6+6)\div3=6$.

::sa
Find the mean of $12$, $14$, $16$ and $18$.
::key 15
$(12+14+16+18)\div4=60\div4=15$.

::sa
The mean of $5$ numbers is $8$. What is the sum of the $5$ numbers?
::key 40
The sum is $8\times5=40$.
:::
