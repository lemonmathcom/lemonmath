:::goals
- Understand that the mean is the amount each number would have if the total were shared equally.
- Find the mean of two, three or four numbers: add them up, then divide by how many there are.
- Solve word problems about the mean, including finding a missing number.
:::

Nam has $6$ marbles, Lan has $8$ marbles and Ha has $10$ marbles. They want to share them out so that each child has the same number. Can you guess how many marbles each child will have?

You already know division from earlier lessons. Division alone is not quite enough here, though, because we have to put the marbles together first. Today you use addition and division as a pair, and it works for any group of numbers, not only marbles.

## Let's explore

Let's share the marbles the way the children would: whoever has more passes some to whoever has fewer.

- Ha has $10$ and Nam has $6$. Ha gives Nam $2$ marbles.
- Now Ha has $8$, Nam has $8$ and Lan still has $8$.
One move, and all three children have the same.

So each child has $8$ marbles. This number has a name: we say $8$ is the **mean** of $6$, $8$ and $10$. In plain words, it is what each child ends up with when everything is shared equally.

Moving marbles one at a time would be slow if there were a lot of children. Here is a quicker way, with no moving. Put all the marbles together: $6+8+10=24$. Then share them equally among $3$ children: $24\div3=8$. We get the same answer again.

:::theorem How to find the mean
To find the mean of several numbers, do two steps:
1. Add up all the numbers to get the sum.
2. Divide the sum by how many numbers there are.
:::

Why does this work? Adding puts everything into one pile, and dividing gives each number an equal share of that pile. That is exactly what sharing equally means, the same as when we moved the marbles.

## Examples

We start with three small numbers.

:::example Find the mean of $4$, $6$ and $8$
**Solution.** There are $3$ numbers, so we divide the sum by $3$:
$$(4+6+8)\div3=18\div3=6.$$
So the mean of $4$, $6$ and $8$ is $6$.
:::

Now try four numbers. Here is where many people slip: how many numbers there are has changed, so what we divide by changes too.

:::example Find the mean of $12$, $15$, $9$ and $16$
**Solution.** There are $4$ numbers, so we divide the sum by $4$:
$$(12+15+9+16)\div4=52\div4=13.$$
So the mean is $13$.
:::

The same idea works in everyday life. Here is one about planting trees.

:::example Planting trees
Four groups planted $7$, $9$, $5$ and $7$ trees. On average, how many trees did each group plant?

**Solution.** "On average each group" means sharing equally among $4$ groups. Add the trees of all four groups, then divide by $4$:
$$(7+9+5+7)\div4=28\div4=7\ \text{(trees)}.$$
So each group planted $7$ trees on average.
:::

Sometimes we know the mean but one number is missing. Don't worry if this feels backwards at first. We find the sum first, then take away the numbers we already know.

:::example Finding a missing number
The mean of three numbers is $9$. Two of the numbers are $7$ and $12$. Find the third number.

**Solution.** Three numbers with mean $9$ have sum $9\times3=27$. The two known numbers add up to $7+12=19$. The third number is:
$$27-19=8.$$
So the third number is $8$.

**Check.** $(7+12+8)\div3=27\div3=9$. Correct.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting to divide.** Wrong: the mean of $6$, $8$, $10$ is $24$. Why it is wrong: $24$ is only the sum, and we still have to share it equally. Right: $24\div3=8$.
- **Dividing by the wrong number.** Wrong: $(4+6+8)\div2=9$. Why it is wrong: there are $3$ numbers, so we divide by $3$. Right: $18\div3=6$.
- **No unit in a word problem.** Wrong: "Answer: $7$". Right: "Answer: $7$ trees".
:::

## Summary

- The mean is the number each value would be if the total were shared equally.
- To find it, divide the sum by how many numbers there are.
- Example: $(4+6+8)\div3=18\div3=6$.
- If you know the mean and how many numbers there are, the sum is the mean times that number.
- In word problems, remember the unit.
Back to Nam, Lan and Ha: put the marbles together, share them out, and you know what each child has. Remember it as add first, divide second. Now try the practice section.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need to remember and a hint to get started. Try it yourself first, and open the solution only when you are stuck. A mistake is not a problem: that is when you learn the most.

:::exercise
Find the mean of $5$, $7$ and $9$.

::hint
**What you need:** the mean is the sum divided by how many numbers there are.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the sum of the three numbers.
2. Divide the sum by $3$.

::solution
To find the mean of several numbers, we add the numbers and divide the sum by how many numbers there are.

The sum of the three numbers $5$, $7$ and $9$ is $5+7+9=21$. There are $3$ numbers, so we divide the sum by $3$:
$$(5+7+9)\div3=21\div3=7.$$

So the mean of $5$, $7$ and $9$ is $7$.

**Answer.** $7$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the mean of $10$, $20$, $30$ and $40$.

::hint
**What you need:** divide the sum by how many numbers there are.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the sum of the four numbers.
2. Divide the sum by $4$.

::solution
To find the mean of several numbers, we add the numbers and divide the sum by how many numbers there are.

The sum of the four numbers $10$, $20$, $30$ and $40$ is $10+20+30+40=100$. There are $4$ numbers, so we divide the sum by $4$:
$$(10+20+30+40)\div4=100\div4=25.$$

So the mean of $10$, $20$, $30$ and $40$ is $25$.

**Answer.** $25$.

:::exercise
Remember dividing by a two-digit number: work out $156\div12$. Then say what the mean is of $12$ numbers whose sum is $156$.

::hint
**What you need:** the mean is the sum divided by how many numbers there are.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $156\div12$ (try $12\times13$).
2. For $12$ numbers with sum $156$, the mean is $156\div12$.

::solution
Work out $156\div12$. We know that $12\times13=156$, so
$$156\div12=13.$$

*Check.* $13\times12=156$. Correct.

To find the mean of $12$ numbers, we divide their sum by $12$. The sum of the $12$ numbers is $156$, so their mean is $156\div12=13$.

So $156\div12=13$ and the mean of $12$ numbers whose sum is $156$ is $13$.

**Answer.** $13$.
:::

:::exercise
Three numbers have a sum of $45$. Find their mean.

::hint
**What you need:** the sum is already given, so just divide by how many numbers there are.

**Steps:**
1. How many numbers are there?
2. Divide $45$ by that number.

::solution
To find the mean of three numbers, we divide their sum by $3$.

The sum of the three numbers is $45$, so their mean is:
$$45\div3=15.$$

So the mean of the three numbers is $15$.

**Answer.** $15$.
:::

:::exercise
Two children put together $20$ marbles and $36$ marbles, then share them equally. How many marbles does each child get? Why do we divide by $2$?

::hint
**What you need:** to share among some children, divide the total by the number of children.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Work out the total, then divide by the number of children.

::solution
Solution

The total number of marbles the two children have is:
$$20+36=56\ \text{(marbles)}$$

Sharing the marbles equally means each child gets the same number. There are $2$ children, so we must divide the total number of marbles by $2$.

The number of marbles each child gets is:
$$56\div2=28\ \text{(marbles)}$$

So each child gets $28$ marbles; we divide by $2$ because the marbles are shared equally between $2$ children.

**Answer.** $28$ marbles.
:::

:::exercise
Mai wrote: "The mean of $4$, $8$, $6$, $10$: the sum is $28$, and $28\div3$ does not work out exactly, so there is no mean." Where did Mai go wrong? Do it correctly.

::hint
**What you need:** divide the sum by how many numbers there are.

**Steps:**
1. Count how many numbers there are.
2. Divide the sum by that number.

::solution
To find the mean of several numbers, we divide their sum by how many numbers there are.

Here there are $4$ numbers, which are $4$, $8$, $6$ and $10$, not $3$ numbers. Mai divided the sum by $3$, which is wrong; we must divide the sum by $4$.

Doing it correctly:
$$(4+8+6+10)\div4=28\div4=7.$$

So Mai went wrong by dividing the sum by $3$ instead of $4$; the correct mean of $4$, $8$, $6$, $10$ is $7$.

**Answer.** $7$.
:::

:::exercise
The midday temperature on five days in a row: Monday $22$ degrees, Tuesday $24$ degrees, Wednesday $25$ degrees, Thursday $23$ degrees, Friday $26$ degrees.
a) Find the mean temperature over the five days.
b) Which days were warmer than the mean?

::hint
**What you need:** the mean temperature is the mean of the five numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Add them up and divide by $5$.
2. Compare each day with your answer.

::solution
Solution

a) The total of the temperatures over the five days is:
$$22+24+25+23+26=120\ \text{(degrees)}$$

The mean temperature over the five days is:
$$120\div5=24\ \text{(degrees)}$$

b) We compare each day's temperature with the mean of $24$ degrees:

- Monday: $22<24$;
- Tuesday: $24=24$;
- Wednesday: $25>24$;
- Thursday: $23<24$;
- Friday: $26>24$.

The days above $24$ degrees are Wednesday ($25$ degrees) and Friday ($26$ degrees). Tuesday is equal to the mean, so it is not warmer.

So the mean temperature over the five days is $24$ degrees; the days warmer than the mean are Wednesday and Friday.

**Answer.** a) $24$ degrees; b) Wednesday and Friday.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
The mean of four numbers is $15$. Three of the numbers are $12$, $18$ and $20$. Find the fourth number.

::hint
**What you need:** the sum is the mean times how many numbers there are.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the sum of all four numbers: $15\times4$.
2. Subtract the sum of the three known numbers.

::solution
Solution

The mean of four numbers is their sum divided by $4$, so the sum of the four numbers equals the mean multiplied by $4$.

The sum of the four numbers is:
$$15\times4=60$$

The sum of the three known numbers is:
$$12+18+20=50$$

The fourth number is:
$$60-50=10$$

*Check.* $(12+18+20+10)\div4=60\div4=15$. Correct.

So the fourth number is $10$.

**Answer.** $10$.
:::
