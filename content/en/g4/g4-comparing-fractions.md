:::goals
- Compare two fractions with the same denominator and two fractions with the same numerator.
- Compare two fractions with different denominators by writing them over a common denominator.
- Compare a fraction with $1$ and use $1$ to help compare fractions.
- Put fractions in order and solve word problems about them.
:::

Suppose that in a PE lesson, Nam runs $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a kilometre and Ha runs $\dfrac{5}{6}$ of a kilometre.

Who runs the longer distance? The two fractions have different numerators and different denominators, so it is hard to tell at once. This lesson helps you compare fractions with confidence.

## Let's explore

Start with two fractions with the same denominator: $\dfrac{3}{8}$ and $\dfrac{5}{8}$. Divide a strip of paper into $8$ equal parts. Taking $3$ parts gives $\dfrac{3}{8}$, and taking $5$ parts gives $\dfrac{5}{8}$. Taking more parts gives more, so $\dfrac{3}{8}<\dfrac{5}{8}$.

Now two fractions with the same numerator: $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\dfrac{1}{5}$. With the same strip of paper, each part is bigger when it is divided into $3$ equal parts than when it is divided into $5$ equal parts. So $\dfrac{1}{3}>\dfrac{1}{5}$.

For two fractions with different denominators, write them over a common denominator and then compare the numerators. You can also compare a fraction with $1$: $\dfrac{3}{8}$ is less than $1$ because it takes $3$ out of $8$ parts, while $\dfrac{9}{8}$ is greater than $1$ because it takes $9$ out of $8$ parts.

:::theorem Comparing fractions
- **Same denominator:** the fraction with the greater numerator is greater; if the numerators are equal, the fractions are equal.
- **Same numerator:** the fraction with the smaller denominator is greater.
- **Different denominators:** write them over a common denominator, then compare the numerators.
- **Comparing with $1$:** if the numerator is less than the denominator, the fraction is less than $1$; if they are equal, the fraction equals $1$; if the numerator is greater than the denominator, the fraction is greater than $1$.
:::

Why can we compare numerators after using a common denominator? Writing over a common denominator does not change the value of the fractions (the basic property of fractions). Afterwards the fractions have the same denominator, so we use the first rule.

**Connection.** This lesson uses the common denominator and simplifying from the last lesson. It prepares you for adding and subtracting fractions with different denominators.

## Examples

We start with two fractions with the same denominator.

:::example Comparing fractions with the same denominator
Compare $\dfrac{3}{8}$ and $\dfrac{5}{8}$.

**Solution.** The two fractions have the same denominator $8$. Since $3<5$, $\dfrac{3}{8}<\dfrac{5}{8}$.

So $\dfrac{3}{8}<\dfrac{5}{8}$.
:::

Next, two fractions with the same numerator.

:::example Comparing fractions with the same numerator
Compare $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $\dfrac{2}{7}$.

**Solution.** The two fractions have the same numerator $2$. Since $3<7$, $\dfrac{2}{3}>\dfrac{2}{7}$.

So $\dfrac{2}{3}>\dfrac{2}{7}$.
:::

Now two fractions with different denominators.

:::example Comparing fractions with different denominators
Compare $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $\dfrac{3}{5}$.

**Solution.** We write them over the common denominator $3\times5=15$:
$$\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{2\times5}{3\times5}=\dfrac{10}{15},\qquad \dfrac{3}{5}=\dfrac{3\times3}{5\times3}=\dfrac{9}{15}.$$
Since $10>9$, $\dfrac{10}{15}>\dfrac{9}{15}$.

So $\dfrac{2}{3}>\dfrac{3}{5}$.
:::

The number $1$ helps you compare quickly without a common denominator.

:::example Comparing with 1
a) Compare $\dfrac{7}{6}$, $\dfrac{5}{5}$ and $\dfrac{4}{9}$ with $1$.

b) Compare $\dfrac{7}{6}$ and $\dfrac{9}{10}$.

**Solution.** a) $\dfrac{7}{6}$ has numerator greater than denominator, so $\dfrac{7}{6}>1$. $\dfrac{5}{5}$ has numerator equal to denominator, so $\dfrac{5}{5}=1$. $\dfrac{4}{9}$ has numerator less than denominator, so $\dfrac{4}{9}<1$.

b) We have $\dfrac{7}{6}>1$ and $\dfrac{9}{10}<1$. Hence $\dfrac{7}{6}>1>\dfrac{9}{10}$.

So $\dfrac{7}{6}>\dfrac{9}{10}$.
:::

With more than two fractions, we put them in order.

:::example Putting fractions in order
Put the fractions $\dfrac{1}{2}$, $\dfrac{3}{4}$, $\dfrac{5}{8}$ in order from least to greatest.

**Solution.** The denominator $8$ is divisible by $2$ and $4$, so we write them over the denominator $8$:
$$\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{4}{8},\qquad \dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{6}{8},\qquad \dfrac{5}{8}\ \text{(unchanged)}.$$
We compare the numerators: $4<5<6$.

So $\dfrac{1}{2}<\dfrac{5}{8}<\dfrac{3}{4}$.
:::

:::example Who runs further?
In a PE lesson, Nam runs $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a kilometre and Ha runs $\dfrac{5}{6}$ of a kilometre. Who runs the longer distance?

**Understand.** Nam runs $\dfrac{3}{4}$ km and Ha runs $\dfrac{5}{6}$ km. We need to compare two fractions with different denominators.

**Plan.** Write the two fractions over a common denominator and compare the numerators.

**Solve.** The smaller common denominator is $12$:
$$\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{3\times3}{4\times3}=\dfrac{9}{12},\qquad \dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{5\times2}{6\times2}=\dfrac{10}{12}.$$
Since $9<10$, $\dfrac{9}{12}<\dfrac{10}{12}$, that is, $\dfrac{3}{4}<\dfrac{5}{6}$.

**Answer:** Ha runs the longer distance.

**Look back.** Let's try another way: $\dfrac{3}{4}$ is still $\dfrac{1}{4}$ short of $1$, while $\dfrac{5}{6}$ is only $\dfrac{1}{6}$ short. Since $\dfrac{1}{6}<\dfrac{1}{4}$, $\dfrac{5}{6}$ is closer to $1$, which means it is greater. The result is the same.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Comparing denominators like numerators when the numerators are the same.** Wrong: $\dfrac{1}{3}<\dfrac{1}{5}$ because $3<5$. Why it is wrong: a greater denominator means more parts, each part smaller. Right: $\dfrac{1}{3}>\dfrac{1}{5}$.
- **Comparing the numerators straight away when the denominators differ.** Wrong: $\dfrac{2}{3}<\dfrac{3}{5}$ because $2<3$. Why it is wrong: numerators can only be compared when the denominators are equal. Right: $\dfrac{10}{15}>\dfrac{9}{15}$, so $\dfrac{2}{3}>\dfrac{3}{5}$.
- **Comparing denominators after using a common denominator.** Wrong: $\dfrac{10}{15}$ and $\dfrac{9}{15}$ have the same denominator, so they are equal. Why it is wrong: with the same denominator, we must compare the numerators. Right: $10>9$, so $\dfrac{10}{15}>\dfrac{9}{15}$.
:::

## Summary

- Same denominator: the greater numerator gives the greater fraction.
- Same numerator: the smaller denominator gives the greater fraction.
- Different denominators: write over a common denominator, then compare the numerators.
- Comparing with $1$: numerator less than, equal to, or greater than the denominator means the fraction is less than, equal to, or greater than $1$.
- You can use $1$ as a "bridge" to compare two fractions.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Put $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the gap:
a) $\dfrac{5}{9}\ldots\dfrac{7}{9}$
b) $\dfrac{4}{11}\ldots\dfrac{4}{7}$
c) $\dfrac{6}{6}\ldots1$

::hint
**What you need:** same denominator: compare numerators; same numerator: compare denominators (the smaller denominator gives the greater fraction).

**Steps:**
1. Part a: which denominator do both fractions share?
2. Part b: which numerator do both share? Part c: how does the numerator compare with the denominator?

::solution
a) Same denominator $9$ and $5<7$, so $\dfrac{5}{9}<\dfrac{7}{9}$.

b) Same numerator $4$ and $11>7$, so $\dfrac{4}{11}<\dfrac{4}{7}$.

c) The numerator equals the denominator, so $\dfrac{6}{6}=1$.

**Answer.** a) $<$. b) $<$. c) $=$.
:::

:::exercise
Compare $\dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\dfrac{7}{10}$.

::hint
**What you need:** the denominator $10$ is divisible by $5$.

**Steps:**
1. Change $\dfrac{3}{5}$ to a fraction with denominator $10$.
2. Compare the two numerators.

::solution
Since $10$ is divisible by $5$, $\dfrac{3}{5}=\dfrac{3\times2}{5\times2}=\dfrac{6}{10}$.

Since $6<7$, $\dfrac{6}{10}<\dfrac{7}{10}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{3}{5}<\dfrac{7}{10}$.
:::

:::exercise
Compare $\dfrac{5}{8}$ and $\dfrac{2}{3}$.

::hint
**What you need:** write them over the common denominator $8\times3$.

**Steps:**
1. Write the two fractions over a common denominator.
2. Compare the two numerators.

::solution
$$\dfrac{5}{8}=\dfrac{5\times3}{8\times3}=\dfrac{15}{24},\qquad \dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{2\times8}{3\times8}=\dfrac{16}{24}.$$
Since $15<16$, $\dfrac{15}{24}<\dfrac{16}{24}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{5}{8}<\dfrac{2}{3}$.
:::

:::exercise
Compare $\dfrac{11}{12}$ and $\dfrac{7}{6}$ by comparing each with $1$.

::hint
**What you need:** compare each fraction with $1$ by comparing its numerator with its denominator.

**Steps:**
1. Is $\dfrac{11}{12}$ greater or less than $1$?
2. Is $\dfrac{7}{6}$ greater or less than $1$?

::solution
$\dfrac{11}{12}$ has numerator less than denominator, so $\dfrac{11}{12}<1$. $\dfrac{7}{6}$ has numerator greater than denominator, so $\dfrac{7}{6}>1$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{11}{12}<\dfrac{7}{6}$.
:::

:::exercise
Put the fractions $\dfrac{2}{3}$, $\dfrac{5}{6}$, $\dfrac{1}{2}$ in order from least to greatest.

::hint
**What you need:** write them over a common denominator, then compare the numerators.

**Steps:**
1. $6$ is divisible by $2$ and $3$; write them over the denominator $6$.
2. Put the numerators in order.

::solution
$$\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{4}{6},\qquad \dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{3}{6},\qquad \dfrac{5}{6}\ \text{(unchanged)}.$$
We have $3<4<5$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{1}{2}<\dfrac{2}{3}<\dfrac{5}{6}$.
:::

:::exercise
Two water tanks have the same capacity. The first tank is $\dfrac{3}{4}$ full and the second is $\dfrac{4}{5}$ full. Which tank holds more water?

::hint
**What you need:** the tanks have the same capacity, so compare the two fractions.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Write over a common denominator, then compare.

::solution
Write over the common denominator $20$:
$$\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{15}{20},\qquad \dfrac{4}{5}=\dfrac{16}{20}.$$
Since $15<16$, $\dfrac{3}{4}<\dfrac{4}{5}$.

**Answer.** The second tank holds more water.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a red rope is $\dfrac{5}{6}$ m long and a blue rope is $\dfrac{7}{9}$ m long. Which rope is longer?

::hint
**What you need:** the smaller common denominator of $6$ and $9$ is $18$.

**Steps:**
1. Write the two fractions over a common denominator.
2. Compare the two numerators.

::solution
Write over the common denominator $18$:
$$\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{5\times3}{6\times3}=\dfrac{15}{18},\qquad \dfrac{7}{9}=\dfrac{7\times2}{9\times2}=\dfrac{14}{18}.$$
Since $15>14$, $\dfrac{5}{6}>\dfrac{7}{9}$.

**Answer.** The red rope is longer.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on simplifying fractions. Simplify $\dfrac{18}{24}$ and then compare it with $\dfrac{4}{5}$.

::hint
**What you need:** simplifying first makes the comparison easier.

**Steps:**
1. Simplify $\dfrac{18}{24}$.
2. Write over a common denominator and compare with $\dfrac{4}{5}$.

::solution
$\dfrac{18}{24}=\dfrac{18\div6}{24\div6}=\dfrac{3}{4}$.

Write over the common denominator $20$: $\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{15}{20}$ and $\dfrac{4}{5}=\dfrac{16}{20}$. Since $15<16$, $\dfrac{3}{4}<\dfrac{4}{5}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{18}{24}<\dfrac{4}{5}$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the natural numbers $x$ such that $\dfrac{3}{7}<\dfrac{x}{7}<\dfrac{6}{7}$.

::hint
**What you need:** the fractions have the same denominator $7$, so compare the numerators.

**Steps:**
1. Write the condition on the numerator $x$.
2. List the natural numbers that satisfy it.

::solution
The fractions have the same denominator $7$, so $3<x<6$.

The natural numbers $x$ that work are $4$ and $5$.

**Answer.** $x=4$ or $x=5$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Compare $\dfrac{99}{100}$ and $\dfrac{100}{101}$ without using a common denominator.

::hint
**What you need:** $\dfrac{99}{100}=1-\dfrac{1}{100}$; see how far each fraction is short of $1$.

**Steps:**
1. How much is each fraction short of $1$?
2. The smaller the shortfall, is the fraction greater or smaller?

::solution
$\dfrac{99}{100}$ is $\dfrac{1}{100}$ short of $1$, and $\dfrac{100}{101}$ is $\dfrac{1}{101}$ short of $1$.

Since $\dfrac{1}{100}>\dfrac{1}{101}$ (same numerator, and $100<101$), $\dfrac{100}{101}$ is short by less, so it is closer to $1$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{99}{100}<\dfrac{100}{101}$.
:::
