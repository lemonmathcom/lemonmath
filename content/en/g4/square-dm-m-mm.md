:::goals
- Know that $1\ \text{dm}^2$, $1\ \text{m}^2$ and $1\ \text{mm}^2$ are the areas of squares with sides $1\ \text{dm}$, $1\ \text{m}$ and $1\ \text{mm}$.
- Remember $1\ \text{dm}^2=100\ \text{cm}^2$, $1\ \text{m}^2=100\ \text{dm}^2$ and $1\ \text{cm}^2=100\ \text{mm}^2$.
- Convert units of area and solve simple problems with these units.
:::

Lan has a square piece of card. Each side is $1\ \text{dm}$ long. She wants to cover it completely with small square pieces of coloured paper. Each small piece has sides of $1\ \text{cm}$. How many pieces of paper does Lan need?

In Grade 3 you learned to measure area in square centimetres ($\text{cm}^2$). Today you learn three new units, for surfaces that are bigger or smaller.

## Let's explore

Remember: $1\ \text{dm}=10\ \text{cm}$. So each side of Lan's card is $10\ \text{cm}$ long.

- One row holds $10$ pieces with sides of $1\ \text{cm}$.
- The card has $10$ rows like that.
- The number of pieces is $10\times10=100$.

Each piece has an area of $1\ \text{cm}^2$. So the card has an area of $100\ \text{cm}^2$.

The card is a square with sides of $1\ \text{dm}$. Its area is called **one square decimetre**, written $1\ \text{dm}^2$. You have just found that $1\ \text{dm}^2=100\ \text{cm}^2$.

We can do the same for square metres. A square with sides of $1\ \text{m}$ has an area of **one square metre** ($1\ \text{m}^2$). Since $1\ \text{m}=10\ \text{dm}$, the square holds $10$ rows, and each row has $10$ squares of $1\ \text{dm}^2$. That is $10\times10=100$ squares.

Now square millimetres. Remember $1\ \text{cm}=10\ \text{mm}$. A square with sides of $1\ \text{cm}$ holds $10\times10=100$ squares with sides of $1\ \text{mm}$. The area of a square with sides of $1\ \text{mm}$ is called **one square millimetre** ($1\ \text{mm}^2$).

:::theorem Converting units of area
Three facts to remember:
- $1\ \text{dm}^2=100\ \text{cm}^2$.
- $1\ \text{m}^2=100\ \text{dm}^2$.
- $1\ \text{cm}^2=100\ \text{mm}^2$.

To change a larger unit into the next smaller unit, **multiply by $100$**. To change a smaller unit into the next larger unit, **divide by $100$**.
:::

Why $100$ and not $10$? When the side is $10$ times as long, the square has $10$ rows with $10$ squares in each row. The area is $10\times10=100$ times as big.

From the first two facts, you can see that $1\ \text{m}^2=100\ \text{dm}^2=10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$, because $100\times100=10\,000$.

## Examples

We start with a conversion from a larger unit to a smaller unit.

:::example Convert $3\ \text{dm}^2$ into $\text{cm}^2$
**Solution.** We change the larger unit ($\text{dm}^2$) into the smaller unit ($\text{cm}^2$), so we multiply by $100$, because $1\ \text{dm}^2=100\ \text{cm}^2$:
$$3\ \text{dm}^2=3\times100\ \text{cm}^2=300\ \text{cm}^2.$$
So $3\ \text{dm}^2=300\ \text{cm}^2$.

**Check.** $300\ \text{cm}^2\div100=3\ \text{dm}^2$. Correct.
:::

Now convert in both directions, with different numbers.

:::example Convert $8\ \text{m}^2$ into $\text{dm}^2$ and $500\ \text{dm}^2$ into $\text{m}^2$
**Solution.** a) Changing $\text{m}^2$ into $\text{dm}^2$ means changing to a smaller unit, so we multiply by $100$:
$$8\ \text{m}^2=8\times100\ \text{dm}^2=800\ \text{dm}^2.$$
b) Changing $\text{dm}^2$ into $\text{m}^2$ means changing to a larger unit, so we divide by $100$:
$$500\ \text{dm}^2=500\div100\ \text{m}^2=5\ \text{m}^2.$$
So $8\ \text{m}^2=800\ \text{dm}^2$ and $500\ \text{dm}^2=5\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

Sometimes a measurement uses two units. Convert each part, then add.

:::example Convert $4\ \text{dm}^2\ 25\ \text{cm}^2$ into $\text{cm}^2$
**Solution.** First change the $4\ \text{dm}^2$ part into $\text{cm}^2$:
$$4\ \text{dm}^2=4\times100\ \text{cm}^2=400\ \text{cm}^2.$$
Add the part that is left, $25\ \text{cm}^2$:
$$400+25=425.$$
So $4\ \text{dm}^2\ 25\ \text{cm}^2=425\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

To compare two measurements, change them into the same unit.

:::example Compare $2\ \text{m}^2\ 8\ \text{dm}^2$ with $280\ \text{dm}^2$
**Solution.** Change $2\ \text{m}^2\ 8\ \text{dm}^2$ into $\text{dm}^2$: $2\ \text{m}^2=200\ \text{dm}^2$, then add $8\ \text{dm}^2$:
$$200+8=208\ \text{dm}^2.$$
Now compare two numbers in the same unit: $208<280$.
So $2\ \text{m}^2\ 8\ \text{dm}^2<280\ \text{dm}^2$.
:::

Finally, use the new units to solve a problem from everyday life.

:::example A tabletop
A study table has a rectangular top $12\ \text{dm}$ long and $6\ \text{dm}$ wide. Find the area of the tabletop in $\text{dm}^2$ and in $\text{cm}^2$.

**Solution.** The area of a rectangle is length times width:
$$12\times6=72\ (\text{dm}^2).$$
Change to $\text{cm}^2$ by multiplying by $100$:
$$72\ \text{dm}^2=72\times100\ \text{cm}^2=7\,200\ \text{cm}^2.$$
So the tabletop has an area of $72\ \text{dm}^2$, which is $7\,200\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Thinking $1\ \text{dm}^2=10\ \text{cm}^2$.** Wrong: $3\ \text{dm}^2=30\ \text{cm}^2$. Why it is wrong: $10$ is only the number of centimetres in one side, but the area is $10\times10$ times as big. Right: $3\ \text{dm}^2=300\ \text{cm}^2$.
- **Forgetting the $0$ in the middle.** Wrong: $4\ \text{dm}^2\ 5\ \text{cm}^2=45\ \text{cm}^2$. Why it is wrong: $4\ \text{dm}^2=400\ \text{cm}^2$, and $400+5=405$. Right: $405\ \text{cm}^2$.
- **Multiplying when you should divide.** Wrong: $500\ \text{dm}^2=50\,000\ \text{m}^2$. Why it is wrong: $\text{m}^2$ is the larger unit, so the number must get smaller. Right: $500\ \text{dm}^2=5\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

## Summary

- $1\ \text{dm}^2$, $1\ \text{m}^2$ and $1\ \text{mm}^2$ are the areas of squares with sides of $1\ \text{dm}$, $1\ \text{m}$ and $1\ \text{mm}$.
- $1\ \text{m}^2=100\ \text{dm}^2$, $1\ \text{dm}^2=100\ \text{cm}^2$ and $1\ \text{cm}^2=100\ \text{mm}^2$.
- To change to a smaller unit, multiply by $100$. To change to a larger unit, divide by $100$.
- $1\ \text{m}^2$ is $10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$.
- To compare or add measurements, change them into the same unit first.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Write the missing number:

a) $1\ \text{dm}^2=\ ?\ \text{cm}^2$.

b) $1\ \text{m}^2=\ ?\ \text{dm}^2$.

c) $1\ \text{cm}^2=\ ?\ \text{mm}^2$.

::hint
**What you need:** each unit of area is $100$ times the next smaller unit.

**Steps:**
1. Look at the facts in the lesson.
2. Write $100$ in each blank.

::solution
a) $1\ \text{dm}^2=100\ \text{cm}^2$. b) $1\ \text{m}^2=100\ \text{dm}^2$. c) $1\ \text{cm}^2=100\ \text{mm}^2$.

**Answer.** All three missing numbers are $100$.
:::

:::exercise
Convert:

a) $5\ \text{dm}^2=\ ?\ \text{cm}^2$.

b) $9\ \text{m}^2=\ ?\ \text{dm}^2$.

c) $2\ \text{cm}^2=\ ?\ \text{mm}^2$.

::hint
**What you need:** to change to a smaller unit, multiply by $100$.

**Steps:**
1. Check which unit you start with and which you want.
2. Multiply the number by $100$.

::solution
a) $5\times100=500$. b) $9\times100=900$. c) $2\times100=200$.

**Answer.** a) $500\ \text{cm}^2$; b) $900\ \text{dm}^2$; c) $200\ \text{mm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson (yến, tạ and tonnes). A lorry carries $3$ tạ of rice and $5$ yến of maize. How many kilograms does it carry altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ tạ $=100\ \text{kg}$; $1$ yến $=10\ \text{kg}$.

**Steps:**
1. Change $3$ tạ and $5$ yến into kilograms.
2. Add the two results.

::solution
$3$ tạ $=3\times100=300\ \text{kg}$. $5$ yến $=5\times10=50\ \text{kg}$. The total is $300+50=350\ (\text{kg})$.

**Answer.** $350\ \text{kg}$.
:::

:::exercise
Convert (this time into a larger unit):

a) $600\ \text{cm}^2=\ ?\ \text{dm}^2$.

b) $800\ \text{dm}^2=\ ?\ \text{m}^2$.

c) $4\,000\ \text{mm}^2=\ ?\ \text{cm}^2$.

::hint
**What you need:** to change to a larger unit, divide by $100$.

**Steps:**
1. Check that the new unit is the larger one.
2. Divide the number by $100$.

::solution
a) $600\div100=6$. b) $800\div100=8$. c) $4\,000\div100=40$.

**Answer.** a) $6\ \text{dm}^2$; b) $8\ \text{m}^2$; c) $40\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
a) To change $35\ \text{dm}^2$ into $\text{cm}^2$, An multiplies by $100$ and Binh divides by $100$. Who is right? Why? Then convert.

b) Which unit is best for measuring the area of a school playground: $\text{mm}^2$, $\text{dm}^2$ or $\text{m}^2$? Why?

::hint
**What you need:** when you change to a smaller unit, the number must get bigger.

**Steps:**
1. Is $\text{cm}^2$ smaller or larger than $\text{dm}^2$?
2. A playground is very big, so choose a unit that is big enough.

::solution
a) $\text{cm}^2$ is smaller than $\text{dm}^2$, so the number must get bigger. An is right: $35\times100=3\,500$.
b) A playground is very big. With $\text{m}^2$ the number is short and easy to read.

**Answer.** a) An is right, $35\ \text{dm}^2=3\,500\ \text{cm}^2$; b) $\text{m}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
Find and correct the mistakes in Mai's working:

a) $6\ \text{dm}^2\ 4\ \text{cm}^2=64\ \text{cm}^2$.

b) $2\,000\ \text{cm}^2=200\ \text{dm}^2$.

::hint
**What you need:** $1\ \text{dm}^2=100\ \text{cm}^2$; to change to a larger unit, divide by $100$.

**Steps:**
1. In part a), first change $6\ \text{dm}^2$ into $\text{cm}^2$.
2. In part b), work out $2\,000\div100$.

::solution
a) Mai did not change $6\ \text{dm}^2$ correctly. The right way is $6\ \text{dm}^2=600\ \text{cm}^2$, then $600+4=604$.
b) Mai divided wrongly. The right way is $2\,000\div100=20$.

**Answer.** a) $604\ \text{cm}^2$; b) $20\ \text{dm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
The floor of a classroom is a rectangle $5\ \text{m}$ long and $4\ \text{m}$ wide.

a) Find the area of the floor in $\text{m}^2$.

b) Change this area into $\text{dm}^2$.

c) If the floor is covered with square tiles with sides of $1\ \text{dm}$, how many tiles are needed?

::hint
**What you need:** the area of a rectangle is length times width; each tile with sides of $1\ \text{dm}$ covers $1\ \text{dm}^2$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $5\times4$.
2. Change $\text{m}^2$ into $\text{dm}^2$ by multiplying by $100$.
3. The number of tiles equals the number of $\text{dm}^2$.

::solution
a) $5\times4=20\ (\text{m}^2)$.
b) $20\times100=2\,000\ (\text{dm}^2)$.
c) Each tile covers $1\ \text{dm}^2$, so $2\,000$ tiles are needed.

**Answer.** a) $20\ \text{m}^2$; b) $2\,000\ \text{dm}^2$; c) $2\,000$ tiles.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A square piece of card has an area of $1\ \text{m}^2$. Ha cuts it into small squares with sides of $1\ \text{dm}$, then puts the small squares end to end in one straight line. How many metres long is the line?

::hint
**What you need:** $1\ \text{m}^2=100\ \text{dm}^2$; $1\ \text{m}=10\ \text{dm}$.

**Steps:**
1. How many small squares with sides of $1\ \text{dm}$ are there?
2. Each square is $1\ \text{dm}$ wide in the line. Find the length of the line in $\text{dm}$.
3. Change it into metres.

::solution
There are $100$ small squares, because $1\ \text{m}^2=100\ \text{dm}^2$. Each is $1\ \text{dm}$ wide, so the line is $100\times1=100\ \text{dm}$ long. In metres: $100\div10=10\ \text{m}$.

**Check.** $10\ \text{m}=100\ \text{dm}$. Correct.

**Answer.** $10\ \text{m}$.
:::
