:::goals
- Recognise perpendicular lines and write $AB\perp CD$.
- Use a set square to check and to draw a line perpendicular to a given line.
- Recognise parallel lines and write $AB\parallel CD$.
- Draw a parallel line with a set square; name the perpendicular and parallel sides of a rectangle.
:::

The mini football pitch at a primary school is a rectangle $ABCD$, $30$ m long and $20$ m wide. The caretaker paints a halfway line joining the midpoint $M$ of the long side $AB$ to the midpoint $N$ of the long side $CD$.

How does the halfway line meet the touchlines? Will the two long touchlines ever meet? This lesson helps you answer both questions.

## Perpendicular lines

### Let's explore

Take a sheet of paper, fold it in half and press the crease. Fold again so that the first crease lies on top of itself, then press the second crease.

Unfold the paper: the two creases cross at a point and make four angles. Put a set square into each angle: all four fit the corner of the set square, so all four are right angles.

:::definition Perpendicular lines
Two lines that cross and make four right angles are called **perpendicular lines**.

We write $AB\perp CD$ and read it as: line $AB$ is perpendicular to line $CD$.
:::

:::diagram Lines $AB\perp CD$
alt: A horizontal line AB and a vertical line CD cross at O, with a right-angle mark at O
point A -3,0 left
point B 3,0 right
point C 0,2 above
point D 0,-2 below
point O 0,0 below-right
segment A B
segment C D
right A O C
:::

Why do you only need to check one angle? If one of the four angles is a right angle, the other three are right angles too (see Example 2). So to decide whether two lines are perpendicular, you only need to place the set square at one angle.

Perpendicular lines do not have to be one horizontal and one vertical. Turn the paper round and they are still perpendicular.

A rectangle has four right angles. We use a rectangle for the first example.

:::diagram Rectangle $ABCD$
alt: Rectangle ABCD with right-angle marks at the four vertices A, B, C and D
point A 0,0 below-left
point B 4,0 below-right
point C 4,3 above-right
point D 0,3 above-left
polygon A B C D
right D A B
right A B C
right B C D
right C D A
:::

:::example Pairs of perpendicular sides
Name the pairs of perpendicular sides in the rectangle $ABCD$ above.

**Solution.** The rectangle $ABCD$ has four right angles at $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$.

Hence $AB\perp BC$, $BC\perp CD$, $CD\perp DA$ and $DA\perp AB$.

So the rectangle $ABCD$ has four pairs of perpendicular sides.
:::

Now we use a right angle to explain why one angle is enough.

:::example One right angle gives three more
Lines $AB$ and $CD$ cross at $O$, and $\angle AOC=90^\circ$. Find $\angle AOD$. Are the lines $AB$ and $CD$ perpendicular?

**Solution.** The points $C$, $O$, $D$ lie on a straight line, so $\angle AOC+\angle AOD=180^\circ$.

Hence $\angle AOD=180^\circ-90^\circ=90^\circ$.

Similarly, $A$, $O$, $B$ lie on a straight line, so $\angle BOC=180^\circ-90^\circ=90^\circ$. Then $\angle BOD=180^\circ-\angle BOC=90^\circ$.

All four angles are right angles. So $AB\perp CD$.
:::

Finally, let's practise drawing.

:::example Drawing a perpendicular line
Given a line $d$ and a point $M$ not on $d$, draw the line through $M$ perpendicular to $d$.

**Solution.**
1. Place one short side of the set square along the line $d$.
2. Slide the set square along $d$ until its other short side passes through $M$.
3. Draw along that side, through $M$, to meet $d$ at $H$.

So the line $MH$ passes through $M$ and is perpendicular to $d$.
:::

## Parallel lines

### Let's explore

Look at the two opposite edges of a ruler. However far we extend them in both directions, they never meet.

The pitch at the start is the same: the long touchlines $AB$ and $CD$ are both perpendicular to the halfway line, and they never meet.

:::definition Parallel lines
Two lines that have no common point, however far they are extended in both directions, are called **parallel lines**.

We write $AB\parallel CD$ and read it as: line $AB$ is parallel to line $CD$.
:::

How can we tell that two lines are parallel without extending them for ever? We use the following rule.

:::theorem Recognising parallel lines
Two different lines that are both perpendicular to a third line are parallel.
:::

:::diagram Lines $AB$ and $CD$ both perpendicular to $EF$
alt: Two horizontal lines AB and CD both cross the vertical line EF and make right angles at H and K
point A -3,2 left
point B 3,2 right
point C -3,0 left
point D 3,0 right
point E 0,3 above
point F 0,-1 below
point H 0,2 above-right
point K 0,0 below-right
segment A B
segment C D
segment E F
right E H A
right E K C
:::

Why is this true? Suppose $AB$ and $CD$ crossed at a point $P$. Then two lines through $P$ would both be perpendicular to $EF$. Try it with a set square: from one point you can draw only one line perpendicular to $EF$. So $AB$ and $CD$ cannot cross.

**Connection.** You already know right, acute and obtuse angles. Perpendicular lines make right angles, and with them you can recognise parallel lines. Both ideas will help you understand parallelograms and rhombuses in the next lesson.

:::example Two pairs of parallel sides in a rectangle
In the rectangle $ABCD$ above, show that $AB\parallel DC$ and $AD\parallel BC$.

**Solution.** Since $\angle DAB=90^\circ$ and $\angle ADC=90^\circ$, we have $AB\perp AD$ and $DC\perp AD$.

The lines $AB$ and $DC$ are both perpendicular to $AD$. Hence $AB\parallel DC$.

Similarly, $AD$ and $BC$ are both perpendicular to $AB$. Hence $AD\parallel BC$.

So the rectangle $ABCD$ has two pairs of parallel opposite sides: $AB\parallel DC$ and $AD\parallel BC$.
:::

Let's go back to the pitch at the start.

:::example The halfway line
A mini football pitch is a rectangle $ABCD$ with $AB=30$ m and $AD=20$ m. $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $N$ is the midpoint of $CD$. The segment $MN$ (the halfway line) splits the pitch into two rectangles $AMND$ and $MBCN$. Find the length of the halfway line and the perimeter of each half.

**Understand.** We know $AB=30$ m, $AD=20$ m, and $M$, $N$ are the midpoints of $AB$ and $CD$. We need $MN$ and the perimeter of the rectangle $AMND$.

**Plan.** Opposite sides of a rectangle are equal, so $MN=AD$. The perimeter of a rectangle is (length + width) $\times\,2$.

**Solve.** Since $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$, $AM=30\div2=15$ (m). Since $AMND$ is a rectangle, $MN=AD=20$ m.

The perimeter of $AMND$ is $(15+20)\times2=70$ (m).

So the halfway line is $20$ m long, and each half has perimeter $70$ m.

**Look back.** The two halves have perimeters adding up to $70+70=140$ m, but the whole pitch has perimeter only $(30+20)\times2=100$ m. Why is it $40$ m more? Because the halfway line is counted twice, once for each half: $100+2\times20=140$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Judging by eye.** Wrong: "These lines look perpendicular, so they are perpendicular." Why it is wrong: an angle of $88^\circ$ looks almost like a right angle but is not one. Right: check with a set square.
- **Thinking lines that do not meet in the picture are parallel.** Wrong: "The two segments in the picture do not cross, so they are parallel." Why it is wrong: two lines that lean differently will meet further away when extended. Right: parallel lines still do not meet when extended for ever, for example when both are perpendicular to a third line.
- **Thinking perpendicular means horizontal and vertical.** Wrong: "Two tilted lines cannot be perpendicular." Why it is wrong: perpendicular describes the angle between the lines, not how the picture is turned. Right: check the angle with a set square, however the picture is placed.
:::

## Summary

- Two lines that cross and make one right angle are **perpendicular**, written $AB\perp CD$. Then all four angles are right angles.
- Use a set square to check and to draw perpendicular lines; do not decide by eye.
- **Parallel** lines never meet, written $AB\parallel CD$.
- Two lines that are both perpendicular to a third line are parallel.
- A rectangle has four pairs of perpendicular sides and two pairs of parallel opposite sides.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Rectangle $MNPQ$ is given. Name the pairs of perpendicular sides.

::hint
**What you need:** a rectangle has four right angles.

**Steps:**
1. Find the right angles of the shape: at $M$, $N$, $P$ and $Q$.
2. Each right angle gives one pair of perpendicular sides.

::solution
The rectangle $MNPQ$ has four right angles at $M$, $N$, $P$ and $Q$. Hence $MN\perp NP$, $NP\perp PQ$, $PQ\perp QM$ and $QM\perp MN$.

**Answer.** Four pairs of perpendicular sides.
:::

:::exercise
Rectangle $EFGH$ is given. Name the pairs of parallel sides.

::hint
**What you need:** opposite sides of a rectangle are parallel.

**Steps:**
1. Write the four sides in order: $EF$, $FG$, $GH$, $HE$.
2. Match each side with the side opposite to it.

::solution
The rectangle $EFGH$ has two pairs of parallel opposite sides: $EF\parallel GH$ and $FG\parallel HE$.

**Answer.** $EF\parallel GH$; $FG\parallel HE$.
:::

:::exercise
Draw a line $d$ and mark a point $O$ on $d$. Use a set square to draw the line $m$ through $O$ perpendicular to $d$.

::hint
**What you need:** a set square has one right angle; its two short sides are perpendicular.

**Steps:**
1. Place one short side along $d$, with the right-angle corner at $O$.
2. Draw along the other short side.

::solution
Place one short side of the set square along $d$, with the right-angle corner at $O$. Draw along the other short side to get the line $m$ through $O$.

**Answer.** The line $m$ passes through $O$ and $m\perp d$.
:::

:::exercise
Given a line $d$ and a point $E$ not on $d$, use a set square to draw the line through $E$ parallel to $d$.

::hint
**What you need:** two lines that are both perpendicular to a third line are parallel.

**Steps:**
1. Draw through $E$ the line $EH$ perpendicular to $d$ ($H$ on $d$).
2. Draw through $E$ the line $n$ perpendicular to $EH$.

::solution
Draw through $E$ the line $EH\perp d$, with $H$ on $d$. Then draw through $E$ the line $n\perp EH$.

The lines $n$ and $d$ are both perpendicular to $EH$. Hence $n\parallel d$.

**Answer.** The line $n$ passes through $E$ and $n\parallel d$.
:::

:::exercise
A garden is a rectangle $ABCD$ with $AB=12$ m and $AD=8$ m. The point $M$ lies on side $AB$ and the point $N$ lies on side $DC$ so that $AMND$ is a rectangle and $AM=5$ m. Find the length of $MN$ and the perimeter of the rectangle $AMND$.

::hint
**What you need:** opposite sides of a rectangle are equal; perimeter $=$ (length $+$ width) $\times\,2$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? $AM=5$ m, $AD=8$ m. What is asked? $MN$ and the perimeter of $AMND$.
2. Which side is opposite to $MN$?
3. Work out the perimeter and write the unit.

::solution
Since $AMND$ is a rectangle, $MN=AD=8$ m.

The perimeter of the rectangle $AMND$ is:
$$(5+8)\times2=26\ \text{(m)}.$$

**Answer.** $MN=8$ m; perimeter $26$ m.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose that on a map of a neighbourhood, Hoa Sen Street and Hoa Mai Street are both straight and both perpendicular to Tran Phu Street. Are Hoa Sen Street and Hoa Mai Street parallel? Why?

::hint
**What you need:** two lines that are both perpendicular to a third line are parallel.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? Which street are both streets perpendicular to?
2. Use the rule for recognising parallel lines.

::solution
Hoa Sen Street and Hoa Mai Street are both perpendicular to Tran Phu Street. Hence the two streets are parallel.

**Answer.** Yes, the streets are parallel.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on acute and obtuse angles. Lines $AB$ and $CD$ cross at $O$, and $\angle AOC$ is an acute angle. a) Are the lines $AB$ and $CD$ perpendicular? b) Is $\angle AOD$ acute, right or obtuse?

::hint
**What you need:** an acute angle is less than $90^\circ$; angles on a straight line add up to $180^\circ$.

**Steps:**
1. If two lines are perpendicular, which angle equals $90^\circ$?
2. $\angle AOC+\angle AOD=180^\circ$; compare $\angle AOD$ with $90^\circ$.

::solution
a) $\angle AOC$ is acute, so it is not $90^\circ$. So $AB$ and $CD$ are not perpendicular.

b) Since $C$, $O$, $D$ lie on a straight line, $\angle AOC+\angle AOD=180^\circ$. Since $\angle AOC<90^\circ$, we get $\angle AOD>90^\circ$.

**Answer.** a) Not perpendicular. b) $\angle AOD$ is obtuse.
:::

:::exercise
A picture frame is a rectangle $24$ cm long and $18$ cm wide. A carpenter adds a wooden bar joining the midpoints of the two long sides, parallel to the short side. How long is the bar in centimetres? What is the perimeter of each of the two smaller frames in centimetres?

::hint
**What you need:** the bar has the same length as the width of the frame; each smaller frame is a rectangle.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? Length $24$ cm, width $18$ cm. What is asked? The length of the bar and the perimeter of a smaller frame.
2. What is the length of a smaller frame?
3. Work out the perimeter and write the unit.

::solution
The bar is parallel to the short side, so it is as long as the short side: $18$ cm.

The length of each smaller frame is $24\div2=12$ (cm). The perimeter of each smaller frame is:
$$(12+18)\times2=60\ \text{(cm)}.$$

**Answer.** The bar is $18$ cm long; each smaller frame has perimeter $60$ cm.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A rectangular piece of card $ABCD$ has $AB=9$ cm and $BC=5$ cm. It is cut along a segment $MN$ parallel to $BC$ ($M$ on $AB$, $N$ on $CD$) into two rectangles $AMND$ and $MBCN$ with equal perimeters. Find the length of $AM$ and the perimeter of each rectangle.

::hint
**What you need:** both rectangles have the same width; if the perimeters are equal, the lengths are equal.

**Steps:**
1. Which side of both rectangles equals $5$ cm?
2. What is the sum of the two lengths $AM$ and $MB$? If they are equal, how long is each?
3. Check: the two perimeters add up to the perimeter of the card plus two times $MN$.

::solution
The rectangles $AMND$ and $MBCN$ both have width $5$ cm. Their perimeters are equal, so $AM=MB$.

Hence $AM=9\div2=4.5$ (cm). The perimeter of each rectangle is:
$$(4.5+5)\times2=19\ \text{(cm)}.$$

**Check.** $19+19=38$ and $(9+5)\times2+2\times5=38$. Correct.

**Answer.** $AM=4.5$ cm; each perimeter is $19$ cm.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
Two lines cross and make a right angle. The two lines are
A. parallel.
B. without a common point.
C. the same line.
D. perpendicular.
::key D
One right angle means the other three are right angles too, so the lines are perpendicular. Options A and B are wrong because the lines already cross.

::mc
Parallel lines are two lines that
A. never cross, however far they are extended in both directions.
B. cross and make a right angle.
C. have exactly one common point.
D. cross and make an acute angle.
::key A
This is the definition of parallel lines. Option B describes perpendicular lines.

::mc
In the rectangle $ABCD$, the side $AB$ is parallel to the side
A. $BC$.
B. $CD$.
C. $DA$.
D. $AC$.
::key B
The side opposite to $AB$ is $CD$, so $AB\parallel CD$. The sides $BC$ and $DA$ are perpendicular to $AB$; $AC$ is not a side of the rectangle.

::mc
Two different lines $a$ and $b$ are both perpendicular to a line $c$. Then
A. $a$ is perpendicular to $b$.
B. $a$ meets $b$ at a point.
C. $a$ is parallel to $b$.
D. $a$ and $b$ make an acute angle.
::key C
Two lines perpendicular to the same line are parallel. Option A confuses "both perpendicular to $c$" with "perpendicular to each other".

::tf
Look at the rectangle $MNPQ$.
a) The side $MN$ is parallel to the side $NP$.
b) The side $MN$ is perpendicular to the side $NP$.
c) The side $MQ$ is parallel to the side $NP$.
d) The side $PQ$ is perpendicular to the side $MN$.
::key F T T F
a) False: $MN$ and $NP$ are adjacent sides, so they are perpendicular. b) True: the angle at $N$ is a right angle. c) True: $MQ$ and $NP$ are opposite sides. d) False: $PQ$ and $MN$ are opposite sides, so they are parallel.

::sa
How many pairs of perpendicular sides does a rectangle have?
::key 4
A rectangle has four right angles, so it has four pairs of perpendicular sides.

::sa
A plot of land is a rectangle $ABCD$ with $AB=16$ m and $AD=7$ m. The point $M$ lies on side $AB$ and the point $N$ on side $DC$ so that $AMND$ is a rectangle with $AM=5$ m. What is the perimeter of the rectangle $AMND$ in metres?
::key 24
$MN=AD=7$ m, so the perimeter is $(5+7)\times2=24$ (m).
:::
