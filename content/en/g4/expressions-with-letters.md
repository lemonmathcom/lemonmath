:::goals
- Recognise expressions with one letter, two letters and three letters.
- Find the value of an expression with letters when the values of the letters are known.
- Use formulas with letters to find the perimeter of a square, a rectangle and a triangle.
- Write an expression with letters from a word problem.
:::

A stationery stall (made-up numbers) sells pens at $5\,000$ dong each. Buying $3$ pens costs $5\,000\times3=15\,000$ dong. Buying $4$ pens costs $5\,000\times4=20\,000$ dong. If you buy $n$ pens, how many dong do you pay?

Every different number of pens gives a different calculation. This lesson shows you how to write all these calculations of the same kind with a single expression.

## Expressions with letters

### Let's explore

Make a table of the money to pay for each number of pens:

| Number of pens | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $n$ |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Money (dong) | $5\,000\times1$ | $5\,000\times2$ | $5\,000\times3$ | $5\,000\times4$ | $5\,000\times n$ |

In the last column, the letter $n$ stands for the number of pens. The number of pens can be $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, $\ldots$ The expression $5\,000\times n$ gives you the money to pay for any number of pens.

If you buy $6$ pens, then $n=6$. Replace $n$ with $6$ and work it out: $5\,000\times6=30\,000$. So $6$ pens cost $30\,000$ dong.

### Expressions with letters and their values

:::theorem Expressions with letters
An expression with letters is made of numbers, letters and operation signs. A letter stands for some number.

To find the value of an expression with letters when the value of the letter is known, we substitute the number for the letter and then do the calculation. The result is the value of the expression.
:::

The expression $5\,000\times n$ has one letter. The expression $a+b$ has two letters. The expression $a+b+c$ has three letters.

Why use letters? Because one expression with letters stands for many calculations of the same kind. Each time you replace the letter with a different number, you get a particular calculation.

When you find a value, you still follow the usual order: brackets first, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction.

We start with an expression with one letter.

:::example Finding the value of an expression with one letter
Find the value of the expression $5\,000\times n$ when $n=3$ and when $n=7$.

**Solution.** When $n=3$, $5\,000\times3=15\,000$. When $n=7$, $5\,000\times7=35\,000$.

So the value of the expression is $15\,000$ when $n=3$ and $35\,000$ when $n=7$.
:::

Now an expression with two letters.

:::example Finding the value of an expression with two letters
Find the value of the expression $a\times3+b$ when $a=12$ and $b=7$.

**Solution.** Substitute $a=12$ and $b=7$ into the expression to get $12\times3+7$. Multiply first, then add: $12\times3=36$ and $36+7=43$.

So the value of the expression is $43$.
:::

An expression with three letters lets you write the formula for the perimeter of a triangle.

:::example The perimeter of a triangle
A triangle with sides of length $a$, $b$, $c$ has perimeter $P=a+b+c$. Find the perimeter of the triangle with $a=7$ cm, $b=8$ cm, $c=9$ cm.

**Solution.** Substitute $a=7$, $b=8$, $c=9$ into the formula to get $P=7+8+9=24$ (cm).

So the perimeter of the triangle is $24$ cm.
:::

The formulas for the perimeter of a square and of a rectangle are also expressions with letters.

:::example The perimeter of a square and of a rectangle
a) A square with side $a$ has perimeter $P=a\times4$. Find the perimeter of a square with side $15$ cm.

b) A rectangle with length $a$ and width $b$ has perimeter $P=(a+b)\times2$. Find the perimeter of a rectangle with length $45$ m and width $30$ m.

**Solution.** a) With $a=15$, we have $P=15\times4=60$ (cm).

b) With $a=45$ and $b=30$, we work out the brackets first: $P=(45+30)\times2=75\times2=150$ (m).

So the perimeter of the square is $60$ cm and the perimeter of the rectangle is $150$ m.
:::

Let us use expressions with letters to solve a real problem.

:::example Buying exercise books and a ruler
An exercise book costs $7\,500$ dong and a ruler costs $4\,000$ dong. How many dong do you pay for $m$ exercise books and $1$ ruler? Work it out when $m=4$.

**Solution.** The cost of $m$ exercise books is $7\,500\times m$ dong. Adding the cost of the ruler, the money to pay is:
$$7\,500\times m+4\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
When $m=4$, the money is $7\,500\times4+4\,000=30\,000+4\,000=34\,000$ (dong).

**Answer.** $34\,000$ dong.
:::

Finally, a problem where you write the expression yourself. We work in four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example Buying shirts and trousers
A shop (made-up numbers) sells shirts at $a$ dong each and trousers at $b$ dong each. Ms Ha buys $2$ shirts and $3$ pairs of trousers. Write an expression for the money Ms Ha pays, then work it out when $a=85\,000$ and $b=110\,000$.

**Understand.** We are told the price of a shirt is $a$ dong, the price of a pair of trousers is $b$ dong, and the numbers of shirts and trousers bought. We are asked for an expression for the money, then its value when $a$ and $b$ are known.

**Plan.** The cost of the shirts is $a\times2$ and the cost of the trousers is $b\times3$. The money to pay is the sum of the two costs.

**Solve.**

Solution

The money Ms Ha pays is:
$$a\times2+b\times3\ \text{(dong)}.$$
When $a=85\,000$ and $b=110\,000$, the money is:
$$85\,000\times2+110\,000\times3=170\,000+330\,000=500\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
Answer: $500\,000$ dong.

**Look back.** Try other numbers: when $a=90\,000$ and $b=100\,000$, the money is $90\,000\times2+100\,000\times3=180\,000+300\,000=480\,000$ dong. With one expression, we can work out the money for any price of shirt and trousers.
:::

**Connection.** The perimeter formulas you have just used are expressions with letters. Later you will meet many more formulas like this, for example the formula for area. Each time, you just replace the letters with numbers and work it out.

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Doing the operations in the wrong order.** Wrong: the value of $a\times3+b$ when $a=12$, $b=7$ is $3\times(12+7)=57$. Why it is wrong: the addition was done before the multiplication, but multiplication comes first. Right: $12\times3+7=43$.
- **Substituting the wrong letter.** Wrong: working out $b-a$ when $a=5$ and $b=8$ gives $5-8$. Why it is wrong: $a$ was put where $b$ belongs, and the other way round. Right: $b-a=8-5=3$.
- **Forgetting the unit.** Wrong: the perimeter of a square with side $15$ cm is $P=60$. Right: $P=60$ cm.
:::

## Summary

- An expression with letters is made of numbers, letters and operation signs; a letter stands for a number. There are expressions with one, two and three letters.
- To find the value of an expression with letters, **substitute numbers for the letters** and work it out in the usual order.
- Perimeter formulas: square $P=a\times4$; rectangle $P=(a+b)\times2$; triangle $P=a+b+c$.
- From a word problem, you can write an expression with letters, then substitute numbers to work it out.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Find the value of the expression $m\times6$ when $m=9$.

::hint
**What you need:** substitute the number for the letter, then work it out.

**Steps:**
1. Substitute $m=9$ into the expression.
2. Do the multiplication.

::solution
When $m=9$, $m\times6=9\times6=54$.

**Answer.** $54$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the value of the expression $125-x$ when $x=48$.

::hint
**What you need:** substitute the number for the letter, then work it out; the starting number is $125$.

**Steps:**
1. Substitute $x=48$ into the expression.
2. Set out the subtraction and subtract.

::solution
When $x=48$, $125-x=125-48=77$.

**Answer.** $77$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the value of the expression $a\times4+b$ when $a=15$ and $b=30$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply first, then add.

**Steps:**
1. Substitute $a=15$ and $b=30$ into the expression.
2. Work out $15\times4$ first, then add $30$.

::solution
When $a=15$ and $b=30$, $a\times4+b=15\times4+30=60+30=90$.

**Answer.** $90$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the value of the expression $(a+b)\times c$ when $a=12$, $b=8$ and $c=5$.

::hint
**What you need:** work out the brackets first.

**Steps:**
1. Substitute $a=12$, $b=8$, $c=5$ into the expression.
2. Work out $12+8$ first, then multiply by $5$.

::solution
When $a=12$, $b=8$, $c=5$, $(a+b)\times c=(12+8)\times5=20\times5=100$.

**Answer.** $100$.
:::

:::exercise
A garden (made-up numbers) is a square with side $25$ m. Find the perimeter of the garden.

::hint
**What you need:** the perimeter of a square with side $a$ is $P=a\times4$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Substitute $a=25$ into the formula.
3. Remember the unit.

::solution
Solution

The perimeter of the garden is:
$$25\times4=100\ \text{(m)}.$$

**Answer.** $100$ m.
:::

:::exercise
A piece of card is a rectangle with length $38$ cm and width $22$ cm. Find the perimeter of the card.

::hint
**What you need:** the perimeter of a rectangle is $P=(a+b)\times2$.

**Steps:**
1. Substitute $a=38$ and $b=22$ into the formula.
2. Work out the brackets first.

::solution
Solution

The perimeter of the card is:
$$(38+22)\times2=60\times2=120\ \text{(cm)}.$$

**Answer.** $120$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
Mai buys $m$ exercise books at $6\,500$ dong each and a ruler for $3\,000$ dong. Write an expression for the money Mai pays, then work it out when $m=5$.

::hint
**What you need:** the cost of the books is $6\,500\times m$, then add the cost of the ruler.

**Steps:**
1. Write the expression for the money.
2. Substitute $m=5$ and work it out.
3. Remember the unit.

::solution
Solution

The money Mai pays is $6\,500\times m+3\,000$ (dong). When $m=5$, the money is:
$$6\,500\times5+3\,000=32\,500+3\,000=35\,500\ \text{(dong)}.$$

**Answer.** $35\,500$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on dividing a five-digit number by a one-digit number. Find the value of the expression $90\,000\div n$ when $n=8$.

::hint
**What you need:** substitute the number for the letter, then divide from left to right.

**Steps:**
1. Substitute $n=8$ into the expression.
2. Divide $90\,000$ by $8$, then check by multiplying.

::solution
When $n=8$, $90\,000\div n=90\,000\div8=11\,250$.

**Check.** $11\,250\times8=90\,000$. Correct.

**Answer.** $11\,250$.
:::

:::exercise
Nam finds the value of the expression $3\times a+b$ when $a=10$ and $b=5$ and gets $3\times(10+5)=45$. Check Nam's answer. If it is wrong, correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply first, then add; only work out brackets when the expression has brackets.

**Steps:**
1. Does the expression $3\times a+b$ have brackets?
2. Substitute $a=10$ and $b=5$ and work it out in the right order.

::solution
The expression $3\times a+b$ has no brackets, so we must multiply first and then add. Nam added $10+5$ first, so he is wrong.

Working it out again: $3\times10+5=30+5=35$.

**Answer.** Nam is wrong; the correct answer is $35$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A rectangle has perimeter $56$ cm and length $18$ cm. Find the width of the rectangle.

::hint
**What you need:** $P=(a+b)\times2$, so $a+b$ equals the perimeter divided by $2$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $a+b=56\div2$.
2. Subtract the length $18$ from $a+b$.

::solution
We have $(a+b)\times2=56$, so $a+b=56\div2=28$ (cm). The width is $b=28-18=10$ (cm).

**Check.** $(18+10)\times2=28\times2=56$. Correct.

**Answer.** $10$ cm.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
The value of the expression $7\times m+5$ when $m=6$ is
A. $77$.
B. $13$.
C. $47$.
D. $42$.
::key C
$7\times6+5=42+5=47$. Option A adds before multiplying, option B adds $7+6$, and option D forgets to add $5$.

::mc
The perimeter of a square with side $12$ m is
A. $24$ m.
B. $144$ m.
C. $16$ m.
D. $48$ m.
::key D
$P=a\times4=12\times4=48$ (m). Option A multiplies by $2$, option B works out $12\times12$, and option C adds $12+4$.

::mc
The value of the expression $(a+b)\times2$ when $a=9$ and $b=6$ is
A. $30$.
B. $21$.
C. $15$.
D. $108$.
::key A
$(9+6)\times2=15\times2=30$. Option B multiplies before adding, option C forgets to multiply by $2$, and option D multiplies all three numbers.

::mc
You buy $m$ cakes at $4\,500$ dong each and a bottle of water for $6\,000$ dong. The money to pay is
A. $4\,500+m\times6\,000$ dong.
B. $4\,500\times m+6\,000$ dong.
C. $m\times4\,500-6\,000$ dong.
D. $(4\,500+6\,000)\times m$ dong.
::key B
The cost of the cakes is $4\,500\times m$, and we add $6\,000$ dong for the bottle of water. Option A multiplies $m$ by the price of the water, option D multiplies both prices by $m$, and option C subtracts instead of adding.

::tf
Look at the expression $a\times5-b$.
a) The expression has only one letter.
b) When $a=8$ and $b=15$, the value of the expression is $55$.
c) When $a=10$ and $b=20$, the value of the expression is $30$.
d) When $a=8$ and $b=40$, the value of the expression is $0$.
::key F F T T
a) False: the expression has two letters, $a$ and $b$. b) False: $8\times5-15=40-15=25$. c) True: $10\times5-20=50-20=30$. d) True: $8\times5-40=40-40=0$.

::sa
Find the value of the expression $9\times m-20$ when $m=5$.
::key 25
$9\times5-20=45-20=25$.

::sa
Find the perimeter (in cm) of a rectangle with length $17$ cm and width $13$ cm.
::key 60
$P=(17+13)\times2=30\times2=60$ (cm).
:::
