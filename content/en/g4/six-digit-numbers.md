:::goals
- Read, write and break down six-digit numbers, for example $345\,678$.
- Know that $1\,000\,000$ is the number after $999\,999$, is read "one million" and has seven digits.
- Name the places and the periods (units period, thousands period) of a number.

A province (made-up numbers) has $345\,678$ people. How do you read the number $345\,678$? Which place is the digit $4$ in, and which period does it belong to?

Six-digit numbers are easy to misread unless you split them into groups of three digits. This lesson shows you how to split them, using places and periods.

## Six-digit numbers

### Let's explore

Count on: $99\,999$ plus $1$ is $100\,000$. The number $100\,000$ is made of $10$ ten-thousands, which is $1$ hundred-thousand. So after the ten-thousands place there is one more place, the hundred-thousands place.

Put the number $345\,678$ into a table of places and periods:

| Thousands period | | | Units period | | |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Hundred-thousands | Ten-thousands | Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones |
| $3$ | $4$ | $5$ | $6$ | $7$ | $8$ |

Read the table: the number $345\,678$ is made of $3$ hundred-thousands, $4$ ten-thousands, $5$ thousands, $6$ hundreds, $7$ tens and $8$ ones. We write:
$$345\,678=300\,000+40\,000+5\,000+600+70+8.$$

You read this number as "three hundred and forty-five thousand, six hundred and seventy-eight". We read the thousands period first (like a three-digit number, then say "thousand"), and then the units period. The digit $4$ is in the ten-thousands place, which belongs to the thousands period.

### The places of a six-digit number

:::theorem Six-digit numbers
A six-digit number has six places. From right to left they are: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten-thousands, hundred-thousands.

Every $10$ units in one place make $1$ unit in the place next to it on the left. The smallest six-digit number is $100\,000$ and the largest is $999\,999$.
:::

Why is the smallest number $100\,000$? Because the digit in the hundred-thousands place cannot be $0$, so the smallest digit there is $1$, and every other place is $0$. The largest is $999\,999$ because every place holds the largest digit.

:::note Reading numbers
Read the thousands period first, then the units period. If the tens digit is $0$ and the ones digit is not $0$, there is no tens word. If the hundreds digit of the units period is $0$, we do not say hundreds: $403\,052$ is "four hundred and three thousand and fifty-two".
:::

We start by reading and writing numbers.

:::example Reading and writing numbers
a) Read the number $450\,302$.
b) Write the number "three hundred and five thousand, two hundred and ten".

**Solution.** a) The thousands period is $450$ and the units period is $302$. We read it as "four hundred and fifty thousand, three hundred and two".

b) "Three hundred and five thousand" is $305\,000$ and "two hundred and ten" is $210$. We write $305\,000+210=305\,210$.

So the number is $305\,210$.
:::

Next, break a number down by places.

:::example Breaking down the number $702\,836$
Write $702\,836$ as a sum. Which place is the digit $7$ in, and what is its value?

**Solution.** The number $702\,836$ has $7$ hundred-thousands, $0$ ten-thousands, $2$ thousands, $8$ hundreds, $3$ tens and $6$ ones. We have:
$$702\,836=700\,000+2\,000+800+30+6.$$
The digit $7$ is in the hundred-thousands place, so its value is $700\,000$.
:::

## The number 1,000,000

The largest six-digit number is $999\,999$. Add $1$ and you get $999\,999+1=1\,000\,000$. The number $1\,000\,000$ is made of $10$ hundred-thousands, which is $1$ million. You read it as "one million".

The number $1\,000\,000$ has seven digits: one digit $1$ and six digits $0$. It is the smallest seven-digit number, and it is the number after $999\,999$.

Find the number before and the number after.

:::example The number before and the number after
Find the number after $999\,999$ and the number before $500\,000$.

**Solution.** The number after is $1$ more than the given number, and the number before is $1$ less. We have:
$$999\,999+1=1\,000\,000,\qquad 500\,000-1=499\,999.$$
So the number after $999\,999$ is $1\,000\,000$ and the number before $500\,000$ is $499\,999$.
:::

You meet one million when you count money.

:::example One million dong
Nam has one $500\,000$ dong note, two $200\,000$ dong notes and one $100\,000$ dong note. Does Nam have $1\,000\,000$ dong?

**Solution.** The money in the two $200\,000$ dong notes is $2\times200\,000=400\,000$ (dong).

Nam's total money is $500\,000+400\,000+100\,000=1\,000\,000$ (dong).

**Answer.** Nam has exactly $1\,000\,000$ dong, which is just enough.
:::

## Places and periods

The digits of a number are split into periods, and each period has three places:

- The **units period** has the ones, tens and hundreds places.
- The **thousands period** has the thousands, ten-thousands and hundred-thousands places.

When you read a number, you read each period from left to right, reading the three digits of the period like a three-digit number and then adding the name of the period (the units period needs no name).

:::example Places and periods of the number $827\,405$
Look at the number $827\,405$. Name the place and the period of each digit. What is the value of the digit $7$?

**Solution.** We make a table:

| Digit | $8$ | $2$ | $7$ | $4$ | $0$ | $5$ |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Place | hundred-thousands | ten-thousands | thousands | hundreds | tens | ones |
| Period | thousands | thousands | thousands | units | units | units |

The digit $7$ is in the thousands place, which belongs to the thousands period, so its value is $7\,000$.
:::

Finally, a problem where you find a number from clues. We work in four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example Finding a number from clues
A number has six digits. The hundred-thousands digit is $4$. The ten-thousands digit is twice the hundred-thousands digit. The thousands digit is the ten-thousands digit minus $5$. The hundreds digit is $0$. The tens digit is the number after the thousands digit. The ones digit is $7$. Write and read the number.

**Understand.** We are given clues for each place, from the hundred-thousands to the ones. We are asked for the number and how to read it.

**Plan.** We find the digit of each place in turn, from left to right, because each clue uses the digit of a place to its left.

**Solve.**

Solution

The hundred-thousands digit is $4$. The ten-thousands digit is $4\times2=8$. The thousands digit is $8-5=3$. The hundreds digit is $0$. The tens digit is the number after $3$, which is $4$. The ones digit is $7$.

The number is $483\,047$, read as four hundred and eighty-three thousand and forty-seven.

Answer: $483\,047$.

**Look back.** We check every clue: $8$ is twice $4$, $3=8-5$, the hundreds digit is $0$, $4$ is the number after $3$, the ones digit is $7$. All are correct.
:::

**Connection.** Splitting a number into periods helps you read big numbers easily. In the next lesson you will meet the millions period and compare numbers with many digits.

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Leaving out a $0$ when writing.** Wrong: "three hundred and five thousand" written as $35\,000$. Why it is wrong: the thousands period is $305$, so the number has six digits. Right: $305\,000$.
- **Mixing up places and periods.** Wrong: the digit $7$ in $827\,405$ belongs to the units period. Why it is wrong: the digit $7$ is in the thousands place, and the thousands place belongs to the thousands period. Right: the digit $7$ belongs to the thousands period.
- **Counting the digits of $1\,000\,000$ wrongly.** Wrong: $1\,000\,000$ has six digits. Why it is wrong: one $0$ was forgotten; the number is made of the digit $1$ and six digits $0$. Right: $1\,000\,000$ has seven digits.
:::

## Summary

- A six-digit number has the places: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten-thousands, hundred-thousands. The smallest is $100\,000$ and the largest is $999\,999$.
- Each period has three places: the units period (ones, tens, hundreds) and the thousands period (thousands, ten-thousands, hundred-thousands). Read each period from left to right.
- Break a number down by places: $345\,678=300\,000+40\,000+5\,000+600+70+8$.
- $999\,999+1=1\,000\,000$, read "one million", with seven digits.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Write the number: eight hundred and twenty thousand, four hundred.

::hint
**What you need:** write the thousands period first, then the units period.

**Steps:**
1. What number is "eight hundred and twenty thousand"?
2. What number is "four hundred"? Put the two periods together.

::solution
"Eight hundred and twenty thousand" is $820\,000$ and "four hundred" is $400$. We have $820\,000+400=820\,400$.

**Answer.** $820\,400$.
:::

:::exercise
Read the number $605\,090$.

::hint
**What you need:** the tens digit of the units period is $9$; the hundreds digit of the units period is $0$.

**Steps:**
1. Read the thousands period: $605$.
2. Read the units period: $090$.

::solution
The number $605\,090$ has thousands period $605$ and units period $090$.

**Answer.** Six hundred and five thousand and ninety.
:::

:::exercise
Write $258\,047$ as a sum.

::hint
**What you need:** each digit has a value that depends on its place.

**Steps:**
1. Make a table of places for $258\,047$.
2. Leave out the place with digit $0$.

::solution
The number $258\,047$ has $2$ hundred-thousands, $5$ ten-thousands, $8$ thousands, $0$ hundreds, $4$ tens and $7$ ones. We have:
$$258\,047=200\,000+50\,000+8\,000+40+7.$$

**Answer.** $200\,000+50\,000+8\,000+40+7$.
:::

:::exercise
In the number $913\,256$, which place and which period do the digits $9$, $3$ and $2$ belong to?

::hint
**What you need:** the thousands period is the first three places from the left; the units period is the last three.

**Steps:**
1. Write the name of the place of each digit.
2. Decide which period that place belongs to.

::solution
The digit $9$ is in the hundred-thousands place, in the thousands period. The digit $3$ is in the thousands place, in the thousands period. The digit $2$ is in the hundreds place, in the units period.

**Answer.** $9$: hundred-thousands place, thousands period; $3$: thousands place, thousands period; $2$: hundreds place, units period.
:::

:::exercise
Find the number before and the number after $400\,000$.

::hint
**What you need:** the number before is $1$ less, the number after is $1$ more.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $400\,000-1$.
2. Work out $400\,000+1$.

::solution
The number before is $400\,000-1=399\,999$. The number after is $400\,000+1=400\,001$.

**Answer.** $399\,999$ and $400\,001$.
:::

:::exercise
Mai has three $200\,000$ dong notes. How many more dong does Mai need to have $1\,000\,000$ dong?

::hint
**What you need:** the money still needed is $1\,000\,000$ minus the money she has.

**Steps:**
1. Work out how much money Mai has.
2. Subtract that from $1\,000\,000$.

::solution
Solution

The money Mai has is:
$$3\times200\,000=600\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
The money Mai still needs is:
$$1\,000\,000-600\,000=400\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$

**Answer.** $400\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
A shop (made-up numbers) has four $200\,000$ dong notes and two $100\,000$ dong notes. Is the total money enough for $1\,000\,000$ dong?

::hint
**What you need:** work out the money in each kind of note, then add.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Work out the money in the $200\,000$ dong notes and in the $100\,000$ dong notes.
3. Compare the total with $1\,000\,000$.

::solution
Solution

The money in the four $200\,000$ dong notes is:
$$4\times200\,000=800\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
The money in the two $100\,000$ dong notes is:
$$2\times100\,000=200\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
The total money is $800\,000+200\,000=1\,000\,000$ (dong).

**Answer.** Exactly $1\,000\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on multiplying a five-digit number by a one-digit number. Work out $25\,000\times4$. How many digits does the result have? Which place and which period is the digit $1$ of the result in?

::hint
**What you need:** multiply from right to left, adding the regrouped number each time.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $25\,000\times4$.
2. Count the digits and find the place and period of the digit $1$.

::solution
We have $25\,000\times4=100\,000$. The result has six digits. The digit $1$ is in the hundred-thousands place, in the thousands period.

**Answer.** $100\,000$; six digits; hundred-thousands place, thousands period.
:::

:::exercise
Lan hears the number "four hundred and three thousand and five" and writes $43\,005$. Check Lan's writing. If it is wrong, correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** the thousands period is written with three digits; the word "and" before a last digit can mean a $0$ in the tens place.

**Steps:**
1. Write the thousands period with three digits.
2. Write the units period with three digits, then put them together.

::solution
The thousands period is "four hundred and three", which is $403$. The units period is "five", which is $005$. The correct number is $403\,005$. The number $43\,005$ has only five digits, so Lan left out a $0$.

**Answer.** Lan is wrong; the correct number is $403\,005$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
How many six-digit numbers are there?

::hint
**What you need:** the smallest six-digit number is $100\,000$ and the largest is $999\,999$.

**Steps:**
1. Recall the smallest and the largest six-digit numbers.
2. The number of whole numbers in a row from $a$ to $b$ is $b-a+1$.

::solution
The six-digit numbers are the numbers from $100\,000$ to $999\,999$. The number of them is:
$$999\,999-100\,000+1=900\,000.$$

**Answer.** $900\,000$ numbers.
:::
