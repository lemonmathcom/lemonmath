:::goals
- Add and subtract numbers within $100\,000$ using written columns.
- Multiply and divide a five-digit number by a one-digit number.
- Check answers and solve word problems with these operations.
:::

In January, a shop sold $23\,456$ exercise books. In February it sold $18\,749$. How many exercise books did the shop sell in the two months altogether?

You have used written columns since Grade 3. This lesson helps you remember all four operations with big numbers.

## Let's explore

"Altogether" means we add. Write the numbers in columns so that digits of the same place line up, then add from right to left:

$$\begin{array}{r} 23\,456\\ +\ 18\,749\\ \hline 42\,205 \end{array}$$

Place by place, from right to left:

- Ones: $6+9=15$, write $5$, regroup $1$.
- Tens: $5+4+1=10$, write $0$, regroup $1$.
- Hundreds: $4+7+1=12$, write $2$, regroup $1$.
- Thousands: $3+8+1=12$, write $2$, regroup $1$.
- Ten thousands: $2+1+1=4$, write $4$.

So the shop sold $42\,205$ exercise books in the two months.

:::theorem Four operations and how to check them
- **Addition and subtraction:** write in columns, work from **right to left**, regroup when needed. Check an addition by subtracting, and check a subtraction by adding.
- **Multiplying by a one-digit number:** multiply each digit from right to left, regrouping when needed.
- **Dividing by a one-digit number:** work from **left to right**, bringing down one digit each time. Check by multiplying.
:::

These checks work because addition and subtraction undo each other, and so do multiplication and division.

## Examples

We start with an addition that regroups many times.

:::example Addition
Work out $35\,628+24\,975$.

**Solution.** Write in columns and add from right to left:

$$\begin{array}{r} 35\,628\\ +\ 24\,975\\ \hline 60\,603 \end{array}$$

The steps: $8+5=13$ (write $3$, regroup $1$); $2+7+1=10$ (write $0$, regroup $1$); $6+9+1=16$ (write $6$, regroup $1$); $5+4+1=10$ (write $0$, regroup $1$); $3+2+1=6$.

So $35\,628+24\,975=60\,603$.
:::

Subtraction is harder when the top number has many zeros.

:::example Subtraction
Work out $70\,000-25\,438$.

**Solution.** In the ones place, $0$ is too small to take away $8$. The nearest place with a non-zero digit is the ten thousands, so we regroup from there, and the places in between become $9$:

$$\begin{array}{r} 70\,000\\ -\ 25\,438\\ \hline 44\,562 \end{array}$$

The steps: $10-8=2$; $9-3=6$; $9-4=5$; $9-5=4$; $6-2=4$.

**Check.** $44\,562+25\,438=70\,000$. Correct.

So $70\,000-25\,438=44\,562$.
:::

Now multiplication.

:::example Multiplication
Work out $12\,405\times6$.

**Solution.** Multiply each digit by $6$, from right to left:

$$\begin{array}{r} 12\,405\\ \times\ 6\\ \hline 74\,430 \end{array}$$

The steps: $5\times6=30$ (write $0$, regroup $3$); $0\times6+3=3$ (write $3$); $4\times6=24$ (write $4$, regroup $2$); $2\times6+2=14$ (write $4$, regroup $1$); $1\times6+1=7$.

So $12\,405\times6=74\,430$.
:::

Division works from left to right.

:::example Division
Work out $48\,372\div6$.

**Solution.** We divide step by step, bringing down one digit each time:

- $48\div6=8$, remainder $0$. Write $8$.
- Bring down $3$: $3\div6=0$, remainder $3$. Write $0$.
- Bring down $7$: $37\div6=6$, remainder $1$. Write $6$.
- Bring down $2$: $12\div6=2$, remainder $0$. Write $2$.

The quotient is $8\,062$ with remainder $0$.

**Check.** $8\,062\times6=48\,372$. Correct.

So $48\,372\div6=8\,062$.
:::

Finally, we use the operations in a word problem.

:::example The rice store
A store has $36\,000$ kg of rice. Each day it sells $4\,250$ kg, for $5$ days. How many kilograms of rice are left?

**Solution.** We work in two steps. First, find the rice sold in $5$ days:
$$4\,250\times5=21\,250\ \text{(kg)}.$$

The rice left is the starting amount minus the rice sold:
$$36\,000-21\,250=14\,750\ \text{(kg)}.$$

So $14\,750$ kg of rice are left.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Columns not lined up.** Wrong: writing $9\,365$ out of line with $25\,480$. Why it is wrong: you add digits from different places. Right: ones under ones, tens under tens, and so on.
- **Forgetting a zero in the quotient.** Wrong: $36\,054\div6=609$. Why it is wrong: when you bring down $0$ and $5$ and cannot divide yet, you must write $0$ in the quotient. Right: $36\,054\div6=6\,009$.
- **Forgetting to regroup.** Wrong: $8+5=13$ and writing $13$ in one place. Why it is wrong: each place holds only one digit. Right: write $3$ and regroup $1$ to the next place.
:::

## Summary

- Add and subtract: write in columns, work from right to left, regroup when needed.
- Multiply by a one-digit number: multiply each digit from right to left, regrouping when needed.
- Divide by a one-digit number: work from left to right, bringing down one digit each step; write $0$ in the quotient when you cannot divide yet.
- Check: addition and subtraction undo each other; multiplication and division undo each other.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out $46\,285+27\,639$.

::hint
**What you need:** write in columns, add from right to left, regroup when needed.

**Steps:**
1. Add the ones: $5+9$.
2. Regroup $1$ to the tens and carry on.

::solution
$5+9=14$ (write $4$, regroup $1$); $8+3+1=12$ (write $2$, regroup $1$); $2+6+1=9$; $6+7=13$ (write $3$, regroup $1$); $4+2+1=7$. The result is $73\,924$.

**Answer.** $73\,924$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $80\,000-36\,457$ and check your answer.

::hint
**What you need:** check a subtraction by adding: difference plus the number taken away gives the starting number.

**Steps:**
1. Regroup from the ten thousands; the places in between become $9$.
2. Add the difference and $36\,457$ to check.

::solution
$10-7=3$; $9-5=4$; $9-4=5$; $9-6=3$; $7-3=4$. The result is $43\,543$.

**Check.** $43\,543+36\,457=80\,000$.

**Answer.** $43\,543$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $14\,308\times5$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply each digit from right to left, regrouping when needed.

**Steps:**
1. $8\times5=40$, write $0$ and regroup $4$.
2. Carry on with the other places.

::solution
$8\times5=40$ (write $0$, regroup $4$); $0\times5+4=4$; $3\times5=15$ (write $5$, regroup $1$); $4\times5+1=21$ (write $1$, regroup $2$); $1\times5+2=7$. The result is $71\,540$.

**Answer.** $71\,540$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $72\,168\div8$ and check your answer.

::hint
**What you need:** divide from left to right, bringing down one digit each step; check by multiplying.

**Steps:**
1. $72\div8=9$.
2. Bring down the other digits one by one.

::solution
$72\div8=9$ remainder $0$. Bring down $1$: $1\div8=0$ remainder $1$. Bring down $6$: $16\div8=2$ remainder $0$. Bring down $8$: $8\div8=1$. The quotient is $9\,021$.

**Check.** $9\,021\times8=72\,168$.

**Answer.** $9\,021$.
:::

:::exercise
$18\,000$ kg of maize is shared equally between $6$ lorries. How many kilograms of maize does each lorry carry? Why do you use division?

::hint
**What you need:** "shared equally" means divide.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? The total maize and the number of lorries. What is asked? The maize for each lorry.
2. Work out $18\,000\div6$ and write the answer with its unit.

::solution
Shared equally means each lorry carries the same amount, so we divide the total maize by the number of lorries:
$$18\,000\div6=3\,000\ \text{(kg)}.$$

**Check.** $3\,000\times6=18\,000$.

**Answer.** $3\,000$ kg of maize.
:::

:::exercise
Nam works out $36\,054\div6=609$. Where did Nam go wrong? Work it out again.

::hint
**What you need:** check by multiplying: quotient times divisor must equal the dividend.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $609\times6$ and compare with $36\,054$.
2. Divide again, and write $0$ when you cannot divide yet.

::solution
$609\times6=3\,654\ne36\,054$, so Nam is wrong. Dividing again: $36\div6=6$. Bring down $0$: $0\div6=0$. Bring down $5$: $5\div6=0$ remainder $5$. Bring down $4$: $54\div6=9$. The quotient is $6\,009$.

**Answer.** Nam forgot a zero; the correct answer is $36\,054\div6=6\,009$.
:::

:::exercise
On the first day a shop sells $15\,360$ kg of rice. On the second day it sells $2\,840$ kg more than on the first day. How many kilograms of rice does the shop sell in the two days?

::hint
**What you need:** "more than" means add; "in the two days" means add.

**Steps:**
1. Find the rice sold on the second day.
2. Add the rice from both days.

::solution
Second day: $15\,360+2\,840=18\,200$ (kg). Both days: $15\,360+18\,200=33\,560$ (kg).

**Answer.** $33\,560$ kg of rice.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
An order needs $90\,000$ bricks. Four lorries have already carried $12\,500$ bricks each. The bricks still needed are carried equally over $8$ days. How many bricks must be carried each day?

::hint
**What you need:** work in steps: bricks already carried, bricks still needed, then share equally.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $12\,500\times4$.
2. Subtract the result from $90\,000$.
3. Divide by $8$.

::solution
Already carried: $12\,500\times4=50\,000$ (bricks). Still needed: $90\,000-50\,000=40\,000$ (bricks). Each day: $40\,000\div8=5\,000$ (bricks).

**Check.** $5\,000\times8=40\,000$.

**Answer.** $5\,000$ bricks.
:::
