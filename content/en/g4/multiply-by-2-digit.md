:::goals
- Set out and multiply by a two-digit number, using two partial products.
- Understand why the second partial product is written one column to the left.
- Multiply by a multiple of ten and by numbers with a digit 0 in a factor.
- Estimate to check an answer and solve word problems.
:::

A bakery makes $235$ cakes every day. It works for $24$ days in a row, making the same number of cakes each day.

How many cakes does the bakery make altogether in $24$ days? The calculation $235\times24$ is much harder to do in your head than multiplying by a one-digit number. This lesson shows you how to break the multiplication into smaller steps.

## Let's explore

Split $24=20+4$ and use the distributive property:
$$\begin{aligned} 235\times24 &= 235\times(20+4) \\ &= 235\times20+235\times4 \\ &= 4\,700+940 \\ &= 5\,640. \end{aligned}$$

Notice: $235\times4=940$ is the product with the ones digit of $24$, and $235\times20=4\,700$ is the product with the tens digit. When you set it out in columns, you write these two products on two lines and then add them:

$$\begin{array}{r} 235 \\ \times\ 24 \\ \hline 940 \\ 470\phantom{0} \\ \hline 5640 \end{array}$$

The first line is $235\times4=940$, called the **first partial product**. The second line is $235\times2$ (tens) $=470$ tens, called the **second partial product**, and it is written one column to the left, because $470$ tens is $4\,700$. Add the two partial products: $940+4\,700=5\,640$.

So the bakery makes $5\,640$ cakes.

:::theorem Multiplying by a two-digit number
To multiply a number by a two-digit number:
1. Set it out: write the second factor under the first, with digits of the same place in the same column.
2. Multiply the first factor by the ones digit to get the **first partial product**.
3. Multiply the first factor by the tens digit to get the **second partial product**. Write it **one column to the left**.
4. Add the two partial products.
:::

Why do we write it one column to the left? The tens digit of $24$ is $2$ tens, so the second partial product is really $235\times20=4\,700$. We leave off the zero at the end and shift one column, just like multiplying by $10$ in the last lesson.

**Connection.** This method uses the distributive property of multiplication over addition and the rule for multiplying by $10$, $100$, $1\,000$. You will use both again in the lesson on dividing by a two-digit number.

## Examples

We start with small factors.

:::example Multiplying two two-digit numbers
Set out and work out $34\times12$.

**Solution.** We set it out, multiply by the digit $2$ first, then by the digit $1$ (tens) and shift one column:
$$\begin{array}{r} 34 \\ \times\ 12 \\ \hline 68 \\ 34\phantom{0} \\ \hline 408 \end{array}$$
$34\times2=68$. $34\times1$ ten $=34$ tens. Add: $68+340=408$.

So $34\times12=408$.
:::

Now carrying in both partial products.

:::example Multiplying with carrying
Set out and work out $56\times23$.

**Solution.** We set it out:
$$\begin{array}{r} 56 \\ \times\ 23 \\ \hline 168 \\ 112\phantom{0} \\ \hline 1288 \end{array}$$
First partial product: $56\times3=168$. Second partial product: $56\times2=112$, written one column to the left (that is, $1\,120$). Add: $168+1\,120=1\,288$.

So $56\times23=1\,288$.
:::

Next, a three-digit first factor.

:::example Multiplying a three-digit number
Set out and work out $145\times36$.

**Solution.** We set it out:
$$\begin{array}{r} 145 \\ \times\ 36 \\ \hline 870 \\ 435\phantom{0} \\ \hline 5220 \end{array}$$
First partial product: $145\times6=870$. Second partial product: $145\times3=435$, written one column to the left (that is, $4\,350$). Add: $870+4\,350=5\,220$.

So $145\times36=5\,220$.
:::

When a factor has a digit 0, you can work more neatly.

:::example A factor with a digit 0
a) Work out $214\times30$.

b) Set out and work out $1\,206\times45$.

**Solution.** a) We have $30=3\times10$, so $214\times30=(214\times3)\times10=642\times10=6\,420$.

b) We set it out:
$$\begin{array}{r} 1206 \\ \times\ \ 45 \\ \hline 6030 \\ 4824\phantom{0} \\ \hline 54270 \end{array}$$
First partial product: $1\,206\times5=6\,030$. Second partial product: $1\,206\times4=4\,824$, written one column to the left (that is, $48\,240$). Add: $6\,030+48\,240=54\,270$.

So $214\times30=6\,420$ and $1\,206\times45=54\,270$.
:::

Now back to the bakery.

:::example The bakery
A bakery makes $235$ cakes every day. How many cakes does it make in $24$ days?

**Understand.** $235$ cakes each day, for $24$ days. We need the total number of cakes.

**Plan.** The number of cakes per day times the number of days: $235\times24$.

**Solve.** The number of cakes the bakery makes in $24$ days is $235\times24=5\,640$ (cakes).

**Answer:** $5\,640$ cakes.

**Look back.** Is the answer sensible? Estimate by rounding: $235$ is close to $200$ and $24$ is close to $25$, and $200\times25=5\,000$. The answer $5\,640$ is close to $5\,000$, so it is sensible. Why is the correct answer a little more than $5\,000$?
:::

Finally, a problem with money.

:::example Admission tickets
Suppose a museum ticket costs $35\,000$ dong per person. Class $4$A has $26$ people going on the visit. How much does the class pay for tickets?

**Solution.** The cost of the tickets for class $4$A is $26\times35\,000$.

We work out $26\times35=910$ and then multiply by $1\,000$:
$$26\times35\,000=910\times1\,000=910\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$

**Answer:** $910\,000$ dong.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Not shifting the second partial product.** Wrong: $235\times24=940+470=1\,410$. Why it is wrong: the second partial product is $235\times20=4\,700$, not $470$; writing it in the same columns treats $2$ tens as $2$ ones. Right: $235\times24=5\,640$.
- **Forgetting to carry.** Wrong: $56\times23=1\,278$. Why it is wrong: $6\times3=18$, write $8$ and carry $1$; forgetting the carry gives $158$ instead of $168$. Right: $56\times23=1\,288$.
- **Forgetting to multiply by $10$ for a multiple of ten.** Wrong: $214\times30=642$. Why it is wrong: $642$ is only $214\times3$, and $30$ is $10$ times $3$. Right: $214\times30=6\,420$.
:::

## Summary

- To multiply by a two-digit number: the ones digit gives the first partial product, the tens digit gives the second partial product (written one column to the left), then add.
- The second partial product is shifted one column because it is a product with tens: $235\times20=4\,700$.
- To multiply by a multiple of ten such as $30$: multiply by $3$, then by $10$.
- Estimate by rounding to check that the answer is sensible.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Set out in columns and work out: $43\times21$.

::hint
**What you need:** two partial products; the second one is shifted one column to the left.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply $43$ by $1$.
2. Multiply $43$ by $2$ (tens), shift one column and add.

::solution
$$\begin{array}{r} 43 \\ \times\ 21 \\ \hline 43 \\ 86\phantom{0} \\ \hline 903 \end{array}$$
$43\times1=43$ and $43\times20=860$. Add: $43+860=903$.

**Answer.** $903$.
:::

:::exercise
Set out in columns and work out: $78\times34$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply by the digit $4$ first, then by the digit $3$ (tens).

**Steps:**
1. $78\times4=?$ (with carrying).
2. $78\times3=?$, shift one column and add.

::solution
$$\begin{array}{r} 78 \\ \times\ 34 \\ \hline 312 \\ 234\phantom{0} \\ \hline 2652 \end{array}$$
$78\times4=312$ and $78\times30=2\,340$. Add: $312+2\,340=2\,652$.

**Answer.** $2\,652$.
:::

:::exercise
Set out in columns and work out: $256\times18$.

::hint
**What you need:** the first factor has three digits; there are still two partial products.

**Steps:**
1. $256\times8=?$
2. $256\times1=?$, shift one column and add.

::solution
$$\begin{array}{r} 256 \\ \times\ 18 \\ \hline 2048 \\ 256\phantom{0} \\ \hline 4608 \end{array}$$
$256\times8=2\,048$ and $256\times10=2\,560$. Add: $2\,048+2\,560=4\,608$.

**Answer.** $4\,608$.
:::

:::exercise
Set out in columns and work out: $1\,305\times23$.

::hint
**What you need:** a digit 0 in a factor is multiplied as usual: $0\times3=0$.

**Steps:**
1. $1\,305\times3=?$
2. $1\,305\times2=?$, shift one column and add.

::solution
$$\begin{array}{r} 1305 \\ \times\ \ 23 \\ \hline 3915 \\ 2610\phantom{0} \\ \hline 30015 \end{array}$$
$1\,305\times3=3\,915$ and $1\,305\times20=26\,100$. Add: $3\,915+26\,100=30\,015$.

**Answer.** $30\,015$.
:::

:::exercise
A shop sells $125$ loaves of bread every day. How many loaves does the shop sell in $30$ days?

::hint
**What you need:** to multiply by $30$, multiply by $3$ and then by $10$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Work out $125\times3$, then write one more zero.

::solution
The number of loaves sold in $30$ days is:
$$125\times30=(125\times3)\times10=375\times10=3\,750\ \text{(loaves)}.$$

**Answer.** $3\,750$ loaves.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a shirt costs $85\,000$ dong. How much must be paid for $12$ such shirts?

::hint
**What you need:** $85\,000=85\times1\,000$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $85\times12$.
2. Multiply the result by $1\,000$.

::solution
We have $85\times12=1\,020$.

The amount to pay is $1\,020\times1\,000=1\,020\,000$ (dong).

**Answer.** $1\,020\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on the distributive property. Calculate in the easy way: $36\times25+36\times75$.

::hint
**What you need:** $a\times b+a\times c=a\times(b+c)$.

**Steps:**
1. Which factor is common to both products?
2. What is $25+75$?

::solution
$$36\times25+36\times75=36\times(25+75)=36\times100=3\,600.$$

**Answer.** $3\,600$.
:::

:::exercise
A rectangular plot of land is $124$ m long and $35$ m wide. Find the area of the plot.

::hint
**What you need:** area of a rectangle $=$ length $\times$ width.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Work out $124\times35$ and remember the unit, square metres.

::solution
The area of the plot is:
$$124\times35=4\,340\ (\text{m}^2).$$

**Check.** Estimate $120\times35=4\,200$, which is close to $4\,340$. Sensible.

**Answer.** $4\,340\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
Estimate by rounding, then work out $198\times52$ and compare the two results.

::hint
**What you need:** round $198$ to $200$ and $52$ to $50$.

**Steps:**
1. Estimate: $200\times50=?$
2. Set out $198\times52$ and compare.

::solution
Estimate: $200\times50=10\,000$.

Exact calculation:
$$\begin{array}{r} 198 \\ \times\ 52 \\ \hline 396 \\ 990\phantom{0} \\ \hline 10296 \end{array}$$
The exact result $10\,296$ is close to the estimate $10\,000$.

**Answer.** $198\times52=10\,296$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
When multiplying a number by $23$ in columns, Hoa wrote the second partial product without shifting it to the left, and got $2\,060$. Find the number and the correct result of the multiplication.

::hint
**What you need:** without the shift, Hoa added the number times $3$ and the number times $2$.

**Steps:**
1. Call the number $a$. How many times $a$ is the wrong result?
2. Find $a$, then work out $a\times23$ again.

::solution
The wrong result is $a\times3+a\times2=a\times5=2\,060$, so $a=2\,060\div5=412$.

The correct result is $412\times23=412\times3+412\times20=1\,236+8\,240=9\,476$.

**Answer.** The number is $412$; the correct result is $9\,476$.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
$56\times23$ equals
A. $280$.
B. $1\,278$.
C. $1\,120$.
D. $1\,288$.
::key D
$56\times3=168$ and $56\times20=1\,120$, so $168+1\,120=1\,288$. Option B forgets to carry, option C is only the second partial product, and option A does not shift the second partial product ($168+112$).

::mc
$214\times30$ equals
A. $6\,420$.
B. $64\,200$.
C. $6\,042$.
D. $642$.
::key A
$214\times30=(214\times3)\times10=642\times10=6\,420$. Option D forgets to multiply by $10$ and option B adds two zeros.

::mc
In the multiplication $235\times24$, the value of $235\times20$ is
A. $470$.
B. $4\,700$.
C. $940$.
D. $47\,000$.
::key B
$235\times2=470$, so $235\times20=4\,700$. Option C is $235\times4$.

::mc
Each box holds $36$ pens. $25$ such boxes hold
A. $61$ pens.
B. $180$ pens.
C. $900$ pens.
D. $9\,000$ pens.
::key C
$36\times25=180+720=900$ (pens). Option A is an addition and option B is $36\times5$.

::tf
Look at the multiplication $42\times35$.
a) The first partial product is $42\times5=210$.
b) $42\times30=1\,260$.
c) $42\times35=210+126=336$.
d) $42\times35$ is greater than $1\,500$.
::key T T F F
a) True: $42\times5=210$. b) True: $42\times3=126$, so $42\times30=1\,260$. c) False: the second partial product must be $1\,260$; $42\times35=210+1\,260=1\,470$. d) False: $1\,470<1\,500$.

::sa
Work out $25\times16$.
::key 400
$25\times6=150$ and $25\times10=250$. So $25\times16=150+250=400$.

::sa
A rectangular yard is $45$ m long and $22$ m wide. What is the area of the yard in square metres?
::key 990
$45\times22=90+900=990$ ($\text{m}^2$).
:::
