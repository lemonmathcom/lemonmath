At $3$ o'clock, the two hands of a clock make a right angle. At $2$ o'clock, do the two hands make an angle bigger or smaller than a right angle? How many degrees is that angle?

To answer, you need to know how to measure an angle and what the different kinds of angle are called. This lesson teaches you both.

## Measuring angles and the degree

### Let's explore

Two arms $BA$ and $BC$ that start from the same point $B$ make an angle. The point $B$ is the vertex of the angle, and $BA$ and $BC$ are its two arms. We call this angle angle $\angle ABC$.

To measure an angle, you use a protractor:

- Put the centre of the protractor on the vertex $B$.
- Line up the $0$ mark of the protractor with the arm $BA$.
- See which mark the arm $BC$ passes through: here it is the mark $50$.

We say the size of angle $\angle ABC$ is $50$ degrees, and we write $\angle ABC=50^\circ$.

The unit for measuring angles is the degree, written $^\circ$. One full turn is $360^\circ$. A clock face has $12$ numbers, so two numbers next to each other are $360^\circ\div12=30^\circ$ apart.

### How to measure an angle

:::theorem Measuring an angle with a protractor
To measure an angle with a protractor:
1. Put the centre of the protractor on the vertex of the angle.
2. Line up the $0$ mark of the protractor with one arm of the angle.
3. Read the number at the mark that the other arm passes through. That number is the size of the angle in degrees.

A right angle measures $90^\circ$.
:::

Why do you only need to read one number? Because the $0$ mark is exactly on the first arm, so the number at the second arm tells you how many degrees that arm has turned from the first arm.

We start by reading the size of an angle.

:::example Reading the size of an angle
How many degrees is angle $\angle ABC$ in the picture above? Is it smaller or bigger than a right angle?

**Solution.** The arm $BC$ passes through the mark $50$ of the protractor, so $\angle ABC=50^\circ$. We have $50^\circ<90^\circ$, so angle $\angle ABC$ is smaller than a right angle.
:::

You also need to know how to draw an angle of a given size.

:::example Drawing an angle of $70^\circ$
Draw an angle $\angle ABC$ of size $70^\circ$.

**Solution.** We do the following:
1. Draw the arm $BA$.
2. Put the centre of the protractor on $B$ and the $0$ mark on $BA$.
3. Mark a point $C$ at the mark $70$.
4. Join $B$ to $C$.

We get the angle $\angle ABC=70^\circ$ as in the picture below.
:::

:::theorem Kinds of angle
- An acute angle measures less than $90^\circ$.
- A right angle measures exactly $90^\circ$.
- An obtuse angle measures more than $90^\circ$ and less than $180^\circ$.
- A straight angle measures exactly $180^\circ$. The two arms of a straight angle make a straight line.
:::

Why is a straight angle $180^\circ$? Because the two arms of a straight angle lie on a straight line, and it is half a turn, while a full turn is $360^\circ$. We have $360^\circ\div2=180^\circ$.

We sort angles by their size.

:::example Sorting angles
The sizes of some angles are $35^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $120^\circ$, $180^\circ$, $75^\circ$, $150^\circ$. Say what kind each angle is.

**Solution.** We compare each size with $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$:
- $35^\circ$ and $75^\circ$ are less than $90^\circ$, so they are acute angles.
- $90^\circ$ is a right angle.
- $120^\circ$ and $150^\circ$ are more than $90^\circ$ and less than $180^\circ$, so they are obtuse angles.
- $180^\circ$ is a straight angle.
:::

You can use this to find the angle between the hands of a clock.

:::example The angle between the hands of a clock
At $2$ o'clock and at $5$ o'clock, how many degrees is the angle between the two hands of a clock? What kind of angle is it?

**Solution.** Each hour on a clock face is $30^\circ$. At $2$ o'clock, the angle between the hands is $2\times30^\circ=60^\circ$, which is an acute angle. At $5$ o'clock, the angle between the hands is $5\times30^\circ=150^\circ$, which is an obtuse angle.
:::

Finally, a problem that needs adding angles. We work in four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example Opening a door
A cupboard door (made-up numbers) is opened through an angle of $40^\circ$, then opened a further $55^\circ$. Through what angle is the door now open? What kind of angle is it?

**Understand.** We are given the first opening of $40^\circ$ and the further opening of $55^\circ$. We are asked for the total opening and the kind of angle.

**Plan.** When two angles are joined side by side, their sizes add. Then we compare the result with $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$.

**Solve.**

Solution

The angle through which the door is open is:
$$40^\circ+55^\circ=95^\circ.$$
Since $90^\circ<95^\circ<180^\circ$, it is an obtuse angle.

Answer: $95^\circ$, an obtuse angle.

**Look back.** Check with subtraction: $95^\circ-55^\circ=40^\circ$. Correct. If the door were opened a further $45^\circ$ instead of $55^\circ$, the angle would be $40^\circ+45^\circ=85^\circ$, an acute angle: a change of just $10^\circ$ changes the kind of angle.
:::

**Connection.** You reuse what you learned about clocks: each hour is $30^\circ$, so the hands of a clock at $3$ o'clock, $6$ o'clock and $9$ o'clock make a right angle, a straight angle and a right angle. In later years you will use angles to draw and study shapes such as triangles and rhombuses.

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Reading the wrong row of numbers on the protractor.** Wrong: reading the angle in the first picture as $130^\circ$. Why it is wrong: a protractor has two rows of numbers, and the row that starts from $0$ at the other arm was read. Right: choose the row whose $0$ mark lines up with the arm $BA$, which gives $50^\circ$.
- **Calling a right angle an obtuse angle.** Wrong: $90^\circ$ is an obtuse angle. Why it is wrong: an obtuse angle must be more than $90^\circ$. Right: $90^\circ$ is a right angle.
- **Forgetting the degree sign.** Wrong: $\angle ABC=50$. Right: $\angle ABC=50^\circ$.
:::

## Summary

- An angle has one vertex and two arms. To measure an angle with a protractor: centre on the vertex, $0$ mark on one arm, read the number at the other arm. Angles are measured in degrees ($^\circ$).
- An acute angle is less than $90^\circ$; a right angle is $90^\circ$; an obtuse angle is more than $90^\circ$ and less than $180^\circ$; a straight angle is $180^\circ$.
- One full turn is $360^\circ$; each hour on a clock is $30^\circ$.
- Joining two angles side by side adds their sizes; splitting an angle subtracts.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
An angle measures $65^\circ$. What kind of angle is it?

::hint
**What you need:** compare the size of the angle with $90^\circ$.

**Steps:**
1. Is $65^\circ$ less than or more than $90^\circ$?
2. Name the angle using the table of kinds of angle.

::solution
We have $65^\circ<90^\circ$, so it is an acute angle.

**Answer.** An acute angle.
:::

:::exercise
Some angles measure $100^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $30^\circ$, $180^\circ$. Say what kind each angle is.

::hint
**What you need:** an acute angle is less than $90^\circ$; an obtuse angle is more than $90^\circ$ and less than $180^\circ$.

**Steps:**
1. Compare each size with $90^\circ$.
2. A size equal to $90^\circ$ or $180^\circ$ has its own name.

::solution
$100^\circ$ is an obtuse angle, $90^\circ$ is a right angle, $30^\circ$ is an acute angle, $180^\circ$ is a straight angle.

**Answer.** Obtuse, right, acute, straight.
:::

:::exercise
At $4$ o'clock, how many degrees is the angle between the two hands of a clock? What kind of angle is it?

::hint
**What you need:** each hour on a clock face is $30^\circ$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $4\times30^\circ$.
2. Compare the result with $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$.

::solution
We have $4\times30^\circ=120^\circ$. Since $90^\circ<120^\circ<180^\circ$, it is an obtuse angle.

**Answer.** $120^\circ$, an obtuse angle.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the gap: $125^\circ\ \ldots\ 90^\circ+35^\circ$.

::hint
**What you need:** work out the right-hand side, then compare.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $90^\circ+35^\circ$.
2. Compare the two sizes.

::solution
We have $90^\circ+35^\circ=125^\circ$. The two sizes are equal.

**Answer.** The sign $=$.
:::

:::exercise
Put the angles of size $150^\circ$, $60^\circ$, $100^\circ$, $25^\circ$ in order from smallest to largest.

::hint
**What you need:** compare the sizes as you compare whole numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Find the smallest size.
2. Find the larger sizes one by one.

::solution
We have $25^\circ<60^\circ<100^\circ<150^\circ$.

**Answer.** $25^\circ$; $60^\circ$; $100^\circ$; $150^\circ$.
:::

:::exercise
A gate (made-up numbers) is opened through an angle of $35^\circ$, then opened a further $50^\circ$. Through what angle is the gate now open? What kind of angle is it?

::hint
**What you need:** joining two angles side by side adds their sizes.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Add the two sizes.
3. Compare the result with $90^\circ$.

::solution
Solution

The angle through which the gate is open is:
$$35^\circ+50^\circ=85^\circ.$$
Since $85^\circ<90^\circ$, it is an acute angle.

**Answer.** $85^\circ$, an acute angle.
:::

:::exercise
A straight angle is split into two angles. One angle measures $65^\circ$. How many degrees is the other angle? What kind of angle is it?

::hint
**What you need:** a straight angle is $180^\circ$; splitting an angle means subtracting.

**Steps:**
1. Write the size of the straight angle.
2. Subtract $65^\circ$ from it.

::solution
Solution

The size of the other angle is:
$$180^\circ-65^\circ=115^\circ.$$
Since $90^\circ<115^\circ<180^\circ$, it is an obtuse angle.

**Answer.** $115^\circ$, an obtuse angle.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on expressions with letters. At $n$ o'clock ($n$ from $1$ to $6$), the two hands of a clock make an angle of $n\times30^\circ$. Find the value of the expression when $n=3$ and when $n=5$.

::hint
**What you need:** substitute the number for the letter, then work it out.

**Steps:**
1. Substitute $n=3$ into the expression.
2. Substitute $n=5$ into the expression.

::solution
When $n=3$, $n\times30^\circ=3\times30^\circ=90^\circ$. When $n=5$, $n\times30^\circ=5\times30^\circ=150^\circ$.

**Answer.** $90^\circ$ and $150^\circ$.
:::

:::exercise
Nam says: "An angle of $120^\circ$ is an acute angle because $120$ is a big number." Check Nam's statement. If it is wrong, correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** an acute angle is less than $90^\circ$.

**Steps:**
1. Compare $120^\circ$ with $90^\circ$.
2. Name the angle correctly.

::solution
We have $120^\circ>90^\circ$, so an angle of $120^\circ$ is not acute. Since $120^\circ<180^\circ$, it is an obtuse angle.

**Answer.** Nam is wrong; an angle of $120^\circ$ is an obtuse angle.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Look at the whole hours from $1$ o'clock to $6$ o'clock. At which times do the two hands of a clock make an acute angle? At which times do they make an obtuse angle?

::hint
**What you need:** at $n$ o'clock, the two hands make an angle of $n\times30^\circ$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the size of the angle at $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, $5$, $6$ o'clock.
2. Compare each size with $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$.

::solution
The sizes of the angle at $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, $5$, $6$ o'clock are $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $120^\circ$, $150^\circ$, $180^\circ$. The angles $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$ are less than $90^\circ$; the angles $120^\circ$ and $150^\circ$ lie between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$.

**Answer.** An acute angle at $1$ o'clock and $2$ o'clock; an obtuse angle at $4$ o'clock and $5$ o'clock.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
Which of these sizes is the size of an obtuse angle?
A. $45^\circ$.
B. $90^\circ$.
C. $135^\circ$.
D. $180^\circ$.
::key C
An obtuse angle is more than $90^\circ$ and less than $180^\circ$, so only $135^\circ$ fits. $90^\circ$ is a right angle, $45^\circ$ is an acute angle and $180^\circ$ is a straight angle.

::mc
At $5$ o'clock, the two hands of a clock make an angle of
A. $120^\circ$.
B. $90^\circ$.
C. $60^\circ$.
D. $150^\circ$.
::key D
Each hour is $30^\circ$, so at $5$ o'clock the angle is $5\times30^\circ=150^\circ$. Option A is $4$ o'clock, option C is $2$ o'clock and option B is $3$ o'clock.

::mc
A door is opened through an angle of $45^\circ$, then opened a further $50^\circ$. The angle through which the door is now open is
A. $95^\circ$, an obtuse angle.
B. $90^\circ$.
C. $95^\circ$, an acute angle.
D. $5^\circ$.
::key A
$45^\circ+50^\circ=95^\circ$. Since $95^\circ>90^\circ$, it is an obtuse angle. Option D is the subtraction, option B is a wrong calculation, and option C names the wrong kind of angle.

::mc
A straight angle is split into two angles, and one of them measures $75^\circ$. The other angle measures
A. $15^\circ$.
B. $105^\circ$.
C. $255^\circ$.
D. $115^\circ$.
::key B
$180^\circ-75^\circ=105^\circ$. Option A takes $90^\circ-75^\circ$, option C takes $180^\circ+75^\circ$, and option D is a wrong subtraction.

Look at these statements.

b) An angle of $120^\circ$ is greater than a right angle.

$180^\circ-72^\circ=108^\circ$.
