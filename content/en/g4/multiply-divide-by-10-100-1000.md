:::goals
- Multiply a number by $10$, $100$ and $1\,000$ in your head.
- Divide multiples of ten, a hundred or a thousand by $10$, $100$ and $1\,000$ in your head.
- Change units of mass and length by multiplying or dividing by $10$, $100$ or $1\,000$.
- Multiply and divide in your head by multiples of ten and a hundred.
:::

A store room has $7$ tonnes of rice. The rice is packed into sacks, each sack holding $100$ kg. Remember that $1$ tonne is $1\,000$ kg.

How many kilograms of rice are in the store room? How many sacks can be filled? Both questions only need multiplying or dividing by $10$, $100$ or $1\,000$, and you can answer them in your head.

## Let's explore

Multiply $35$ by $10$, $100$ and $1\,000$ in turn:

- $35\times10$: $35$ tens is $350$.
- $35\times100$: $35$ hundreds is $3\,500$.
- $35\times1\,000$: $35$ thousands is $35\,000$.

Notice: multiplying by $10$ adds $1$ zero on the right, multiplying by $100$ adds $2$ zeros, and multiplying by $1\,000$ adds $3$ zeros.

Now go backwards. Since $35\times100=3\,500$, we have $3\,500\div100=35$. In the same way $350\div10=35$ and $35\,000\div1\,000=35$. Division here removes zeros.

:::theorem Multiplying and dividing by 10, 100, 1,000, ...
- To **multiply** a number by $10$, $100$, $1\,000$, ... we write one, two, three, ... zeros on the right of the number.
- To **divide** a multiple of ten, a hundred, a thousand, ... by $10$, $100$, $1\,000$, ... we remove one, two, three, ... zeros from the right of the dividend.
:::

Why does this work? Each digit of $35$ moves to a higher place: multiplying by $10$ moves every digit up one place, and multiplying by $100$ moves it up two places. The ones and tens places are left empty, so we fill them with zeros. Division is the reverse: every digit moves down one, two or three places.

**Connection.** You can already multiply by a one-digit number. From this rule you will multiply by a multiple of ten such as $30$ by multiplying by $3$ and then by $10$. That prepares you for multiplying by a two-digit number.

## Examples

We start with multiplication.

:::example Multiplying in your head
Work out in your head: $47\times10$; $47\times100$; $47\times1\,000$.

**Solution.** We write extra zeros on the right of $47$:
- $47\times10=470$ (add $1$ zero).
- $47\times100=4\,700$ (add $2$ zeros).
- $47\times1\,000=47\,000$ (add $3$ zeros).

So the results are $470$, $4\,700$ and $47\,000$.
:::

Next, division.

:::example Dividing in your head
Work out in your head: $8\,000\div10$; $8\,000\div100$; $8\,000\div1\,000$.

**Solution.** We remove zeros from the right of $8\,000$:
- $8\,000\div10=800$ (remove $1$ zero).
- $8\,000\div100=80$ (remove $2$ zeros).
- $8\,000\div1\,000=8$ (remove $3$ zeros).

So the results are $800$, $80$ and $8$.
:::

Now we use the rule to change units. In Vietnam the unit **tạ** is also used: $1$ tạ is $100$ kg.

:::example Changing units
Fill in the missing numbers.
a) $5$ tonnes $=\ ?$ kg
b) $3\,400$ kg $=\ ?$ tạ

**Solution.** We have $1$ tonne $=1\,000$ kg and $1$ tạ $=100$ kg.

a) $5$ tonnes $=5\times1\,000=5\,000$ kg.

b) $3\,400$ kg $=3\,400\div100=34$ tạ.

So $5$ tonnes $=5\,000$ kg and $3\,400$ kg $=34$ tạ.
:::

We extend the idea to multiples of ten.

:::example Multiplying by a multiple of ten
Work out $45\times30$ in your head.

**Solution.** We have $30=3\times10$. Multiply $45$ by $3$ and then by $10$:
$$\begin{aligned} 45\times30 &= (45\times3)\times10 \\ &= 135\times10 \\ &= 1\,350. \end{aligned}$$
So $45\times30=1\,350$.
:::

What about division?

:::example Dividing by a multiple of a hundred
Work out $7\,200\div400$ in your head.

**Solution.** Dividing both the dividend and the divisor by $100$ (removing two zeros from each) does not change the quotient:
$$7\,200\div400=72\div4=18.$$
So $7\,200\div400=18$.
:::

Finally, back to the store room.

:::example The rice store room
A store room has $7$ tonnes of rice, packed into sacks each holding $100$ kg. How many kilograms of rice are in the store room, and how many sacks can be filled?

**Understand.** There are $7$ tonnes of rice, each sack holds $100$ kg, and $1$ tonne $=1\,000$ kg. We need the kilograms of rice and the number of sacks.

**Plan.** Change $7$ tonnes to kilograms by multiplying by $1\,000$, then divide by $100$ to find the number of sacks.

**Solve.** The kilograms of rice in the store room are $7\times1\,000=7\,000$ (kg).

The number of sacks filled is $7\,000\div100=70$ (sacks).

**Answer:** $7\,000$ kg of rice; $70$ sacks.

**Look back.** Check: $70\times100=7\,000$. Correct. If each sack held only $50$ kg, would the number of sacks go up or down? Let's try: $7\,000\div50=700\div5=140$ (sacks).
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Adding too few zeros.** Wrong: $35\times100=350$. Why it is wrong: multiplying by $100$ adds two zeros, but $350$ has only one zero added. Right: $35\times100=3\,500$.
- **Removing too many zeros.** Wrong: $4\,000\div100=4$. Why it is wrong: dividing by $100$ removes only two zeros, not all three. Right: $4\,000\div100=40$.
- **Removing more zeros than the number has.** Wrong: $4\,250\div100=425$. Why it is wrong: $4\,250$ has only one zero at the end, so it divides exactly by $10$ only; two zeros cannot be removed. Right: $4\,250\div10=425$.
:::

## Summary

- To multiply by $10$, $100$, $1\,000$: write $1$, $2$, $3$ zeros on the right of the number.
- To divide a multiple of ten, a hundred or a thousand by $10$, $100$, $1\,000$: remove $1$, $2$, $3$ zeros from the right of the dividend.
- Changing units: $1$ tonne $=1\,000$ kg, $1$ tạ $=100$ kg, $1$ km $=1\,000$ m, $1$ m $=100$ cm.
- To multiply by a multiple of ten such as $30$: multiply by $3$, then by $10$. To work out $7\,200\div400$: remove two zeros from both numbers and divide $72\div4$.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out in your head: $36\times10$; $36\times100$; $36\times1\,000$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiplying by $10$, $100$, $1\,000$ adds $1$, $2$, $3$ zeros.

**Steps:**
1. Write the number $36$.
2. Add $1$, $2$, $3$ zeros on the right in turn.

::solution
$36\times10=360$; $36\times100=3\,600$; $36\times1\,000=36\,000$.

**Answer.** $360$; $3\,600$; $36\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out in your head: $5\,000\div10$; $5\,000\div100$; $5\,000\div1\,000$.

::hint
**What you need:** dividing by $10$, $100$, $1\,000$ removes $1$, $2$, $3$ zeros.

**Steps:**
1. How many zeros are at the end of $5\,000$?
2. Remove $1$, $2$, $3$ zeros in turn.

::solution
$5\,000\div10=500$; $5\,000\div100=50$; $5\,000\div1\,000=5$.

**Answer.** $500$; $50$; $5$.
:::

:::exercise
Fill in the missing numbers.
a) $24\times\square=2\,400$
b) $9\,000\div\square=90$

::hint
**What you need:** multiplying adds zeros; dividing removes zeros.

**Steps:**
1. Part a: how many more zeros does $2\,400$ have than $24$?
2. Part b: how many zeros must be removed from $9\,000$ to get $90$?

::solution
a) $2\,400$ has two more zeros than $24$, so $24\times100=2\,400$.

b) Removing two zeros from $9\,000$ gives $90$, so $9\,000\div100=90$.

**Answer.** a) $100$. b) $100$.
:::

:::exercise
Fill in the missing numbers.
a) $8$ tạ $=\ ?$ kg
b) $6\,000$ kg $=\ ?$ tonnes

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ tạ $=100$ kg; $1$ tonne $=1\,000$ kg.

**Steps:**
1. Part a: multiply $8$ by $100$.
2. Part b: divide $6\,000$ by $1\,000$.

::solution
a) $8$ tạ $=8\times100=800$ kg.

b) $6\,000$ kg $=6\,000\div1\,000=6$ tonnes.

**Answer.** a) $800$ kg. b) $6$ tonnes.
:::

:::exercise
A piece of cloth $25$ m long is cut into pieces, each $10$ cm long. How many pieces can be cut?

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ m $=100$ cm.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Change $25$ m to centimetres, then divide by $10$.

::solution
$25$ m $=25\times100=2\,500$ (cm).

The number of pieces is $2\,500\div10=250$ (pieces).

**Answer.** $250$ pieces.
:::

:::exercise
A school buys $100$ boxes of chalk, each box holding $48$ sticks. The chalk is shared equally among $10$ classes. How many sticks of chalk does each class get?

::hint
**What you need:** multiply by $100$, then divide by $10$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Work out the total number of sticks, then share it equally among $10$ classes.

::solution
The number of sticks the school buys is $48\times100=4\,800$ (sticks).

The number of sticks each class gets is $4\,800\div10=480$ (sticks).

**Answer.** $480$ sticks of chalk.
:::

:::exercise
Work out in your head: $62\times40$ and $8\,400\div200$.

::hint
**What you need:** $40=4\times10$; to divide by $200$, remove two zeros from both numbers.

**Steps:**
1. $62\times40=(62\times4)\times10$.
2. $8\,400\div200=84\div2$.

::solution
$62\times40=(62\times4)\times10=248\times10=2\,480$.

$8\,400\div200=84\div2=42$.

**Answer.** $2\,480$ and $42$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on properties of multiplication. Calculate in the easy way: $5\times37\times20$.

::hint
**What you need:** $5\times20=100$; the commutative and associative properties.

**Steps:**
1. Group two factors to make a round hundred.
2. Multiply the result by $37$.

::solution
$$5\times37\times20=(5\times20)\times37=100\times37=3\,700.$$

**Answer.** $3\,700$.
:::

:::exercise
One lap of a running track at a stadium is $400$ m. An athlete runs $10$ laps. How many metres does the athlete run, and how many kilometres is that?

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ km $=1\,000$ m.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the metres: $400\times10$.
2. Change to kilometres: divide by $1\,000$.

::solution
The number of metres the athlete runs is $400\times10=4\,000$ (m).

In kilometres: $4\,000\div1\,000=4$ (km).

**Answer.** $4\,000$ m, which is $4$ km.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A number is multiplied by $100$ and then divided by $10$, giving $3\,500$. Find the number.

::hint
**What you need:** work backwards: dividing by $10$ is the reverse of multiplying by $10$.

**Steps:**
1. What was the result before dividing by $10$?
2. The number multiplied by $100$ gives that result; find the number.

::solution
Before dividing by $10$ we had $3\,500\times10=35\,000$.

The number multiplied by $100$ gives $35\,000$, so the number is $35\,000\div100=350$.

**Check.** $350\times100=35\,000$ and $35\,000\div10=3\,500$. Correct.

**Answer.** $350$.
:::
