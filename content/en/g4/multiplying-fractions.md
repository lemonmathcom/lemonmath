Suppose a rectangular piece of card is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ m long and $\dfrac{1}{2}$ m wide. Somewhere else, a jug holds $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a litre of water, and the water is poured into cups, each cup holding $\dfrac{1}{8}$ of a litre.

What is the area of the piece of card in square metres? How many cups can be filled? Both problems need multiplying or dividing two fractions. This lesson shows you how to do both.

## Multiplying fractions

### Let's explore

Draw a rectangle divided into $3$ columns and $2$ rows, which makes $6$ equal cells, and treat the whole rectangle as $1$ unit of area. Taking $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of the width and $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of the height gives the shaded part, made of $2$ columns and $1$ row.

The shaded part has $2$ cells out of $6$, so its area is $\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{1}{3}$ of a unit. This method gives:
$$\dfrac{2}{3}\times\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{2\times1}{3\times2}=\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{1}{3}.$$

Notice: the numerator $2$ takes $2$ columns and the numerator $1$ takes $1$ row, so there are $2\times1$ cells; the denominator $3\times2$ is the total number of cells in the whole rectangle.

:::theorem Multiplying two fractions
To multiply two fractions, multiply the numerators together and multiply the denominators together:
$$\dfrac{a}{b}\times\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{a\times c}{b\times d}.$$

To multiply a fraction by a natural number, multiply the numerator by the natural number and keep the denominator:
$$\dfrac{a}{b}\times n=\dfrac{a\times n}{b}.$$
Simplify the answer if possible.
:::

Why does multiplying by a natural number work this way? Because $n=\dfrac{n}{1}$, so $\dfrac{a}{b}\times\dfrac{n}{1}=\dfrac{a\times n}{b\times1}=\dfrac{a\times n}{b}$.

**Connection.** This lesson uses multiplying natural numbers, simplifying fractions and the area of a rectangle. It prepares you for the lesson on finding a fraction of an amount.

We start with the piece of card.

:::example Area of a piece of card
A rectangular piece of card is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ m long and $\dfrac{1}{2}$ m wide. Find the area of the card.

**Solution.** The area of a rectangle is length times width:
$$\dfrac{2}{3}\times\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{2\times1}{3\times2}=\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{1}{3}\ (\text{m}^2).$$
So the area of the card is $\dfrac{1}{3}\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

Next, two fractions whose product cannot be simplified.

:::example Multiplying two fractions
Work out $\dfrac{3}{5}\times\dfrac{2}{7}$.

**Solution.** We multiply the numerators together and the denominators together:
$$\dfrac{3}{5}\times\dfrac{2}{7}=\dfrac{3\times2}{5\times7}=\dfrac{6}{35}.$$
The fraction $\dfrac{6}{35}$ cannot be simplified. So $\dfrac{3}{5}\times\dfrac{2}{7}=\dfrac{6}{35}$.
:::

Now multiplying a fraction by a natural number.

:::example Multiplying a fraction by a natural number
Work out $\dfrac{3}{8}\times4$.

**Solution.** We multiply the numerator by $4$ and keep the denominator:
$$\dfrac{3}{8}\times4=\dfrac{3\times4}{8}=\dfrac{12}{8}.$$
Both $12$ and $8$ are divisible by $4$, so $\dfrac{12}{8}=\dfrac{3}{2}$.

So $\dfrac{3}{8}\times4=\dfrac{3}{2}$.
:::

You can simplify to make the numbers smaller.

:::example Simplifying when multiplying
Work out $\dfrac{4}{9}\times\dfrac{3}{8}$.

**Solution.** We have $\dfrac{4}{9}\times\dfrac{3}{8}=\dfrac{4\times3}{9\times8}=\dfrac{12}{72}$. Both $12$ and $72$ are divisible by $12$, so:
$$\dfrac{12}{72}=\dfrac{1}{6}.$$
So $\dfrac{4}{9}\times\dfrac{3}{8}=\dfrac{1}{6}$.
:::

## Dividing fractions

### Let's explore

Now we answer the question about the jug. The jug holds $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a litre and each cup holds $\dfrac{1}{8}$ of a litre, so the number of cups is $\dfrac{3}{4}\div\dfrac{1}{8}$. Change $\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{6}{8}$ of a litre. In $\dfrac{6}{8}$ of a litre there are $6$ eighths of a litre, and each cup is $1$ eighth of a litre, so $6$ cups can be filled.

Notice: $1$ litre holds $8$ cups of $\dfrac{1}{8}$ of a litre. Dividing by $\dfrac{1}{8}$ means multiplying by $8$. We have
$$\dfrac{3}{4}\div\dfrac{1}{8}=\dfrac{3}{4}\times8=\dfrac{24}{4}=6.$$

The fraction $\dfrac{8}{1}$ (that is, $8$) is the reciprocal of $\dfrac{1}{8}$: swap the numerator and the denominator. (In Vietnam this is called the "inverted fraction", phân số đảo ngược.)

:::theorem Dividing two fractions
To divide a fraction by a fraction that is not $0$, multiply the first fraction by the **reciprocal** of the second:
$$\dfrac{a}{b}\div\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{a}{b}\times\dfrac{d}{c}.$$

The reciprocal of $\dfrac{c}{d}$ is $\dfrac{d}{c}$ (swap the numerator and the denominator). Dividing by a natural number other than $0$ is multiplying by its reciprocal: $\dfrac{a}{b}\div n=\dfrac{a}{b}\times\dfrac{1}{n}=\dfrac{a}{b\times n}$.
:::

Why does this work? Dividing by $\dfrac{c}{d}$ asks how many parts of size $\dfrac{c}{d}$ there are in the amount. Each unit holds $\dfrac{d}{c}$ such parts, so dividing by $\dfrac{c}{d}$ is also multiplying by $\dfrac{d}{c}$.

:::example Dividing two fractions
Work out $\dfrac{2}{5}\div\dfrac{3}{4}$.

**Solution.** The reciprocal of $\dfrac{3}{4}$ is $\dfrac{4}{3}$. We multiply by it:
$$\dfrac{2}{5}\div\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{2}{5}\times\dfrac{4}{3}=\dfrac{2\times4}{5\times3}=\dfrac{8}{15}.$$
So $\dfrac{2}{5}\div\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{8}{15}$.
:::

Dividing a fraction by a natural number, and a natural number by a fraction.

:::example Dividing with natural numbers
Work out:
a) $\dfrac{3}{5}\div2$
b) $4\div\dfrac{2}{3}$

**Solution.** a) Dividing by $2$ is multiplying by $\dfrac{1}{2}$:
$$\dfrac{3}{5}\div2=\dfrac{3}{5}\times\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{3}{10}.$$
b) The reciprocal of $\dfrac{2}{3}$ is $\dfrac{3}{2}$. We multiply:
$$4\div\dfrac{2}{3}=4\times\dfrac{3}{2}=\dfrac{12}{2}=6.$$
So a) $\dfrac{3}{10}$; b) $6$.
:::

Now we solve the problem about pouring water.

:::example Pouring water into cups
A jug holds $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a litre of water. The water is poured into cups, each holding $\dfrac{1}{8}$ of a litre. How many cups can be filled?

**Understand.** There is $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a litre of water and each cup holds $\dfrac{1}{8}$ of a litre. We need the number of cups.

**Plan.** The number of cups is the water divided by the amount in each cup: $\dfrac{3}{4}\div\dfrac{1}{8}$.

**Solve.** The number of cups that can be filled is:
$$\dfrac{3}{4}\div\dfrac{1}{8}=\dfrac{3}{4}\times\dfrac{8}{1}=\dfrac{24}{4}=6\ \text{(cups)}.$$

**Answer:** $6$ cups.

**Look back.** Check by multiplying: $6\times\dfrac{1}{8}=\dfrac{6}{8}=\dfrac{3}{4}$ (litres), exactly the water in the jug. If each cup held $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of a litre, how many cups could be filled?
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Using a common denominator and then multiplying, as in addition.** Wrong: $\dfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{3}{6}\times\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{6}{6}=1$. Why it is wrong: multiplying two fractions less than $1$ cannot give $1$. Right: $\dfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{1\times1}{2\times3}=\dfrac{1}{6}$.
- **Inverting the wrong fraction.** Wrong: $\dfrac{3}{4}\div\dfrac{1}{8}=\dfrac{4}{3}\times\dfrac{1}{8}=\dfrac{1}{6}$. Why it is wrong: we must invert the second fraction (the divisor), not the first. Right: $\dfrac{3}{4}\div\dfrac{1}{8}=\dfrac{3}{4}\times8=6$.
- **Multiplying the denominator by the natural number as well.** Wrong: $\dfrac{3}{8}\times4=\dfrac{3}{32}$. Why it is wrong: the result $\dfrac{3}{32}$ is less than $\dfrac{3}{8}$, but multiplying by $4$ must give a greater number. Right: $\dfrac{3}{8}\times4=\dfrac{12}{8}=\dfrac{3}{2}$.
:::

## Summary

- Multiplying two fractions: multiply numerator by numerator and denominator by denominator; simplify the answer.
- Multiplying a fraction by a natural number: $\dfrac{a}{b}\times n=\dfrac{a\times n}{b}$.
- Dividing by a fraction is multiplying by its reciprocal: $\dfrac{a}{b}\div\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{a}{b}\times\dfrac{d}{c}$.
- Dividing by a natural number $n$ is multiplying by $\dfrac{1}{n}$; to divide a natural number by a fraction, multiply by the reciprocal.
- Check a division by multiplying.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out $\dfrac{3}{4}\times\dfrac{2}{5}$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply numerator by numerator and denominator by denominator.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply the two numerators and the two denominators.
2. Simplify the answer.

::solution
$$\dfrac{3}{4}\times\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{3\times2}{4\times5}=\dfrac{6}{20}=\dfrac{3}{10}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{3}{10}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $\dfrac{5}{12}\times3$.

::hint
**What you need:** $\dfrac{a}{b}\times n=\dfrac{a\times n}{b}$.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply the numerator by $3$ and keep the denominator.
2. Simplify the answer.

::solution
$$\dfrac{5}{12}\times3=\dfrac{5\times3}{12}=\dfrac{15}{12}=\dfrac{5}{4}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{5}{4}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $\dfrac{6}{7}\times\dfrac{14}{15}$.

::hint
**What you need:** you can simplify the answer using a large common divisor.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply numerator by numerator and denominator by denominator.
2. Simplify the fraction.

::solution
$$\dfrac{6}{7}\times\dfrac{14}{15}=\dfrac{6\times14}{7\times15}=\dfrac{84}{105}=\dfrac{4}{5}.$$
In the last step, we divide both $84$ and $105$ by $21$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{4}{5}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $\dfrac{3}{4}\div\dfrac{5}{6}$.

::hint
**What you need:** dividing by a fraction is multiplying by its reciprocal.

**Steps:**
1. What is the reciprocal of $\dfrac{5}{6}$?
2. Multiply, then simplify.

::solution
$$\dfrac{3}{4}\div\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{3}{4}\times\dfrac{6}{5}=\dfrac{18}{20}=\dfrac{9}{10}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{9}{10}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out:
a) $\dfrac{7}{8}\div\dfrac{7}{4}$
b) $5\div\dfrac{5}{6}$

::hint
**What you need:** the natural number $5$ can be thought of as $\dfrac{5}{1}$.

**Steps:**
1. Write the multiplication by the reciprocal of the divisor.
2. Simplify the answer.

::solution
a) $\dfrac{7}{8}\div\dfrac{7}{4}=\dfrac{7}{8}\times\dfrac{4}{7}=\dfrac{28}{56}=\dfrac{1}{2}$.

b) $5\div\dfrac{5}{6}=5\times\dfrac{6}{5}=\dfrac{30}{5}=6$.

**Answer.** a) $\dfrac{1}{2}$. b) $6$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose each bow needs $\dfrac{2}{5}$ m of ribbon. How many metres of ribbon are needed to make $6$ such bows?

::hint
**What you need:** the metres needed are the metres for each bow times the number of bows.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Multiply $\dfrac{2}{5}$ by $6$ and write the unit, metres.

::solution
The number of metres of ribbon needed is:
$$\dfrac{2}{5}\times6=\dfrac{2\times6}{5}=\dfrac{12}{5}\ \text{(m)}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{12}{5}$ m.
:::

:::exercise
A rectangular pane of glass is $\dfrac{3}{4}$ m long and $\dfrac{2}{3}$ m wide. Find the area of the pane.

::hint
**What you need:** the area of a rectangle is length times width.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Multiply the two fractions, simplify, and write the unit, square metres.

::solution
The area of the pane is:
$$\dfrac{3}{4}\times\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{6}{12}=\dfrac{1}{2}\ (\text{m}^2).$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{1}{2}\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose $\dfrac{5}{6}$ kg of rice is shared equally among $5$ small bags. How many kilograms of rice are in each bag?

::hint
**What you need:** dividing by $5$ is multiplying by $\dfrac{1}{5}$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Work out $\dfrac{5}{6}\div5$ and simplify.

::solution
The number of kilograms of rice in each bag is:
$$\dfrac{5}{6}\div5=\dfrac{5}{6}\times\dfrac{1}{5}=\dfrac{5}{30}=\dfrac{1}{6}\ \text{(kg)}.$$

**Check.** $\dfrac{1}{6}\times5=\dfrac{5}{6}$. Correct.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{1}{6}$ kg.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on adding fractions. Work out $\dfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{6}$.

::hint
**What you need:** do the multiplication first, then add.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $\dfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{1}{3}$.
2. Add $\dfrac{1}{6}$ and simplify.

::solution
$\dfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{1}{6}$.

So $\dfrac{1}{6}+\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{1}{3}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{1}{3}$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A rectangle has area $\dfrac{3}{5}\ \text{m}^2$ and width $\dfrac{1}{2}$ m. Find the length of the rectangle.

::hint
**What you need:** area $=$ length $\times$ width, so length $=$ area $\div$ width.

**Steps:**
1. Write the division you need.
2. Multiply by the reciprocal, then check.

::solution
The length of the rectangle is:
$$\dfrac{3}{5}\div\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{3}{5}\times2=\dfrac{6}{5}\ \text{(m)}.$$

**Check.** $\dfrac{6}{5}\times\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{6}{10}=\dfrac{3}{5}$. Correct.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{6}{5}$ m.
:::
