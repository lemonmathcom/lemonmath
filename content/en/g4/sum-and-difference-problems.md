:::goals
- Recognise the problem "find two numbers when you know their sum and their difference".
- Draw a bar model to find a way to solve it.
- Find the smaller and the larger number in two ways: smaller number $=(\text{sum}-\text{difference})\div2$, larger number $=(\text{sum}+\text{difference})\div2$.
- Solve word problems and rectangle perimeter problems this way, then check the answer.
:::

Mai and Lan (made-up numbers) have $70$ story books altogether. Mai has $10$ more books than Lan. How many story books does each girl have?

The problem gives the sum of two numbers, $70$, and the difference of the two numbers, $10$. You need to find the two numbers. This lesson teaches you one method that works for every problem of this kind.

## Finding two numbers from their sum and difference

### Let's explore

Draw a bar model: Lan's number of books is one bar, and Mai's number of books is a longer bar, longer by $10$ books.

:::diagram Bar model of Lan's and Mai's story books

You can see that Mai's bar is made of a part equal to Lan's bar plus the extra part, which is $10$ books. If you take away the extra part, the two bars are equal. Then:

- The total of the two equal bars is $70-10=60$ (books).
- Each bar is $60\div2=30$ (books). That is Lan's number of books.
- Mai has $10$ more books than Lan, so Mai has $30+10=40$ (books).

So Lan has $30$ story books and Mai has $40$ story books. Check: $30+40=70$ and $40-30=10$. Correct.

### How to find the two numbers

:::theorem Finding two numbers from their sum and difference
When you know the sum and the difference of two numbers, you can find the two numbers like this:
- Smaller number $=(\text{Sum}-\text{Difference})\div2$.
- $\text{Larger number}=\text{Smaller number}+\text{Difference}$, or larger number $=(\text{Sum}+\text{Difference})\div2$.
:::

Why does this work? If you take the extra part away from the sum, what is left is two times the smaller number, so dividing by $2$ gives the smaller number. The larger number is exactly the difference more than the smaller number, so we add the difference to the smaller number. If you add the extra part to the sum, you get two times the larger number, so $(\text{sum}+\text{difference})\div2$ gives the larger number.

We start with a problem that has only numbers.

:::example Finding two numbers
The sum of two numbers is $96$ and the difference of the two numbers is $18$. Find the two numbers.

**Solution.** The smaller number is $(96-18)\div2=78\div2=39$. The larger number is $39+18=57$.

**Check.** $39+57=96$ and $57-39=18$. Correct.

So the two numbers are $39$ and $57$.
:::

Find the larger number first, a different way.

:::example Finding the larger number first
The sum of two numbers is $150$ and the difference of the two numbers is $40$. Find the two numbers.

**Solution.** The larger number is $(150+40)\div2=190\div2=95$. The smaller number is $95-40=55$.

**Check.** $95+55=150$ and $95-55=40$. Correct.

So the two numbers are $95$ and $55$.
:::

## Word problems

Use the method above to solve word problems. In each problem, say clearly which is the sum and which is the difference.

:::example Two rice stores
Two stores (made-up numbers) hold $480$ tonnes of rice altogether. Store A holds $60$ tonnes more rice than store B. How many tonnes of rice does each store hold?

**Solution.** The sum is $480$ tonnes and the difference is $60$ tonnes. The rice in store B (the smaller number) is:
$$(480-60)\div2=210\ \text{(tonnes)}.$$
The rice in store A (the larger number) is:
$$210+60=270\ \text{(tonnes)}.$$
**Answer.** Store A: $270$ tonnes; store B: $210$ tonnes.
:::

Do the same with money.

:::example The money of two girls
Mai and Lan (made-up numbers) have $54\,000$ dong altogether. Mai has $12\,000$ dong more than Lan. How many dong does each girl have?

**Solution.** Lan's money (the smaller number) is:
$$(54\,000-12\,000)\div2=21\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
Mai's money (the larger number) is:
$$21\,000+12\,000=33\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
**Answer.** Mai: $33\,000$ dong; Lan: $21\,000$ dong.
:::

Use the perimeter of a rectangle to find the sum.

:::example Length and width
A garden (made-up numbers) is a rectangle with perimeter $56$ m, and its length is $8$ m more than its width. Find the length and the width of the garden.

**Solution.** The perimeter of a rectangle is $P=(a+b)\times2$, so half the perimeter is the sum of the length and the width. Half the perimeter is:
$$56\div2=28\ \text{(m)}.$$
The sum of the length and the width is $28$ m and the difference is $8$ m. The width is:
$$(28-8)\div2=10\ \text{(m)}.$$
The length is:
$$10+8=18\ \text{(m)}.$$
**Answer.** Length $18$ m; width $10$ m.
:::

Finally, a problem with several steps. We work in four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example Plain rice and sticky rice
A shop (made-up numbers) has $128$ kg of plain rice and sticky rice altogether. There is $24$ kg more plain rice than sticky rice. The shop sells $30$ kg of plain rice. Which kind of rice is there more of now, and by how many kilograms?

**Understand.** We are told the total amount of rice is $128$ kg, the difference between plain rice and sticky rice is $24$ kg, and the plain rice sold is $30$ kg. We are asked which kind of rice there is more of now and by how much.

**Plan.** First find the amounts of plain rice and sticky rice at the start by finding two numbers from their sum and difference. Then subtract $30$ kg from the plain rice and compare with the sticky rice.

**Solve.**

Solution

The number of kilograms of sticky rice at the start (the smaller number) is:
$$(128-24)\div2=52\ \text{(kg)}.$$
The number of kilograms of plain rice at the start (the larger number) is:
$$52+24=76\ \text{(kg)}.$$
The number of kilograms of plain rice left is:
$$76-30=46\ \text{(kg)}.$$
Since $52>46$, there is more sticky rice than plain rice, by:
$$52-46=6\ \text{(kg)}.$$
Answer: there is $6$ kg more sticky rice than plain rice.

**Look back.** Check the starting amounts: $52+76=128$ and $76-52=24$. Correct. Before the sale there was $24$ kg more plain rice; after selling $30$ kg of plain rice, the extra $24$ kg turns into "less" by $30-24=6$ kg. Both ways give the same result.
:::

**Connection.** You reuse the rectangle perimeter formula from the lesson on expressions with letters and the way to solve multi-step problems. In later years you will meet problems that ask you to find two numbers when you know their sum and their ratio.

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting to divide by $2$.** Wrong: if the sum is $96$ and the difference is $18$, the smaller number is $96-18=78$. Why it is wrong: $78$ is two times the smaller number. Right: $(96-18)\div2=39$.
- **Adding instead of subtracting to find the smaller number.** Wrong: the smaller number is $(96+18)\div2=57$. Why it is wrong: $(96+18)\div2$ gives the larger number, not the smaller number. Right: the smaller number is $(96-18)\div2=39$.
- **Finding the larger number wrongly.** Wrong: $\text{larger number}=\text{smaller number}-\text{difference}=39-18=21$. Why it is wrong: the larger number is more than the smaller number, so we must add the difference. Right: larger number $=39+18=57$.
:::

## Summary

- A problem that gives the sum and difference of two numbers: draw a bar model; take away the extra part and the two numbers are equal.
- Smaller number $=(\text{sum}-\text{difference})\div2$. $\text{Larger number}=\text{smaller number}+\text{difference}$, or $(\text{sum}+\text{difference})\div2$.
- For a rectangle: the sum of the length and the width is half the perimeter.
- Always check: the two numbers you find must have the sum and difference given in the problem.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
The sum of two numbers is $84$ and the difference of the two numbers is $12$. Find the two numbers.

::hint
**What you need:** smaller number $=(\text{sum}-\text{difference})\div2$; $\text{larger number}=\text{smaller number}+\text{difference}$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the smaller number.
2. Work out the larger number, then check.

::solution
The smaller number is $(84-12)\div2=36$. The larger number is $36+12=48$.

**Check.** $36+48=84$ and $48-36=12$. Correct.

**Answer.** $36$ and $48$.
:::

:::exercise
The sum of two numbers is $125$ and the difference of the two numbers is $35$. Find the two numbers.

::hint
**What you need:** you can find the larger number first with $(\text{sum}+\text{difference})\div2$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the larger number $(125+35)\div2$.
2. Subtract the difference from the larger number to find the smaller number.

::solution
The larger number is $(125+35)\div2=160\div2=80$. The smaller number is $80-35=45$.

**Check.** $80+45=125$ and $80-45=35$. Correct.

**Answer.** $80$ and $45$.
:::

:::exercise
The sum of two numbers is $1\,000$ and the difference of the two numbers is $250$. Find the two numbers.

::hint
**What you need:** divide by $2$ after subtracting or adding.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the larger number $(1\,000+250)\div2$.
2. Work out the smaller number, then check.

::solution
The larger number is $(1\,000+250)\div2=1\,250\div2=625$. The smaller number is $625-250=375$.

**Check.** $625+375=1\,000$ and $625-375=250$. Correct.

**Answer.** $625$ and $375$.
:::

:::exercise
Nam and Viet (made-up numbers) have $45$ marbles altogether. Nam has $9$ more marbles than Viet. How many marbles does each boy have?

::hint
**What you need:** say clearly which is the sum, the difference, the smaller number and the larger number.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Find Viet's number of marbles (the smaller number).
3. Find Nam's number of marbles (the larger number).

::solution
Solution

Viet's number of marbles is:
$$(45-9)\div2=18\ \text{(marbles)}.$$
Nam's number of marbles is:
$$18+9=27\ \text{(marbles)}.$$

**Answer.** Nam: $27$ marbles; Viet: $18$ marbles.
:::

:::exercise
A farm (made-up numbers) keeps $360$ chickens and ducks altogether. There are $40$ more chickens than ducks. How many chickens and how many ducks does the farm keep?

::hint
**What you need:** the sum is $360$ and the difference is $40$.

**Steps:**
1. Is the number of ducks the smaller or the larger number?
2. Find the number of ducks, then the number of chickens.

::solution
Solution

The number of ducks is:
$$(360-40)\div2=160\ \text{(ducks)}.$$
The number of chickens is:
$$160+40=200\ \text{(chickens)}.$$

**Answer.** Chickens: $200$; ducks: $160$.
:::

:::exercise
A plot of land (made-up numbers) is a rectangle with perimeter $80$ m, and its length is $10$ m more than its width. Find the length and the width.

::hint
**What you need:** half the perimeter is the sum of the length and the width.

**Steps:**
1. Work out half the perimeter.
2. Find the width, then the length.

::solution
Solution

Half the perimeter is:
$$80\div2=40\ \text{(m)}.$$
The width is:
$$(40-10)\div2=15\ \text{(m)}.$$
The length is:
$$15+10=25\ \text{(m)}.$$

**Answer.** Length $25$ m; width $15$ m.
:::

:::exercise
Two sisters (made-up numbers) have $100\,000$ dong altogether. The older sister has $24\,000$ dong more than the younger one. How many dong does each have?

::hint
**What you need:** the younger sister has less money, so she has the smaller number.

**Steps:**
1. Find the younger sister's money.
2. Find the older sister's money, then check.

::solution
Solution

The younger sister's money is:
$$(100\,000-24\,000)\div2=38\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
The older sister's money is:
$$38\,000+24\,000=62\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$

**Answer.** Older sister: $62\,000$ dong; younger sister: $38\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on adding numbers with many digits. The sum of two numbers is $1\,000\,000$ and the difference of the two numbers is $246\,000$. Find the two numbers.

::hint
**What you need:** set out the subtraction, then divide by $2$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $1\,000\,000-246\,000$.
2. Divide the result by $2$ to find the smaller number, then find the larger number.

::solution
The smaller number is $(1\,000\,000-246\,000)\div2=754\,000\div2=377\,000$. The larger number is $377\,000+246\,000=623\,000$.

**Check.** $377\,000+623\,000=1\,000\,000$ and $623\,000-377\,000=246\,000$. Correct.

**Answer.** $377\,000$ and $623\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
Nam solves the problem: "The sum of two numbers is $70$ and the difference of the two numbers is $14$." Nam writes: the larger number is $(70+14)\div2=42$; the smaller number is $42+14=56$. Check Nam's working. If it is wrong, correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** the smaller number is less than the larger number.

**Steps:**
1. Is the larger number $42$ correct?
2. Should the smaller number be more or less than the larger number?

::solution
The larger number $(70+14)\div2=42$ is correct. But the smaller number must be less than the larger number, so the smaller number is $42-14=28$, not $42+14=56$.

**Check.** $42+28=70$ and $42-28=14$. Correct.

**Answer.** Nam is wrong in the last step; the two numbers are $42$ and $28$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Are there two natural numbers whose sum is $70$ and whose difference is $15$? Why or why not?

::hint
**What you need:** the smaller number $(\text{sum}-\text{difference})\div2$ must be a natural number.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the sum minus the difference.
2. Is the result divisible by $2$?

::solution
We have $70-15=55$, which is odd, so it is not divisible by $2$. So there is no natural number that could be the smaller number, and no two natural numbers have sum $70$ and difference $15$.

**Answer.** No, because $70-15=55$ is odd.
:::
