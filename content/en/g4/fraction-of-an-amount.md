:::goals
- Understand that finding a fraction of an amount means splitting it into equal parts and taking some of them.
- Find a fraction of an amount by dividing and then multiplying, or by multiplying fractions.
- Find a fraction of a fraction.
- Solve word problems involving a fraction of an amount.
:::

Class $4$A has $36$ students. At break time, $\dfrac{5}{9}$ of the students in the class play in the school yard.

How many students play in the yard? This lesson shows you how to find "$\dfrac{5}{9}$ of $36$" and other fractions of an amount.

## Let's explore

Draw a strip of paper to stand for the $36$ students and divide it into $9$ equal parts.

Each part has $36\div9=4$ students. Taking $5$ parts gives $5\times4=20$ students. So $\dfrac{5}{9}$ of $36$ is $20$:
$$36\div9\times5=4\times5=20.$$

You can also use multiplication of fractions: $36\times\dfrac{5}{9}=\dfrac{36\times5}{9}=\dfrac{180}{9}=20$. Both ways give the same result.

:::theorem Finding a fraction of an amount
To find $\dfrac{a}{b}$ of an amount $n$, divide $n$ by $b$ (the denominator) and then multiply by $a$ (the numerator):
$$\dfrac{a}{b}\ \text{of}\ n=n\div b\times a=n\times\dfrac{a}{b}.$$
:::

Why does this work? The denominator $b$ tells us to split the amount $n$ into $b$ equal parts, so each part is $n\div b$. The numerator $a$ tells us to take $a$ such parts, so we multiply by $a$.

**Connection.** This lesson uses the division and multiplication of fractions you have just learned. You will meet this method again in many problems about ratios and percentages later on.

## Examples

We start with a small number.

:::example A fraction of a number
Find $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of $12$.

**Solution.** Split $12$ into $3$ equal parts; each part is $12\div3=4$. Taking $2$ parts gives $2\times4=8$.

So $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of $12$ is $8$.
:::

Next, a calculation with a unit of measurement.

:::example A fraction of a measurement
Find $\dfrac{3}{5}$ of $45$ kg.

**Solution.** Each part is $45\div5=9$ (kg). Taking $3$ parts gives $9\times3=27$ (kg).

So $\dfrac{3}{5}$ of $45$ kg is $27$ kg.
:::

Sometimes multiplying first and then simplifying is convenient.

:::example Using multiplication of fractions
Find $\dfrac{5}{6}$ of $18$.

**Solution.** We multiply the number by the fraction:
$$\dfrac{5}{6}\ \text{of}\ 18=18\times\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{18\times5}{6}=\dfrac{90}{6}=15.$$
So $\dfrac{5}{6}$ of $18$ is $15$.
:::

Now we answer the question at the start.

:::example Students playing in the yard
Class $4$A has $36$ students. At break time, $\dfrac{5}{9}$ of the students play in the school yard. How many students play in the yard? How many do not play in the yard?

**Understand.** The class has $36$ students. The number playing in the yard is $\dfrac{5}{9}$ of $36$. We need the number playing in the yard and the number not playing in the yard.

**Plan.** Split $36$ into $9$ equal parts and take $5$ parts for the students playing in the yard. The rest is $9-5=4$ parts.

**Solve.** Each part is $36\div9=4$ (students). The number playing in the yard is $4\times5=20$ (students).

The number not playing in the yard is $36-20=16$ (students).

**Answer:** $20$ students play in the yard; $16$ students do not.

**Look back.** The number not playing in the yard is also $\dfrac{4}{9}$ of $36$, which is $4\times4=16$ (students). Both ways give the same result, and $20+16=36$, so it is correct.
:::

Now a problem with several steps.

:::example Selling rice
A shop has $240$ kg of rice. On the first day the shop sells $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of the rice, and on the second day it sells $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of the rice. How many kilograms of rice are left after the two days?

**Solution.** The kilograms of rice sold on the first day are $240\div8\times3=90$ (kg).

The kilograms of rice sold on the second day are $240\div4=60$ (kg).

The kilograms of rice left are $240-90-60=90$ (kg).

**Answer:** $90$ kg.
:::

Finally, a fraction of a fraction.

:::example A fraction of a fraction
Find $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of $\dfrac{3}{4}$ m.

**Solution.** We multiply the two fractions:
$$\dfrac{3}{4}\times\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{3\times1}{4\times2}=\dfrac{3}{8}\ \text{(m)}.$$
So $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of $\dfrac{3}{4}$ m is $\dfrac{3}{8}$ m.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Dividing by the numerator and multiplying by the denominator.** Wrong: $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of $30$ is $30\div2\times5=75$. Why it is wrong: we must divide by the **denominator** and multiply by the **numerator**. Right: $30\div5\times2=12$.
- **Multiplying by the denominator without dividing.** Wrong: $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of $24$ is $24\times4=96$. Why it is wrong: $\dfrac{1}{4}$ is less than $1$, so the result must be less than $24$. Right: $24\div4=6$.
- **Forgetting the unit.** Wrong: "Answer: $20$". Why it is wrong: the question asks for a number of students, so the answer must state the unit. Right: "Answer: $20$ students".
:::

## Summary

- To find $\dfrac{a}{b}$ of $n$: divide $n$ by $b$ and multiply by $a$, or work out $n\times\dfrac{a}{b}$.
- The denominator tells you the number of equal parts, and the numerator tells you the number of parts taken.
- When $\dfrac{a}{b}$ is less than $1$, the result is less than the original amount.
- To find a fraction of a fraction, multiply the two fractions.
- Remember to write the unit in the answer.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Find $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of $20$.

::hint
**What you need:** divide by the denominator and multiply by the numerator.

**Steps:**
1. Each part is $20\div4=?$
2. Take $3$ parts.

::solution
Each part is $20\div4=5$. Taking $3$ parts gives $5\times3=15$.

**Answer.** $15$.
:::

:::exercise
Find $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of $30$.

::hint
**What you need:** $\dfrac{a}{b}$ of $n$ is $n\div b\times a$.

**Steps:**
1. Divide $30$ by the denominator.
2. Multiply by the numerator.

::solution
$$30\div5\times2=6\times2=12.$$

**Answer.** $12$.
:::

:::exercise
Find $\dfrac{7}{8}$ of $64$.

::hint
**What you need:** split into $8$ equal parts and take $7$ parts.

**Steps:**
1. Each part is $64\div8=?$
2. Take $7$ parts.

::solution
$$64\div8\times7=8\times7=56.$$

**Answer.** $56$.
:::

:::exercise
There are $60$ minutes in an hour. How many minutes is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of an hour?

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ hour $=60$ minutes; find $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of $60$ minutes.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Divide $60$ by $3$ and multiply by $2$.

::solution
The number of minutes in $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of an hour is:
$$60\div3\times2=20\times2=40\ \text{(minutes)}.$$

**Answer.** $40$ minutes.
:::

:::exercise
A tank holds $480$ litres of water. $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of the water in the tank has been used. How many litres have been used, and how many litres are left in the tank?

::hint
**What you need:** the litres used are $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of $480$; the litres left are $480$ minus the litres used.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Find $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of $480$, then subtract.

::solution
The litres of water used are $480\div8\times3=60\times3=180$ (litres).

The litres of water left are $480-180=300$ (litres).

**Answer.** $180$ litres used; $300$ litres left.
:::

:::exercise
A length of cloth is $18$ m long. $\dfrac{2}{9}$ of the cloth is used to make a shirt and $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of the cloth is used to make trousers. How many metres of cloth are left?

::hint
**What you need:** find each fraction of $18$, then subtract.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the metres used for the shirt and the metres used for the trousers.
2. Subtract both from $18$.

::solution
The metres of cloth for the shirt are $18\div9\times2=4$ (m).

The metres of cloth for the trousers are $18\div3=6$ (m).

The metres of cloth left are $18-4-6=8$ (m).

**Answer.** $8$ m.
:::

:::exercise
Nam has $150\,000$ dong and spends $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of it on books. How much money does Nam spend on books?

::hint
**What you need:** find $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of $150\,000$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Divide $150\,000$ by $5$ and multiply by $2$.

::solution
The amount Nam spends on books is:
$$150\,000\div5\times2=30\,000\times2=60\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$

**Answer.** $60\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on multiplying fractions. Find $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of $\dfrac{3}{5}$.

::hint
**What you need:** find a fraction of a fraction by multiplying.

**Steps:**
1. Write the multiplication $\dfrac{3}{5}\times\dfrac{2}{3}$.
2. Simplify the answer.

::solution
$$\dfrac{3}{5}\times\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{6}{15}=\dfrac{2}{5}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{2}{5}$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lessons on comparing and subtracting fractions. A class has $40$ students, of whom $\dfrac{3}{5}$ like Maths and $\dfrac{1}{4}$ like Literature. How many more students like Maths than like Literature?

::hint
**What you need:** find each fraction of $40$, then subtract.

**Steps:**
1. Find the number who like Maths and the number who like Literature.
2. Subtract the smaller number from the larger.

::solution
The number who like Maths is $40\div5\times3=24$ (students).

The number who like Literature is $40\div4=10$ (students).

The number who like Maths exceeds the number who like Literature by $24-10=14$ (students).

**Answer.** $14$ students.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
The Year $4$ group of a school has a number of students. $\dfrac{3}{5}$ of the students in the group is $36$ students. How many students are in the Year $4$ group?

::hint
**What you need:** draw a strip divided into $5$ equal parts; $3$ parts stand for $36$ students.

**Steps:**
1. $3$ parts is $36$ students; how many students is $1$ part?
2. The whole group is $5$ parts.

::solution
$3$ parts is $36$ students, so $1$ part is $36\div3=12$ (students).

The whole group is $5$ parts, which is $12\times5=60$ (students).

**Check.** $\dfrac{3}{5}$ of $60$ is $60\div5\times3=36$. Correct.

**Answer.** $60$ students.
:::

::mc
$\dfrac{2}{3}$ of $12$ is
A. $6$.
B. $18$.
C. $4$.
D. $8$.
::key D
Each part is $12\div3=4$; taking $2$ parts gives $4\times2=8$. Option C is only $12\div3$.

::mc
$\dfrac{3}{5}$ of $45$ kg is
A. $27$ kg.
B. $9$ kg.
C. $75$ kg.
D. $15$ kg.
::key A
$45\div5\times3=9\times3=27$ (kg). Option B forgets to multiply by $3$, and option C divides by the numerator and multiplies by the denominator.

::mc
Nam has $48$ marbles and gives his friend $\dfrac{1}{6}$ of them. Nam gave his friend
A. $288$ marbles.
B. $8$ marbles.
C. $42$ marbles.
D. $6$ marbles.
::key B
$48\div6=8$ (marbles). Option A is $48\times6$, a result greater than the original number of marbles.

::mc
Which calculation finds $\dfrac{5}{9}$ of $36$?
A. $36\div5\times9$.
B. $36\times9\div5$.
C. $36\div9\times5$.
D. $36+\dfrac{5}{9}$.
::key C
Divide by the denominator $9$ and then multiply by the numerator $5$.

::tf
Look at $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of $40$.
a) $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of $40$ equals $40\div3\times8$.
b) $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of $40$ equals $120$.
c) $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of $40$ equals $15$.
d) $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of $40$ is greater than $40$.
::key F F T F
a) False: we divide by the denominator $8$ and multiply by the numerator $3$. b) False: $120=40\times3$ has not been divided by $8$. c) True: $40\div8\times3=15$. d) False: $\dfrac{3}{8}<1$, so the result is less than $40$.

::key 20
$35\div7\times4=5\times4=20$.

::sa
