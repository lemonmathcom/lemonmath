:::goals
- Read a problem and say what is given and what must be found in a three-step word problem.
- Work backwards from the question to find the steps to do.
- Write out a solution with explanatory sentences, calculations, units and an answer.
- Check the result and solve problems of these types: "twice as many", finding one unit first, perimeter and sharing equally.
:::

Lan's mother (made-up numbers) buys $3$ boxes of cakes at $28\,000$ dong a box and $1$ bottle of water for $12\,000$ dong. She gives the shop assistant $150\,000$ dong. How much change does the shop assistant give her?

This problem cannot be solved with one calculation. You need to do three steps in turn, and each step gives a new number that is used in the next step.

## Solving three-step word problems

### Let's explore

Work backwards from the question:

- To find the change, you need to know how much she has to pay.
- To find how much she has to pay, you need the cost of the cakes and the cost of the water. The water costs $12\,000$ dong, but the cost of the cakes is not yet known.
- So the first step is to find the cost of the cakes.

Now do the three steps:

- Step $1$: the cost of $3$ boxes of cakes is $3\times28\,000=84\,000$ (dong).
- Step $2$: the money to pay is $84\,000+12\,000=96\,000$ (dong).
- Step $3$: the change is $150\,000-96\,000=54\,000$ (dong).

So the shop assistant gives her $54\,000$ dong in change.

### The steps to follow

:::theorem Solving a three-step word problem
To solve a three-step word problem, do the following:
1. Read the problem carefully: what is given, and what is asked?
2. Work backwards from the question: "To find ..., I need to know ...", to find the missing numbers and the order of the steps.
3. Write out the solution: each step has an explanatory sentence, a calculation and a unit.
4. Write the answer and check the result.
:::

Why work backwards from the question? Because the question tells you the final number you need. From there you see which numbers you need, which are given and which must be found first. The numbers to be found first are the first steps.

We start with a problem that has multiplication, addition and subtraction.

:::example Buying sweets and cake
Ms Hoa buys $4$ packets of sweets at $15\,000$ dong a packet and $1$ box of cake for $35\,000$ dong. She pays with $100\,000$ dong. How much change does she get?

**Solution.** The money for $4$ packets of sweets is:
$$4\times15\,000=60\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
The money Ms Hoa has to pay is:
$$60\,000+35\,000=95\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
The change Ms Hoa gets is:
$$100\,000-95\,000=5\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
**Answer.** $5\,000$ dong.
:::

The next problem is of the "find one first" type: find the price of one, then multiply.

:::example Finding one unit first
$5$ exercise books cost $40\,000$ dong. How much do $8$ of these exercise books and $1$ pen at $6\,000$ dong cost?

**Solution.** The price of one exercise book is:
$$40\,000\div5=8\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
The money for $8$ exercise books is:
$$8\,000\times8=64\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
The total money to pay is:
$$64\,000+6\,000=70\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
**Answer.** $70\,000$ dong.
:::

The next problem has the words "twice as many", which means multiply by $2$.

:::example Selling rice
A shop (made-up numbers) has $600$ kg of rice. In the morning it sells $125$ kg of rice, and in the afternoon it sells twice as much as in the morning. How many kilograms of rice does the shop have left?

**Solution.** The rice sold in the afternoon is:
$$125\times2=250\ \text{(kg)}.$$
The rice sold in the whole day is:
$$125+250=375\ \text{(kg)}.$$
The rice left is:
$$600-375=225\ \text{(kg)}.$$
**Answer.** $225$ kg of rice.
:::

The next problem needs multiplication, addition and division.

:::example Sharing equally among classes
A school (made-up numbers) has $5$ classes of $32$ pupils each. Then $14$ more pupils join the school. All the pupils are shared equally among $6$ classes. How many pupils are in each class?

**Solution.** The number of pupils at the start is:
$$32\times5=160\ \text{(pupils)}.$$
The number of pupils after $14$ more join is:
$$160+14=174\ \text{(pupils)}.$$
The number of pupils in each class is:
$$174\div6=29\ \text{(pupils)}.$$
**Answer.** $29$ pupils.
:::

Finally, a problem that uses the perimeter formula. We work in four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example Fencing a plot of land
A plot of land (made-up numbers) is a rectangle with width $18$ m, and its length is twice its width. A fence is built all the way round the plot, and each metre of fence costs $25\,000$ dong. How much does the fence cost?

**Understand.** We are given the width of $18$ m, the length that is twice the width, and the price of $25\,000$ dong per metre of fence. We are asked for the total cost of the fence.

**Plan.** To find the cost of the fence, we need the length of the fence, which is the perimeter. To work out the perimeter, we need the length of the plot. The length comes from the width. Three steps: the length, the perimeter, the cost.

**Solve.**

Solution

The length of the plot is:
$$18\times2=36\ \text{(m)}.$$
The perimeter of the plot is:
$$(36+18)\times2=108\ \text{(m)}.$$
The cost of the fence is:
$$108\times25\,000=2\,700\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
Answer: $2\,700\,000$ dong.

**Look back.** Check: $36+18=54$ and $54\times2=108$. Correct. Also, $108\,\text{m}$ of fence at $25\,000$ dong a metre must cost more than $100\times25\,000=2\,500\,000$ dong. This agrees with the result $2\,700\,000$ dong.
:::

**Connection.** You have used the operations and the perimeter formula from earlier lessons. A three-step problem is just three one-step problems joined together, and the answer to one step is given information for the next.

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Missing a step.** Wrong: in Lan's mother's problem, the change is $150\,000-84\,000=66\,000$ dong. Why it is wrong: the cost of the bottle of water, $12\,000$ dong, was forgotten. Right: $150\,000-96\,000=54\,000$ dong.
- **Misunderstanding "twice as many".** Wrong: sold twice as much in the afternoon as in the morning, so $125+2=127$ kg. Why it is wrong: twice as many means multiply by $2$, not add $2$. Right: $125\times2=250$ kg.
- **Forgetting the unit in the answer.** Wrong: "Answer: $225$". Right: "Answer: $225$ kg of rice".
:::

## Summary

- A three-step problem needs three calculations in a chain; the result of one step is used in the next.
- Work backwards from the question: "To find ..., I need to know ..." to find the steps.
- Each step in the solution has an explanatory sentence, a calculation and a unit; end with the answer.
- "Twice as many" means multiply by $2$. For the "one unit first" type: find the price of one, then multiply.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
A workshop (made-up numbers) makes $1\,250$ shirts on the first day, twice as many on the second day as on the first day, and $300$ fewer on the third day than on the second day. How many shirts does the workshop make in the three days altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** "twice as many" means multiply by $2$; "fewer" means subtract.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Find the shirts made on the second day, then on the third day.
3. Add the shirts made on all three days.

::solution
Solution

The number of shirts made on the second day is:
$$1\,250\times2=2\,500\ \text{(shirts)}.$$
The number of shirts made on the third day is:
$$2\,500-300=2\,200\ \text{(shirts)}.$$
The number of shirts made in the three days is:
$$1\,250+2\,500+2\,200=5\,950\ \text{(shirts)}.$$

**Answer.** $5\,950$ shirts.
:::

:::exercise
Mai has $200\,000$ dong. She buys $3$ story books at $35\,000$ dong each and $1$ box of pens for $48\,000$ dong. How much money does Mai have left?

::hint
**What you need:** the money left equals the money she had minus the money she spent.

**Steps:**
1. Find the cost of the $3$ story books.
2. Find the total money she spent.
3. Find the money left.

::solution
Solution

The cost of $3$ story books is:
$$3\times35\,000=105\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
The money Mai spent is:
$$105\,000+48\,000=153\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
The money Mai has left is:
$$200\,000-153\,000=47\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$

**Answer.** $47\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
$6$ boxes of pencils cost $90\,000$ dong. How much do $9$ of these boxes of pencils and $1$ school bag at $120\,000$ dong cost?

::hint
**What you need:** find one unit first: find the price of one box of pencils.

**Steps:**
1. Find the price of one box of pencils.
2. Find the cost of $9$ boxes.
3. Add the price of the bag.

::solution
Solution

The price of one box of pencils is:
$$90\,000\div6=15\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
The cost of $9$ boxes of pencils is:
$$15\,000\times9=135\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
The total money to pay is:
$$135\,000+120\,000=255\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$

**Answer.** $255\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
A garden (made-up numbers) is a rectangle with width $25$ m, and its length is $3$ times its width. A fence is built all the way round the garden, and each metre of fence costs $12\,000$ dong. How much does the fence cost?

::hint
**What you need:** the perimeter of a rectangle is $P=(a+b)\times2$.

**Steps:**
1. Find the length.
2. Find the perimeter.
3. Find the cost of the fence.

::solution
Solution

The length of the garden is:
$$25\times3=75\ \text{(m)}.$$
The perimeter of the garden is:
$$(75+25)\times2=200\ \text{(m)}.$$
The cost of the fence is:
$$200\times12\,000=2\,400\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$

**Answer.** $2\,400\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
A store has $1\,500$ kg of rice, shared equally into $6$ sacks. A lorry takes away $4$ sacks. How many kilograms of rice are left in the store?

::hint
**What you need:** first find the rice in each sack.

**Steps:**
1. Find the rice in one sack.
2. Find the rice the lorry takes away.
3. Find the rice left.

::solution
Solution

The rice in each sack is:
$$1\,500\div6=250\ \text{(kg)}.$$
The rice the lorry takes away is:
$$250\times4=1\,000\ \text{(kg)}.$$
The rice left in the store is:
$$1\,500-1\,000=500\ \text{(kg)}.$$

**Answer.** $500$ kg of rice.
:::

:::exercise
A piece of cloth is $48$ m long. $5$ outfits are cut from it, using $3$ m of cloth for each outfit. The cloth left is cut into scarves, with $2$ m for each scarf. What is the greatest number of scarves that can be cut?

::hint
**What you need:** division with a remainder: the remainder is not enough for another scarf.

**Steps:**
1. Find the cloth used for the outfits.
2. Find the cloth left.
3. Divide the cloth left by $2$.

::solution
Solution

The number of metres of cloth used for outfits is:
$$3\times5=15\ \text{(m)}.$$
The number of metres of cloth left is:
$$48-15=33\ \text{(m)}.$$
We have $33\div2=16$ (remainder $1$), so $16$ scarves can be cut with $1$ m of cloth left over.

**Answer.** $16$ scarves.
:::

:::exercise
A family buys $2$ adult tickets at $45\,000$ dong each and $3$ children's tickets at $20\,000$ dong each. How much does the family pay altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** find the cost of each kind of ticket, then add.

**Steps:**
1. Find the cost of the $2$ adult tickets.
2. Find the cost of the $3$ children's tickets.
3. Add the two amounts.

::solution
Solution

The cost of $2$ adult tickets is:
$$2\times45\,000=90\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
The cost of $3$ children's tickets is:
$$3\times20\,000=60\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
The money the family pays is:
$$90\,000+60\,000=150\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$

**Answer.** $150\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on expressions with letters. An exercise book costs $7\,000$ dong and a pen costs $5\,000$ dong. How much do $m$ exercise books and $2$ pens cost? Write an expression, then work it out when $m=6$.

::hint
**What you need:** the cost of the books is $7\,000\times m$; the cost of the pens is $5\,000\times2$.

**Steps:**
1. Write the expression for the money.
2. Substitute $m=6$ and work it out in the right order.

::solution
The money to pay is $7\,000\times m+5\,000\times2$ (dong). When $m=6$, the money is:
$$7\,000\times6+5\,000\times2=42\,000+10\,000=52\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$

**Answer.** $52\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
A shop has $125$ kg of sugar. It sells $40$ kg in the morning and $35$ kg in the afternoon. Nam solves it like this: "$125-40=85$ (kg); $85+35=120$ (kg). Answer: $120$ kg." Check Nam's solution. If it is wrong, correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** selling makes the amount of sugar go down, so we subtract.

**Steps:**
1. In the afternoon the shop sells sugar: does the amount go up or down?
2. Do the second step again correctly.

::solution
Nam is wrong in the second step: in the afternoon the shop sells another $35$ kg, so the amount of sugar must go down, and we need to subtract, not add.

The correct solution: $125-40=85$ (kg); $85-35=50$ (kg).

**Check.** $50+35+40=125$. Correct.

**Answer.** Nam is wrong; $50$ kg of sugar is left.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A shop receives $4$ cartons of pens, with $12$ boxes in each carton and $10$ pens in each box. The shop sells $275$ pens. How many pens does the shop have left?

::hint
**What you need:** find the number of boxes, then the number of pens, then the pens left.

**Steps:**
1. How many boxes of pens are there in $4$ cartons?
2. How many pens are there in that many boxes?
3. Subtract the pens sold.

::solution
Solution

The number of boxes of pens in $4$ cartons is:
$$4\times12=48\ \text{(boxes)}.$$
The number of pens received is:
$$48\times10=480\ \text{(pens)}.$$
The number of pens left is:
$$480-275=205\ \text{(pens)}.$$

**Answer.** $205$ pens.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
A mother buys $3$ boxes of milk at $25\,000$ dong each and $1$ box of cake for $18\,000$ dong. She pays with $200\,000$ dong. Her change is
A. $125\,000$ dong.
B. $107\,000$ dong.
C. $93\,000$ dong.
D. $293\,000$ dong.
::key B
The milk costs $3\times25\,000=75\,000$ dong; the money to pay is $75\,000+18\,000=93\,000$ dong; the change is $200\,000-93\,000=107\,000$ dong. Option A forgets the cake, option C is the money to pay, and option D is an addition.

::mc
A shop receives $3$ cartons of pens with $12$ boxes in each carton and $10$ pens in each box. The shop sells $250$ pens. The shop has left
A. $360$ pens.
B. $610$ pens.
C. $110$ pens.
D. $11$ pens.
::key C
The number of boxes is $3\times12=36$; the number of pens is $36\times10=360$; the number of pens left is $360-250=110$. Option A has not subtracted the pens sold, option B adds instead of subtracting, and option D works out $36-25$, mixing up boxes and pens.

::mc
$5$ packets of sweets cost $30\,000$ dong. $8$ of these packets of sweets and $1$ box of cake at $25\,000$ dong cost
A. $48\,000$ dong.
B. $55\,000$ dong.
C. $265\,000$ dong.
D. $73\,000$ dong.
::key D
The price of one packet is $30\,000\div5=6\,000$ dong; $8$ packets cost $6\,000\times8=48\,000$ dong; the total is $48\,000+25\,000=73\,000$ dong. Option A forgets the cake, option C multiplies $30\,000$ by $8$, and option B adds $30\,000+25\,000$.

::mc
A plot of land is a rectangle with width $20$ m, and its length is twice its width. A fence is built all round it at $10\,000$ dong a metre. The cost of the fence is
A. $1\,200\,000$ dong.
B. $600\,000$ dong.
C. $800\,000$ dong.
D. $400\,000$ dong.
::key A
The length is $20\times2=40$ m; the perimeter is $(40+20)\times2=120$ m; the cost is $120\times10\,000=1\,200\,000$ dong. Option B forgets to multiply by $2$ for the perimeter, option C counts only the two long sides, and option D counts only one long side.

::tf
Look at the problem: "A shop has $600$ kg of rice. In the morning it sells $125$ kg, and in the afternoon it sells twice as much as in the morning."
a) The shop sells $250$ kg of rice in the afternoon.
b) The shop sells $375$ kg of rice in the whole day.
c) The shop has $275$ kg of rice left.
d) The problem needs three steps.
::key T T F T
a) True: $125\times2=250$. b) True: $125+250=375$. c) False: $600-375=225$ kg; $275$ is a wrong result. d) True: find the rice sold in the afternoon, the rice sold in the whole day, then the rice left.

::sa
A piece of cloth is $48$ m long. $5$ outfits of $3$ m each are cut from it, and the rest is cut into scarves of $2$ m each. What is the greatest number of scarves?
::key 16
$3\times5=15$ m; $48-15=33$ m; $33\div2=16$ (remainder $1$). So $16$ scarves can be cut.

::sa
A shop receives $3$ cartons of pens with $15$ boxes in each carton and $10$ pens in each box, and sells $300$ pens. How many pens does the shop have left?
::key 150
$3\times15=45$ boxes; $45\times10=450$ pens; $450-300=150$ pens.
:::
