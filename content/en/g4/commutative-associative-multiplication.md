A stationery shop packs pens into boxes: each box holds $25$ pens, and each carton holds $4$ boxes. The shop's store room has $7$ such cartons.

How many pens are in the store room altogether? The calculation $25\times4\times7$ has three factors. How can you work it out in your head without setting it out in columns? This lesson gives you three multiplication tricks for calculating quickly and without mistakes.

## Commutative and associative properties of multiplication

### Let's explore

Arrange squares in $3$ rows with $4$ squares in each row. The number of squares is $3\times4=12$. Turn the picture round: now there are $4$ rows with $3$ squares in each, and the number of squares is $4\times3=12$. The number of squares has not changed, so $3\times4=4\times3$.

With three factors, try $2\times3\times4$ in two ways:

- $(2\times3)\times4=6\times4=24$.
- $2\times(3\times4)=2\times12=24$.

The two results are equal. Try other numbers, for example $25\times4=4\times25=100$, and then $(25\times4)\times7=100\times7=700$.

:::theorem Commutative and associative properties of multiplication
**Commutative property.** Swapping two factors in a product does not change the product: $a\times b=b\times a$.

**Associative property.** When multiplying a product of two numbers by a third number, we can multiply the first number by the product of the second and third: $(a\times b)\times c=a\times(b\times c)$.
:::

Why are they true? Commutative: turning the array of squares round does not change the number of squares. Associative: for $25\times4\times7$ we can count the pens in one carton first ($25\times4=100$ pens) and then multiply by $7$ cartons, or count the boxes first ($4\times7=28$ boxes) and then multiply by $25$ pens. Either way gives the same number of pens.

Thanks to these two properties, you can swap and group factors to make round products of tens and hundreds. Learn these common pairs:

- $2\times5=10$,
- $4\times25=100$,
- $8\times125=1\,000$.

We start with a multiplication containing the pair $4\times25$.

:::example Calculating in the easy way (1)
Calculate in the easy way: $4\times37\times25$.

**Solution.** We have $4\times25=100$, a round hundred, so we swap and group these two factors:
$$\begin{aligned} 4\times37\times25 &= (4\times25)\times37 \\ &= 100\times37 \\ &= 3\,700. \end{aligned}$$
So $4\times37\times25=3\,700$.
:::

Next, the pair $8\times125$.

:::example Calculating in the easy way (2)
Calculate in the easy way: $125\times7\times8$.

**Solution.** We have $125\times8=1\,000$, so:
$$\begin{aligned} 125\times7\times8 &= (125\times8)\times7 \\ &= 1\,000\times7 \\ &= 7\,000. \end{aligned}$$
So $125\times7\times8=7\,000$.
:::

Now let's go back to the stationery shop.

:::example The pen store room
A carton holds $4$ boxes, and each box holds $25$ pens. The store room has $7$ such cartons. How many pens are in the store room altogether?

**Solution.** The number of pens in the store room is $25\times4\times7$.

Method 1: multiply $25\times4$ first, giving the pens in one carton:
$$(25\times4)\times7=100\times7=700\ \text{(pens)}.$$
Method 2: multiply $4\times7$ first, giving the boxes in the store room:
$$25\times(4\times7)=25\times28=700\ \text{(pens)}.$$
Both methods give the same result, but method 1 is easier to do in your head.

**Answer:** $700$ pens.
:::

## The distributive property of multiplication over addition

### Let's explore

A rectangular piece of card is $3$ cm high and $6$ cm wide. Draw a line splitting it into two parts, one $4$ cm wide and the other $2$ cm wide.

:::diagram Splitting a rectangle into two parts

point B 6,0 below-right
point C 6,3 above-right
point D 0,3 above-left

point N 4,3 above

label 2,1.5 "3 × 4"
label 5,1.5 "3 × 2"
:::

The area of the whole rectangle is $3\times6=3\times(4+2)=18$. The areas of the two parts add up to $3\times4+3\times2=12+6=18$. Both calculations give the same area, so $3\times(4+2)=3\times4+3\times2$.

:::theorem The distributive property of multiplication over addition
To multiply a number by a sum, we can multiply the number by each addend of the sum and then add the results:
$$a\times(b+c)=a\times b+a\times c.$$
:::

Why is it true? As in the picture, $a\times(b+c)$ is the area of a rectangle of height $a$ and width $b+c$. This rectangle is split into two parts with areas $a\times b$ and $a\times c$.

You can use this property in both directions. Left to right, it helps you work out products in your head; right to left, it lets you take a common factor outside the brackets.

**Connection.** The distributive property is the reason you could multiply a large number by a one-digit number place by place in the last lesson: $1\,245\times3=(1\,000+200+40+5)\times3$. Later you will use it to multiply by a two-digit number.

:::example Working out by splitting a number
Work out $23\times4$ in your head.

**Solution.** We split $23=20+3$ and use the distributive property:
$$\begin{aligned} 23\times4 &= (20+3)\times4 \\ &= 20\times4+3\times4 \\ &= 80+12 \\ &= 92. \end{aligned}$$
So $23\times4=92$.
:::

Now we use the property in the other direction.

:::example Taking out a common factor
Calculate in the easy way: $36\times4+36\times6$.

**Solution.** The two products have a common factor $36$. We take $36$ outside and work out the sum in brackets:
$$\begin{aligned} 36\times4+36\times6 &= 36\times(4+6) \\ &= 36\times10 \\ &= 360. \end{aligned}$$
So $36\times4+36\times6=360$.
:::

Finally, a problem from everyday life.

:::example School uniforms
A school uniform consists of $1$ shirt costing $120\,000$ dong and $1$ pair of trousers costing $80\,000$ dong. Suppose a school buys $5$ uniforms. How much must the school pay?

**Understand.** One uniform is a shirt at $120\,000$ dong and trousers at $80\,000$ dong; $5$ uniforms are bought. We need the total cost.

**Plan.** The cost is $5\times(120\,000+80\,000)$. We can work out the sum in brackets first, or use the distributive property.

**Solve.** The price of one uniform is $120\,000+80\,000=200\,000$ (dong).

The cost is $5\times200\,000=1\,000\,000$ (dong).

**Answer:** $1\,000\,000$ dong.

**Look back.** We check another way: $5\times120\,000+5\times80\,000=600\,000+400\,000=1\,000\,000$. Both ways give the same result. Which way is easier to do in your head here, and why?
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Using the distributive property on a multiplication.** Wrong: $6\times(4\times5)=6\times4+6\times5$. Why it is wrong: the distributive property only works when there is an addition in the brackets; $6\times(4\times5)=120$, but $6\times4+6\times5=54$. Right: $6\times(4\times5)=(6\times4)\times5=120$.
- **Multiplying only the first addend.** Wrong: $5\times(2+3)=5\times2+3=13$. Why it is wrong: the $5$ must multiply both addends. Right: $5\times(2+3)=5\times2+5\times3=25$.
- **Thinking every operation can be swapped.** Wrong: "$8\div2=2\div8$ because multiplication can be swapped." Why it is wrong: the commutative property is only for multiplication (and addition); $8\div2=4$ but $2\div8$ is not $4$. Right: $8\times2=2\times8=16$.
:::

## Summary

- Commutative: $a\times b=b\times a$. Associative: $(a\times b)\times c=a\times(b\times c)$.
- Use these two properties to group factors into round products: $2\times5=10$, $4\times25=100$, $8\times125=1\,000$.
- Distributive: $a\times(b+c)=a\times b+a\times c$; it works in both directions.
- The distributive property does not apply to a multiplication in brackets; the commutative property does not apply to division.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Fill in the missing numbers.
a) $8\times9=9\times\square$
b) $(3\times5)\times2=3\times(\square\times2)$

::hint
**What you need:** the commutative and associative properties of multiplication.

**Steps:**
1. Part a: swap the two factors.
2. Part b: which number is in the middle on the right-hand side?

::solution
a) By the commutative property, $8\times9=9\times8$.

b) By the associative property, $(3\times5)\times2=3\times(5\times2)$.

**Answer.** a) $8$. b) $5$.
:::

:::exercise
Calculate in the easy way: $2\times59\times5$.

::hint
**What you need:** $2\times5=10$.

**Steps:**
1. Find two factors whose product is a round ten.
2. Swap and group those two factors.

::solution
$$2\times59\times5=(2\times5)\times59=10\times59=590.$$

**Answer.** $590$.
:::

:::exercise
Calculate in the easy way: $125\times9\times8$.

::hint
**What you need:** $125\times8=1\,000$.

**Steps:**
1. Group $125$ with $8$.
2. Multiply the result by $9$.

::solution
$$125\times9\times8=(125\times8)\times9=1\,000\times9=9\,000.$$

**Answer.** $9\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $7\times(5+3)$ in two ways.

::hint
**What you need:** $a\times(b+c)=a\times b+a\times c$.

**Steps:**
1. Method 1: work out the sum in brackets first.
2. Method 2: multiply $7$ by each addend, then add.

::solution
Method 1: $7\times(5+3)=7\times8=56$.

Method 2: $7\times(5+3)=7\times5+7\times3=35+21=56$.

**Answer.** $56$.
:::

:::exercise
Calculate in the easy way: $48\times7+48\times3$.

::hint
**What you need:** take out the common factor: $a\times b+a\times c=a\times(b+c)$.

**Steps:**
1. Which factor is common to both products?
2. Add the other two factors, then multiply.

::solution
$$48\times7+48\times3=48\times(7+3)=48\times10=480.$$

**Answer.** $480$.
:::

:::exercise
A carton holds $5$ boxes of biscuits, and each box has $20$ biscuits. The store room has $6$ such cartons. How many biscuits are in the store room altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** multiply three numbers and choose a grouping that gives a round product.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. The calculation is $20\times5\times6$; which two numbers do we group?

::solution
The number of biscuits in the store room is:
$$20\times5\times6=(20\times5)\times6=100\times6=600\ \text{(biscuits)}.$$

**Answer.** $600$ biscuits.
:::

:::exercise
Lan buys $6$ exercise books at $9\,000$ dong each and $6$ pens at $6\,000$ dong each. How much does Lan pay altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** $6\times9\,000+6\times6\,000=6\times(9\,000+6\,000)$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Group in a convenient way: how much does "one book and one pen" cost?

::solution
The amount Lan pays is:
$$6\times9\,000+6\times6\,000=6\times(9\,000+6\,000)=6\times15\,000=90\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$

**Answer.** $90\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on multiplying by a one-digit number. Split $2\,315=2\,000+300+15$ and work out $2\,315\times4$.

::hint
**What you need:** $(a+b+c)\times d=a\times d+b\times d+c\times d$.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply each addend by $4$.
2. Add the three products.

::solution
$$\begin{aligned} 2\,315\times4 &= (2\,000+300+15)\times4 \\ &= 8\,000+1\,200+60 \\ &= 9\,260. \end{aligned}$$

**Answer.** $9\,260$.
:::

:::exercise
A rectangular garden is $7$ m wide. The vegetable part is $12$ m long and the flower part is $8$ m long, side by side. What is the area of the whole garden in square metres?

::hint
**What you need:** area of a rectangle $=$ length $\times$ width; $a\times(b+c)=a\times b+a\times c$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the area of each part.
2. Add them, or find the length of the whole garden and multiply by the width.

::solution
The area of the whole garden is:
$$7\times(12+8)=7\times12+7\times8=84+56=140\ (\text{m}^2).$$

**Check.** $7\times20=140$. Correct.

**Answer.** $140\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Calculate in the easy way: $25\times36$.

::hint
**What you need:** $4\times25=100$ and $36=4\times9$.

**Steps:**
1. Write $36$ as a product of two numbers, one of them $4$.
2. Use the associative property to group $25$ with $4$.

::solution
Since $36=4\times9$:
$$\begin{aligned} 25\times36 &= 25\times(4\times9) \\ &= (25\times4)\times9 \\ &= 100\times9 \\ &= 900. \end{aligned}$$

**Answer.** $900$.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
Given that $a\times7=7\times9$, the value of $a$ is
A. $16$.
B. $7$.
C. $63$.
D. $9$.
::key D
By the commutative property, $7\times9=9\times7$, so $a=9$. Option C is the product $7\times9$ and option A is the sum $7+9$.

::mc
The value of $4\times63\times25$ is
A. $6\,300$.
B. $1\,575$.
C. $630$.
D. $25\,200$.
::key A
$4\times63\times25=(4\times25)\times63=100\times63=6\,300$. Option C is missing a $0$, option B is $63\times25$ and option D is $252\times100$.

::mc
The value of $7\times(20+3)$ is
A. $143$.
B. $161$.
C. $30$.
D. $140$.
::key B
$7\times(20+3)=7\times20+7\times3=140+21=161$. Option A multiplies $7$ by only the first addend and then adds $3$; option D forgets to multiply the second addend.

::mc
Which expression is equal to $9\times4+9\times6$?
A. $9\times(4\times6)$.
B. $9+(4+6)$.
C. $9\times(4+6)$.
D. $(9+4)\times6$.
::key C
Take the common factor $9$ outside: $9\times4+9\times6=9\times(4+6)$.

::tf
Look at the following equalities.
a) $(3\times4)\times5=3\times(4\times5)$.
b) $5\times(2+3)=5\times2+3$.
c) $25\times4\times9=100\times9$.
d) $6\times(4\times5)=6\times4+6\times5$.
::key T F T F
a) True: the associative property; both sides equal $60$. b) False: $5\times(2+3)=25$ but $5\times2+3=13$. c) True: $25\times4=100$. d) False: $6\times(4\times5)=120$ but $6\times4+6\times5=54$.
