:::goals
- State and write the commutative and associative properties of addition.
- Know that adding $0$ to a number gives the same number.
- Use the properties to add several numbers quickly.
- Solve word problems by grouping the addends in a convenient way.
:::

Nam collects waste paper (made-up numbers): $38$ kg in the first week, $47$ kg in the second week and $62$ kg in the third week. How many kilograms of waste paper does Nam collect in the three weeks altogether?

You could add from left to right. But if you notice that $38+62=100$, the calculation becomes much quicker. This lesson shows you why you are allowed to do this.

## The commutative property

### Let's explore

Work out two sums: $38+47=85$ and $47+38=85$. The two sums are equal. Try two other numbers: $125+378=503$ and $378+125=503$. Equal again.

When you swap two addends, the sum does not change. This is the commutative property of addition.

### The commutative property

:::theorem The commutative property of addition
When you swap the addends in a sum, the sum does not change:
$$a+b=b+a.$$
:::

Why is it true? If one bag holds $a$ marbles and another bag holds $b$ marbles, the total number of marbles is $a+b$. You can combine the two bags in either order and get the same number of marbles, so $a+b=b+a$.

:::note Adding zero
Adding $0$ to a number gives the same number: $a+0=0+a=a$. For example, $25+0=0+25=25$.
:::

:::example The commutative property
Work out $125+378$ and $378+125$. What do you notice?

**Solution.** We have $125+378=503$ and $378+125=503$. The two sums are equal, as the commutative property $a+b=b+a$ says.
:::

## The associative property

When you add three numbers, you can add the first two first or the last two first. Try it with $38$, $47$, $62$:

- $(38+47)+62=85+62=147$.
- $38+(47+62)=38+109=147$.

Both ways give the same result. This is the associative property of addition.

:::theorem The associative property of addition
When you add a sum of two numbers to a third number, you can add the first number to the sum of the other two:
$$(a+b)+c=a+(b+c).$$
:::

Why is it true? Both sides are the total of the three numbers $a$, $b$, $c$. The brackets only show which pair you add first, and that order does not change the sum.

:::example The associative property
Work out $(46+54)+17$ and $46+(54+17)$. What do you notice?

**Solution.** We have $(46+54)+17=100+17=117$. We also have $54+17=71$ and $46+(54+17)=46+71=117$. The two results are equal, as the associative property says.
:::

## Calculating quickly

The commutative and associative properties let you swap and group the addends in any way you like. It is best to group numbers whose sum is a round number of tens, hundreds or thousands, to make the calculation quick.

Add three numbers quickly.

:::example Calculating in a convenient way
Work out: a) $38+47+62$; b) $125+267+75$.

**Solution.** a) Since $38+62=100$ is a round number of hundreds, swap and group these two numbers:
$$\begin{aligned} 38+47+62 &= (38+62)+47 \\ &= 100+47 \\ &= 147. \end{aligned}$$

b) Since $125+75=200$ is a round number of hundreds, group these two numbers:
$$\begin{aligned} 125+267+75 &= (125+75)+267 \\ &= 200+267 \\ &= 467. \end{aligned}$$
:::

With four addends, you can make two pairs.

:::example Adding four numbers quickly
Work out in a convenient way: $25+38+75+62$.

**Solution.** We have $25+75=100$ and $38+62=100$. Make two pairs:
$$\begin{aligned} 25+38+75+62 &= (25+75)+(38+62) \\ &= 100+100 \\ &= 200. \end{aligned}$$
:::

Go back to the problem at the start.

:::example Collecting waste paper
Nam collects waste paper (made-up numbers): $38$ kg in the first week, $47$ kg in the second week and $62$ kg in the third week. How many kilograms of waste paper does Nam collect in the three weeks altogether?

**Solution.** The number of kilograms of waste paper collected in the three weeks is:
$$38+47+62=(38+62)+47=100+47=147\ \text{(kg)}.$$
**Answer.** $147$ kg of waste paper.
:::

Finally, a problem with three steps. We work in four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example Selling rice over three days
A rice shop (made-up numbers) has $2\,000$ kg of rice. On the first day the shop sells $475$ kg, on the second day it sells $328$ kg and on the third day it sells $525$ kg. How many kilograms of rice does the shop have left after three days?

**Understand.** We are given the starting amount of rice and the amount sold on three days. We are asked for the amount left.

**Plan.** To find the amount left, we need the total amount sold in the three days. To add quickly, we group $475+525=1\,000$, a round number of thousands. Then we subtract that total from $2\,000$.

**Solve.**

Solution

The number of kilograms of rice sold in the three days is:
$$475+328+525=(475+525)+328=1\,000+328=1\,328\ \text{(kg)}.$$
The number of kilograms of rice left is:
$$2\,000-1\,328=672\ \text{(kg)}.$$
Answer: $672$ kg of rice.

**Look back.** Try adding in a different order: $(475+328)+525=803+525=1\,328$. The same result, as the associative property says. Check the subtraction: $672+1\,328=2\,000$. Correct.
:::

**Connection.** These two properties help you calculate quickly and check results. In later lessons you will meet similar properties of multiplication.

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Using the commutative property for subtraction.** Wrong: $8-3=3-8$. Why it is wrong: subtraction is not commutative; $8-3=5$, but $3-8$ cannot be worked out with natural numbers. Right: only swap addends in an addition.
- **Leaving out an addend when grouping.** Wrong: $38+47+62=(38+62)=100$. Why it is wrong: the addend $47$ was forgotten. Right: $38+47+62=(38+62)+47=147$.
- **Getting a round sum wrong.** Wrong: $38+62=90$. Why it is wrong: $8+2=10$ was worked out in the ones, but the $1$ was not regrouped. Right: $38+62=100$.
:::

## Summary

- The commutative property: $a+b=b+a$. Swapping the addends does not change the sum.
- The associative property: $(a+b)+c=a+(b+c)$. Grouping the addends in a different way does not change the sum.
- Adding $0$: $a+0=0+a=a$.
- To calculate quickly, group numbers whose sum is a round number of tens, hundreds or thousands. Subtraction is not commutative.

Work out in a convenient way: $36+17+64+83$.

::hint
**What you need:** group into two pairs with sums that are round numbers of hundreds.

**Steps:**
1. Find the pair that makes $100$ with $36$.
2. Find the pair that makes $100$ with $17$.

::solution

Find $x$: $x+245=245+78$.

::hint
**What you need:** use the commutative property to compare the two sides.

**Steps:**
1. Rewrite the right-hand side using the commutative property.
2. Compare the two sides to find $x$.

::solution
By the commutative property, $245+78=78+245$. So $x+245=78+245$, which gives $x=78$.

**Check.** $78+245=245+78$. Correct.

Three classes (made-up numbers) collect $125$ kg, $247$ kg and $175$ kg of waste paper. How many kilograms of waste paper do the three classes collect altogether?

:::exercise
A shop (made-up numbers) sells $2\,450\,000$ dong of goods on the first day, $1\,380\,000$ dong on the second day and $550\,000$ dong on the third day. How many dong of goods does the shop sell in the three days altogether?

Remember the lesson on adding numbers with many digits. Work out in a convenient way: $348\,650+275\,480+651\,350$.

:::exercise
Lan works out: $37+48+63=(37+63)+48=100+48=138$. Check Lan's working. If it is wrong, correct it.

**Steps:**

:::exercise Challenge
Calculate quickly: $1+2+3+4+5+6+7+8+9$.

::hint
**What you need:** make pairs whose sum is $10$.

**Steps:**
1. What do you add to $1$ to make $10$? Do the same for $2$, $3$ and $4$.

**Answer.** $45$.

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
The right number to write in $245+138=138+\ \ldots$ is
A. $138$.
B. $383$.
C. $107$.
D. $245$.
::key D
By the commutative property, $245+138=138+245$. Option A repeats an addend that is already there, option B is the sum $245+138$, and option C is the difference $245-138$.

::mc
The value of $38+47+62$ is
A. $147$.
B. $100$.
C. $137$.
D. $157$.
::key A
$38+62=100$ and then $100+47=147$. Option B forgets the addend $47$, and options C and D make a mistake in the last step.

::mc
Work out in a convenient way: $25+38+75+62$.
A. $100$.
B. $200$.
C. $190$.
D. $300$.
::key B
$25+75=100$ and $38+62=100$, so the sum is $100+100=200$. Option A adds only one pair, and options C and D are wrong calculations.

::mc
Which of these statements is false?
A. $a+b=b+a$.
B. $(a+b)+c=a+(b+c)$.
C. $a-b=b-a$.
D. $a+0=a$.
::key C
Subtraction is not commutative; for example $8-3=5$, but $3-8$ cannot be worked out. Statements A, B and D are all true.

Look at the sum $125+267+75$.
a) $125+267+75=(125+75)+267$.
b) $125+267+75=457$.
c) $125+267=267+125$.

::key T F T T
a) True: swap and group the addends. b) False: $125+267+75=467$. c) True: the commutative property. d) True: the associative property.
