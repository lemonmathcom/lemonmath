Pick up a pair of scissors and open the blades to an angle of $40^\circ$. The blades are two lines crossing at the pivot, and the handles are the continuation of those lines behind it. Open the blades wider and the handles open wider too.

If the blades make an angle of $40^\circ$, what is the angle between the handles, and what are the other two angles? One angle is enough to find them all. Carpenters, tailors and technical draughtspeople do this every day, and this lesson shows you why it works.

## Adjacent, supplementary and adjacent supplementary angles

### Exploration: measuring four angles

Draw two lines that cross, then measure all four angles with a protractor. Suppose you get $70^\circ$, $110^\circ$, $70^\circ$, $110^\circ$ going round the crossing point. Two angles side by side add up to $70^\circ+110^\circ=180^\circ$; two angles opposite each other are equal. Is this true however you draw the lines? We shall name these pairs and then explain why.

:::definition Adjacent, supplementary and adjacent supplementary angles
- Two angles are **adjacent** if they have the same vertex, share one arm, and their other two arms lie on opposite sides of the line through the shared arm.
- Two angles are **supplementary** if their sizes add up to $180^\circ$.
- Two angles are **adjacent supplementary angles** if they are both adjacent and supplementary. Then their two unshared arms are opposite rays.
:::

If $\angle xOy$ and $\angle yOz$ are adjacent supplementary angles then $\angle xOy+\angle yOz=180^\circ$. Watch the difference: "supplementary" is only about the sum $180^\circ$, while "adjacent supplementary" also needs the angles to be adjacent. Angles of $100^\circ$ and $80^\circ$ in two different places are still supplementary, but they are not adjacent supplementary angles.

## Vertically opposite angles

:::definition Vertically opposite angles
Two angles are **vertically opposite** if each arm of one angle is the opposite ray of an arm of the other.
:::

:::theorem Vertically opposite angles
Vertically opposite angles are equal.
:::

**Why?** The lines $xx'$ and $yy'$ meet at $O$. The angles $\angle xOy$ and $\angle yOx'$ are adjacent supplementary, so $\angle xOy+\angle yOx'=180^\circ$. The angles $\angle x'Oy'$ and $\angle yOx'$ are also adjacent supplementary, so $\angle x'Oy'+\angle yOx'=180^\circ$. The two sums are equal and share the term $\angle yOx'$, so $\angle xOy=\angle x'Oy'$. A protractor only shows us a few cases; this argument works for every case.

:::example Recognising pairs of angles
The lines $AB$ and $CD$ meet at $O$ as in the figure above.

a) Name two pairs of vertically opposite angles.

b) Name the angles that are adjacent supplementary to $\angle AOC$.

**Solution.** Since $A$, $O$, $B$ are collinear, $OA$ and $OB$ are opposite rays; since $C$, $O$, $D$ are collinear, $OC$ and $OD$ are opposite rays.

a) Each arm of $\angle AOC$ is the opposite ray of an arm of $\angle BOD$ ($OA$ opposite $OB$, $OC$ opposite $OD$), so $\angle AOC$ and $\angle BOD$ are vertically opposite. Similarly $\angle AOD$ and $\angle BOC$ are vertically opposite.

b) The angles $\angle AOC$ and $\angle AOD$ share the arm $OA$ and their other arms $OC$, $OD$ are opposite rays, so they are adjacent supplementary. The angles $\angle AOC$ and $\angle COB$ share the arm $OC$ and their other arms $OA$, $OB$ are opposite rays, so they are adjacent supplementary too.

So a) $\angle AOC$ and $\angle BOD$; $\angle AOD$ and $\angle BOC$; b) $\angle AOD$ and $\angle COB$.

:::example Finding an adjacent supplementary angle
$\angle xOy$ and $\angle yOz$ are adjacent supplementary angles and $\angle xOy=65^\circ$. Find $\angle yOz$.

**Solution.** Since $\angle xOy$ and $\angle yOz$ are adjacent supplementary, $\angle xOy+\angle yOz=180^\circ$. Hence
$$\angle yOz=180^\circ-65^\circ=115^\circ.$$

So $\angle yOz=115^\circ$.

:::example Finding angles from one given angle
The lines $AB$ and $CD$ meet at $O$ and $\angle AOC=70^\circ$ (figure above). Find $\angle BOD$, $\angle AOD$, $\angle BOC$.

**Solution.** Since $\angle AOC$ and $\angle BOD$ are vertically opposite, $\angle BOD=\angle AOC=70^\circ$.

Since $\angle AOC$ and $\angle AOD$ are adjacent supplementary, $\angle AOD=180^\circ-70^\circ=110^\circ$.

Since $\angle BOC$ and $\angle AOD$ are vertically opposite, $\angle BOC=\angle AOD=110^\circ$.

So $\angle BOD=70^\circ$, $\angle AOD=110^\circ$, $\angle BOC=110^\circ$.

## The bisector of an angle

:::definition Bisector of an angle
The ray $Oz$ is the **bisector** of $\angle xOy$ if $Oz$ lies between the rays $Ox$ and $Oy$ and $\angle xOz=\angle zOy$. Then
$$\angle xOz=\angle zOy=\frac12\angle xOy.$$
:::

To draw the bisector of an $80^\circ$ angle, draw a ray making $40^\circ$ with one arm. Another way is to fold the paper so that the two arms lie exactly on top of each other: the crease is the bisector.

:::diagram Ray $Oz$ bisects $\angle xOy$

:::example Angle bisectors
$\angle xOy=80^\circ$. The ray $Oz$ bisects $\angle xOy$, and the ray $Ot$ bisects $\angle zOy$. Find $\angle xOz$, $\angle zOt$, $\angle xOt$.

**Solution.** Since $Oz$ bisects $\angle xOy$, $\angle xOz=\angle zOy=\dfrac{80^\circ}2=40^\circ$.

Since $Ot$ bisects $\angle zOy$, $\angle zOt=\dfrac{40^\circ}2=20^\circ$.

The ray $Oz$ lies between $Ox$ and $Ot$, so $\angle xOt=\angle xOz+\angle zOt=40^\circ+20^\circ=60^\circ$.

So $\angle xOz=40^\circ$, $\angle zOt=20^\circ$, $\angle xOt=60^\circ$.
:::

:::example The scissors
Suppose the blades of a pair of scissors make an angle of $40^\circ$ (figure above). What are the angle between the handles and the other two angles?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* The blades and handles lie on two lines crossing at the pivot. The angle between the blades is $40^\circ$; we need the angle $\alpha$ between the handles and the other two angles $\beta$.

*Plan.* The angle between the handles is vertically opposite the angle between the blades; each angle $\beta$ is adjacent supplementary to the $40^\circ$ angle.

*Solve.* The angles $\alpha$ and $40^\circ$ are vertically opposite, so $\alpha=40^\circ$. The angles $\beta$ and $40^\circ$ are adjacent supplementary, so $\beta=180^\circ-40^\circ=140^\circ$. The other angle $\beta$ is vertically opposite this $\beta$, so it is also $140^\circ$.

So the angle between the handles is $40^\circ$, and the other two angles are both $140^\circ$.

*Look back.* The four angles round the pivot add up to $40^\circ+140^\circ+40^\circ+140^\circ=360^\circ$, one full turn. If we open the blades wider, to $60^\circ$, the angle between the handles also grows to $60^\circ$, while the other two shrink to $120^\circ$.
:::

:::example Bisectors of two adjacent supplementary angles
$\angle xOy$ and $\angle yOz$ are adjacent supplementary angles and $\angle xOy=50^\circ$. The ray $Om$ bisects $\angle xOy$ and the ray $On$ bisects $\angle yOz$. Find $\angle mOn$.

**Solution.** Since $\angle xOy$ and $\angle yOz$ are adjacent supplementary, $\angle yOz=180^\circ-50^\circ=130^\circ$.

Since $Om$ bisects $\angle xOy$, $\angle mOy=\dfrac{50^\circ}2=25^\circ$. Since $On$ bisects $\angle yOz$, $\angle yOn=\dfrac{130^\circ}2=65^\circ$.

The ray $Oy$ lies between $Om$ and $On$, so $\angle mOn=\angle mOy+\angle yOn=25^\circ+65^\circ=90^\circ$.

So $\angle mOn=90^\circ$.

**Remark.** The number $50^\circ$ does not matter: $\angle mOn=\dfrac12\angle xOy+\dfrac12\angle yOz=\dfrac12\cdot180^\circ=90^\circ$ for every pair of adjacent supplementary angles.
:::

:::warning Common mistakes
- **Mixing up vertically opposite and adjacent supplementary angles.** Wrong: "$\angle AOC+\angle BOD=180^\circ$ because they are vertically opposite". Why it is wrong: vertically opposite angles are equal; the sum $180^\circ$ belongs to adjacent supplementary angles. Right: $\angle AOC=\angle BOD$ and $\angle AOC+\angle AOD=180^\circ$.
- **Thinking supplementary angles are always adjacent.** Wrong: "$100^\circ$ and $80^\circ$ are supplementary, so they are adjacent supplementary". Why it is wrong: adjacent supplementary angles need a common vertex and a common arm. Right: "supplementary" is only about the sum.
- **Dropping the condition "lies between".** Wrong: "$\angle xOz=\angle zOy$, so $Oz$ bisects $\angle xOy$". Why it is wrong: the opposite ray of the bisector also makes equal angles with $Ox$ and $Oy$ (both $140^\circ$ when $\angle xOy=80^\circ$) but does not lie between the arms. Right: we need both conditions.
:::

## Summary

- Adjacent supplementary angles share a vertex and an arm, their other arms are opposite rays, and they add up to $180^\circ$.
- Supplementary angles only need to add up to $180^\circ$.
- Vertically opposite angles are equal, because both are adjacent supplementary to the same third angle.
- The ray $Oz$ bisects $\angle xOy$ when $Oz$ lies between $Ox$ and $Oy$ and $\angle xOz=\angle zOy=\dfrac12\angle xOy$.
- The bisectors of two adjacent supplementary angles are perpendicular.

:::exercise
The lines $xx'$ and $yy'$ meet at $O$. Name:

a) The pairs of vertically opposite angles.

b) The angles that are adjacent supplementary to $\angle xOy$.

::hint
**What you need:** vertically opposite angles have arms that are opposite rays; adjacent supplementary angles share one arm and their other arms are opposite rays.

**Steps:**
1. Find the pairs of opposite rays: $Ox$ with $Ox'$, $Oy$ with $Oy'$.
2. For $\angle xOy$, find the angles sharing the arm $Ox$ or the arm $Oy$ with it.

::solution
Since $x$, $O$, $x'$ are collinear and $y$, $O$, $y'$ are collinear, $Ox$, $Ox'$ are opposite rays and $Oy$, $Oy'$ are opposite rays.

a) The two pairs of vertically opposite angles are $\angle xOy$ and $\angle x'Oy'$; $\angle yOx'$ and $\angle y'Ox$.

b) $\angle xOy$ and $\angle yOx'$ share the arm $Oy$ and their other arms $Ox$, $Ox'$ are opposite, so they are adjacent supplementary. $\angle xOy$ and $\angle xOy'$ share the arm $Ox$ and their other arms $Oy$, $Oy'$ are opposite, so they are adjacent supplementary.

So a) $\angle xOy$ and $\angle x'Oy'$; $\angle yOx'$ and $\angle y'Ox$; b) $\angle yOx'$ and $\angle xOy'$.

**Answer.** a) $\angle xOy$ and $\angle x'Oy'$; $\angle yOx'$ and $\angle y'Ox$. b) $\angle yOx'$; $\angle xOy'$.
:::

:::exercise
$\angle aOb$ and $\angle bOc$ are adjacent supplementary angles. Find $\angle bOc$ when:

a) $\angle aOb=120^\circ$.

b) $\angle aOb=35^\circ$.

c) $\angle aOb=90^\circ$.

::hint
**What you need:** adjacent supplementary angles add up to $180^\circ$.

**Steps:**
1. Write $\angle aOb+\angle bOc=180^\circ$.
2. Substitute and work out $\angle bOc$.

::solution
Two adjacent supplementary angles add up to $180^\circ$. Since $\angle aOb$ and $\angle bOc$ are adjacent supplementary angles,
$$\angle bOc=180^\circ-\angle aOb.$$

a) For $\angle aOb=120^\circ$, we have $\angle bOc=180^\circ-120^\circ=60^\circ$.

b) For $\angle aOb=35^\circ$, we have $\angle bOc=180^\circ-35^\circ=145^\circ$.

c) For $\angle aOb=90^\circ$, we have $\angle bOc=180^\circ-90^\circ=90^\circ$.

So $\angle bOc$ equals $60^\circ$, $145^\circ$, $90^\circ$ respectively.

**Answer.** a) $60^\circ$; b) $145^\circ$; c) $90^\circ$.
:::

:::exercise
The lines $AB$ and $CD$ meet at $O$ and $\angle AOC=48^\circ$. Find $\angle BOD$, $\angle AOD$, $\angle BOC$.

::hint
**What you need:** vertically opposite angles are equal; adjacent supplementary angles add up to $180^\circ$.

**Steps:**
1. Which angle is vertically opposite $\angle BOD$?
2. Which angle is adjacent supplementary to $\angle AOD$? Then use vertically opposite angles for $\angle BOC$.

::solution
Since $\angle BOD$ and $\angle AOC$ are vertically opposite, $\angle BOD=\angle AOC=48^\circ$.

Since $\angle AOD$ and $\angle AOC$ are adjacent supplementary, $\angle AOD=180^\circ-48^\circ=132^\circ$.

Since $\angle BOC$ and $\angle AOD$ are vertically opposite, $\angle BOC=\angle AOD=132^\circ$.

So $\angle BOD=48^\circ$, $\angle AOD=132^\circ$, $\angle BOC=132^\circ$.

**Answer.** $48^\circ$; $132^\circ$; $132^\circ$.
:::

:::exercise
a) The ray $Oz$ bisects $\angle xOy=130^\circ$. Find $\angle xOz$.

b) The ray $Om$ bisects $\angle aOb$ and $\angle aOm=17^\circ$. Find $\angle aOb$.

::hint
**What you need:** a bisector splits an angle into two equal angles, each half the original.

**Steps:**
1. a) Divide $130^\circ$ by $2$.
2. b) The original angle is twice $\angle aOm$.

::solution
a) Since $Oz$ bisects $\angle xOy$, $\angle xOz=\dfrac{130^\circ}2=65^\circ$.

b) Since $Om$ bisects $\angle aOb$, $\angle aOb=2\cdot\angle aOm=2\cdot17^\circ=34^\circ$.

So a) $\angle xOz=65^\circ$; b) $\angle aOb=34^\circ$.

**Answer.** a) $65^\circ$; b) $34^\circ$.
:::

:::exercise
Two adjacent supplementary angles differ by $40^\circ$. Find each angle.

::hint
**What you need:** adjacent supplementary angles add up to $180^\circ$; the larger angle is the smaller one plus $40^\circ$.

**Steps:**
1. Let the smaller angle be $y$ (degrees) and the larger $y+40$.
2. Form the equation $y+(y+40)=180$.

::solution
Let the smaller angle be $y$ degrees, $0<y<90$. The larger angle is $y+40$. Since the angles are adjacent supplementary,
$$y+(y+40)=180\Leftrightarrow 2y=140\Leftrightarrow y=70.$$

So the larger angle is $70+40=110$ (degrees). These satisfy the condition and add up to $180$.

So the angles are $70^\circ$ and $110^\circ$.

**Answer.** $70^\circ$ and $110^\circ$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose two straight roads cross at a crossroads, and one of the four angles between them is $62^\circ$. Find the other three angles.

::hint
**What you need:** two crossing lines make two pairs of vertically opposite angles, and each angle is adjacent supplementary to the other two.

**Steps:**
1. What is the angle vertically opposite $62^\circ$?
2. The other two angles are adjacent supplementary to $62^\circ$.

::solution
Call the $62^\circ$ angle $\alpha$. The angle vertically opposite $\alpha$ equals $\alpha$, that is $62^\circ$.

Each remaining angle is adjacent supplementary to $\alpha$, so it equals $180^\circ-62^\circ=118^\circ$, and those two angles are vertically opposite, so they are equal.

So the other three angles are $62^\circ$, $118^\circ$, $118^\circ$.

**Answer.** $62^\circ$; $118^\circ$; $118^\circ$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a ladder leans against a wall. Its foot rests on level ground, and the ladder makes an angle of $75^\circ$ with the ground on the side of the wall. Find the angle the ladder makes with the ground on the other side of its foot.

::hint
**What you need:** the ground is a straight line, so the angles on the two sides of the ladder are adjacent supplementary.

**Steps:**
1. Identify the two adjacent supplementary angles that share the ladder as an arm.
2. Subtract $75^\circ$ from $180^\circ$.

::solution
The ground is a straight line through the foot of the ladder, so the two angles the ladder makes with the ground on either side of its foot are adjacent supplementary. The required angle is $180^\circ-75^\circ=105^\circ$.

So the angle on the other side is $105^\circ$.

**Answer.** $105^\circ$.
:::

:::exercise
The ray $OB$ lies between the rays $OA$ and $OC$, with $\angle AOB=40^\circ$ and $\angle BOC=35^\circ$. Find $\angle AOC$, then find the angle adjacent supplementary to $\angle AOC$. (A reminder about adding angles from Year 6.)

::hint
**What you need:** if the ray $OB$ lies between $OA$ and $OC$ then $\angle AOB+\angle BOC=\angle AOC$.

**Steps:**
1. Add the two given angles.
2. Subtract the result from $180^\circ$.

::solution
Since the ray $OB$ lies between $OA$ and $OC$, $\angle AOC=\angle AOB+\angle BOC=40^\circ+35^\circ=75^\circ$.

The angle adjacent supplementary to $\angle AOC$ is $180^\circ-75^\circ=105^\circ$.

So $\angle AOC=75^\circ$ and the angle adjacent supplementary to it is $105^\circ$.

**Answer.** $75^\circ$; $105^\circ$.
:::

:::exercise
$\angle xOy=100^\circ$, the ray $Oz$ bisects $\angle xOy$, and the ray $Ot$ is the opposite ray of $Oz$. Find $\angle xOt$.

::hint
**What you need:** a bisector halves the angle; $\angle xOz$ and $\angle xOt$ share the arm $Ox$, and $Oz$, $Ot$ are opposite rays.

**Steps:**
1. Find $\angle xOz$.
2. Use adjacent supplementary angles to find $\angle xOt$.

::solution
Since $Oz$ bisects $\angle xOy$, $\angle xOz=\dfrac{100^\circ}2=50^\circ$.

The angles $\angle xOz$ and $\angle xOt$ share the arm $Ox$ and their other arms $Oz$, $Ot$ are opposite rays, so they are adjacent supplementary. Hence $\angle xOt=180^\circ-50^\circ=130^\circ$.

So $\angle xOt=130^\circ$.

**Answer.** $130^\circ$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
The lines $AB$ and $CD$ meet at $O$. The ray $OM$ bisects $\angle AOC$ and the ray $OM'$ is the opposite ray of $OM$. Prove that $OM'$ bisects $\angle BOD$.

::hint
**What you need:** vertically opposite angles are equal; a bisector lies between the arms and splits the angle into two equal angles.

**Steps:**
1. Find the angle vertically opposite $\angle AOM$ and the angle vertically opposite $\angle MOC$.
2. Use $\angle AOM=\angle MOC$ to show the two angles at $OM'$ are equal.

::solution
Since $OM$ bisects $\angle AOC$, $\angle AOM=\angle MOC$.

Since $OA$, $OB$ are opposite and $OM$, $OM'$ are opposite, $\angle AOM$ and $\angle BOM'$ are vertically opposite, so $\angle BOM'=\angle AOM$. Since $OC$, $OD$ are opposite and $OM$, $OM'$ are opposite, $\angle MOC$ and $\angle M'OD$ are vertically opposite, so $\angle M'OD=\angle MOC$.

Hence $\angle BOM'=\angle M'OD$. The ray $OM$ lies inside $\angle AOC$, so its opposite ray $OM'$ lies inside the vertically opposite angle $\angle BOD$.

So $OM'$ bisects $\angle BOD$.

**Answer.** Proved.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc NB
The sum of two adjacent supplementary angles is
A. $180^\circ$.
B. $100^\circ$.
C. $90^\circ$.
D. $360^\circ$.
::key A
Two adjacent supplementary angles make a straight angle, so the sum is $180^\circ$. $90^\circ$ is the sum of complementary angles and $360^\circ$ is a full turn.

C. $20^\circ$.
D. $290^\circ$.
::key B
$\angle BOD$ and $\angle AOC$ are vertically opposite, so they are equal. B is the adjacent supplementary angle, C is $90^\circ-70^\circ$, D is $360^\circ-70^\circ$.

A. $168^\circ$.
B. $84^\circ$.
C. $42^\circ$.
D. $96^\circ$.
::key C
$\angle xOz=\dfrac{84^\circ}2=42^\circ$. A doubles instead of halving, B keeps the whole angle, D is $180^\circ-84^\circ$.

::mc VD
Two adjacent supplementary angles, one of which is $3$ times the other. The larger angle equals
A. $90^\circ$.
B. $45^\circ$.
C. $120^\circ$.
D. $135^\circ$.
::key D
Let the smaller angle be $y$; then $y+3y=180^\circ$, so $y=45^\circ$ and the larger angle is $135^\circ$. D is the smaller angle.
