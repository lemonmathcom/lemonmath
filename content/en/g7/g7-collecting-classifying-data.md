:::goals
- Tell qualitative data from quantitative data.
- Spot data that is unreasonable or unrepresentative, and build a frequency table from a pile of survey answers.
- Read a pie chart, and work out the percentage and the angle at the centre of each sector.
- Draw a pie chart from a table of data, remembering that the percentages add up to $100\%$.
:::

Class 7A has $40$ pupils. Their form teacher wants to know how everyone gets to school, so that the class can talk about bicycle parking and arrival times. She hands out a slip and each pupil writes exactly one answer: on foot, by bicycle, by bus, or on a motorbike driven by a parent. When the slips come back she counts: on foot $4$, bicycle $12$, bus $8$, motorbike $16$ (made-up data).

Those four numbers answer the question, but they are not easy to take in at a glance. Suppose she wants the whole class to see at once "out of every $10$ pupils, how many are taken by a parent". How should she show it? This lesson has two halves. The first is about collecting and sorting data so that it can be trusted. The second introduces a chart that shows the share each group takes of the whole.

## Collecting and classifying data

### Collecting data

**Data** is the information or the numbers we record in order to study a question. There are two usual ways to collect it. One is **collecting it directly**: observing, measuring, interviewing, or handing out a survey like the form teacher of 7A. The other is to use a **ready-made source**: books, newspapers, reliable websites.

### Two kinds of data

Look again at the teacher's slip. One column says "way of getting to school": on foot, bicycle, bus… Another column says "number of pupils": $4$, $12$, $8$, $16$. What is the difference? We can add $4+12+8+16$ to get the class size, but adding "bicycle" to "bus" means nothing. That difference is how we classify data.

:::definition Qualitative and quantitative data
- **Qualitative data** is not numerical: it describes a quality or puts each item in a category (favourite sport, colour, grade).
- **Quantitative data** is numerical, found by counting or measuring (height, number of siblings, test score), and we can calculate with it.
:::

:::note Be careful
Some data looks like numbers but is only a label, such as a footballer's shirt number or a phone number. We cannot add them or find their mean, so they are still qualitative data.
:::

### Frequency tables and reasonable data

After collecting, we classify the data and count how many times each value appears, then record the counts in a **frequency table**. A handy way to count is to tally: for each slip, draw one stroke next to the matching group.

But a neat table is not necessarily a trustworthy one. Before using data, we ask three questions:

- **Is it representative?** We asked the right people, enough of them, and not just one type of person.
- **Is it accurate?** There are no impossible numbers caused by writing or measuring mistakes.
- **Does it add up?** Each person is in exactly one group, and the groups together make the number of people asked.

Here are the first three examples.

:::example Classifying data
Is each of the following qualitative or quantitative data?

a) Each pupil's favourite sport.

b) Each pupil's height (cm).

c) The number of brothers and sisters each pupil has.

d) Each pupil's grade (Excellent, Good, Pass, Fail).

**Solution.**

We check whether the data is a number from counting or measuring.

a) A sport is a name, not a number, so this is qualitative data.

b) Height is a measurement in centimetres, so this is quantitative data.

c) The number of brothers and sisters is a count ($0$, $1$, $2$…), so this is quantitative data.

d) A grade is the name of a level, not a number, so this is qualitative data.

So a) and d) are qualitative; b) and c) are quantitative.
:::

Once the data is classified, we should check whether it can be used.

:::example Reasonable data
Hà wants to find out the favourite sport of the $800$ pupils in her school.

a) Hà asks only $10$ pupils from the school football team. Is the data reasonable?

b) On another occasion Hà writes down the ages of $6$ pupils in Year 7 as $12$; $13$; $13$; $12$; $31$; $13$. Which value looks suspicious?

**Solution.**

a) Members of the football team are likely to enjoy football more than other pupils, and $10$ pupils is far too few for $800$. So the sample does not represent the whole school and the data is not reasonable. Hà should ask more pupils from several year groups.

b) Pupils in Year 7 are usually $12$ or $13$ years old, so $31$ is impossible. Perhaps Hà wrote the digits of $13$ the wrong way round. We do not change it ourselves; we ask that pupil again.

So a) the data is not representative; b) the value $31$ looks suspicious.
:::

Finally, the most familiar job: turning a pile of slips into a table.

:::example Building a frequency table
Hà asks $20$ pupils in Class 7A for their favourite sport (each chooses exactly one). The answers, in the order of the slips, are written with FB: football, BD: badminton, SW: swimming, BK: basketball:

FB, BD, SW, FB, BK, FB, BD, SW, FB, BD, BK, FB, SW, BD, FB, BK, FB, BD, SW, FB.

a) What kind of data is this?

b) Make a frequency table of the number of pupils choosing each sport and check the total.

**Solution.**

a) A sport is a name, not a number, so this is qualitative data.

b) We read the $20$ slips in turn and count each sport:

Football (FB) appears on slips $1$; $4$; $6$; $9$; $12$; $15$; $17$; $20$, which is $8$ times.

Badminton (BD) appears on slips $2$; $7$; $10$; $14$; $18$, which is $5$ times.

Swimming (SW) appears on slips $3$; $8$; $13$; $19$, which is $4$ times.

Basketball (BK) appears on slips $5$; $11$; $16$, which is $3$ times.

| Sport | Football | Badminton | Swimming | Basketball | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pupils | $8$ | $5$ | $4$ | $3$ | $20$ |

We have $8+5+4+3=20$, the number of slips, so the table agrees with the data.

So the frequency table is the one above.
:::

## Pie charts

A frequency table gives exact numbers, but there is a more visual way to see "the share of each group in the whole". Back to Class 7A. What percentage of the class does each group make up? The whole circle stands for $40$ pupils, so how big a "slice" should each group get?

| Way of getting to school | Pupils | Percentage | Angle at the centre |
|---|---|---|---|
| On foot | $4$ | $10\%$ | $36^\circ$ |
| Bicycle | $12$ | $30\%$ | $108^\circ$ |
| Bus | $8$ | $20\%$ | $72^\circ$ |
| Motorbike | $16$ | $40\%$ | $144^\circ$ |
| Total | $40$ | $100\%$ | $360^\circ$ |

Look at how we calculate. The "on foot" group is $\dfrac{4}{40}=0.1=10\%$ of the class. The whole circle is $360^\circ$ and stands for $40$ pupils, so each pupil gets $360^\circ\div40=9^\circ$. The $4$ pupils who walk get $4\cdot9^\circ=36^\circ$. The $16$ pupils on motorbikes get $16\cdot 9^\circ=144^\circ$.

:::definition Pie chart
A **sector** is the part of a circle bounded by two radii and the arc between them. The angle between those two radii is the **angle at the centre** of the sector.

A **pie chart** is a circle divided into sectors. The whole circle represents all of the data ($100\%$) and each sector represents one group; the bigger a group's share, the bigger the angle at the centre of its sector.
:::

:::theorem Percentage and angle at the centre
Suppose the whole data set has $N$ items and one group has $n$ items. Then:

$$\text{percentage}=\dfrac{n}{N}\cdot100\%,\qquad \text{angle at the centre}=\dfrac{n}{N}\cdot360^\circ.$$

The percentages of all the groups add up to $100\%$, and the angles at the centre add up to $360^\circ$.
:::

**Why is this true?** The whole circle stands for all $N$ items, that is $360^\circ$ and $100\%$. A group that makes up $\dfrac{n}{N}$ of the data also makes up $\dfrac{n}{N}$ of the circle, so its angle is $\dfrac{n}{N}\cdot360^\circ$. Since $360\div100=3.6$, there is a quick conversion: **the angle at the centre equals the percentage times $3.6^\circ$**. For example $25\%$ gives $25\cdot3.6^\circ=90^\circ$, exactly a quarter of the circle.

Now let us practise reading it.

:::example Reading a pie chart
Look at the pie chart "How the $40$ pupils of Class 7A get to school".

a) Which way of getting to school is chosen by the most pupils, and which by the fewest?

b) How many pupils come by bicycle?

c) How many times as many pupils come by motorbike as on foot?

**Solution.**

a) The motorbike sector is the largest ($40\%$) and the on-foot sector the smallest ($10\%$). So the motorbike is chosen by the most pupils and walking by the fewest.

b) The bicycle sector is $30\%$ of $40$ pupils, so the number of pupils who cycle is
$$40\cdot30\%=40\cdot0.3=12\ (\text{pupils}).$$

c) We have $40\%\div10\%=4$, so there are $4$ times as many pupils on motorbikes as on foot (also $16\div4=4$).

So a) motorbike most, on foot fewest; b) $12$ pupils; c) $4$ times as many.
:::

Reading is one thing; now we draw. You need a pair of compasses and a protractor.

:::tip Steps for drawing a pie chart
1. Make the frequency table, find the total $N$, then find each group's percentage. Check that they add up to $100\%$.
2. Find each group's angle at the centre. Check that they add up to $360^\circ$.
3. Draw a circle with centre $O$ and one radius $OA$. With the protractor, draw the sectors one after another, in the same direction, using the angles you found.
4. Label each sector with the group's name and percentage, and give the chart a title.
:::

:::example Percentages, angles at the centre and drawing the chart
From the frequency table of favourite sports of $20$ pupils (football $8$, badminton $5$, swimming $4$, basketball $3$), find the percentage and the angle at the centre for each sport, then describe how to draw the pie chart.

**Solution.**

The total is $N=20$ pupils. We use $\dfrac{n}{N}\cdot100\%$ and $\dfrac{n}{N}\cdot360^\circ$.

Football: $\dfrac{8}{20}=0.4=40\%$; angle at the centre $0.4\cdot360^\circ=144^\circ$.

Badminton: $\dfrac{5}{20}=0.25=25\%$; angle at the centre $0.25\cdot360^\circ=90^\circ$.

Swimming: $\dfrac{4}{20}=0.2=20\%$; angle at the centre $0.2\cdot360^\circ=72^\circ$.

Basketball: $\dfrac{3}{20}=0.15=15\%$; angle at the centre $0.15\cdot360^\circ=54^\circ$.

Check: $40+25+20+15=100$ and $144+90+72+54=360$.

Draw a circle with centre $O$ and a vertical radius $OA$. With a protractor, draw in turn, clockwise, the sectors with angles $144^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $72^\circ$, $54^\circ$. Label each sector with the sport and its percentage.

So the chart has four sectors with angles $144^\circ$; $90^\circ$; $72^\circ$; $54^\circ$, as in the figure above.
:::

The last example is a real-life situation, so we go slowly, in four steps.

:::example A family's spending
Suppose a family's monthly income of $12$ million đồng is used as follows: food $4.2$ million đồng; housing, electricity and water $3$ million đồng; education $2.4$ million đồng; savings $1.8$ million đồng; travel $0.6$ million đồng.

a) Find the percentage and the angle at the centre for each item in a pie chart.

b) Which item is exactly one quarter of the income?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* The items add up to $4.2+3+2.4+1.8+0.6=12$ (million đồng), exactly the income, so the whole circle stands for $12$ million đồng.

*Plan.* Divide each amount by $12$ to get its share, then multiply by $360^\circ$ for the angle. One quarter of the income is $25\%$.

*Solve.*

a) Food: $\dfrac{4.2}{12}=0.35=35\%$; angle at the centre $0.35\cdot360^\circ=126^\circ$.

Housing, electricity and water: $\dfrac{3}{12}=0.25=25\%$; angle at the centre $90^\circ$.

Education: $\dfrac{2.4}{12}=0.2=20\%$; angle at the centre $72^\circ$.

Savings: $\dfrac{1.8}{12}=0.15=15\%$; angle at the centre $54^\circ$.

Travel: $\dfrac{0.6}{12}=0.05=5\%$; angle at the centre $18^\circ$.

b) Only housing, electricity and water is $25\%$, which is one quarter.

So a) the percentages are $35\%$; $25\%$; $20\%$; $15\%$; $5\%$ and the angles are $126^\circ$; $90^\circ$; $72^\circ$; $54^\circ$; $18^\circ$; b) housing, electricity and water.

*Look back.* The percentages add up to $35+25+20+15+5=100$ and the angles to $126+90+72+54+18=360$, so the results agree. The travel sector has an angle of only $18^\circ$ and is very thin, so on a real chart we would write its figure beside the sector. If the income rose but every item kept the same share, would the angles change? (No, because an angle depends only on the share.)
:::

:::warning Common mistakes
- **Percentages that do not add up to $100\%$.** Wrong: writing $35\%$; $25\%$; $20\%$; $15\%$; $10\%$ on one chart (that is $105\%$). Why it is wrong: the sectors must fit together to make exactly one circle. Right: always add them up to check they make $100\%$ (after rounding the total may be slightly off, for example $99.9\%$).
- **Using the percentage as the angle.** Wrong: drawing a $25\%$ group with an angle of $25^\circ$. Why it is wrong: the whole circle is $360^\circ$, not $100^\circ$. Right: the angle is $25\cdot3.6^\circ=90^\circ$.
- **Dividing by the largest group instead of the total.** Wrong: the share of the bicycle group is $12:16=75\%$. Why it is wrong: the whole circle is all $40$ pupils. Right: $12:40=30\%$.
- **Letting each pupil choose several answers, then dividing by the number of pupils.** Wrong: the shares add up to more than $100\%$ (see exercise 9). Why it is wrong: a pie chart only works when every item is in exactly one group. Right: divide by the total number of choices.
- **Forgetting to ask who was surveyed.** However neat the chart, it cannot rescue data that is not representative.
:::

## Summary

- Qualitative data is a name or a quality; quantitative data is a count or a measurement that we can calculate with.
- Before using data we check: is it representative, are there impossible values, and do the groups add up to the number of people asked?
- A frequency table records how many times each group appears; the total equals the number of items surveyed.
- The percentage of a group is $\dfrac{n}{N}\cdot100\%$; its angle at the centre is $\dfrac{n}{N}\cdot360^\circ$, or the percentage times $3.6^\circ$.
- In a pie chart the percentages add up to $100\%$ and the angles at the centre add up to $360^\circ$.

Back to the form teacher's question: the motorbike sector of Class 7A has an angle of $144^\circ$ and makes up $40\%$, which means that out of every $10$ pupils, $4$ are taken to school on a motorbike. A bar chart shows how many; a pie chart shows what share, and the two views complete each other. In the next lesson we follow how data changes over time with line graphs. Do a few of the exercises so that these calculations become routine.

## Practice

Each exercise gives what you need and hints step by step. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Is each of the following qualitative or quantitative data?

a) Each pupil's favourite colour.

b) The number of books each pupil reads in a month.

c) Each pupil's time (in seconds) for the $50$ m run.

d) The musical instrument each pupil would like to learn.

::hint
**What you need:** counts and measurements are quantitative; names and qualities are qualitative.

**Steps:**
1. For each part ask: is each pupil's answer a number or a name?
2. Part b) counts books, part c) measures time.

::solution
We ask whether each pupil's answer is a number (from counting or measuring) or a name.

a) A colour is a name, so this is qualitative data.

b) The number of books is a count, so this is quantitative data.

c) The running time is a measurement, so this is quantitative data.

d) An instrument is a name, so this is qualitative data.

So a) and d) are qualitative; b) and c) are quantitative.

**Answer.** a) qualitative; b) quantitative; c) quantitative; d) qualitative.
:::

:::exercise
a) The hours of sleep per night for $6$ pupils are recorded as $8$; $9$; $7.5$; $30$; $8$; $8.5$. Which value is not reasonable? Why?

b) Mai wants to know the favourite type of music in the whole of Year 7, so she gives the survey only to members of the music club. Is this reasonable?

::hint
**What you need:** reasonable data must be accurate and representative of the people we want to study.

**Steps:**
1. A day has only $24$ hours: can someone sleep $30$ hours?
2. Do members of the music club have the same taste in music as the whole of Year 7?

::solution
a) A day has only $24$ hours, so nobody sleeps $30$ hours a night. The value $30$ is not reasonable; it may be a slip (for example $3$ or $8$). We ask that pupil again rather than correct it ourselves.

b) Members of the music club are likely to care about music more than other pupils, so this sample leans towards one group. The data does not represent the whole of Year 7. Mai should give the survey to pupils in many classes with many different interests.

So a) the value $30$ is not reasonable; b) the method is not reasonable.

**Answer.** a) $30$; b) not reasonable.
:::

:::exercise
The numbers of brothers and sisters of $15$ pupils in Class 7B are recorded as:

$1$; $2$; $0$; $1$; $1$; $3$; $2$; $1$; $0$; $2$; $1$; $1$; $2$; $0$; $1$.

a) Make a frequency table of the number of pupils for each value $0$; $1$; $2$; $3$.

b) How many pupils have more than $1$ brother or sister?

::hint
**What you need:** a frequency table records how many times each value appears; the total must be $15$.

**Steps:**
1. Count how many times $0$ appears, then $1$, $2$, $3$.
2. "More than $1$" means $2$ or $3$.

::solution
a) We count how many times each value appears among the $15$ numbers: the value $0$ appears $3$ times; the value $1$ appears $7$ times; the value $2$ appears $4$ times; the value $3$ appears $1$ time.

| Brothers and sisters | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pupils | $3$ | $7$ | $4$ | $1$ | $15$ |

We have $3+7+4+1=15$, which agrees with the number of pupils asked.

b) Pupils with more than $1$ brother or sister are those with $2$ or $3$, that is $4+1=5$ (pupils).

So $5$ pupils have more than $1$ brother or sister.

**Answer.** a) the table above; b) $5$ pupils.
:::

:::exercise
Use the pie chart "How the $40$ pupils of Class 7A get to school".

a) What percentage do the bicycle and bus groups make up together?

b) How many pupils are in those two groups altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** add the percentages; number of pupils $=$ percentage $\cdot$ class size.

**Steps:**
1. Read the bicycle share ($30\%$) and the bus share ($20\%$) and add them.
2. Multiply the total percentage by $40$.

::solution
a) The bicycle sector is $30\%$ and the bus sector is $20\%$. So together they make $30\%+20\%=50\%$.

b) The number of pupils in the two groups is $40\cdot50\%=40\cdot0.5=20$ (pupils).

Check: $12+8=20$, which agrees with the original data.

So a) $50\%$; b) $20$ pupils.

**Answer.** a) $50\%$; b) $20$ pupils.
:::

:::exercise
A survey of $20$ pupils in Class 7B asks for their favourite type of film (each chooses one): animation $9$ pupils, adventure $6$ pupils, science fiction $3$ pupils, comedy $2$ pupils. Find the percentage and the angle at the centre of each type for a pie chart.

::hint
**What you need:** percentage $=\dfrac{n}{N}\cdot100\%$, angle at the centre $=\dfrac{n}{N}\cdot360^\circ$, with $N=20$.

**Steps:**
1. Write $\dfrac{9}{20}$, $\dfrac{6}{20}$, $\dfrac{3}{20}$, $\dfrac{2}{20}$ as decimals.
2. Convert to percentages and multiply by $360^\circ$; finally add up to check.

::solution
The total number of pupils is $9+6+3+2=20$.

Animation: $\dfrac{9}{20}=0.45=45\%$; angle at the centre $0.45\cdot360^\circ=162^\circ$.

Adventure: $\dfrac{6}{20}=0.3=30\%$; angle at the centre $0.3\cdot360^\circ=108^\circ$.

Science fiction: $\dfrac{3}{20}=0.15=15\%$; angle at the centre $0.15\cdot360^\circ=54^\circ$.

Comedy: $\dfrac{2}{20}=0.1=10\%$; angle at the centre $0.1\cdot360^\circ=36^\circ$.

Check: $45+30+15+10=100$ and $162+108+54+36=360$.

So the percentages are $45\%$; $30\%$; $15\%$; $10\%$ and the angles at the centre are $162^\circ$; $108^\circ$; $54^\circ$; $36^\circ$.

**Answer.** $45\%$, $162^\circ$; $30\%$, $108^\circ$; $15\%$, $54^\circ$; $10\%$, $36^\circ$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a neighbourhood collects $400$ kg of waste in a week. A pie chart of the types of waste has these angles at the centre: food waste $162^\circ$, plastic $90^\circ$, paper $72^\circ$, other $36^\circ$.

a) Find the mass of each type of waste.

b) What percentage do plastic and paper make up together?

::hint
**What you need:** mass of a group $=\dfrac{\text{angle at the centre}}{360^\circ}\cdot400$ kg; percentage $=$ angle at the centre $\div3.6$.

**Steps:**
1. A $90^\circ$ sector is $\dfrac{90}{360}=\dfrac14$ of the circle, so $\dfrac14$ of $400$ kg.
2. Do the same for the other three types, then add the masses to check you get $400$.

::solution
a) The whole circle stands for $400$ kg and $360^\circ$, so the mass of each type is $\dfrac{\text{angle}}{360^\circ}\cdot400$.

Food waste: $\dfrac{162}{360}\cdot400=180$ (kg).

Plastic: $\dfrac{90}{360}\cdot400=100$ (kg).

Paper: $\dfrac{72}{360}\cdot400=80$ (kg).

Other: $\dfrac{36}{360}\cdot400=40$ (kg).

Check: $180+100+80+40=400$.

b) Plastic is $90\div3.6=25\%$ and paper is $72\div3.6=20\%$. So together they make $25\%+20\%=45\%$ (that is $100+80=180$ kg, which is $45\%$ of $400$ kg).

So a) $180$ kg; $100$ kg; $80$ kg; $40$ kg; b) $45\%$.

**Answer.** a) $180$; $100$; $80$; $40$ (kg); b) $45\%$.
:::

:::exercise
A pie chart shows the grades of $40$ pupils in Class 7C with these shares: Excellent $25\%$, Good $45\%$, Pass $20\%$, Fail $x\%$.

a) Find $x$.

b) Find the number of pupils with each grade.

c) Find the angle at the centre of the "Good" sector.

::hint
**What you need:** the percentages add up to $100\%$; number of pupils $=$ percentage $\cdot$ $40$; angle at the centre $=$ percentage $\cdot3.6^\circ$.

**Steps:**
1. Add $25+45+20$ and subtract the result from $100$.
2. Multiply each percentage by $40$, then add to check you get $40$.

::solution
a) Since the percentages add up to $100\%$,
$$x=100-(25+45+20)=100-90=10.$$
So $x=10$, that is, "Fail" makes up $10\%$.

b) The number of pupils with each grade is:

Excellent: $40\cdot25\%=10$ (pupils).

Good: $40\cdot45\%=18$ (pupils).

Pass: $40\cdot20\%=8$ (pupils).

Fail: $40\cdot10\%=4$ (pupils).

Check: $10+18+8+4=40$.

c) The angle at the centre of the "Good" sector is $45\cdot3.6^\circ=162^\circ$.

So a) $x=10$; b) $10$; $18$; $8$; $4$ pupils; c) $162^\circ$.

**Answer.** a) $10$; b) $10$, $18$, $8$, $4$; c) $162^\circ$.
:::

:::exercise
Class 7A has $45$ pupils, of whom $18$ like football. Class 7B has $30$ pupils, of whom $14$ like football. Which class has the higher percentage of pupils who like football? (Give your answer to 1 decimal place.)

::hint
**What you need:** to compare groups of different sizes we compare percentages, not the numbers of pupils.

**Steps:**
1. Calculate $\dfrac{18}{45}\cdot100\%$.
2. Calculate $\dfrac{14}{30}\cdot100\%$ and compare the two results.

::solution
The percentage of Class 7A who like football is
$$\dfrac{18}{45}\cdot100\%=40\%.$$

The percentage of Class 7B who like football is
$$\dfrac{14}{30}\cdot100\%\approx46.7\%.$$

Since $46.7\%>40\%$, Class 7B has the higher percentage, even though fewer pupils in 7B ($14$) like football than in 7A ($18$).

So Class 7B has the higher percentage of football fans ($46.7\%$ compared with $40\%$).

**Answer.** Class 7B ($46.7\%$ compared with $40\%$).

:::exercise Challenge
Someone asks $20$ pupils in Class 7A about their favourite sports, and each pupil may choose several. The results: football $12$ choices, badminton $9$ choices, swimming $8$ choices, basketball $5$ choices.

a) If we compare each sport with the $20$ pupils, what do the percentages add up to? Can we draw a pie chart?

b) If we use the total number of choices instead, what are the percentage and the angle at the centre of the football sector? (Give the percentage to 1 decimal place and the angle to the nearest degree.)

::hint
**What you need:** a pie chart can be used only when the percentages add up to $100\%$, that is, when every item is in exactly one group.

**Steps:**
1. Calculate $\dfrac{12}{20}$, $\dfrac{9}{20}$, $\dfrac{8}{20}$, $\dfrac{5}{20}$ and add them.
2. Find the total number of choices $12+9+8+5$, then divide $12$ by it.

::solution
a) Compared with $20$ pupils, the percentages are $\dfrac{12}{20}=60\%$; $\dfrac{9}{20}=45\%$; $\dfrac{8}{20}=40\%$; $\dfrac{5}{20}=25\%$. Their sum is $60+45+40+25=170$, that is $170\%$, more than $100\%$. Because each pupil may choose several sports, the groups overlap and cannot fit together into one circle. So we cannot draw a pie chart this way.

b) The total number of choices is $12+9+8+5=34$. The percentage for football is
$$\dfrac{12}{34}\cdot100\%\approx35.3\%.$$
The angle at the centre is
$$\dfrac{12}{34}\cdot360^\circ\approx127.06^\circ\approx127^\circ.$$

So a) the total is $170\%$, so no pie chart; b) about $35.3\%$ and $127^\circ$.

**Remark.** The chart in part b) shows shares of the choices, not of the pupils. To compare how many pupils chose each sport, a bar chart is more suitable.

**Answer.** a) $170\%$; b) $35.3\%$ and $127^\circ$.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
Which of the following is qualitative data?
A. The heights of the pupils in a class.
B. Each pupil's favourite fruit.
C. The number of brothers and sisters of each pupil.
D. The weekly pocket money of each pupil.
::key B
A fruit is a name, so it is qualitative; the other three are measurements or counts, so they are quantitative.

::mc
Out of $40$ pupils, $14$ come by bus. The percentage who come by bus is
A. $14\%$.
B. $28\%$.
C. $35\%$.
D. $65\%$.
::key C
$\dfrac{14}{40}\cdot100\%=35\%$. A uses the number of pupils as a percentage; D is the percentage who do not come by bus.

::mc
A sector of a pie chart makes up $25\%$. The angle at the centre of that sector is
A. $100^\circ$.
B. $25^\circ$.
C. $75^\circ$.
D. $90^\circ$.
::key D
$25\cdot3.6^\circ=90^\circ$, a quarter of $360^\circ$. B confuses the percentage with degrees.

::mc
A sector has an angle at the centre of $72^\circ$. What percentage of the whole circle is it?
A. $20\%$.
B. $36\%$.
C. $5\%$.
D. $72\%$.
::key A
$72^\circ\div3.6=20$, so the sector is $20\%$ (because $\dfrac{72}{360}=\dfrac15$). C comes from dividing the wrong way round, $360\div72$.

::tf
Use the pie chart "How the $40$ pupils of Class 7A get to school" (on foot $10\%$, bicycle $30\%$, bus $20\%$, motorbike $40\%$). Decide whether each statement is true or false.
a) The motorbike sector has an angle at the centre of $40^\circ$.
b) $12$ pupils come by bicycle.
c) The on-foot and bus groups together make up $30\%$.
d) The number of pupils on motorbikes equals the number of pupils on bicycles and buses together.
::key F T T F
a) False: the angle is $40\cdot3.6^\circ=144^\circ$. b) True: $40\cdot30\%=12$. c) True: $10\%+20\%=30\%$. d) False: motorbike is $16$ pupils, while bicycle and bus together are $12+8=20$ pupils.

::sa
In a pie chart of $40$ pupils, the "Bus" sector has an angle at the centre of $72^\circ$. How many pupils come by bus?
::key 8
$\dfrac{72}{360}\cdot40=8$ (pupils).

::sa
A class has $25$ pupils, of whom $7$ like basketball. What is the angle at the centre of the "Basketball" sector, in degrees? (Give your answer to the nearest whole number.)
::key 101
$\dfrac{7}{25}\cdot360^\circ=100.8^\circ\approx101^\circ$.
