- Split the time allowed according to the marks for each question.
- Build a revision plan by sharing out hours in a ratio and track progress with mock papers.

Suppose Minh has $10$ weeks left before the entrance exam for grade 10. The maths paper in Minh's province lasts $120$ minutes and is marked out of $10$. Minh can spend $6$ hours a week on maths and does not know where to start. Revising everything from grade 6 to grade 9 equally will not fit in the time, while practising only the hardest questions risks losing marks on the basic ones.

The exam differs from province to province (the number of questions, the marks, the time), so look at the **sample paper** and the notice from the Department of Education and Training where you will sit the exam. This lesson is not about one particular paper. It shows how to look at an exam through numbers: which groups of questions it has, how marks and time are shared out, and how to revise sensibly. How should Minh split the $120$ minutes and the $60$ hours of revision?

## What topics are in the paper?

### Explore: reading a paper like a map

The entrance maths paper is usually a written paper of about $4$ to $6$ questions, each split into parts a), b), c). The parts of one question are usually **linked** and go from easy to hard: the first part is basic calculation, and later parts need more reasoning. So getting the first parts right already earns most of the marks of a question.

The topics that appear most often (and the revision lessons that follow this one) are:

| Topic | Often appears as |
|---|---|
| Expressions with roots | Simplify $P$; find the value of $P$ when $x=\dots$ |
| Quadratic equations, Viète's theorem | Solve an equation; find $x_1^2+x_2^2$ from $x_1+x_2$ and $x_1x_2$ |
| Word problems | Form an equation or a system of equations |
| Functions and graphs | Parabola $y=ax^2$ and line $y=kx+b$; find the intersection points |
| Geometry | Circles, cyclic quadrilaterals, trigonometry and lengths in right-angled triangles |
| Statistics and probability | Make a frequency table; find the probability of an event |

Below we use a **made-up** paper for our examples (it is not the paper of any province): time allowed $120$ minutes, marked out of $10$, with $5$ questions.

| Question | Content | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Expressions with roots | $2.0$ |
| 2 | Word problem (equations, systems) | $2.0$ |
| 3 | Equations, systems, functions | $2.0$ |
| 4 | Geometry | $3.0$ |
| 5 | Higher-level application | $1.0$ |

:::definition Mark scheme and levels of demand
A **mark scheme** says how many marks each question and each part is worth; the marks of the paper add up to $10$. The parts of a paper are usually arranged by level: **recall** (apply a rule or formula directly), **understanding** (transform and present a familiar solution) and **application** (combine several ideas or handle a new situation).
:::

**Why share out the time by marks?** Every mark on the paper is worth the same, so a question with more marks deserves more time. If $120$ minutes are spent on $10$ marks, the average is $12$ minutes per mark. But we also need time to read the paper and check our work, so only part of the time is used for solving.

:::example Recognising the topic of each question
Which topic in the table does each question belong to?

a) Given $P=\dfrac{\sqrt x+1}{\sqrt x-1}$ with $x\geq0$, $x\neq1$. Find the value of $P$ when $x=9$.

b) The equation $x^2-5x+3=0$ has two roots $x_1$ and $x_2$. Without solving the equation, find $x_1^2+x_2^2$.

c) A team of workers has to finish a job in a certain number of days. Form a system of equations to find the number of days each group needs working alone.

d) Given the parabola $(P)\colon y=x^2$ and the line $(d)\colon y=2x+3$. Find the coordinates of the intersection points of $(P)$ and $(d)$.

**Solution.** We look at the key words of each question.

a) There is $\sqrt x$ and a value at $x=9$, so this is an **expression with roots**.

b) There are the words "without solving" and $x_1^2+x_2^2$, so this is a **quadratic equation and Viète's theorem**.

c) There are the words "form a system of equations", so this is a **word problem** (solving a problem by forming an equation or a system).

d) There are a parabola and a line, so this is **functions and graphs**.

**Answer.** a) Expression with roots; b) Quadratic equation, Viète; c) Word problem; d) Functions and graphs.
:::

:::example Reading a mark scheme and finding the score
For the made-up paper above, a student's mock paper is marked as follows: question 1 gets $2.0$ marks, question 2 gets $1.5$ marks, question 3 gets $2.0$ marks, question 4 gets $2.0$ marks, question 5 gets $0$ marks. How many marks does the student get? How many marks are still needed to reach a target of $8.5$? What percentage of the marks of the paper do the first three questions make up?

**Solution.** The student's total is
$$2.0+1.5+2.0+2.0+0=7.5\ (\text{marks}).$$

The marks still needed for the target are $8.5-7.5=1.0$ (mark).

The first three questions are worth $2.0+2.0+2.0=6.0$ out of $10$ marks, that is $\dfrac{6}{10}=60\%$ of the marks of the paper.

So the student gets $7.5$ marks, needs $1.0$ more, and the first three questions make up $60\%$ of the marks of the paper.

**Remark.** The first three questions alone make up $60\%$ of the marks. Getting these right is the foundation of every target score.
:::

## Splitting the time allowed

An exam has two kinds of time: time for **solving** and time for **reading the paper and checking**. We work in two steps: take away the reserve time, then share the rest in proportion to the marks of each question.

:::example Splitting the time between questions
The made-up paper has $120$ minutes. A student plans to spend $20$ minutes reading the paper and checking the answers. Share the remaining time between the $5$ questions in proportion to their marks.

**Solution.**

*Understand.* The time allowed is $120$ minutes; $20$ minutes go on reading and checking. The marks of the questions are $2$, $2$, $2$, $3$, $1$ (total $10$).

*Plan.* We find the time for solving, divide it by the total marks to get the minutes for each mark, then multiply by the marks of each question.

*Solve.* The time for solving is $120-20=100$ (minutes). Each mark gets $100\div10=10$ (minutes). So:

- questions 1, 2, 3 ($2$ marks each): $2\cdot10=20$ minutes;
- question 4 ($3$ marks): $3\cdot10=30$ minutes;
- question 5 ($1$ mark): $1\cdot10=10$ minutes.

*Look back.* The time shared out is $20+20+20+30+10=100$ minutes, exactly the time for solving. If question 1 is finished in $12$ minutes, there are $20-12=8$ minutes to spare for later questions. If a question is "stuck" beyond its time, it is better to move on and come back later.
:::

## Planning your revision

Good revision means knowing **what to revise and for how many hours**. We split the available time into two parts: revising the topics and doing mock papers. Then we share the topic hours in proportion to the marks of each group.

:::example Minh's revision plan
Minh has $10$ weeks, $6$ hours a week. Minh spends $25\%$ of the total hours on mock papers. The remaining hours go on the four topic groups matching questions 1, 2, 3, 4 of the made-up paper, shared out in the ratio of the marks of the questions ($2:2:2:3$). How many hours does each group get?

**Solution.** The total is $10\cdot6=60$ (hours). The hours for mock papers are $60\cdot25\%=15$ (hours), so the hours for topic revision are $60-15=45$ (hours).

The parts of the ratio add up to $2+2+2+3=9$, so each part is $45\div9=5$ (hours). So:

- questions 1, 2, 3: $2\cdot5=10$ hours for each group;
- question 4 (geometry): $3\cdot5=15$ hours.

So each of the groups for questions 1, 2, 3 gets $10$ hours, the geometry group gets $15$ hours, and $15$ hours are left for mock papers.

**Remark.** $10+10+10+15+15=60$ hours, exactly the available time. Geometry is worth $3$ marks, so it gets the most hours.
:::

Finally, a sample question in the style of the exam, so you can see how to present a solution and how the marks are shared among the parts.

:::example A sample exam question
**Question 1.** ($2.0$ marks) a) Solve the equation $2x^2-5x+2=0$. b) Solve the system $\begin{cases}x+2y=7\\3x-y=7.\end{cases}$

**Solution.**

a) ($1.0$ mark) We have $\Delta=(-5)^2-4\cdot2\cdot2=25-16=9>0$, so the equation has two distinct roots
$$x_1=\frac{5+3}{4}=2,\qquad x_2=\frac{5-3}{4}=\frac12.$$

b) ($1.0$ mark) From the second equation, $y=3x-7$. Substituting into the first equation, $x+2(3x-7)=7$, so $7x=21$, that is $x=3$ and $y=3\cdot3-7=2$.

So a) the equation has the roots $x=2$ and $x=\dfrac12$; b) the system has the solution $(x,y)=(3,2)$.

**A note on presentation.** Each part is marked by steps: computing $\Delta$ (or the substitution), the correct result, the conclusion. Write all the steps to earn the full marks, especially when a small slip makes the result wrong.
:::

:::warning Common mistakes in exam revision
- **Revising everything equally and running out of time.** Wrong: spending the same time on every topic, including topics worth few marks. Why it is wrong: time is limited, while the first questions and geometry carry most of the marks. Right: share the hours in proportion to the marks, as in $2:2:2:3$ above.
- **Getting stuck on the hardest question.** Wrong: spending $30$ minutes on question 5 ($1$ mark) while question 1 has not been checked. Why it is wrong: one mark on a hard question costs far more time than one mark on a basic question. Right: secure the first parts of every question, then come back to the hard one.
- **Skipping the conditions or the conclusion.** Wrong: solving an equation and writing only the roots, without checking the conditions of the question (for example $x\geq0$, $x\neq1$) and without writing "So …". Why it is wrong: marks can be lost for a missing condition, a root that does not fit, or no conclusion. Right: state the conditions, compare the roots with them and write a conclusion.
- **Doing a mock paper without studying the mistakes.** Wrong: writing down the score and moving on to a new paper. Why it is wrong: the same mistakes come back in the next paper. Right: record each lost mark by cause (arithmetic slip, missing condition, did not know how, wrong diagram) and revise exactly that.
:::

## Summary

- The entrance maths paper is usually a few written questions, each with several parts from easy to hard. The usual topics are: expressions with roots, quadratic equations and Viète, word problems, functions and graphs, geometry, statistics and probability.
- Papers differ from place to place: look at the sample paper and the notice from the Department of Education and Training where you will sit the exam.
- Splitting time: subtract the reading and checking time, then share the rest by marks. Each mark gets $\dfrac{\text{time for solving}}{10}$ minutes.
- Revision plan: set aside hours for mock papers, then share the topic hours in proportion to the marks. After each mock paper, record and fix each type of mistake.
- Present all the steps, state the conditions, compare the roots with them and write a conclusion.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Which topic (expressions with roots; quadratic equations, Viète; word problems; functions and graphs; geometry; statistics and probability) does each question belong to?

a) In the right-angled triangle $ABC$ with $\angle A=90^\circ$ and altitude $AH$, prove that $AB^2=BH\cdot BC$.

b) A fair die is rolled. Find the probability of the event "the number of dots is a prime number".

c) Simplify $A=\sqrt{50}-\sqrt{18}+\sqrt2$.

d) A motorboat travels downstream and upstream. Form a system of equations to find the speed of the boat in still water.

::hint
**What you need:** the key words of a question tell you its topic.

**Steps:**
1. Look for key words such as "right-angled triangle", "probability", "simplify", "form a system".
2. Compare them with the table of topics.

::solution
a) A right-angled triangle, an altitude and a relation: **geometry**.

b) Rolling a die and probability: **statistics and probability**.

c) A root expression to simplify: **expressions with roots**.

d) The words "form a system": **word problems**.

**Answer.** a) Geometry; b) Statistics and probability; c) Expressions with roots; d) Word problems.
:::

:::exercise
For the made-up paper in the lesson, a student is marked: question 1 gets $1.5$ marks; question 2 gets $2.0$ marks; question 3 gets $1.5$ marks; question 4 gets $2.5$ marks; question 5 gets $0.5$ marks. How many marks does the student get? How many more are needed to reach $9.0$?

::hint
**What you need:** the score of the paper is the sum of the marks of the questions.

**Steps:**
1. Add the marks of the five questions.
2. Subtract the score from the target.

::solution
The total is $1.5+2.0+1.5+2.5+0.5=8.0$ (marks). The student still needs $9.0-8.0=1.0$ (mark).

**Answer.** $8.0$ marks; $1.0$ mark short.
:::

:::exercise
Another made-up paper has $90$ minutes allowed and is marked out of $10$. A student spends $10$ minutes reading and checking, and shares the rest in proportion to the marks. How many minutes does each mark get? How many minutes does the $3$-mark geometry question get?

::hint
**What you need:** the time for solving is the time allowed minus the reserve time.

**Steps:**
1. Find the time for solving and divide it by $10$.
2. Multiply by $3$.

::solution
The time for solving is $90-10=80$ (minutes). Each mark gets $80\div10=8$ (minutes). The $3$-mark geometry question gets $3\cdot8=24$ (minutes).

**Answer.** $8$ minutes per mark; $24$ minutes for geometry.
:::

:::exercise
Ha has $8$ weeks left and revises $5$ hours a week. Ha spends $30\%$ of the total hours on mock papers and shares the remaining hours between three topic groups in the ratio $1:2:3$. How many hours does the group with the most hours get?

::hint
**What you need:** sharing in a ratio: find the value of one part, then multiply by the number of parts.

**Steps:**
1. Find the total hours and the hours for mock papers.
2. Split the remaining hours into $1+2+3=6$ parts.

::solution
The total is $8\cdot5=40$ (hours). The hours for mock papers are $40\cdot30\%=12$ (hours), so $28$ hours remain for topics. One part is $28\div6=\dfrac{14}{3}$ hours; the largest group gets $3\cdot\dfrac{14}{3}=14$ (hours).

**Answer.** $14$ hours.
:::

:::exercise
**Question 1.** ($1.5$ marks) a) Solve the equation $x^2-7x+10=0$. b) Solve the system $\begin{cases}2x+y=5\\x-y=1.\end{cases}$

::hint
**What you need:** the quadratic formula; elimination or substitution for systems.

**Steps:**
1. Find $\Delta$, then the two roots.
2. Add the two equations of the system to eliminate $y$.

::solution
a) ($0.75$ marks) We have $\Delta=49-40=9>0$, so $x_1=\dfrac{7+3}{2}=5$ and $x_2=\dfrac{7-3}{2}=2$.

b) ($0.75$ marks) Adding the two equations gives $3x=6$, so $x=2$. From $x-y=1$ we get $y=1$.

**Answer.** a) $x=5$ or $x=2$; b) $(x,y)=(2,1)$.
:::

:::exercise
**Question 2.** ($2.0$ marks) a) Simplify $A=\sqrt{50}-\sqrt{18}+\sqrt2$. b) Solve the equation $(x-1)^2=9$.

::hint
**What you need:** taking a factor out of a root; the equation $X^2=a$ with $a>0$.

**Steps:**
1. Write $50=25\cdot2$ and $18=9\cdot2$.
2. Take square roots of both sides in part b).

::solution
a) ($1.0$ mark) $A=5\sqrt2-3\sqrt2+\sqrt2=3\sqrt2$.

b) ($1.0$ mark) From $(x-1)^2=9$ we have $x-1=3$ or $x-1=-3$, so $x=4$ or $x=-2$.

**Answer.** a) $A=3\sqrt2$; b) $x=4$ or $x=-2$.
:::

:::exercise
After three mock papers, a student records the marks lost: arithmetic slips $1.5$ marks; missing conditions or conclusions $1.0$ mark; did not know how $2.5$ marks; wrong diagram $1.0$ mark. Find the percentage of the marks lost through "avoidable" mistakes (arithmetic slips, missing conditions, wrong diagram) out of all the marks lost (round to 1 decimal place).

::hint
**What you need:** a percentage is the part divided by the total, times $100\%$.

**Steps:**
1. Find the total marks lost and the marks lost through avoidable mistakes.
2. Divide, then convert to a percentage.

::solution
The total marks lost are $1.5+1.0+2.5+1.0=6.0$ (marks). The marks lost through avoidable mistakes are $1.5+1.0+1.0=3.5$ (marks). The percentage is $\dfrac{3.5}{6.0}\approx0.583=58.3\%$.

**Answer.** About $58.3\%$.
:::

:::exercise
A student needs $1.5$ more marks to reach a target. From experience, every $0.5$ marks takes about $6$ hours of revision. The student revises $5$ hours a week. What is the least number of weeks needed?

::hint
**What you need:** find the hours needed, then divide by the hours per week.

**Steps:**
1. Find how many times $0.5$ marks go into $1.5$ marks.
2. Divide the hours by $5$ and round up.

::solution
$1.5$ marks is $3$ lots of $0.5$ marks, so $3\cdot6=18$ hours are needed. The number of weeks is $18\div5=3.6$, so at least $4$ weeks are needed (rounding up).

**Answer.** $4$ weeks.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A student's first three mock papers score $6.5$, $7.0$ and $7.5$. What is the least score the fourth paper must get for the mean of the four papers to be at least $7.5$? Is a mean of at least $8.0$ possible?

::hint
**What you need:** the mean is the total score divided by the number of papers.

**Steps:**
1. Find the total score needed for four papers.
2. Subtract the total of the three papers already marked.

::solution
For a mean of $7.5$ over four papers, the total must be $4\cdot7.5=30$. The total of the first three papers is $6.5+7.0+7.5=21$, so the fourth paper needs at least $30-21=9.0$ marks.

For a mean of $8.0$ the total must be $4\cdot8.0=32$, so the fourth paper would need $32-21=11>10$. The maximum score is $10$, so this is **not possible**.

**Answer.** At least $9.0$ marks; a mean of $8.0$ is not possible.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
An exam allows $90$ minutes and is marked out of $10$. A student spends $10$ minutes reading and checking, and shares the rest by marks. The time for a $3$-mark question is
A. $30$ minutes.
B. $27$ minutes.
C. $24$ minutes.
D. $8$ minutes.
::key C
$(90-10)\div10=8$ minutes per mark, so a $3$-mark question gets $3\cdot8=24$ minutes. Option B does not subtract the reserve time, A uses $10$ minutes per mark, and D is only the time for one mark.

::mc
A budget of $36$ hours is shared between three topic groups in the ratio $1:2:3$. The group with the most hours gets
A. $12$ hours.
B. $6$ hours.
C. $9$ hours.
D. $18$ hours.
::key D
The parts add up to $6$, one part is $36\div6=6$ hours, and the largest group gets $3\cdot6=18$ hours. Option A is the middle group, B is the smallest group, and C is $36\div4$.

::mc
Out of $6.0$ marks lost, $1.5$ marks were lost through arithmetic slips. The percentage lost through arithmetic slips is
A. $25\%$.
B. $1.5\%$.
C. $15\%$.
D. $75\%$.
::key A
$\dfrac{1.5}{6.0}=0.25=25\%$. Option C divides by $10$ instead of $6$, B misplaces the decimal point, and D is the percentage lost through the other mistakes.

::mc
A student's first four mock papers score $7.5$, $8.0$, $8.5$ and $7.0$. For the mean of five papers to be at least $8.0$, the fifth paper needs at least
A. $8.0$ marks.
B. $9.0$ marks.
C. $8.5$ marks.
D. $10$ marks.
::key B
The total needed is $5\cdot8.0=40$ and the total of four papers is $31$, so the fifth needs $40-31=9.0$. Option A just repeats the target, C is the score of one paper, and D wrongly assumes full marks.

::tf
A made-up paper allows $120$ minutes, with $20$ minutes kept for reading and checking. The questions are worth $2$, $2$, $2$, $3$ and $1$ marks. Decide whether each statement is true or false.
a) The first three questions make up $70\%$ of the marks.
b) Question $4$ should get $30$ minutes.
c) Question $5$ should get $15$ minutes.
d) The total time for solving the five questions is $100$ minutes.
::key F T F T
a) False: $6$ out of $10$ marks is $60\%$. b) True: each mark gets $10$ minutes and question $4$ has $3$ marks. c) False: question $5$ has $1$ mark, so it gets $10$ minutes. d) True: $120-20=100$.

::sa
A student needs $2.0$ more marks to reach a target. Every $0.5$ marks takes about $6$ hours of revision. How many hours of revision are needed?
::key 24
$2.0$ is $4$ lots of $0.5$ marks, so $4\cdot6=24$ hours are needed.

::sa
An exam allows $90$ minutes and is marked out of $10$, with $15$ minutes kept for reading and checking. How many minutes should a $3$-mark question get?
::key 22.5
The time for solving is $75$ minutes, each mark gets $7.5$ minutes, so a $3$-mark question gets $3\cdot7.5=22.5$ minutes.
:::
