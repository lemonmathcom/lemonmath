Paper 2 keeps the same routine but changes the types of question: a word problem about a rectangle, Vieta's formulas, and geometry about two tangents from an external point. If a particular group of mistakes cost you marks in paper 1, watch for that group especially in this paper.

I wrote this paper myself following the common structure of grade 10 entrance papers; it is not the paper of any particular province. Set a $120$-minute timer, do the whole paper and only then open the solutions to mark it.

## Sitting the paper a second time: what should be different?

:::definition Three things to do better in the next paper
1. **Reserve time for checking.** Keep about $15$ minutes at the end to check the roots and review conditions and conclusions.
2. **Check in a different way.** For a quadratic equation, use the sum and product of the roots (Vieta's formulas) to check the roots you found.
3. **Look at your old error log.** Before starting, reread the group of mistakes that lost most marks last time, to avoid repeating it.
:::

:::example Planning the time with a checking period
Paper 2 allows $120$ minutes and the marks of the questions are $2.0$, $2.0$, $2.5$, $3.0$, $0.5$. A student spends the first $15$ minutes reading and the last $15$ minutes checking. Share the remaining time between the questions according to the marks.

**Solution.**

*Understand.* The total time is $120$ minutes, of which $15+15=30$ minutes are for reading and checking. The marks add up to $2+2+2.5+3+0.5=10$.

*Plan.* The time for solving is $120-30=90$ minutes; divide by $10$ marks, then multiply by the marks of each question.

*Solve.* Each mark gets $90\div10=9$ (minutes). Question $1$: $2\cdot9=18$ minutes; question $2$: $18$ minutes; question $3$: $2.5\cdot9=22.5$ minutes; question $4$: $3\cdot9=27$ minutes; question $5$: $0.5\cdot9=4.5$ minutes.

*Look back.* The total is $18+18+22.5+27+4.5=90$ minutes, exactly the time for solving. So question $4$ ($3.0$ marks) deserves the most time.
:::

:::example Marking when an arithmetic slip leads to a wrong conclusion
Question $2$ of paper 2 ($2.0$ marks) has this marking guide: choosing the unknown and stating the condition $0.5$ marks; forming the equation $0.5$ marks; solving the equation $0.5$ marks; checking and writing a conclusion $0.5$ marks. A student sets the unknown, states the condition and forms the correct equation $t^2-35t+300=0$, but calculates $\Delta=1225-1200=125$ (wrong), so finds two wrong roots and draws the conclusion from them. How many marks does the student get?

**Solution.** Choosing the unknown and stating the condition is correct: $0.5$ marks. Forming the equation is correct: $0.5$ marks. Solving the equation is wrong because $\Delta$ is wrong: $0.5$ marks lost. The conclusion rests on wrong roots, so it earns nothing: $0.5$ marks lost. The total is $0.5+0.5+0+0=1.0$ (mark).

So the student gets $1.0$ mark. One slip in $\Delta$ cost $1.0$ mark, because once one step is wrong the later steps are no longer correct.
:::

:::example Checking roots with Vieta's formulas
A student solves $x^2-7x+10=0$ like this: "$\Delta=49-40=9$, so $x=\dfrac{7\pm9}2$, that is $x_1=8$ and $x_2=-1$." Use Vieta's formulas to find the mistake and solve again.

**Solution.** By Vieta's formulas, the roots must have sum $7$ and product $10$. For $x_1=8$ and $x_2=-1$: the sum $8+(-1)=7$ is right but the product $8\cdot(-1)=-8\neq10$ is wrong. So the roots are wrong. The mistake is that $\sqrt\Delta=\sqrt9=3$, not $9$.

Solving again: $x=\dfrac{7\pm3}2$, that is $x_1=5$ and $x_2=2$. Check: $5+2=7$ and $5\cdot2=10$, which is correct.

So the equation has roots $5$ and $2$. Checking only the sum and forgetting the product is not enough to catch the mistake, so check both.
:::

:::example Analysing lost marks with an error log
After sitting paper 2, a student is marked as follows: question $1$ gets $1.5$ out of $2.0$; question $2$ gets $1.0$ out of $2.0$; question $3$ gets $2.0$ out of $2.5$; question $4$ gets $1.5$ out of $3.0$; question $5$ gets $0$ out of $0.5$. Find the total score and say which question loses the most marks and what percentage of the lost marks it makes up.

**Solution.** The total score is $1.5+1.0+2.0+1.5+0=6.0$ (marks). The marks lost on each question are $0.5$, $1.0$, $0.5$, $1.5$, $0.5$ and the total lost is $4.0$. Question $4$ loses the most, $1.5$ marks, which is $\dfrac{1.5}{4.0}=37.5\%$ of the lost marks.

So the student scores $6.0$ marks and should give priority to revising geometry (question $4$), then word problems (question $2$).
:::

:::warning Common mistakes in a second mock paper
- **Skipping the checking period.** Wrong: handing in as soon as the last question is done. Why it is wrong: arithmetic and sign slips go undetected. Right: keep the last $15$ minutes to check the roots.
- **Checking only half of Vieta's formulas.** Wrong: being satisfied because the sum of the roots is right. Why it is wrong: the sum can be right while the product is wrong. Right: check both the sum and the product.
- **Spending too long on a hard part.** Wrong: staying on the last part of question $4$ while question $5$ and secure marks elsewhere are unchecked. Why it is wrong: easy marks are lost for lack of time. Right: follow the time plan and move on when a question's time is up.
- **Forgetting to draw the figure first.** Wrong: proving a geometry result with no clear figure. Why it is wrong: vertices and angles get confused. Right: draw the figure as described and write the given values on it.
:::

## Summary

- Reserve time for reading and checking, then share the rest according to the marks of each question.
- Check the roots of a quadratic equation using both the sum and the product of the roots.
- One arithmetic slip in the middle of a question can lose the marks of all later steps; marking against the guide makes this clear.
- Keep an error log by question and by cause, and revise first the group of mistakes that loses the most marks.

:::exercise
**Question 1.** ($2.0$ marks) Given the expression
$$P=\frac{\sqrt x}{\sqrt x-2}-\frac4{\sqrt x+2}-\frac8{x-4}\quad\text{with }x\geq0,\ x\neq4.$$
a) Simplify $P$. b) Find the value of $P$ when $x=16$. c) Find $x$ such that $P=\dfrac13$.

::hint
**What you need:** $x-4=(\sqrt x-2)(\sqrt x+2)$; common denominators and simplifying fractions.

**Steps:**
1. Write the three fractions over the common denominator $x-4$.
2. Factorise the numerator and cancel.

::solution
a) ($1.0$ mark) Over the common denominator $x-4$ the numerator is $\sqrt x(\sqrt x+2)-4(\sqrt x-2)-8=x+2\sqrt x-4\sqrt x+8-8=x-2\sqrt x=\sqrt x(\sqrt x-2)$. So
$$P=\frac{\sqrt x(\sqrt x-2)}{(\sqrt x-2)(\sqrt x+2)}=\frac{\sqrt x}{\sqrt x+2}.$$

b) ($0.5$ marks) For $x=16$ (which satisfies the condition), $\sqrt x=4$, so $P=\dfrac4{4+2}=\dfrac23$.

c) ($0.5$ marks) $P=\dfrac13$ means $3\sqrt x=\sqrt x+2$, so $2\sqrt x=2$, $\sqrt x=1$ and $x=1$. This value satisfies the condition.

**Answer.** a) $P=\dfrac{\sqrt x}{\sqrt x+2}$; b) $\dfrac23$; c) $x=1$.
:::

:::exercise
**Question 2.** ($2.0$ marks) A rectangular garden has a perimeter of $70$ m and an area of $300$ m$^2$. Find the length and the width of the garden. (The figures in this question are made up.)

::hint
**What you need:** half the perimeter is the sum of the two dimensions; two numbers with a known sum and product are the roots of a quadratic equation.

**Steps:**
1. Find the half-perimeter, let one dimension be $x$ and write the other in terms of $x$.
2. Form an equation from the area and solve it.

::solution
The half-perimeter is $70\div2=35$ (m). Let the width be $x$ (m), $0<x<35$. ($0.5$ marks) Then the length is $35-x$ (m).

The area is $300$ m$^2$, so $x(35-x)=300$, that is $x^2-35x+300=0$. ($0.5$ marks)

We have $\Delta=35^2-4\cdot300=1225-1200=25$, so $x=\dfrac{35+5}2=20$ or $x=\dfrac{35-5}2=15$. ($0.5$ marks)

Both values satisfy $0<x<35$. The two dimensions are $15$ m and $20$ m, so the width is $15$ m and the length is $20$ m. ($0.5$ marks) Check: the perimeter is $2(15+20)=70$ m and the area is $15\cdot20=300$ m$^2$.

**Answer.** Length $20$ m, width $15$ m.
:::

:::exercise
**Question 3.** ($2.5$ marks) a) Solve the system $\begin{cases}2x+y=4\\x-y=5.\end{cases}$ b) The equation $x^2-6x+4=0$ has two roots $x_1$, $x_2$. Without solving the equation, find $A=x_1^2+x_2^2$. c) Form a quadratic equation whose roots are $x_1+1$ and $x_2+1$.

::hint
**What you need:** the elimination method; Vieta's formulas; $x_1^2+x_2^2=(x_1+x_2)^2-2x_1x_2$.

**Steps:**
1. In a), add the two equations to eliminate $y$.
2. In b) and c), find the sum and product of the roots, then the sum and product of the new roots.

::solution
a) ($0.75$ marks) Adding the two equations: $3x=9$, so $x=3$. Substituting into the second equation: $3-y=5$, so $y=-2$. The system has the solution $(x,y)=(3,-2)$.

b) ($0.75$ marks) We have $\Delta'=9-4=5>0$, so the equation has two roots. By Vieta's formulas, $x_1+x_2=6$ and $x_1x_2=4$. So $A=(x_1+x_2)^2-2x_1x_2=36-8=28$.

c) ($1.0$ mark) The sum of the new roots is $(x_1+1)+(x_2+1)=6+2=8$. The product is $(x_1+1)(x_2+1)=x_1x_2+(x_1+x_2)+1=4+6+1=11$. These two numbers are the roots of $y^2-8y+11=0$.

**Answer.** a) $(3,-2)$; b) $A=28$; c) $y^2-8y+11=0$.
:::

:::exercise
**Question 4.** ($3.0$ marks) Given a circle $(O;3\text{ cm})$ and a point $A$ outside the circle with $OA=5$ cm. Draw the tangents $AB$ and $AC$ to $(O)$ ($B$ and $C$ are the points of contact). Let $H$ be the intersection of $OA$ and $BC$. a) Prove that the quadrilateral $ABOC$ is cyclic. b) Prove that $OA\perp BC$ at $H$ and $OH\cdot OA=OB^2$. c) Find $AB$, $OH$ and $BC$.

::hint
**What you need:** a tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact; the property of two tangents from an external point; relations in a right-angled triangle.

**Steps:**
1. In a), prove that $\angle OBA+\angle OCA=180^\circ$.
2. In b), prove that $OA$ is the perpendicular bisector of $BC$, then use the right-angled triangle $OBA$ with altitude $BH$.

::solution
a) ($1.0$ mark) Since $AB$ and $AC$ are tangents, $OB\perp AB$ and $OC\perp AC$, that is $\angle OBA=\angle OCA=90^\circ$. So $\angle OBA+\angle OCA=180^\circ$, and the quadrilateral $ABOC$ is cyclic (with diameter $OA$).

b) ($1.0$ mark) By the property of two tangents from an external point, $AB=AC$; also $OB=OC=3$ cm. So $A$ and $O$ are each equidistant from $B$ and $C$, hence $OA$ is the perpendicular bisector of $BC$. Therefore $OA\perp BC$ at $H$, and $H$ is the midpoint of $BC$.

In triangle $OBA$, right-angled at $B$, $BH$ is the altitude to the hypotenuse $OA$. By the relations in a right-angled triangle, $OB^2=OH\cdot OA$.

c) ($1.0$ mark) In triangle $OBA$, right-angled at $B$: $AB=\sqrt{OA^2-OB^2}=\sqrt{25-9}=4$ (cm). From $OB^2=OH\cdot OA$ we get $OH=\dfrac95=1.8$ (cm). The altitude is $BH=\dfrac{OB\cdot AB}{OA}=\dfrac{3\cdot4}5=2.4$ (cm), so $BC=2BH=4.8$ (cm). Check: $OH^2+BH^2=3.24+5.76=9=OB^2$.

**Answer.** $AB=4$ cm, $OH=1.8$ cm, $BC=4.8$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
**Question 5.** ($0.5$ marks) For $x>0$, find the minimum value of $A=x+\dfrac4x$.

::hint
**What you need:** the AM–GM inequality for two positive numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Apply the AM–GM inequality to $x$ and $\dfrac4x$.
2. Find $x$ for which equality holds.

::solution
Since $x>0$, both $x$ and $\dfrac4x$ are positive. By the AM–GM inequality, $x+\dfrac4x\geq2\sqrt{x\cdot\dfrac4x}=2\cdot2=4$. Equality holds when $x=\dfrac4x$, that is $x^2=4$, $x=2$ (since $x>0$).

**Answer.** The minimum value of $A$ is $4$, reached when $x=2$.
:::

:::exercise
A student sits paper $2$ and is marked as follows: question $1$ gets $1.5$ marks; question $2$ gets $1.5$ marks; question $3$ gets $2.0$ marks; question $4$ gets $2.5$ marks; question $5$ gets $0$ marks. Find the student's total score and the number of marks still needed to reach a target of $8.5$.

::hint
**What you need:** the total score is the sum of the marks of the questions.

**Steps:**
1. Add the marks of the five questions.
2. Subtract the total score from the target.

::solution
The total score is $1.5+1.5+2.0+2.5+0=7.5$ (marks). The marks still needed are $8.5-7.5=1.0$ (mark).

**Answer.** $7.5$ marks; $1.0$ mark short.
:::

:::exercise
After paper $2$, the student keeps an error log: arithmetic slips lost $1.25$ marks; missing conditions or conclusions lost $0.75$ marks; did not know how lost $1.0$ mark; wrong diagram lost $0.5$ marks. Find the total marks lost and the percentage of the marks lost through "avoidable" mistakes (arithmetic slips, missing conditions or conclusions, wrong diagram), rounded to 1 decimal place.

::hint
**What you need:** percentage $=\dfrac{\text{part}}{\text{total}}\cdot100\%$.

**Steps:**
1. Find the total marks lost and the marks lost through avoidable mistakes.
2. Divide, then convert to a percentage.

::solution
The total marks lost are $1.25+0.75+1.0+0.5=3.5$ (marks). The marks lost through avoidable mistakes are $1.25+0.75+0.5=2.5$ (marks). The percentage is $\dfrac{2.5}{3.5}\approx0.714=71.4\%$.

**Answer.** $3.5$ marks; $\approx71.4\%$.
:::

:::exercise
The student still needs $1.0$ mark to reach the target. From experience, every $0.5$ marks takes about $5$ hours of revision, and the student revises $3$ hours a week. What is the least number of weeks needed?

::hint
**What you need:** find the hours of revision needed, divide by the hours per week and round up.

**Steps:**
1. Find how many lots of $0.5$ marks make $1.0$ mark.
2. Multiply by $5$ hours, then divide by $3$.

::solution
$1.0$ mark is $\dfrac{1.0}{0.5}=2$ lots of $0.5$ marks, so $2\cdot5=10$ hours are needed. The number of weeks is $10\div3\approx3.33$, so at least $4$ weeks are needed (rounding up).

**Answer.** $4$ weeks.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
For $x>1$, find the minimum value of $B=x+\dfrac4{x-1}$.

::hint
**What you need:** write $B=(x-1)+\dfrac4{x-1}+1$ and use the result of Question $5$.

**Steps:**
1. Let $t=x-1>0$.
2. Apply the AM–GM inequality to $t$ and $\dfrac4t$.

::solution
Let $t=x-1$; since $x>1$, $t>0$. Then $B=t+\dfrac4t+1\geq2\sqrt{t\cdot\dfrac4t}+1=4+1=5$. Equality holds when $t=2$, that is $x=3$.

**Answer.** The minimum value of $B$ is $5$, reached when $x=3$.
:::
