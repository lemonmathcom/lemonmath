Suppose you have finished revising the topics and there is one week left before the exam. The question is no longer "what do I know?" but "how many marks can I score when I sit in front of a real paper, for $120$ minutes, with no notes?" Only a timed mock paper can answer that, and, more importantly, it shows where the marks are being lost.

This lesson has two parts. The first is about **how to sit a mock paper**: planning time, marking, keeping an error log. The second is **paper 1**, with five questions and a solution after each one. I wrote this paper myself following the common structure of grade 10 entrance papers; it is not the paper of any particular province. Attempt the paper first (set a $120$-minute timer) and only then open the solutions to mark it.

## How to sit a mock paper

:::definition The routine for a mock paper
1. **Plan the time.** Subtract the time for reading and checking, then share the rest according to the marks for each question.
2. **Do the secure marks first.** Do the first parts of each question, then the harder parts and the last question.
3. **Present every step.** State conditions, give a reason at each step, check the roots and write a conclusion.
4. **Mark the paper and keep an error log.** Mark with the marking guide, and record each lost mark and its cause.
:::

**Why keep an error log?** If we do more papers without studying our mistakes, the old mistakes keep coming back. When we record mistakes by cause (arithmetic slips, missing conditions, did not know how, wrong diagram), we can see at once which group of mistakes loses the most marks and what to revise first.

:::example Planning the time for paper 1
Paper 1 below allows $120$ minutes. The marks of the questions are $2.0$, $2.5$, $2.0$, $3.0$, $0.5$. A student plans to spend $20$ minutes reading and checking. Share the remaining time between the questions according to the marks.

**Solution.**

*Understand.* There are $120$ minutes, of which $20$ are for reading and checking. The marks add up to $2+2.5+2+3+0.5=10$.

*Plan.* The time for solving is $120-20=100$ minutes; divide by $10$ marks to find the minutes per mark, then multiply by the marks of each question.

*Solve.* Each mark gets $100\div10=10$ (minutes). So question $1$ ($2.0$ marks): $20$ minutes; question $2$ ($2.5$ marks): $25$ minutes; question $3$ ($2.0$ marks): $20$ minutes; question $4$ ($3.0$ marks): $30$ minutes; question $5$ ($0.5$ marks): $5$ minutes.

*Look back.* The total is $20+25+20+30+5=100$ minutes, exactly the time for solving. Question $5$ is worth only $0.5$ marks, so attempt it only when the earlier questions are finished.
:::

:::example Marking with a marking guide
Question $2$ of paper 1 ($2.5$ marks) has this marking guide: choosing the unknown and stating the condition $0.5$ marks; forming the equation $0.75$ marks; solving the equation $0.75$ marks; checking the condition and writing a conclusion $0.5$ marks. A student lets an unknown be $x$ but does not state a condition, forms and solves the equation correctly, then writes the answer without a conclusion. How many marks does the student get?

**Solution.** The student did not state a condition and loses $0.5$ marks for the first step; forming the equation correctly earns $0.75$ marks; solving correctly earns $0.75$ marks; there is no check and no conclusion, so the student loses the $0.5$ marks of the last step. The total is $0+0.75+0.75+0=1.5$ (marks).

So the student gets $1.5$ marks, losing $1.0$ mark only for a missing condition and conclusion, even though the calculation was entirely correct.
:::

:::example Finding a mistake by checking back
A student solves the system $\begin{cases}2x-y=5\\3x+2y=4\end{cases}$ like this: "From the first equation, $y=2x+5$. Substituting into the second equation: $3x+2(2x+5)=4$, so $7x=-6$, $x=-\dfrac67$." Find the mistake and solve again.

**Solution.** Check: if $x=-\dfrac67$ then $y=2x+5=\dfrac{23}7$, and $2x-y=-\dfrac{12}7-\dfrac{23}7=-5\neq5$, so the solution does not satisfy the first equation. The mistake is in the first step: from $2x-y=5$ we get $y=2x-5$, not $2x+5$.

Solving again: substitute $y=2x-5$ into the second equation: $3x+2(2x-5)=4$, so $7x=14$, $x=2$ and $y=2\cdot2-5=-1$. Check: $2\cdot2-(-1)=5$ and $3\cdot2+2\cdot(-1)=4$, which is correct.

So the system has the solution $(x,y)=(2,-1)$. The habit of checking a solution back in the question catches a sign slip on the spot.
:::

:::example Analysing lost marks with an error log
After sitting paper 1, a student is marked as follows: question $1$ gets $1.5$ out of $2.0$; question $2$ gets $1.5$ out of $2.5$; question $3$ gets $1.5$ out of $2.0$; question $4$ gets $2.0$ out of $3.0$; question $5$ gets $0$ out of $0.5$. Find the total score, the marks lost on each question and say which question loses the most marks.

**Solution.** The total score is $1.5+1.5+1.5+2.0+0=6.5$ (marks). The marks lost on each question are $0.5$, $1.0$, $0.5$, $1.0$, $0.5$ and the total lost is $3.5$. The two questions that lose the most marks are questions $2$ and $4$, each losing $1.0$ mark, that is $\dfrac{1.0}{3.5}\approx28.6\%$ of the lost marks.

So the student scores $6.5$ marks and should give priority to revising word problems (question $2$) and geometry (question $4$).
:::

:::warning Common mistakes in mock papers
- **Doing the paper without a timer.** Wrong: working while looking things up and taking long breaks. Why it is wrong: you do not learn how many marks you can earn in real time. Right: set a $120$-minute timer and look at the solutions only after time is up.
- **Marking too generously.** Wrong: giving full marks whenever the answer is right. Why it is wrong: exams are marked step by step. Right: mark with the marking guide and deduct marks for missing conditions, reasons or conclusions.
- **Doing papers without correcting mistakes.** Wrong: doing five papers in a row without looking back. Why it is wrong: the same mistakes appear in the next paper. Right: after each paper, update the error log and revise the group of mistakes that loses the most marks.
- **Not checking the roots.** Wrong: handing in as soon as a result is found. Why it is wrong: a sign slip can be found in a few seconds of checking. Right: substitute the solution back into the question.
:::

## Practice

Below is **paper 1**. Set a $120$-minute timer, do the paper and only then open the solutions. Questions $1$ to $5$ are the exam; the remaining exercises are about marking and planning after the paper.

:::exercise
**Question 1.** ($2.0$ marks) Given the expression
$$B=\frac{\sqrt x}{\sqrt x-1}-\frac1{\sqrt x+1}-\frac{2\sqrt x}{x-1}\quad\text{with }x\geq0,\ x\neq1.$$
a) Simplify $B$. b) Find the value of $B$ when $x=9$. c) Find $x$ such that $B=\dfrac13$.

::hint
**What you need:** $x-1=(\sqrt x-1)(\sqrt x+1)$; common denominators and simplifying.

**Steps:**
1. Write the three fractions over the common denominator $x-1$.
2. Simplify the numerator to a perfect square.

::solution
a) ($1.0$ mark) Over the common denominator $x-1$ the numerator is $\sqrt x(\sqrt x+1)-(\sqrt x-1)-2\sqrt x=x+\sqrt x-\sqrt x+1-2\sqrt x=x-2\sqrt x+1=(\sqrt x-1)^2$. So
$$B=\frac{(\sqrt x-1)^2}{(\sqrt x-1)(\sqrt x+1)}=\frac{\sqrt x-1}{\sqrt x+1}.$$

b) ($0.5$ marks) For $x=9$ (which satisfies the condition), $\sqrt x=3$, so $B=\dfrac{3-1}{3+1}=\dfrac12$.

c) ($0.5$ marks) $B=\dfrac13$ means $3(\sqrt x-1)=\sqrt x+1$, so $2\sqrt x=4$, $\sqrt x=2$ and $x=4$. This value satisfies the condition.

**Answer.** a) $B=\dfrac{\sqrt x-1}{\sqrt x+1}$; b) $\dfrac12$; c) $x=4$.
:::

:::exercise
**Question 2.** ($2.5$ marks) A garment workshop plans to make $1200$ shirts in a certain number of days. Thanks to a technical improvement, the workshop makes $20$ more shirts per day than planned and so finishes $3$ days earlier than planned. How many shirts per day was the workshop supposed to make according to the plan? (The figures in this question are made up.)

::hint
**What you need:** number of days $=\dfrac{\text{total shirts}}{\text{shirts per day}}$; form a quadratic equation and check the condition.

**Steps:**
1. Let $x$ be the number of shirts made per day according to the plan.
2. Form the equation for the $3$-day difference.

::solution
Let the number of shirts per day according to the plan be $x$, $x>0$. ($0.5$ marks)

The planned time is $\dfrac{1200}x$ days. In practice the workshop makes $x+20$ shirts per day, so the actual time is $\dfrac{1200}{x+20}$ days. Since it finishes $3$ days earlier, $\dfrac{1200}x-\dfrac{1200}{x+20}=3$. ($0.75$ marks)

Clearing denominators: $1200\cdot20=3x(x+20)$, so $x^2+20x-8000=0$. We have $\Delta'=100+8000=8100=90^2$, so $x=-10+90=80$ or $x=-10-90=-100$. ($0.75$ marks)

The value $x=-100$ does not satisfy $x>0$, so we reject it. So according to the plan the workshop must make $80$ shirts per day. ($0.5$ marks) Check: $\dfrac{1200}{80}=15$ days and $\dfrac{1200}{100}=12$ days, which differ by $3$ days.

**Answer.** $80$ shirts per day.
:::

:::exercise
**Question 3.** ($2.0$ marks) a) Solve the system $\begin{cases}2x-y=5\\3x+2y=4.\end{cases}$ b) Given the parabola $(P)\colon y=-x^2$ and the line $(d)\colon y=x-2$, find the coordinates of the intersection points $A$ and $B$ of $(P)$ and $(d)$. c) Find the area of triangle $OAB$ ($O$ is the origin).

::hint
**What you need:** substitution; the equation for the $x$-coordinates of the intersection points; splitting triangle $OAB$ along the $y$-axis.

**Steps:**
1. From the first equation, write $y$ in terms of $x$.
2. Solve $-x^2=x-2$, then find the point $C$ where $(d)$ meets the $y$-axis.

::solution
a) ($0.75$ marks) From $2x-y=5$ we get $y=2x-5$. Substituting into the second equation: $3x+2(2x-5)=4$, so $7x=14$, $x=2$ and $y=-1$. The system has the solution $(x,y)=(2,-1)$.

b) ($0.75$ marks) The equation for the $x$-coordinates: $-x^2=x-2$, that is $x^2+x-2=0$. Since $a+b+c=1+1-2=0$, we have $x=1$ or $x=-2$. For $x=1$ we get $y=-1$; for $x=-2$ we get $y=-4$. So $A(1,-1)$ and $B(-2,-4)$.

c) ($0.5$ marks) The line $(d)$ meets the $y$-axis at $C(0,-2)$, so $OC=2$. So $S_{OAB}=\dfrac12\cdot OC\cdot(|x_A|+|x_B|)=\dfrac12\cdot2\cdot(1+2)=3$.

**Answer.** a) $(2,-1)$; b) $A(1,-1)$, $B(-2,-4)$; c) $3$ (square units).
:::

:::exercise
**Question 4.** ($3.0$ marks) Given a circle $(O)$ with diameter $AB=10$ cm. Take a point $M$ on the circle ($M$ different from $A$ and $B$) such that $AM=6$ cm. The tangent to $(O)$ at $A$ meets the line $BM$ at $C$. a) Prove that $\angle AMB=90^\circ$ and find $BM$. b) Prove that $AM^2=MB\cdot MC$. c) Find $BC$, $MC$ and $AC$.

::hint
**What you need:** the angle in a semicircle; a tangent is perpendicular to the radius; relations in the right-angled triangle $ABC$ (right-angled at $A$, altitude $AM$).

**Steps:**
1. In part a), $AB$ is a diameter; use Pythagoras' theorem to find $BM$.
2. In parts b) and c), use $AM^2=MB\cdot MC$ and $AB^2=BM\cdot BC$.

::solution
a) ($1.0$ mark) Since $\angle AMB$ is an inscribed angle standing on the semicircle with diameter $AB$, we have $\angle AMB=90^\circ$. In triangle $AMB$, right-angled at $M$: $BM=\sqrt{AB^2-AM^2}=\sqrt{100-36}=8$ (cm).

b) ($1.0$ mark) Since $AC$ is the tangent at $A$, $AC\perp AB$, that is triangle $ABC$ is right-angled at $A$. Since $\angle AMB=90^\circ$, $AM\perp BC$ at $M$, so $AM$ is the altitude to the hypotenuse $BC$. By the relations in a right-angled triangle, $AM^2=MB\cdot MC$.

c) ($1.0$ mark) Also by the relations in a right-angled triangle, $AB^2=BM\cdot BC$, so $BC=\dfrac{100}8=12.5$ (cm). So $MC=BC-BM=12.5-8=4.5$ (cm). Check: $MB\cdot MC=8\cdot4.5=36=AM^2$. In triangle $ABC$, right-angled at $A$: $AC=\sqrt{BC^2-AB^2}=\sqrt{156.25-100}=7.5$ (cm).

**Answer.** a) $BM=8$ cm; c) $BC=12.5$ cm, $MC=4.5$ cm, $AC=7.5$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
**Question 5.** ($0.5$ marks) Let $a$ and $b$ be positive real numbers with $a+b=2$. Prove that $ab\leq1$.

::hint
**What you need:** $(a-b)^2\geq0$ for all $a$, $b$.

**Steps:**
1. Expand $(a-b)^2\geq0$.
2. Compare with $(a+b)^2$.

::solution
Since $(a-b)^2\geq0$, we have $a^2+b^2\geq2ab$. So $(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2\geq4ab$. With $a+b=2$ we get $4\geq4ab$, that is $ab\leq1$. Equality holds when $a=b=1$.

**Answer.** $ab\leq1$.
:::

:::exercise
A student sits paper $1$ and is marked as follows: question $1$ gets $2.0$ marks; question $2$ gets $2.0$ marks; question $3$ gets $1.25$ marks; question $4$ gets $2.5$ marks; question $5$ gets $0$ marks. Find the student's total score and the number of marks still needed to reach a target of $8.5$.

::hint
**What you need:** the total score is the sum of the marks of the questions.

**Steps:**
1. Add the marks of the five questions.
2. Subtract the total score from the target.

::solution
The total score is $2.0+2.0+1.25+2.5+0=7.75$ (marks). The marks still needed are $8.5-7.75=0.75$ (marks).

**Answer.** $7.75$ marks; $0.75$ marks short.
:::

:::exercise
After paper $1$, the student keeps an error log: arithmetic slips lost $1.0$ mark; missing conditions or conclusions lost $0.5$ marks; did not know how lost $1.25$ marks; wrong diagram lost $0.25$ marks. Find the total marks lost and the percentage of the marks lost through "avoidable" mistakes (arithmetic slips, missing conditions, wrong diagram), rounded to 1 decimal place.

::hint
**What you need:** percentage $=\dfrac{\text{part}}{\text{total}}\cdot100\%$.

**Steps:**
1. Find the total marks lost and the marks lost through avoidable mistakes.
2. Divide, then convert to a percentage.

::solution
The total marks lost are $1.0+0.5+1.25+0.25=3.0$ (marks). The marks lost through avoidable mistakes are $1.0+0.5+0.25=1.75$ (marks). The percentage is $\dfrac{1.75}{3.0}\approx0.583=58.3\%$.

**Answer.** $3.0$ marks; $\approx58.3\%$.
:::

:::exercise
The student still needs $0.75$ marks to reach the target. From experience, every $0.5$ marks takes about $6$ hours of revision, and the student revises $4$ hours a week. What is the least number of weeks needed?

::hint
**What you need:** find the hours of revision needed, divide by the hours per week and round up.

**Steps:**
1. Find how many lots of $0.5$ marks make $0.75$ marks.
2. Multiply by $6$ hours, then divide by $4$.

::solution
$0.75$ marks is $\dfrac{0.75}{0.5}=1.5$ lots of $0.5$ marks, so $1.5\cdot6=9$ hours are needed. The number of weeks is $9\div4=2.25$, so at least $3$ weeks are needed (rounding up).

**Answer.** $3$ weeks.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Let $a$ and $b$ be positive real numbers with $a+b=2$. Prove that $\dfrac1a+\dfrac1b\geq2$.

::hint
**What you need:** the result of Question $5$: $ab\leq1$.

**Steps:**
1. Write $\dfrac1a+\dfrac1b$ as a single fraction.
2. Use $a+b=2$ and $ab\leq1$.

::solution
We have $\dfrac1a+\dfrac1b=\dfrac{a+b}{ab}=\dfrac2{ab}$. Since $0<ab\leq1$, we have $\dfrac1{ab}\geq1$, so $\dfrac2{ab}\geq2$. So $\dfrac1a+\dfrac1b\geq2$, with equality when $a=b=1$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac1a+\dfrac1b\geq2$.
:::

B. $(2,1)$.
C. $(-1,2)$.

::key A
Substituting $y=2x-5$ into the second equation gives $7x=14$, $x=2$ and $y=-1$. Option C swaps the two unknowns, B has the wrong sign for $y$, and D does not satisfy the second equation.

A. $6.4$.
B. $12.5$.
C. $8$.
D. $12$.
::key B
$AB^2=BM\cdot BC$, so $BC=\dfrac{100}8=12.5$. Option A is $\dfrac{BM^2}{AB}$, C is $BM$, and D is $AB+2$.

::mc
A workshop plans to make $1200$ shirts at $80$ shirts a day. The planned number of days is
A. $80$.
B. $12$.
C. $15$.
D. $10$.
::key C
$1200\div80=15$ days. Option B is the actual number of days ($1200\div100$), A is the number of shirts per day, and D is $1200\div120$.

Given the parabola $(P)\colon y=-x^2$ and the line $(d)\colon y=x-2$. Decide whether each statement is true or false.
a) $(d)$ is tangent to $(P)$.
b) The point $(1,1)$ is an intersection point of $(P)$ and $(d)$.
c) The point $(-2,-4)$ is an intersection point of $(P)$ and $(d)$.

::key F F T F
a) False: $\Delta=1+8=9>0$, so $(d)$ meets $(P)$ at two points. b) False: the intersection point is $(1,-1)$. c) True: $-(-2)^2=-4$ and $-2-2=-4$. d) False: the area is $3$.
