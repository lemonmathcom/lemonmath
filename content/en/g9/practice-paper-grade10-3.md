This is the third mock paper. After two papers you have an error log and you know which groups of mistakes keep coming back. What matters now is not just doing more papers but **checking that your marks are improving** and that your way of working has become steadier.

I wrote this paper myself following the common structure of grade 10 entrance papers; it is not the paper of any particular province. Set a $120$-minute timer, do the whole paper and only then open the solutions to mark it.

## Sitting the paper a third time: priorities and progress

:::definition Two tools for the final stretch
1. **Order of priority.** Work out $\dfrac{\text{marks}}{\text{minutes}}$ for each part: do the parts with a high ratio (many marks, little time) first and those with a low ratio later.
2. **Progress table.** Record the score of each paper in a table, find the mean score of the papers and compare it with the target to see how many marks are still missing.
:::

**Note.** The order of priority only helps arrange the work during the exam. Every part must still be written out in full, because missing steps lose marks even when the answer is right.

:::example Choosing the order of work by marks per minute
A student estimates the time for some parts of paper 3: part 1a ($0.75$ marks) takes $6$ minutes; part 3b ($0.75$ marks) takes $5$ minutes; part 4c ($1.0$ mark) takes $15$ minutes; question $5$ ($0.5$ marks) takes $10$ minutes. Put the parts in the order they should be done.

**Solution.**

*Understand.* We must rank four parts by how much they pay in marks compared with the time they take.

*Plan.* Work out $\dfrac{\text{marks}}{\text{minutes}}$ for each part and rank from largest to smallest.

*Solve.* Part 1a: $\dfrac{0.75}6=0.125$. Part 3b: $\dfrac{0.75}5=0.15$. Part 4c: $\dfrac{1.0}{15}\approx0.067$. Question 5: $\dfrac{0.5}{10}=0.05$. From largest to smallest: part 3b, part 1a, part 4c, question 5.

*Look back.* This order shows that parts 3b and 1a should come first and question $5$ last. In practice, though, it is still better to work question by question, which is easier to check, and to jump to another question only when stuck.
:::

:::example Marking a geometry question with a marking guide
Question $4$ of paper 3 ($3.0$ marks) has this marking guide: part a $1.0$ mark; part b $1.0$ mark; part c consists of proving $AD\perp BC$ ($0.5$ marks) and proving $AH\cdot AD=AF\cdot AB$ ($0.5$ marks). A student does part a correctly; in part b proves the two triangles similar correctly but forgets to mention the common angle and loses $0.25$ marks; in part c proves only $AD\perp BC$. How many marks does the student get for question $4$?

**Solution.** Part a earns $1.0$ mark. Part b earns $1.0-0.25=0.75$ (marks). Part c earns $0.5$ marks for proving $AD\perp BC$ and $0$ for the rest, that is $0.5$ marks. The total is $1.0+0.75+0.5=2.25$ (marks).

So the student gets $2.25$ out of $3.0$ marks. The marks were lost mainly through one missing reason and an unfinished part c.
:::

:::example Checking an answer by substituting it into the question
A student solves the motion problem of paper 3 (distance $AB=90$ km; the car is $15$ km/h faster than the motorbike and arrives $1$ hour earlier) and concludes "the speed of the motorbike is $45$ km/h". Check the answer and correct it if it is wrong.

**Solution.** If the motorbike travels at $45$ km/h then the car travels at $60$ km/h. The motorbike takes $\dfrac{90}{45}=2$ (hours) and the car takes $\dfrac{90}{60}=1.5$ (hours). The difference is $2-1.5=0.5$ hours, not $1$ hour, so the answer is wrong.

Trying $30$ km/h: the motorbike takes $\dfrac{90}{30}=3$ hours and the car at $45$ km/h takes $\dfrac{90}{45}=2$ hours, a difference of $1$ hour, which is correct.

So the speed of the motorbike is $30$ km/h. Substituting an answer into the question takes about a minute but also catches the mistake of picking the wrong root.
:::

:::example Tracking progress over three papers
Three mock papers score $6.5$, $7.5$ and $8.0$. Find the mean score of the three papers (to 2 decimal places), the increase from paper $1$ to paper $3$ as a percentage of the score of paper $1$ (to 1 decimal place), and the marks still missing from paper $3$ to reach a target of $8.5$.

**Solution.** The mean score is $\dfrac{6.5+7.5+8.0}3=\dfrac{22}3\approx7.33$. The increase from paper $1$ to paper $3$ is $8.0-6.5=1.5$, which is $\dfrac{1.5}{6.5}\approx0.231=23.1\%$ of paper $1$. From paper $3$ the marks still missing are $8.5-8.0=0.5$ (marks).

So the mean is about $7.33$, the increase is about $23.1\%$ and $0.5$ marks are still missing. From this table you can see you are heading in the right direction.
:::

:::warning Common mistakes in the final stretch
- **Looking only at the total score.** Wrong: being content when the score goes up and not rereading the mistakes. Why it is wrong: old mistakes may still be there. Right: compare this paper's error log with the previous one.
- **Doing new papers and skipping the old mistakes.** Wrong: doing several papers in a day. Why it is wrong: you get tired and do not fix the mistakes. Right: after each paper, take time to correct it and revise the parts that lost marks.
- **Using the order of priority as an excuse to skip working.** Wrong: rushing the "easy marks" and leaving out steps. Why it is wrong: missing steps still lose marks. Right: use the order to decide what to do first, but still write every step.
- **Not checking the answer to a real-life problem.** Wrong: writing the conclusion straight after solving the equation. Why it is wrong: you may pick the wrong root or misread a quantity. Right: substitute the answer into the question.
:::

**Connections.** Paper 3 reuses the topics already revised: square roots, inequalities, probability and statistics, motion problems, parabolas and lines, cyclic quadrilaterals and inequalities. The progress table continues the error logs of the two earlier papers.

:::exercise
**Question 1.** ($2.0$ marks) a) Simplify $A=\sqrt{7-4\sqrt3}+\sqrt{12}$. b) Solve the inequality $\dfrac{x-1}2\geq\dfrac{2x+1}3-1$. c) A box contains $12$ balls of the same size: $5$ green, $4$ red and $3$ yellow. One ball is chosen at random. Find the probability that the ball is not red.

::hint
**What you need:** $7-4\sqrt3=(2-\sqrt3)^2$; clearing denominators when solving an inequality; probability is the number of favourable outcomes divided by the number of possible outcomes.

**Steps:**
1. In a), write the expression under the first root as a square.
2. In b), multiply both sides by $6$.
3. In c), count the balls that are not red.

::solution
a) ($0.75$ marks) We have $7-4\sqrt3=(2-\sqrt3)^2$ and $2-\sqrt3>0$, so $\sqrt{7-4\sqrt3}=2-\sqrt3$. Also $\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt3$. So $A=2-\sqrt3+2\sqrt3=2+\sqrt3$.

b) ($0.75$ marks) Multiplying both sides by $6$: $3(x-1)\geq2(2x+1)-6$, that is $3x-3\geq4x-4$. So $x\leq1$. The solution of the inequality is $x\leq1$.

c) ($0.5$ marks) There are $12$ equally likely outcomes. The number of balls that are not red is $5+3=8$. The required probability is $\dfrac8{12}=\dfrac23$.

**Answer.** a) $A=2+\sqrt3$; b) $x\leq1$; c) $\dfrac23$.
:::

:::exercise
**Question 2.** ($2.0$ marks) A motorbike and a car both travel from $A$ to $B$, a distance of $90$ km. The speed of the car is $15$ km/h greater than the speed of the motorbike, so the car reaches $B$ $1$ hour earlier than the motorbike. Find the speed of each vehicle. (The figures in this question are made up.)

::hint
**What you need:** time $=\dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}$; form an equation from the difference in times.

**Steps:**
1. Let the speed of the motorbike be $x$ (km/h) and write the speed of the car in terms of $x$.
2. Form the equation for the difference of $1$ hour.

::solution
Let the speed of the motorbike be $x$ (km/h), $x>0$. ($0.5$ marks) Then the speed of the car is $x+15$ (km/h).

The motorbike takes $\dfrac{90}x$ hours and the car takes $\dfrac{90}{x+15}$ hours. The car arrives $1$ hour earlier, so $\dfrac{90}x-\dfrac{90}{x+15}=1$. ($0.5$ marks)

Clearing denominators: $90\cdot15=x(x+15)$, that is $x^2+15x-1350=0$. We have $\Delta=225+5400=5625=75^2$, so $x=\dfrac{-15+75}2=30$ or $x=\dfrac{-15-75}2=-45$. ($0.5$ marks)

The value $x=-45$ does not satisfy $x>0$, so we reject it. So the speed of the motorbike is $30$ km/h and the speed of the car is $45$ km/h. ($0.5$ marks) Check: $\dfrac{90}{30}-\dfrac{90}{45}=3-2=1$ hour.

**Answer.** Motorbike $30$ km/h; car $45$ km/h.
:::

:::exercise
**Question 3.** ($2.5$ marks) a) The results of a test for $20$ students are recorded as follows: $2$ students scored $5$; $4$ students scored $6$; $6$ students scored $7$; $5$ students scored $8$; $3$ students scored $9$. Find the relative frequency of the score $7$ and the probability that a student chosen at random scored $8$ or more. b) Given the parabola $(P)\colon y=x^2$ and the line $(d)\colon y=2x+3$, find the coordinates of the intersection points $A$ and $B$ of $(P)$ and $(d)$ (with $x_A<x_B$), then find the area of triangle $OAB$. (The data in part a are made up.)

::hint
**What you need:** relative frequency $=\dfrac{\text{frequency}}{\text{total}}$; the equation for the $x$-coordinates of the intersection points; splitting triangle $OAB$ along the $y$-axis.

**Steps:**
1. In a), divide the frequency by $20$; the number of students scoring $8$ or more is $5+3$.
2. In b), solve $x^2=2x+3$ and find the point $C$ where $(d)$ meets the $y$-axis.

::solution
a) ($1.0$ mark) The relative frequency of the score $7$ is $\dfrac6{20}=0.3=30\%$. The number of students scoring $8$ or more is $5+3=8$, so the required probability is $\dfrac8{20}=\dfrac25=0.4$.

b) ($1.5$ marks) The equation for the $x$-coordinates: $x^2=2x+3$, that is $x^2-2x-3=0$. Since $a-b+c=1+2-3=0$, we have $x=-1$ or $x=3$. For $x=-1$ we get $y=1$; for $x=3$ we get $y=9$. So $A(-1,1)$ and $B(3,9)$. ($0.75$ marks)

The line $(d)$ meets the $y$-axis at $C(0,3)$, so $OC=3$. So $S_{OAB}=\dfrac12\cdot OC\cdot(|x_A|+|x_B|)=\dfrac12\cdot3\cdot(1+3)=6$. ($0.75$ marks)

**Answer.** a) $30\%$; $\dfrac25$; b) $A(-1,1)$, $B(3,9)$; area $6$ (square units).
:::

:::exercise
**Question 4.** ($3.0$ marks) Given an acute triangle $ABC$ with $AB<AC$. The altitudes $BE$ and $CF$ meet at $H$ ($E$ on $AC$, $F$ on $AB$). a) Prove that the quadrilateral $BCEF$ is cyclic. b) Prove that $AE\cdot AC=AF\cdot AB$. c) Let $D$ be the intersection of $AH$ and $BC$. Prove that $AD\perp BC$ and $AH\cdot AD=AF\cdot AB$.

::hint
**What you need:** the angle in a semicircle; two right-angled triangles with a common acute angle are similar; the three altitudes of a triangle pass through one point.

**Steps:**
1. In a), let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$ and prove that $E$ and $F$ lie on the circle with centre $M$ and diameter $BC$.
2. In b), prove that $\triangle ABE\backsim\triangle ACF$.
3. In c), prove that $\triangle AFH\backsim\triangle ADB$.

::solution
a) ($1.0$ mark) Since $BE\perp AC$ and $CF\perp AB$, we have $\angle BEC=\angle BFC=90^\circ$. Let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$; then $ME=MF=MB=MC=\dfrac{BC}2$ (the median to the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle). So the four points $B$, $C$, $E$, $F$ lie on the circle with centre $M$, that is the quadrilateral $BCEF$ is cyclic.

b) ($1.0$ mark) Consider $\triangle ABE$ and $\triangle ACF$: $\angle AEB=\angle AFC=90^\circ$ and they share the angle $\angle A$. So $\triangle ABE\backsim\triangle ACF$ (AA), giving $\dfrac{AE}{AF}=\dfrac{AB}{AC}$, that is $AE\cdot AC=AF\cdot AB$.

c) ($1.0$ mark) Since $BE$ and $CF$ are two altitudes of triangle $ABC$ meeting at $H$, $H$ is the orthocentre; so $AH$ is also an altitude, that is $AD\perp BC$. ($0.5$ marks)

Consider $\triangle AFH$ and $\triangle ADB$: $\angle AFH=\angle ADB=90^\circ$ and they share the angle $\angle A$. So $\triangle AFH\backsim\triangle ADB$ (AA), giving $\dfrac{AF}{AD}=\dfrac{AH}{AB}$, that is $AH\cdot AD=AF\cdot AB$. ($0.5$ marks)

**Answer.** The proofs are as above. (For the figure in the paper, $AH=4$ and $AD=6$, so $AF\cdot AB=24$.)
:::

:::exercise
**Question 5.** ($0.5$ marks) Let $x$ and $y$ be positive numbers. Prove that $(x+y)\left(\dfrac1x+\dfrac1y\right)\geq4$.

::hint
**What you need:** the AM–GM inequality for two positive numbers, or $(x-y)^2\geq0$.

**Steps:**
1. Expand the left-hand side.
2. Use $\dfrac xy+\dfrac yx\geq2$.

::solution
We have $(x+y)\left(\dfrac1x+\dfrac1y\right)=1+\dfrac xy+\dfrac yx+1=2+\dfrac xy+\dfrac yx$. Since $x$, $y>0$, by the AM–GM inequality $\dfrac xy+\dfrac yx\geq2\sqrt{\dfrac xy\cdot\dfrac yx}=2$. So the left-hand side is at least $4$. Equality holds when $x=y$.

**Answer.** $(x+y)\left(\dfrac1x+\dfrac1y\right)\geq4$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Let $x$, $y$, $z$ be three positive numbers. Prove that $(x+y+z)\left(\dfrac1x+\dfrac1y+\dfrac1z\right)\geq9$.

::hint
**What you need:** for two positive numbers $a$, $b$ we have $\dfrac ab+\dfrac ba\geq2$.

**Steps:**
1. Expand the left-hand side as $3$ plus six fractions.
2. Group the fractions into three pairs of reciprocals.

::solution
Expanding: $(x+y+z)\left(\dfrac1x+\dfrac1y+\dfrac1z\right)=3+\left(\dfrac xy+\dfrac yx\right)+\left(\dfrac yz+\dfrac zy\right)+\left(\dfrac zx+\dfrac xz\right)$. Each bracket is at least $2$, so the left-hand side is at least $3+2+2+2=9$. Equality holds when $x=y=z$.

**Answer.** The inequality is proved.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
The value of $A=\sqrt{7-4\sqrt3}+\sqrt{12}$ is
A. $2+\sqrt3$.
B. $2-\sqrt3$.
C. $4-\sqrt3$.
D. $\sqrt3$.
::key A
$\sqrt{7-4\sqrt3}=2-\sqrt3$ and $\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt3$, so $A=2+\sqrt3$. Option B forgets to add $\sqrt{12}$.

::mc
A box contains $12$ balls: $5$ green, $4$ red, $3$ yellow. One ball is chosen at random. The probability that it is not red is
A. $\dfrac13$.
B. $\dfrac23$.
C. $\dfrac34$.
D. $\dfrac5{12}$.
::key B
There are $8$ balls that are not red out of $12$, so the probability is $\dfrac8{12}=\dfrac23$. Option A is the probability of choosing a red ball.

::mc
A car and a motorbike travel $90$ km; the car is $15$ km/h faster and arrives $1$ hour earlier. The speed of the car is
A. $30$ km/h.
B. $15$ km/h.
C. $45$ km/h.
D. $60$ km/h.
::key C
The motorbike goes at $30$ km/h and the car at $30+15=45$ km/h. Option A is the speed of the motorbike.

::mc
The parabola $y=x^2$ meets the line $y=2x+3$ at $A(-1,1)$ and $B(3,9)$. The area of triangle $OAB$ is
A. $3$.
B. $12$.
C. $9$.
D. $6$.
::key D
$S=\dfrac12\cdot3\cdot(1+3)=6$. Option B is twice the correct area.

::tf
The scores of $20$ students: $2$ students scored $5$, $4$ scored $6$, $6$ scored $7$, $5$ scored $8$ and $3$ scored $9$. Decide whether each statement is true or false.
a) The relative frequency of the score $7$ is $25\%$.
b) $40\%$ of the students scored $8$ or more.
c) The probability that a student chosen at random scored $9$ is $\dfrac14$.
d) The probability that a student chosen at random scored below $6$ is $0.2$.
::key F T F F
a) False: $\dfrac6{20}=30\%$. b) True: $\dfrac{5+3}{20}=40\%$. c) False: $\dfrac3{20}$. d) False: $2$ students scored $5$, so the probability is $\dfrac2{20}=0.1$.

::sa
Find the greatest value of $x$ satisfying $\dfrac{x-1}2\geq\dfrac{2x+1}3-1$.
::key 1
The inequality is equivalent to $x\leq1$, so the greatest value of $x$ is $1$.

::sa
In an acute triangle $ABC$ the altitudes $BE$ and $CF$ meet at $H$, and $AH$ meets $BC$ at $D$. Given $AH=4$ cm and $AD=6$ cm, find $AF\cdot AB$ (in cm$^2$).
::key 24
$AF\cdot AB=AH\cdot AD=4\cdot6=24$.
