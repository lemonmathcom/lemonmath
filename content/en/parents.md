You do not need to be good at maths to help your child. You need one lesson that matches what they are learning, one printed sheet and 15 minutes sitting next to them. Every lesson on Lemonmath is free, follows Vietnam's national curriculum and comes in English and Vietnamese.

## Fifteen minutes a day

1. **Pick the lesson.** Open your child's grade below and find the lesson that matches what they are doing at school.
2. **Read it together for 5 minutes.** Each lesson starts with an everyday situation, then explains the idea step by step. Ask your child: "Can you explain it back to me?"
3. **Print the self-study sheet and let them work alone for 10 minutes.** The **Print a self-study sheet** button sits at the top of every lesson. Your child works without looking at the solutions, then checks the answers at the end of the sheet.
4. **Redo it after 1, 3 and 7 days.** The sheet has boxes for this. Spaced practice helps children remember far longer than rereading ([why learn on paper](../learn-on-paper/)).

When your child gets something wrong, don't rush to give the answer. Every lesson has a **Mistakes to watch out for** box: ask your child whether they made one of those mistakes. A mistake they find themselves sticks better than one you correct.

## Questions parents ask

**Does Lemonmath cost anything?** Every lesson and self-study sheet on the website is free.

**My child follows a different curriculum. Is it still useful?** Yes. The maths is the same everywhere; use the grade pages to find the topic your child is studying. Grade numbers follow Vietnam's system, where grade 1 starts at age 6.

**Can my child learn in Vietnamese and English?** Yes. Every lesson has both versions and a **Key terms** list linking the English and Vietnamese words.
