:::goals
- Name the tens and the ones of a two-digit number, for example $27$.
- Subtract a one-digit number from a two-digit number (no regrouping), for example $27-4$.
- Solve a word problem with one subtraction.
:::

Nam has $27$ marbles. He gives $4$ marbles to a friend. Think about it: how many marbles does Nam have left?

You could count back one at a time: $26, 25, 24, 23$. That works. But if Nam gave away many more marbles, counting back would take a long time, and it is easy to lose your place. There is a quicker way: split the number into tens and ones, and subtract only in the ones.

## Let's explore

Look closely at the number $27$ first. It has $2$ tens and $7$ ones.

- Nam has $2$ tens of marbles and $7$ single marbles.
- He gives his friend $4$ single marbles, so the single marbles left are $7-4=3$.
- He gives away no group of ten, so he still has $2$ tens.
Only the single marbles are taken away. The groups of ten stay as they were.

Put them back together: $2$ tens and $3$ ones make $23$. So $27-4=23$.

:::theorem How to subtract a one-digit number from a two-digit number
To work out $27-4$:
1. Subtract the ones: $7-4=3$.
2. Keep the tens the same: $2$ tens.
3. Write the answer: $2$ tens and $3$ ones make $23$.
:::

Why do we only subtract the ones? Because Nam gave away only single marbles and no group of ten. The number of tens does not change, so we keep the tens the same.

## Examples

We work out Nam's subtraction again, one step at a time.

:::example Work out $27-4$
**Solution.** $27$ has $2$ tens and $7$ ones. Subtract the ones: $7-4=3$. Keep the tens the same: $2$:
$$27-4=23.$$
So $27-4=23$.
:::

Here is another subtraction, with different numbers. We follow the same steps, so see whether the method feels familiar.

:::example Work out $58-6$
**Solution.** $58$ has $5$ tens and $8$ ones. Subtract the ones: $8-6=2$. Keep the tens the same: $5$:
$$58-6=52.$$
So $58-6=52$.
:::

One case can look odd. When the number you subtract equals the ones digit, no ones are left at all. Do not worry: the steps stay the same.

:::example Work out $45-5$
**Solution.** $45$ has $4$ tens and $5$ ones. Subtract the ones: $5-5=0$. Keep the tens the same: $4$:
$$45-5=40.$$
So $45-5=40$.
:::

Subtraction is useful in everyday life too, for example on a bus. Here is a problem like that.

:::example On the bus
There are $36$ people on a bus. At a stop, $3$ people get off. How many people are left on the bus?

**Solution.** "Get off" and "left" mean taking away, so we subtract. Subtract the ones: $6-3=3$. Keep the tens the same: $3$:
$$36-3=33\ \text{(people)}.$$
So $33$ people are left on the bus.

**Check.** Check with addition: $33+3=36$. The answer is correct.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting the tens.** Wrong: $27-4=3$. Why it is wrong: you only worked out $7-4$ and forgot the $2$ tens. Right: $27-4=23$.
- **Mixing up subtraction and addition.** Wrong: $27-4=31$. Why it is wrong: you worked out $27+4$. Right: "left" means taking away, so $27-4=23$.
- **No unit in a word problem.** Wrong: "Answer: $33$". Why it is wrong: the number on its own does not say what we are counting. Right: "Answer: $33$ people".
Many children slip up here, so do not worry. When you finish, check with addition.
:::

## Summary

- To subtract a one-digit number from a two-digit number (no regrouping), subtract the ones and keep the tens the same.
- Example: $27-4=23$ because $7-4=3$ and the tens stay $2$.
- Check with addition: $23+4=27$.
- Questions with "left" or "take away" usually need subtraction; remember the unit in the answer.
You can now answer the question about Nam's marbles without counting back one at a time. Remember to subtract the ones, keep the tens the same, and practise a few questions until it feels easy.

## Practice

Each exercise has a reminder of what you need and a hint to get started. Try it yourself first, and take your time. When you finish, or if you get stuck, open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out $38-5$.

::hint
**What you need:** subtract the ones, keep the tens the same.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $8-5$.
2. The tens stay $3$.

::solution
We subtract place by place, starting with the ones.

Ones: $8-5=3$, write $3$.

Tens: $38$ has $3$ tens and there is nothing to take away from the tens, so the tens stay $3$.

Hence $38-5=33$.

Check: $33+5=38$. Correct.

So $38-5=33$.

**Answer.** $33$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $79-6$.

::hint
**What you need:** $79$ has $7$ tens and $9$ ones.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $9-6$.
2. Keep the $7$ tens.

::solution
We subtract place by place, starting with the ones.

Ones: $9-6=3$, write $3$.

Tens: $79$ has $7$ tens and there is nothing to take away from the tens, so the tens stay $7$.

Hence $79-6=73$.

Check: $73+6=79$. Correct.

So $79-6=73$.

**Answer.** $73$.
:::

Work out $64-4$.

::hint
**What you need:** if you subtract the ones digit, no ones are left.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $4-4$.
2. Keep the $6$ tens.

::solution
We subtract place by place, starting with the ones.

Ones: $4-4=0$, write $0$.

Tens: $64$ has $6$ tens and there is nothing to take away from the tens, so the tens stay $6$.

Hence $64-4=60$.

Check: $60+4=64$. Correct.

So $64-4=60$.

**Answer.** $60$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the box: $48-5 \;\square\; 40$.

::hint
**What you need:** work out the difference first, then compare the two numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $48-5$.
2. Compare the result with $40$.

::solution
We work out the left side first. Ones: $8-5=3$. The tens stay $4$. So $48-5=43$.

We compare $43$ with $40$. Both numbers have $4$ tens; $43$ has $3$ ones and $40$ has $0$ ones, and $3>0$, so $43>40$.

Hence $48-5>40$.

So we write the sign $>$.

**Answer.** The sign $>$.
:::

:::exercise
A box has $45$ sweets. You eat $3$ of them. How many sweets are left in the box?

::hint
**What you need:** "eat" and "left" mean taking away: use subtraction.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? $45$ sweets and $3$ are eaten. What is asked? How many are left.
2. Work out $45-3$ and write the answer with its unit.

::solution
Solution

The number of sweets left in the box is:
$$45-3=42\ \text{(sweets)}.$$

Check: $42+3=45$. Correct.

So there are $42$ sweets left in the box.

**Answer.** $42$ sweets.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson: adding two two-digit numbers. Work out $32+45$, then subtract $6$ from the result.

::hint
**What you need:** add the ones to the ones and the tens to the tens; then subtract the ones.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $32+45$.
2. Subtract $6$ from the result.

::solution
The problem has two steps: add first, then subtract $6$ from the result.

Step 1: add two two-digit numbers. Ones: $2+5=7$. Tens: $3+4=7$. So $32+45=77$.

Step 2: subtract $6$ from the result. Ones: $7-6=1$. The tens stay $7$. So $77-6=71$.

Check: $71+6=77$. Correct.

So $32+45=77$ and $77-6=71$.

**Answer.** $71$.
:::

:::exercise
Lan writes: $46-3=16$. Is Lan right or wrong? Why? Correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** a one-digit number is subtracted from the ones only.

**Steps:**
1. Which place did Lan subtract $3$ from?
2. Work out $6-3$ again and keep the tens the same.

::solution
We check Lan's method. Lan took $3$ away from the tens, so $4$ tens became $1$ ten, which gave $16$.

That method is wrong, because the $3$ in $46-3$ is $3$ ones, so it must be taken away from the ones.

The right method: ones $6-3=3$, and the tens stay $4$. So $46-3=43$.

Check: $43+3=46$. Correct. But $16+3=19$, which is not $46$, so Lan's result $16$ is wrong.

So Lan is wrong; the correct result is $46-3=43$.

**Answer.** Wrong; the correct answer is $43$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find the missing number: $\square-5=32$.

::hint
**What you need:** the ones digit of the difference is $2$. Which number minus $5$ makes $2$?

**Steps:**
1. Ask: $?-5=2$, so $?=7$.
2. The tens stay $3$.

::solution
We look for the number that leaves $32$ when we take away $5$.

Ones: since $7-5=2$, the ones digit of the missing number is $7$.

Tens: the result $32$ has $3$ tens and there is nothing to take away from the tens, so the missing number has $3$ tens.

Hence the missing number is $37$.

Check: $37-5=32$. Correct.

So the number that goes in the box is $37$.

**Answer.** $37$.
:::

Look at the subtraction $76-4$.
a) The number $76$ has $7$ tens.
