:::goals
- Name the tens and the ones of a two-digit number, for example $23$.
- Add a one-digit number to a two-digit number (no regrouping), for example $23+4$.
- Solve a word problem with one addition.
:::

Nam has $23$ marbles. His friend Minh gives him $4$ more marbles. How many marbles does Nam have altogether?

You could count on one at a time: $24, 25, 26, 27$. But if you add many marbles, counting takes a long time. This lesson shows you a quicker way, using tens and ones.

## Let's explore

You learned in the last lesson that a two-digit number is made of tens and ones. The number $23$ has $2$ tens and $3$ ones.

- Nam has $2$ tens of marbles and $3$ single marbles.
- Minh gives $4$ more single marbles. Now the single marbles are $3+4=7$.
- The tens stay at $2$ tens, because Nam did not get any new group of ten.

So $2$ tens and $7$ ones make $27$. We have $23+4=27$.

:::theorem How to add a one-digit number to a two-digit number
To work out $23+4$:
1. Add the ones to the one-digit number: $3+4=7$.
2. Keep the tens the same: $2$ tens.
3. Write the answer: $2$ tens and $7$ ones make $27$.
:::

This works because you only add single marbles. The number of tens does not change, so you keep the tens the same.

## Examples

We start with the simplest sum.

:::example Work out $23+4$
**Solution.** $23$ has $2$ tens and $3$ ones. Add the ones: $3+4=7$. Keep the tens the same: $2$:
$$23+4=27.$$
So $23+4=27$.
:::

Now a similar sum with different numbers.

:::example Work out $52+6$
**Solution.** $52$ has $5$ tens and $2$ ones. Add the ones: $2+6=8$. Keep the tens the same: $5$:
$$52+6=58.$$
So $52+6=58$.
:::

When the ones digit is $0$, the sum is even easier.

:::example Work out $40+5$
**Solution.** $40$ has $4$ tens and $0$ ones. Add the ones: $0+5=5$. Keep the tens the same: $4$:
$$40+5=45.$$
So $40+5=45$.
:::

When the one-digit number comes first, you can swap the two numbers to make it easier.

:::example Work out $6+31$
**Solution.** Swapping the addends does not change the sum, so $6+31=31+6$. Add the ones: $1+6=7$. Keep the tens the same: $3$:
$$31+6=37.$$
So $6+31=37$.
:::

Now use addition to answer a question from everyday life.

:::example Reading
Ha has read $35$ pages of a storybook. Today she reads $3$ more pages. How many pages has she read altogether?

**Solution.** "Reads more" means adding on, so we add. Add the ones: $5+3=8$. Keep the tens the same: $3$:
$$35+3=38.$$
So Ha has read $38$ pages altogether.

**Check.** Count on from $35$: $36, 37, 38$. The answer is correct.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Adding to the tens.** Wrong: $23+4=63$. Why it is wrong: you added $4$ to the $2$ tens. Right: $4$ is $4$ ones, so $23+4=27$.
- **Forgetting the tens.** Wrong: $23+4=7$. Why it is wrong: you only worked out $3+4$ and forgot the $2$ tens. Right: $23+4=27$.
- **No unit in a word problem.** Wrong: "Answer: $38$". Right: "Answer: $38$ pages".
:::

## Summary

- A two-digit number is made of tens and ones. For example, $23$ is $2$ tens and $3$ ones.
- To add a one-digit number to a two-digit number, add the ones and keep the tens the same.
- Example: $23+4=27$ because $3+4=7$ and the tens stay $2$.
- Swapping the addends does not change the sum: $6+31=31+6$.
- Questions with "more" or "altogether" usually need addition; remember the unit in the answer.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out $36+2$.

::hint
**What you need:** add the ones, keep the tens the same.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $6+2$.
2. The tens stay $3$.

::solution
$6+2=8$ and the tens are $3$, so $36+2=38$.

**Answer.** $38$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $71+8$.

::hint
**What you need:** $71$ has $7$ tens and $1$ one.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $1+8$.
2. Keep the $7$ tens.

::solution
$1+8=9$ and the tens are $7$, so $71+8=79$.

**Answer.** $79$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $5+44$.

::hint
**What you need:** swapping the addends does not change the sum.

**Steps:**
1. Write $5+44$ as $44+5$.
2. Add the ones, keep the tens the same.

::solution
$5+44=44+5$. We have $4+5=9$ and the tens are $4$, so $44+5=49$.

**Answer.** $49$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the box: $25+4 \;\square\; 30$.

::hint
**What you need:** work out the sum first, then compare the two numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $25+4$.
2. Compare the result with $30$.

::solution
$5+4=9$, so $25+4=29$. Since $29<30$, we have $25+4<30$.

**Answer.** The sign $<$.
:::

:::exercise
There are $42$ hens in a yard. Mum buys $5$ more hens. How many hens are in the yard altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** "more" and "altogether" mean putting together: use addition.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? $42$ hens and $5$ more. What is asked? How many hens altogether.
2. Work out $42+5$ and write the answer with its unit.

::solution
The number of hens altogether is:
$$42+5=47\ \text{(hens)}.$$

**Answer.** $47$ hens.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson on units of length. One piece of string is $12$ cm long and another is $6$ cm long. How long are the two pieces altogether, in centimetres?

::hint
**What you need:** the unit of length here is the centimetre, written cm.

**Steps:**
1. Put the two lengths together: use addition.
2. Work out $12+6$ and write the unit cm.

::solution
$2+6=8$ and the tens are $1$, so $12+6=18$ (cm).

**Answer.** $18$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
Lan writes: $34+5=84$. Is Lan right or wrong? Why? Correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** a one-digit number is added to the ones only.

**Steps:**
1. Which place did Lan add $5$ to?
2. Work out $4+5$ again and keep the tens the same.

::solution
Lan is wrong, because she added $5$ to the $3$ tens. The right way: $4+5=9$ and the tens are $3$, so $34+5=39$.

**Answer.** Wrong; the correct answer is $39$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find the missing number: $\square+5=67$.

::hint
**What you need:** the ones digit of the sum is $7$. Which number plus $5$ makes $7$?

**Steps:**
1. Ask: $?+5=7$, so $?=2$.
2. The tens stay $6$.

::solution
Ones: $2+5=7$. The tens are $6$, so the missing number is $62$.

**Check.** $62+5=67$. Correct.

**Answer.** $62$.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
$34+5$ equals
A. $29$.
B. $84$.
C. $39$.
D. $9$.
::key C
$4+5=9$ and the tens are $3$, so $34+5=39$. Option B is the mistake of adding $5$ to the tens, and D forgets the tens.

::mc
$60+7$ equals
A. $7$.
B. $13$.
C. $77$.
D. $67$.
::key D
$0+7=7$ and the tens are $6$, so $60+7=67$. Option B is $6+7$, mixing up the tens and the ones.

::mc
Which sum equals $48$?
A. $40+8$.
B. $43+4$.
C. $44+5$.
D. $45+2$.
::key A
$40+8=48$. The other sums are $47$, $49$ and $47$.

::mc
Hoa has $25$ stickers and her mum gives her $3$ more. How many stickers does Hoa have altogether?
A. $22$ stickers.
B. $28$ stickers.
C. $55$ stickers.
D. $8$ stickers.
::key B
"Gives her more" means putting together: $25+3=28$. Option A is $25-3$, a subtraction by mistake.

::tf
Look at the sum $52+6$.
a) The number $52$ has $5$ tens.
b) The ones are added: $2+6=8$.
c) $52+6=68$.
d) $52+6$ is less than $60$.
::key T T F T
a) True: $52$ has $5$ tens and $2$ ones. b) True: $2+6=8$. c) False: the tens stay $5$, so $52+6=58$. d) True: $58<60$.

::sa
Work out $41+7$.
::key 48
$1+7=8$ and the tens are $4$, so $41+7=48$.

::sa
A basket has $36$ oranges and $3$ more are added. How many oranges are in the basket altogether?
::key 39
$36+3=39$ (oranges).
:::
