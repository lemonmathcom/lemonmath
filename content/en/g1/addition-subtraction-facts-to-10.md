Lan writes $3+2=5$. From the three numbers $2$, $3$ and $5$, what other sums can Lan write?

You already know addition and subtraction. In this lesson you see that they belong to one family.

## Let's explore

You have three numbers $2$, $3$, $5$. The number $5$ is the biggest, made of $2$ and $3$.

- Put the two parts together: $2+3=5$ and $3+2=5$.
- Take one part from $5$: $5-2=3$ and $5-3=2$.

From one addition you can write three more sums, and all four sums use the same three numbers.

The table below shows the additions that make $6$.

| Addition | $1+5$ | $2+4$ | $3+3$ | $4+2$ | $5+1$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | $6$ | $6$ | $6$ | $6$ | $6$ |

:::theorem Addition and subtraction go together
1. Of three numbers, the biggest is the two others put together: $2+3=5$.
2. Swap the two parts: $3+2=5$.
3. Take one part from the biggest and the other part is left: $5-2=3$ and $5-3=2$.
4. To find the answer to a subtraction, think of the matching addition.
:::

This works because putting two parts together makes the whole group, and taking one part from the whole group leaves the other part.

## Examples

We start with a set of three numbers.

:::example Four sums from the numbers $3$, $4$, $7$
From $4+3=7$ write the other sums.

**Solution.** The biggest number is $7$, and the two parts are $3$ and $4$. Swap the two parts: $3+4=7$. Take one part from $7$: $7-3=4$ and $7-4=3$.

So the other three sums are $3+4=7$, $7-3=4$, $7-4=3$.
:::

Now use addition to work out a subtraction.

:::example Work out $6-2$
**Solution.** Think: $2$ plus what makes $6$? Count from $2$ to $6$: "three, four, five, six", which is $4$ numbers, so $2+4=6$.

So $6-2=4$.
:::

Next, find a missing number.

:::example Find the missing number
Fill in the box: $8-\square=3$.

**Solution.** Think: $3$ plus what makes $8$? Count from $3$ to $8$: "four, five, six, seven, eight", which is $5$ numbers, so $3+5=8$.

So the missing number is $5$.

**Check.** $8-5=3$. Correct.
:::

You can also use a table to find sums.

:::example Reading the addition table
Use the table in the explore part to find the additions that make $6$ where the first number is less than $4$.

**Solution.** Read the top row and pick the first numbers less than $4$: $1$, $2$, $3$.

So the additions are $1+5=6$, $2+4=6$, $3+3=6$.
:::

Last, solve a word problem.

:::example Rabbits in a hutch and in the yard
There are $7$ rabbits. $3$ rabbits are in the hutch and the rest are in the yard. How many rabbits are in the yard?

**Solution.** Take the $3$ in the hutch away from the $7$, so we subtract: $7-3$. Think $3+4=7$:
$$7-3=4\ \text{(rabbits)}.$$
So there are $4$ rabbits in the yard.

**Check.** $3+4=7$. Correct.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Taking the bigger number from the smaller.** Wrong: from the numbers $3$, $4$, $7$ writing $3-4=7$. Why it is wrong: you cannot take $4$ away from $3$. Right: $7-4=3$.
- **Putting the biggest number in the wrong place.** Wrong: $3+7=4$. Why it is wrong: the biggest number must be the answer of the addition. Right: $3+4=7$.
- **Missing a sum.** Wrong: writing only two sums from three numbers. Why it is wrong: the swapped sum is missing. Right: there are four sums.
:::

## Summary

- From three numbers you can write two additions and two subtractions: $2+3=5$, $3+2=5$, $5-2=3$, $5-3=2$.
- The biggest number is the answer of the addition and the first number of the subtraction.
- To work out $6-2$, think $2+\square=6$.
- Use a table to find sums with the same answer quickly.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
You know $2+3=5$. Work out $5-2$.

::hint
**What you need:** take one part from the biggest number and the other part is left.

**Steps:**
1. Which is the biggest number?
2. Take $2$ from $5$.

::solution
$5$ is made of $2$ and $3$; take away $2$ and $3$ is left.

**Answer.** $5-2=3$.
:::

:::exercise
You know $4+3=7$. Work out $7-4$.

::hint
**What you need:** use the matching addition.

**Steps:**
1. Which addition gives $7$?
2. Take $4$ from $7$.

::solution
$7$ is made of $4$ and $3$; take away $4$ and $3$ is left.

**Answer.** $7-4=3$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson: work out $9-4$ by counting back, then check with addition.

::hint
**What you need:** count back four numbers from $9$; check with addition.

**Steps:**
1. Count back: eight, seven, six, five.
2. Work out $\square+4$ and see if it equals $9$.

::solution
Counting back gives $5$. Check: $5+4=9$. Correct.

**Answer.** $9-4=5$.
:::

:::exercise
Fill in the missing number:
a) $6-\square=2$.
b) $\square-5=3$.

::hint
**What you need:** think of the matching addition: $2+\square=6$ and $3+5=\square$.

**Steps:**
1. Count from $2$ to $6$.
2. Put $3$ and $5$ together.

::solution
a) From $2$ to $6$ is $4$ numbers, so $6-4=2$. b) $3+5=8$, so $8-5=3$.

**Answer.** a) $4$. b) $8$.
:::

:::exercise
Lan writes from $3+4=7$ the subtraction $7-3=3$. Is Lan right? Why?

::hint
**What you need:** take one part from $7$ and the other part is left.

**Steps:**
1. Take $3$ from $7$: which part is left?
2. Which number is that?

::solution
$7$ is made of $3$ and $4$. Take away $3$ and $4$ is left, not $3$.

**Answer.** No. $7-3=4$.
:::

:::exercise
An writes four sums from the numbers $3$, $4$, $7$: $3+4=7$, $4+3=7$, $7-3=4$, $3-4=7$. Which sum is wrong? Write it correctly.

::hint
**What you need:** the biggest number must come first in a subtraction.

**Steps:**
1. Check each sum.
2. Find the one that takes the bigger number from the smaller.

::solution
The sum $3-4=7$ is wrong because you cannot take $4$ away from $3$. The right sum is $7-4=3$.

**Answer.** $3-4=7$ is wrong; it should be $7-4=3$.
:::

:::exercise
There are $9$ oranges. $4$ oranges are in a basket and the rest are on the table. How many oranges are on the table?

::hint
**What you need:** take the ones in the basket from the whole group: use subtraction.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? There are $9$ oranges and $4$ are in the basket. What is asked? How many are on the table.
2. Work out $9-4$ and write the answer with its unit.

::solution
The number of oranges on the table is:
$$9-4=5\ \text{(oranges)}.$$

**Check.** $4+5=9$. Correct.

**Answer.** $5$ oranges.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
From the three numbers $3$, $6$, $9$, write all four addition and subtraction sums.

::hint
**What you need:** the biggest number is $9$; the two parts are $3$ and $6$.

**Steps:**
1. Write the two additions.
2. Write the two subtractions.

::solution
Two additions: $3+6=9$ and $6+3=9$. Two subtractions: $9-3=6$ and $9-6=3$.

**Check.** $3+6=9$, so $9-3=6$ and $9-6=3$. Correct.

**Answer.** $3+6=9$; $6+3=9$; $9-3=6$; $9-6=3$.
:::
