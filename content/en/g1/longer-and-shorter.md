- Compare the lengths of two or three objects: longer, shorter, longest, shortest.
- Know that the centimetre (cm) is a unit of length and use a ruler to measure.
- Place the zero mark of the ruler correctly and read the measurement.
- Add and subtract measurements in centimetres and solve word problems.

Nam has a pencil that is 15 cm long and Lan has a pencil that is 12 cm long, but neither of them knows these measurements yet. They put the two pencils side by side and argue about which one is longer.

Looking with your eyes can fool you, especially when the pencils are placed unevenly. How can they be sure which pencil is longer, and by how much?

## Longer and shorter

Try it with Nam and Lan: place the two pencils so that **one end of each pencil lines up** (say, the left ends). Now look at the other ends. The pencil whose other end sticks out further is the longer one.

:::definition Comparing lengths
To compare the lengths of two objects, place them so that one end of each lines up, then look at the other ends.
- The object whose other end sticks out further is **longer**, and the other is **shorter**.
- If the other ends line up too, the objects are **the same length**.
- With three objects, find the **longest** and the **shortest**.
:::

Why must one end line up? If you place them unevenly, one pencil may stick out further just because it was placed further along, not because it is longer.

:::example Three pencils
A red, a blue and a yellow pencil are placed with their left ends lined up, as in the picture above. Which pencil is the longest? Which is the shortest?

**Solution.** The left ends line up, so we look at the right end of each pencil. The red one sticks out the furthest, the blue one the least, and the yellow one is in between.

So the red pencil is the longest and the blue pencil is the shortest.

## Units of length: the centimetre

Nam measures the edge of a table with his handspan and gets 5 handspans. Lan measures the same edge, also with her handspan, and gets 6. They both measured correctly, but their hands are different sizes.

To make everyone get the same number, we need a **common unit**. One unit that is often used is the **centimetre**, written **cm**. The marks on your ruler are 1 cm apart.

:::theorem How to measure with a ruler
1. Lay the ruler along the object so that the **zero mark** of the ruler lines up with one end of the object.
2. Look at the other end of the object and read the number at that mark.
3. Write the measurement with the unit cm.
:::

:::graph On the ruler: the line segment from the 0 mark to the 7 mark is 7 cm long
alt: A horizontal line segment on a ruler, starting at the 0 mark and ending at the 7 mark
x: 0..10
y: -1..1

point: (0, 0) "0" below

:::

Why start at the 0 mark? From the 0 mark to the 7 mark there are exactly 7 gaps, each 1 cm long, so the object is 7 cm long. A ruler is like a number line lying on its side, and you have already used number lines for adding and subtracting.

:::example Measuring a line segment
The left end of line segment AB is at the 0 mark of the ruler and the right end is at the 7 mark (as in the picture above). How many centimetres long is AB?

**Solution.** The 0 mark lines up with end A, and end B lines up with the 7 mark. So AB is 7 cm long.
:::

Measurements with a unit can be added and subtracted just like ordinary numbers.

:::example Work out
Work out: a) $5\ \text{cm}+3\ \text{cm}$; b) $9\ \text{cm}-4\ \text{cm}$.

**Solution.** Add or subtract the numbers, then write the unit cm in the answer:
$$5\ \text{cm}+3\ \text{cm}=8\ \text{cm},$$
$$9\ \text{cm}-4\ \text{cm}=5\ \text{cm}.$$
So a) $8$ cm; b) $5$ cm.
:::

Now we return to Nam and Lan's question.

:::example Nam's and Lan's pencils
Nam's pencil is 15 cm long and Lan's pencil is 12 cm long. Whose pencil is longer, and by how many centimetres?

**Solution.** We have $15>12$, so Nam's pencil is longer. The difference is:
$$15\ \text{cm}-12\ \text{cm}=3\ \text{cm}.$$
So Nam's pencil is 3 cm longer than Lan's.
:::

Finally, a word problem.

:::example The class bean plant (Apply it)
Suppose your class grows a bean plant. In the first week it is 5 cm tall, and in the next week it is 9 cm tall. By how many centimetres did the plant grow in that week?

**Understand.** We know the height of the plant in two weeks: 5 cm and 9 cm. We are asked how much it grew.

**Plan.** "Grew" means the difference between the two measurements, so we subtract.

**Solve.**

Solution

The plant grew by:
$$9-5=4\ \text{(cm)}.$$
Answer: 4 cm.

**Look back.** Check with addition: $5+4=9$, which is correct. What if the plant were 12 cm tall in the next week? Then it would have grown $12-5=7$, which is 7 cm.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Not starting to measure at the 0 mark.** Wrong: one end of the object is at the 1 mark and the other at the 8 mark, and you read 8 cm. Why it is wrong: from the 1 mark to the 8 mark there are only 7 gaps. Right: the object is 7 cm long.
- **Comparing when the objects do not line up at one end.** Wrong: pencil A sticks out further, so A is longer, even though A's left end was placed further to the right. Why it is wrong: the left ends do not line up. Right: line up the left ends first, then compare.
- **Forgetting the unit.** Wrong: "Answer: 4". Right: "Answer: 4 cm".
:::

## Summary

- To compare lengths, line up one end of the objects. The one whose other end sticks out further is longer.
- With three objects you can find the longest and the shortest.
- The centimetre (cm) is a unit of length. Everyone who measures in cm gets the same number.
- To measure with a ruler, line up the 0 mark with one end, read the number at the other end and write the unit cm.
- Measurements with the same unit are added and subtracted like ordinary numbers. Remember the unit in the answer.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
The red string is longer than the blue string, and the blue string is longer than the yellow string. Which string is the longest? Which is the shortest?

::hint
**What you need:** the longest is longer than all the others, and the shortest is shorter than all the others.

**Steps:**
1. Put the three strings in order from longest to shortest.
2. Which string is first and which is last?

::solution
Red is longer than blue, and blue is longer than yellow. So the order from longest to shortest is red, blue, yellow.

**Answer.** The red string is the longest and the yellow string is the shortest.
:::

:::exercise
A line segment has its left end at the 0 mark of a ruler and its right end at the 9 mark. How many centimetres long is the line segment?

::hint
**What you need:** line up the 0 mark with one end of the object, then read the number at the other end.

**Steps:**
1. Which end of the line segment is at the 0 mark?
2. Read the number at the other end and write the unit.

::solution
The line segment starts at the 0 mark and ends at the 9 mark, so it is 9 cm long.

**Answer.** 9 cm.
:::

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $6\ \text{cm}+9\ \text{cm}$

b) $14\ \text{cm}-6\ \text{cm}$

c) $8\ \text{cm}+5\ \text{cm}-3\ \text{cm}$

::hint
**What you need:** work with the numbers, then write the unit cm in the answer.

**Steps:**
1. Parts a and b: add and subtract bridging through 10.
2. Part c: work from left to right.

::solution
a) $6\ \text{cm}+9\ \text{cm}=15\ \text{cm}$.

b) $14\ \text{cm}-6\ \text{cm}=8\ \text{cm}$.

c) $8\ \text{cm}+5\ \text{cm}-3\ \text{cm}=13\ \text{cm}-3\ \text{cm}=10\ \text{cm}$.

**Answer.** a) 15 cm; b) 8 cm; c) 10 cm.
:::

:::exercise
Ha's pencil is 12 cm long and Minh's pencil is 9 cm long. Whose pencil is longer? By how many centimetres?

::hint
**What you need:** compare the two measurements, then subtract the smaller from the larger to find how much longer.

**Steps:**
1. Compare 12 and 9.
2. Work out the difference between the two measurements.

::solution
We have $12>9$, so Ha's pencil is longer. The difference is:
$$12\ \text{cm}-9\ \text{cm}=3\ \text{cm}.$$

**Answer.** Ha's pencil is longer, by 3 cm.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a plant in a pot is 8 cm tall. Two weeks later it is 15 cm tall. By how many centimetres did the plant grow?

::hint
**What you need:** "grew" means the difference between two measurements: use subtraction.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. How do we solve it? Which calculation do you use?
3. Check with addition.

::solution
Solution

The plant grew by:
$$15-8=7\ \text{(cm)}.$$
Answer: 7 cm.

**Check.** $8+7=15$, which is correct.
:::

:::exercise
The red string is 8 cm long. The blue string is 5 cm longer than the red string. How many centimetres long is the blue string?

::hint
**What you need:** "5 cm longer" means 5 cm more: use addition.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. Is the blue string longer or shorter than the red one? Which calculation do you use?
3. Check: is the answer greater than 8?

::solution
Solution

The blue string is this many centimetres long:
$$8+5=13\ \text{(cm)}.$$
Answer: 13 cm.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the addition facts bridging through 10. Line segment AB is 9 cm long and line segment BC is 6 cm long. If the two segments are joined end to end, how many centimetres long is the whole line?

::hint
**What you need:** "joined" means put together: use addition. To bridge through 10: $9+6=9+1+5$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. Split 6 into 1 and 5 to bridge through 10.

::solution
Solution

The joined line is this many centimetres long:
$$9+6=15\ \text{(cm)}.$$
Answer: 15 cm.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Mai lays a pencil along a ruler, but the left end of the pencil is at the 2 mark and the right end is at the 9 mark. How many centimetres long is the pencil?

::hint
**What you need:** the distance from one mark to another is the number of 1 cm gaps between the two marks.

**Steps:**
1. How many 1 cm gaps are there from the 0 mark to the 9 mark?
2. How many gaps are there from the 0 mark to the 2 mark? Which part must you leave out?

::solution
From the 0 mark to the 9 mark there are 9 gaps, and from the 0 mark to the 2 mark there are 2 gaps. The part of the pencil between the 2 mark and the 9 mark has:
$$9-2=7\ \text{(gaps)}.$$
Each gap is 1 cm long, so the pencil is 7 cm long.

**Answer.** 7 cm.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
Two pencils are placed with their left ends lined up. The right end of the blue pencil sticks out further than the right end of the red pencil. Which statement is correct?
A. The blue pencil is shorter than the red pencil.
B. They cannot be compared.
C. The two pencils are the same length.
D. The blue pencil is longer than the red pencil.
::key D
The pencils line up at one end, so the one whose other end sticks out further is longer. Option A says the opposite.

::mc
A piece of string has one end at the 0 mark of a ruler and the other end at the 8 mark. The string is
A. $8$ cm long.
B. $7$ cm long.
C. $8$ m long.
D. $9$ cm long.
::key A
From the 0 mark to the 8 mark there are 8 gaps, each 1 cm. Options D and B miscount the gaps, and option C uses the wrong unit.

::mc
$7\ \text{cm}+5\ \text{cm}$ equals
A. $2$ cm.
B. $12$ cm.
C. $11$ cm.
D. $75$ cm.
::key B
$7+5=12$, and the unit is cm. Option A is a subtraction, and option D writes the two numbers side by side.

::mc
A red ribbon is 14 cm long and a blue ribbon is 9 cm long. The red ribbon is longer than the blue ribbon by
A. $23$ cm.
B. $6$ cm.
C. $5$ cm.
D. $4$ cm.
::key C
We work out $14-9=5$, which is 5 cm. Option A is an addition, and options B and D are mistakes in subtracting through 10.

::tf
The red string is 11 cm long and the blue string is 6 cm long.
a) The blue string is shorter than the red string.
b) The red string is 5 cm longer than the blue string.
c) Joining the two strings gives a string 16 cm long.
d) The blue string is longer than 10 cm.
::key T T F F
a) True: $6<11$. b) True: $11-6=5$. c) False: $11+6=17$, not 16. d) False: the blue string is 6 cm long, which is shorter than 10 cm.

::sa
Line segment AB is 8 cm long and line segment BC is 7 cm long. How many centimetres long is the line when the two segments are joined?
::key 15
$8+7=15$ (cm).

::sa
A flower is 17 cm tall and a blade of grass is 9 cm tall. How many centimetres taller is the flower than the grass?
::key 8
$17-9=8$ (cm).
:::
