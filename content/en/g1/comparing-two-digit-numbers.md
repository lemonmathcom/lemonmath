:::goals
- Compare two two-digit numbers by comparing the tens, then the ones.
- Use the signs $>$, $<$, $=$ correctly.
- Find the greatest and the least number and put numbers in order.
:::

Lan has $35$ marbles and Nam has $42$ marbles. Who has more marbles?

You already know that a two-digit number is made of tens and ones. That is all you need to compare two numbers. You do not have to count the marbles one by one.

## Let's explore

We start by splitting each number into tens and ones.

- $35$ is $3$ tens and $5$ ones.
- $42$ is $4$ tens and $2$ ones.

Look at the tens first: $4$ tens are more than $3$ tens. So $42$ is greater than $35$. Many children find this odd at first, because $2$ ones are fewer than $5$ ones. But the tens decide.

We write $42>35$. That answers the question at the start: Nam has more marbles.

:::theorem Comparing two-digit numbers
1. Compare the tens digits first. The number with the greater tens digit is greater.
2. If the tens digits are equal, compare the ones digits. The number with the greater ones digit is greater.
3. If both digits are equal, the numbers are equal.
:::

Why do the tens come first? Because each ten is $10$ ones. One more ten is always worth more than a few loose ones.

## Examples

We answer the opening question first, about Lan's and Nam's marbles.

:::example Compare $35$ and $42$
**Solution.** Tens digits: $3$ and $4$. Since $4>3$, we have $42>35$.

So $35<42$.
:::

What if the tens digits are the same? Then the tens cannot tell the two numbers apart, so we look at the ones.

:::example Compare $56$ and $52$
**Solution.** The tens digits are both $5$. Compare the ones digits: $6$ and $2$. Since $6>2$, we have $56>52$.

So $56>52$.
:::

Once you can compare two numbers, you can put three in order. We go from least to greatest.

:::example Putting numbers in order
Put the numbers $61, 16, 58$ in order from least to greatest.

**Solution.** Compare the tens digits: $1$, $5$, $6$. The number with the least tens digit is $16$, then $58$, then $61$.

So the order is $16, 58, 61$.

**Check.** $16<58$ and $58<61$. Correct.
:::

Here is a problem like the ones you meet every day: two friends each have some labels, and you want to know who has more.

:::example Exercise-book labels
Mai has $24$ exercise-book labels and Ha has $27$. Who has more labels?

**Solution.** The tens digits are both $2$. Compare the ones digits: $4$ and $7$. Since $7>4$, we have $27>24$.

So Ha has more labels.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Comparing the ones first.** This is an easy slip to make. Wrong: $19>21$ because $9>1$. Why it is wrong: you must compare the tens first, and $1$ ten is less than $2$ tens. Right: $19<21$.
- **Thinking a big digit makes a big number.** Wrong: $38>41$ because it has the digit $8$. Why it is wrong: $3$ tens are less than $4$ tens, however big the digit $8$ looks. Right: $38<41$.
- **Opening the sign the wrong way.** Wrong: $42<35$. Why it is wrong: the mouth must open towards the greater number, and $42$ is greater than $35$. Right: $42>35$.
:::

## Summary

- Compare the tens first; the number with the greater tens digit is greater.
- If the tens are equal, compare the ones.
- If both digits are equal, the numbers are equal.
- The signs $>$ and $<$ open towards the greater number.

Back to the opening question: Nam has more marbles, because Nam has more tens. Try a few exercises so this starts to feel natural.

## Practice

Each exercise has the key idea and a hint to get you started. Try it yourself first, look at the hint if you are stuck, and open the solution last. Going slowly is fine.

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$: $45 \;\square\; 38$.

::hint
**What you need:** compare the tens first.

**Steps:**
1. What are the tens digits of $45$ and $38$?
2. Which number has the greater tens digit?

::solution
To compare two two-digit numbers, we compare the tens digits first.

The number $45$ has $4$ tens and the number $38$ has $3$ tens. Since $4>3$, we have $45>38$.

So the sign to write is $>$, and $45>38$.

**Answer.** $45>38$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$: $52 \;\square\; 57$.

::hint
**What you need:** if the tens are equal, compare the ones.

**Steps:**
1. Compare the tens.
2. Compare the ones.

::solution
To compare two two-digit numbers, we compare the tens digits first.

The tens digits of $52$ and $57$ are both $5$, so we compare the ones digits next.

Ones digits: $2<7$, so $52<57$.

So the sign to write is $<$, and $52<57$.

**Answer.** $52<57$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson: write the number made of $6$ tens and $2$ ones, then compare it with $26$.

::hint
**What you need:** write the tens digit first; compare the tens first.

**Steps:**
1. Write the number $6$ tens $2$ ones.
2. Compare the tens of $62$ and $26$.

::solution
The number made of $6$ tens and $2$ ones is $62$.

We compare $62$ with $26$. The tens digit of $62$ is $6$ and the tens digit of $26$ is $2$. Since $6>2$, we have $62>26$.

So the number is $62$ and $62>26$.

**Answer.** $62>26$.
:::

:::exercise
a) Which number is greater: $73$ or $37$?
b) Which number is less: $91$ or $90$?

::hint
**What you need:** compare the tens; if equal, compare the ones.

**Steps:**
1. Part a: compare the tens $7$ and $3$.
2. Part b: the tens are both $9$, so compare the ones.

::solution
a) The tens digit of $73$ is $7$ and the tens digit of $37$ is $3$. Since $7>3$, we have $73>37$, so the greater number is $73$.

b) The tens digits of $91$ and $90$ are both $9$, so we compare the ones digits: $1$ and $0$. Since $0<1$, we have $90<91$, so the lesser number is $90$.

So a) the greater number is $73$; b) the lesser number is $90$.

**Answer.** a) $73$. b) $90$.
:::

:::exercise
Nam says: "$19>21$ because $9>1$." Is Nam right? Why?

::hint
**What you need:** you must compare the tens first.

**Steps:**
1. What are the tens digits of $19$ and $21$?
2. Which number has the greater tens digit?

::solution
Nam compared the ones digits ($9$ and $1$) first. But when we compare two-digit numbers, we must compare the tens digits first.

The tens digit of $19$ is $1$ and the tens digit of $21$ is $2$. Since $1<2$, we have $19<21$.

So Nam is wrong; the correct statement is $19<21$.

**Answer.** No. $19<21$.
:::

:::exercise
Lan puts $34, 28, 41$ "from least to greatest" as $41, 34, 28$. Where did Lan go wrong? Put them in the right order.

::hint
**What you need:** from least to greatest means starting with the least number.

**Steps:**
1. Compare the tens digits: $3$, $2$, $4$.
2. Find the least number, then the next.

::solution
Lan wrote the numbers as $41, 34, 28$, which is from greatest to least. The question asks for the order from least to greatest, so this is where Lan went wrong.

The tens digits of $34$, $28$, $41$ are $3$, $2$, $4$. Since $2<3<4$, we have $28<34<41$.

The least number is $28$, then $34$, and the greatest number is $41$.

So the correct order from least to greatest is $28, 34, 41$.

**Answer.** $28, 34, 41$.
:::

:::exercise
The table shows how many pages four children have read:

| Child | An | Binh | Chi | Dung |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pages | $45$ | $54$ | $45$ | $38$ |

a) Who has read the most pages?
b) Who has read the fewest pages?
c) Which two children have read the same number of pages?

::hint
**What you need:** compare the tens first, then the ones.

**Steps:**
1. Read the numbers: $45, 54, 45, 38$.
2. Compare each one.

::solution
The numbers of pages read by An, Binh, Chi and Dung are $45$, $54$, $45$, $38$. Their tens digits are $4$, $5$, $4$, $3$.

a) The greatest tens digit is $5$, in the number $54$. So $54$ is the greatest number, and Binh has read the most pages.

b) The least tens digit is $3$, in the number $38$. So $38$ is the least number, and Dung has read the fewest pages.

c) An and Chi both read $45$ pages, so these two numbers are equal.

So Binh has read the most pages, Dung has read the fewest pages, and An and Chi have read the same number of pages.

**Answer.** a) Binh. b) Dung. c) An and Chi.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find the digit for the box so that $47<4\square<50$.

::hint
**What you need:** the number has $4$ in the tens place and must be greater than $47$ and less than $50$.

**Steps:**
1. List the numbers greater than $47$ with $4$ tens.
2. Choose those less than $50$.

::solution
The number $4\square$ has $4$ tens, and its ones digit is the digit we need to find.

Since $4\square>47$ and both numbers have $4$ tens, the ones digit of $4\square$ must be greater than $7$. The digit is $8$ or $9$, giving the numbers $48$ and $49$.

Both $48$ and $49$ have $4$ tens, which is less than $5$ tens, so both are less than $50$.

*Check.* $47<48<50$ and $47<49<50$. Correct.

So the digit for the box is $8$ or $9$.

**Answer.** The digit $8$ or $9$.
:::

## Mini test

::mc TH

A. $<$.
B. $>$.
C. $=$.
D. cannot tell.
::key B
Tens digits: $6>5$, so $62>59$. Option A is the mistake of comparing the ones first.

::mc NB
Which sign goes in $47 \;\square\; 47$?
A. $<$.
B. $>$.
C. $=$.
D. cannot tell.
::key C
The tens and the ones are both equal.

::mc TH
The greatest number in $38, 83, 29, 80$ is
A. $38$.
B. $83$.
C. $29$.
D. $80$.
::key B
The greatest tens digit is $8$, in $83$ and $80$; the ones digit $3>0$, so $83$ is the greatest.

::mc TH
Putting $25, 52, 22$ in order from least to greatest gives
A. $22, 25, 52$.
B. $52, 25, 22$.
C. $25, 22, 52$.
D. $22, 52, 25$.
::key A
Tens digits: $2, 5, 2$. The numbers $22$ and $25$ both have $2$ tens, and $2<5$ in the ones. Option B is from greatest to least.
