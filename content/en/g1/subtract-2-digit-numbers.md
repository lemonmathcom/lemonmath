:::goals
- Know the tens and the ones of two two-digit numbers.
- Subtract a two-digit number from a two-digit number (no regrouping), such as $57-23$.
- Solve a word problem with one subtraction.
:::

Lan has $57$ stickers. Lan gives $23$ stickers to a friend. How many stickers does Lan have left?

Counting back one by one takes a long time. This lesson helps you subtract quickly by splitting each number into tens and ones.

## Let's explore

You learned in the last lesson: for $27-4$, you subtract in the ones and keep the tens the same. Now we subtract the tens as well.

We split each number into tens and ones:

| Number | Tens | Ones |
|---|---|---|
| $57$ | $5$ | $7$ |
| $23$ | $2$ | $3$ |

- Take ones from ones: $7-3=4$.
- Take tens from tens: $5-2=3$.
- $3$ tens and $4$ ones make $34$.

So $57-23=34$.

:::theorem How to subtract two two-digit numbers
To work out $57-23$:
1. Take ones from ones: $7-3=4$.
2. Take tens from tens: $5-2=3$.
3. Put them together: $3$ tens and $4$ ones make $34$.
:::

This works because you take tens away from the tens and single ones away from the ones, so you never take away too many or too few stickers.

## Examples

We start with the simplest kind of subtraction.

:::example Work out $57-23$
**Solution.** Ones: $7-3=4$. Tens: $5-2=3$:
$$57-23=34.$$
So $57-23=34$.
:::

Now we do the same kind of subtraction with different numbers.

:::example Work out $86-41$
**Solution.** Ones: $6-1=5$. Tens: $8-4=4$:
$$86-41=45.$$
So $86-41=45$.
:::

When the number you take away is a multiple of ten, the ones of the first number stay the same.

:::example Work out $78-30$
**Solution.** Ones: $8-0=8$. Tens: $7-3=4$:
$$78-30=48.$$
So $78-30=48$.
:::

You can check a subtraction with an addition.

:::example Work out $95-54$ and check
**Solution.** Ones: $5-4=1$. Tens: $9-5=4$:
$$95-54=41.$$
Check with an addition: $41+54=95$. The answer is right.

So $95-54=41$.
:::

Now use subtraction to answer a question from everyday life.

:::example Boys in the class
A class has $68$ pupils, and $36$ of them are girls. How many boys are in the class?

**Solution.** The boys are what is left after taking away the girls, so you subtract. Ones: $8-6=2$. Tens: $6-3=3$:
$$68-36=32\ \text{(pupils)}.$$
So the class has $32$ boys.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting the tens.** Wrong: $57-23=4$. Why it is wrong: you only subtracted the ones. Right: there are still $5-2=3$ tens, so $57-23=34$.
- **Swapping tens and ones.** Wrong: $57-23=43$. Why it is wrong: you wrote the ones before the tens. Right: $3$ tens and $4$ ones make $34$.
- **Leaving out the unit in a word problem.** Wrong: "Answer: $32$". Right: "Answer: $32$ pupils".
:::

## Summary

- To subtract two two-digit numbers (no regrouping), take ones from ones and tens from tens.
- Example: $57-23=34$ because $7-3=4$ and $5-2=3$.
- Write the tens first, then the ones: $3$ tens and $4$ ones make $34$.
- Check with an addition: $34+23=57$.
- Word problems that ask "how many are left" or "how many after taking away" usually need a subtraction; remember to write the unit in the answer.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out $68-35$.

::hint
**What you need:** take ones from ones and tens from tens.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $8-5$.
2. Work out $6-3$.

::solution
Ones: $8-5=3$. Tens: $6-3=3$. So $68-35=33$.

**Answer.** $33$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $94-72$.

::hint
**What you need:** $94$ has $9$ tens and $4$ ones; $72$ has $7$ tens and $2$ ones.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $4-2$.
2. Work out $9-7$.

::solution
Ones: $4-2=2$. Tens: $9-7=2$. So $94-72=22$.

**Answer.** $22$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $79-40$.

::hint
**What you need:** a multiple of ten has $0$ ones.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $9-0$.
2. Work out $7-4$.

::solution
Ones: $9-0=9$. Tens: $7-4=3$. So $79-40=39$.

**Answer.** $39$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the box: $76-32 \;\square\; 40$.

::hint
**What you need:** work out the difference first, then compare the two numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $76-32$.
2. Compare the answer with $40$.

::solution
$6-2=4$ and $7-3=4$, so $76-32=44$. Since $44>40$, $76-32>40$.

**Answer.** The sign $>$.
:::

:::exercise
A basket has $76$ oranges. The seller sells $34$ of them. How many oranges are left in the basket?

::hint
**What you need:** "sells" and "left" mean taking away: use a subtraction.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? $76$ oranges, $34$ sold. What is asked? How many are left.
2. Work out $76-34$ and write the answer with its unit.

::solution
The number of oranges left is:
$$76-34=42\ \text{(oranges)}.$$

**Answer.** $42$ oranges.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson: subtracting a one-digit number from a two-digit number. Work out $48-5$, then subtract $21$ from the answer.

::hint
**What you need:** a one-digit number is taken away only from the ones; $21$ is $2$ tens and $1$ one.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $48-5$.
2. Subtract $21$ from the answer.

::solution
$8-5=3$, so $48-5=43$. Then $3-1=2$ and $4-2=2$, so $43-21=22$.

**Answer.** $22$.
:::

:::exercise
Ha writes: "$86-24$: ones $6-4=2$, tens $8-2=6$, so $86-24=26$". Is Ha right or wrong? Why? Correct the answer.

::hint
**What you need:** write the tens first, then the ones.

**Steps:**
1. Are Ha's two subtractions right?
2. Put them together: how many tens, how many ones?

::solution
Ha's two subtractions are right, but Ha put the digits in the wrong places. There are $6$ tens and $2$ ones, so $86-24=62$.

**Answer.** Wrong; the right answer is $62$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find the digit that goes in the box: $7\square-24=51$.

::hint
**What you need:** the difference has $1$ in the ones. Which digit minus $4$ gives $1$?

**Steps:**
1. Ask: $?-4=1$, so $?=5$.
2. Check the tens: $7-2=5$.

::solution
Ones: $5-4=1$. The number is $75$, so the missing digit is $5$.

**Check.** $75-24=51$. Correct.

**Answer.** $5$.
:::
