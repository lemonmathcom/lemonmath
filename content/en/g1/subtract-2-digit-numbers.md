:::goals
- Know the tens and the ones of two two-digit numbers.
- Subtract a two-digit number from a two-digit number (no regrouping), such as $57-23$.
- Solve a word problem with one subtraction.
:::

Lan has $57$ stickers. Lan gives $23$ stickers to a friend. How many stickers does Lan have left?

Counting back one by one takes a long time, and with this many stickers it is easy to lose your place. There is a quicker way: split each number into tens and ones, then subtract one part at a time. Once you know it, you can subtract these numbers without counting back at all.

## Let's explore

In the last lesson you worked out $27-4$: you subtracted in the ones and kept the tens the same. This time the number we take away has tens too, so we subtract both places.

To keep things tidy, we split each number into tens and ones. Look at the table:

| Number | Tens | Ones |
|---|---|---|
| $57$ | $5$ | $7$ |
| $23$ | $2$ | $3$ |

- First take ones from ones: $7-3=4$.
- Then take tens from tens: $5-2=3$.
- Put them together: $3$ tens and $4$ ones make $34$.

So Lan has $57-23=34$ stickers left.

:::theorem How to subtract two two-digit numbers
To work out $57-23$:
1. Take ones from ones: $7-3=4$.
2. Take tens from tens: $5-2=3$.
3. Put them together: $3$ tens and $4$ ones make $34$.
:::

Why does this work? Tens come off the tens and ones come off the ones, so each sticker is taken away exactly once, never too many and never too few.

## Examples

Let us try the sum from the start of the lesson and watch each step appear.

:::example Work out $57-23$
**Solution.** Ones: $7-3=4$. Tens: $5-2=3$:
$$57-23=34.$$
So $57-23=34$.
:::

Now we keep the method and change only the numbers. Before you read on, guess: what is the ones subtraction, and what is the tens subtraction?

:::example Work out $86-41$
**Solution.** Ones: $6-1=5$. Tens: $8-4=4$:
$$86-41=45.$$
So $86-41=45$.
:::

Here is a case you will meet often: the number we take away is a multiple of ten. Then the ones of the first number are not touched, so they stay the same.

:::example Work out $78-30$
**Solution.** Ones: $8-0=8$. Tens: $7-3=4$:
$$78-30=48.$$
So $78-30=48$.
:::

When you have finished, it is wise to check. Addition helps: the difference plus the number taken away must give back the first number.

:::example Work out $95-54$ and check
**Solution.** Ones: $5-4=1$. Tens: $9-5=4$:
$$95-54=41.$$
Check with an addition: $41+54=95$. The answer is right.

So $95-54=41$.
:::

Subtraction also answers everyday questions, the kind you might ask in your own class. Have a look at this one.

:::example Boys in the class
A class has $68$ pupils, and $36$ of them are girls. How many boys are in the class?

**Solution.** The boys are what is left after taking away the girls, so you subtract. Ones: $8-6=2$. Tens: $6-3=3$:
$$68-36=32\ \text{(pupils)}.$$
So the class has $32$ boys.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting the tens.** Wrong: $57-23=4$. Why it is wrong: you subtracted the ones and then stopped. Right: the tens still need subtracting, $5-2=3$ tens, so $57-23=34$.
- **Swapping tens and ones.** Wrong: $57-23=43$. Why it is wrong: you wrote the ones before the tens. Right: $3$ tens and $4$ ones make $34$, with the tens first and the ones second.
- **Leaving out the unit in a word problem.** Wrong: "Answer: $32$". Why it is wrong: a number on its own does not say what was counted. Right: "Answer: $32$ pupils".
Many pupils slip up here, so go slowly and check each place one at a time.
:::

## Summary

- To subtract two two-digit numbers (no regrouping), take ones from ones and tens from tens.
- Example: $57-23=34$ because $7-3=4$ and $5-2=3$.
- Write the tens first, then the ones: $3$ tens and $4$ ones make $34$.
- Check with an addition: $34+23=57$.
- Word problems that ask "how many are left" or "how many after taking away" usually need a subtraction; remember to write the unit in the answer.
Lan's question at the start works out the same way: tens from tens, ones from ones. Practise a few more sums until it feels natural.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with a reminder of what you need and a hint to start you off. Try it yourself first. If you get stuck, or when you have finished, open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out $68-35$.

::hint
**What you need:** take ones from ones and tens from tens.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $8-5$.
2. Work out $6-3$.

::solution
We subtract place by place, starting with the ones.

Ones: $8-5=3$, write $3$.

Tens: $6-3=3$, write $3$.

Hence $68-35=33$.

Check: $33+35=68$. Correct.

So $68-35=33$.

**Answer.** $33$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $94-72$.

::hint
**What you need:** $94$ has $9$ tens and $4$ ones; $72$ has $7$ tens and $2$ ones.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $4-2$.
2. Work out $9-7$.

::solution
We subtract place by place, starting with the ones.

Ones: $4-2=2$, write $2$.

Tens: $9-7=2$, write $2$.

Hence $94-72=22$.

Check: $22+72=94$. Correct.

So $94-72=22$.

**Answer.** $22$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $79-40$.

::hint
**What you need:** a multiple of ten has $0$ ones.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $9-0$.
2. Work out $7-4$.

::solution
We subtract place by place, starting with the ones.

Ones: $9-0=9$, write $9$.

Tens: $7-4=3$, write $3$.

Hence $79-40=39$.

Check: $39+40=79$. Correct.

So $79-40=39$.

**Answer.** $39$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the box: $76-32 \;\square\; 40$.

::hint
**What you need:** work out the difference first, then compare the two numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $76-32$.
2. Compare the answer with $40$.

::solution
We work out the left side first. Ones: $6-2=4$. Tens: $7-3=4$. So $76-32=44$.

We compare $44$ with $40$. Both numbers have $4$ tens; $44$ has $4$ ones and $40$ has $0$ ones, and $4>0$, so $44>40$.

Hence $76-32>40$.

So we write the sign $>$.

**Answer.** The sign $>$.
:::

:::exercise
A basket has $76$ oranges. The seller sells $34$ of them. How many oranges are left in the basket?

::hint
**What you need:** "sells" and "left" mean taking away: use a subtraction.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? $76$ oranges, $34$ sold. What is asked? How many are left.
2. Work out $76-34$ and write the answer with its unit.

::solution
Solution

The number of oranges left in the basket is:
$$76-34=42\ \text{(oranges)}.$$

Check: $42+34=76$. Correct.

So there are $42$ oranges left in the basket.

**Answer.** $42$ oranges.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson: subtracting a one-digit number from a two-digit number. Work out $48-5$, then subtract $21$ from the answer.

::hint
**What you need:** a one-digit number is taken away only from the ones; $21$ is $2$ tens and $1$ one.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $48-5$.
2. Subtract $21$ from the answer.

::solution
The problem has two steps: work out $48-5$ first, then subtract $21$ from the answer.

Step 1: $48-5$. Ones: $8-5=3$. The tens stay $4$. So $48-5=43$.

Step 2: $43-21$. Ones: $3-1=2$. Tens: $4-2=2$. So $43-21=22$.

Check: $22+21=43$ and $43+5=48$. Correct.

So $48-5=43$ and $43-21=22$.

**Answer.** $22$.
:::

:::exercise
Ha writes: "$86-24$: ones $6-4=2$, tens $8-2=6$, so $86-24=26$". Is Ha right or wrong? Why? Correct the answer.

::hint
**What you need:** write the tens first, then the ones.

**Steps:**
1. Are Ha's two subtractions right?
2. Put them together: how many tens, how many ones?

::solution
We check each of Ha's steps.

Ones: $6-4=2$, which is right. Tens: $8-2=6$, which is right.

But the result has $6$ tens and $2$ ones, so the tens digit must be $6$ and the ones digit must be $2$. Ha wrote $26$, that is, $2$ in the tens place and $6$ in the ones place, so the digits were put in the wrong places.

The correct way to write it is $62$.

Check: $62+24=86$. Correct.

So Ha is wrong; the right answer is $86-24=62$.

**Answer.** Wrong; the right answer is $62$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find the digit that goes in the box: $7\square-24=51$.

::hint
**What you need:** the difference has $1$ in the ones. Which digit minus $4$ gives $1$?

**Steps:**
1. Ask: $?-4=1$, so $?=5$.
2. Check the tens: $7-2=5$.

::solution
The number we want has the form $7\square$, that is, it has $7$ tens.

Ones: taking away $4$ ones must leave $1$ (the ones digit of $51$). Since $5-4=1$, the digit in the box is $5$.

Tens: $7-2=5$, which matches the tens digit of $51$.

Hence the number we want is $75$.

Check: $75-24=51$. Correct.

So the digit that goes in the box is $5$.

**Answer.** $5$.
:::
