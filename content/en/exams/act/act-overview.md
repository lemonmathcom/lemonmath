The ACT is a college admissions test used mainly by universities in the United States, alongside the SAT. Its Math section is a fast test of school mathematics: many short questions, each with one correct answer, and very little time for each. For students who have studied the Vietnamese curriculum, most of the mathematics is familiar; the challenges are speed, reading word problems in English, and a few topics taught differently.

## The format of the enhanced ACT

The "enhanced ACT" applies to online testing from April 2025 and to paper testing from September 2025.

| | Enhanced ACT Math |
|---|---|
| Questions | 45, each with 4 answer options |
| Time | 50 minutes, about 67 seconds per question |
| Calculator | allowed for the whole Math test |
| Wrong answers | no penalty |
| Score | 1 to 36 |

Before the change, the Math test had 60 questions in 60 minutes with 5 options, so older practice books do not match the current format. Among the 45 questions, 4 are experimental and do not count towards your score; you cannot tell which ones they are, so treat every question seriously.

The **Composite score** is the average of English, Math and Reading. Science is now optional.

## What the questions cover

ACT groups its Math questions into these areas:

- **Preparing for Higher Math**, made up of **Number and Quantity**, **Algebra**, **Functions**, **Geometry**, and **Statistics and Probability**.
- **Integrating Essential Skills**: rates, percentages, proportions, area and volume, averages and similar everyday mathematics, often combined in one problem.
- **Modeling**: questions in which you build, use or interpret a mathematical model of a real situation.

ACT publishes the details of each area on its official site; check act.org for the latest breakdown rather than relying on percentages from older books.

## Pacing: 67 seconds per question

The ACT rewards steady speed more than deep insight. Three habits help.

1. **Two passes.** On the first pass answer every question you can do quickly; mark the slow ones and return to them.
2. **Never leave a blank.** There is no penalty for a wrong answer, so in the last minute fill in every remaining question.
3. **Use the calculator wisely.** It is allowed throughout, but mental arithmetic is often faster for simple steps; save the calculator for decimals, square roots and checking.

## Three sample questions

:::example A rate question
A printer prints $24$ pages in $1.5$ minutes. At this rate, how many pages does it print in $10$ minutes?
A. $144$
B. $160$
C. $240$
D. $36$

**Solution.** First we find the rate per minute:
$$\frac{24}{1.5}=16\text{ pages per minute}.$$

In $10$ minutes the printer prints $16\times10=160$ pages.

So the printer prints $160$ pages, answer **B**. Option C multiplies $24$ by $10$ and forgets the $1.5$ minutes.
:::

:::example A linear function
The cost $C$, in dollars, of renting a bike for $h$ hours is $C=4h+6$. What does the number $4$ represent?
A. The fixed cost of renting the bike
B. The total cost for one hour
C. The additional cost for each hour
D. The number of hours rented

**Solution.** In $C=4h+6$, each increase of $1$ in $h$ increases $C$ by $4$, so $4$ is the rate of change of the cost per hour. The number $6$ is the cost when $h=0$, the fixed fee.

So $4$ is the additional cost for each hour, answer **C**.
:::

:::example A probability question
A bag contains $5$ red, $3$ blue and $2$ green marbles. One marble is chosen at random. What is the probability that it is not blue?
A. $\dfrac{3}{10}$
B. $\dfrac{1}{2}$
C. $\dfrac{3}{7}$
D. $\dfrac{7}{10}$

**Solution.** There are $5+3+2=10$ marbles, and $10-3=7$ of them are not blue.

The probability is therefore $\dfrac{7}{10}$.

So the probability that the marble is not blue is $\dfrac{7}{10}$, answer **D**. Option A is the probability that it is blue.
:::

## How the ACT compares with the SAT

Both tests are accepted by US universities, and many students try both. The digital SAT Math section has two modules of 22 questions, 35 minutes each, with a built-in Desmos calculator and some questions where you type the answer. The ACT has more questions per minute, all multiple choice. If you are fast and accurate, the ACT format may suit you; if you prefer more time per question and adaptive modules, look at our [SAT section](lesson:sat-overview). Taking one practice test of each is the best way to decide.

## An eight-week plan

:::note Weeks before the test
1. **Week 1:** take a full practice test under time; list the topics where you lost questions.
2. **Weeks 2 to 5:** study one topic area each week with the lessons in this section: [Number, quantity and proportion](lesson:act-number-quantity), [Algebra and functions](lesson:act-algebra-functions), then geometry and statistics. Finish each week with mixed timed questions.
3. **Week 6:** modeling and integrated problems; practice reading long word problems quickly.
4. **Weeks 7 to 8:** one full timed test each week, then review every mistake in an error log.
:::

:::warning Common traps
- **Practicing with the old format.** Old books have 60 questions with 5 options; adjust your pacing to 45 questions in 50 minutes.
- **Spending three minutes on one question.** Mark it, move on, and come back.
- **Reading too fast.** Many wrong answers come from missing a word such as "not", "except" or "least".
- **Leaving blanks.** A guess can only help.
:::
