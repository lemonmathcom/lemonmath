Number and quantity questions are some of the fastest marks on the ACT, but they are also where careless errors cost the most: a percent applied to the wrong amount, a unit left unconverted, a sign lost in a power of $i$. This lesson collects the ideas you need and shows how to set out a quick, reliable solution for each type.

## Percentages

A change of $p\%$ multiplies a quantity by a **multiplier**: an increase of $p\%$ multiplies by $1+\dfrac{p}{100}$, a decrease by $1-\dfrac{p}{100}$. Successive changes multiply their multipliers, so they do not simply add.

:::example Up 20%, then down 20%
A jacket costs $50$ dollars. Its price is increased by $20\%$, and later the new price is decreased by $20\%$. What is the final price?
A. $50$ dollars
B. $48$ dollars
C. $46$ dollars
D. $52$ dollars

**Solution.** The increase multiplies the price by $1.2$ and the decrease multiplies it by $0.8$.

So the final price is
$$50\times1.2\times0.8=50\times0.96=48.$$

So the final price is $48$ dollars, answer **B**. Option A assumes that $+20\%$ and $-20\%$ cancel out.
:::

## Ratios, proportions and scale

If two quantities are in the ratio $a:b$, write them as $ak$ and $bk$ for some number $k$. For a proportion $\dfrac ab=\dfrac cd$, cross-multiply: $ad=bc$.

:::example A map scale
On a map, $1$ cm represents $2.5$ km. Two towns are $7.2$ cm apart on the map. What is the actual distance between them?
A. $18$ km
B. $2.88$ km
C. $9.7$ km
D. $180$ km

**Solution.** Each centimeter on the map represents $2.5$ km, so $7.2$ cm represents
$$7.2\times2.5=18\text{ km}.$$

So the towns are $18$ km apart, answer **A**. Option B divides instead of multiplying.
:::

## Rates and unit conversion

Write units in every step and cancel them like numbers. For example, $1$ km $=1000$ m and $1$ h $=3600$ s, so to convert km/h to m/s, multiply by $\dfrac{1000}{3600}=\dfrac{1}{3.6}$.

:::example From km/h to m/s
A train travels at $72$ km/h. What is its speed in meters per second?
A. $259.2$
B. $7.2$
C. $20$
D. $1.2$

**Solution.** We convert kilometers to meters and hours to seconds:
$$72\ \frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}=\frac{72\times1000\text{ m}}{3600\text{ s}}=20\ \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}.$$

So the speed is $20$ m/s, answer **C**. Option A multiplies by $3.6$ instead of dividing.
:::

## Exponents, radicals and scientific notation

:::note Rules to know
$$a^ma^n=a^{m+n},\qquad\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n},\qquad\left(a^m\right)^n=a^{mn},\qquad a^{-n}=\frac{1}{a^n},$$
$$\sqrt{ab}=\sqrt a\sqrt b,\qquad a^{1/n}=\sqrt[n]{a}.$$
A number in scientific notation has the form $a\times10^n$ with $1\le a<10$.
:::

:::example Powers of 2 and a radical sum
Which of the following is equal to $\dfrac{2^5\cdot4^3}{8^3}$?
A. $2$
B. $8$
C. $16$
D. $4$

**Solution.** We write every factor as a power of $2$: $4^3=\left(2^2\right)^3=2^6$ and $8^3=\left(2^3\right)^3=2^9$.

Then
$$\frac{2^5\cdot2^6}{2^9}=2^{5+6-9}=2^2=4.$$

So the expression equals $4$, answer **D**.

In the same way, radicals are simplified by taking out square factors: $\sqrt{50}+\sqrt{18}=5\sqrt2+3\sqrt2=8\sqrt2$.
:::

:::example Scientific notation
What is $\dfrac{6\times10^8}{3\times10^{-2}}$ in scientific notation?
A. $2\times10^{6}$
B. $2\times10^{10}$
C. $3\times10^{10}$
D. $2\times10^{-16}$

**Solution.** We divide the numbers and the powers of $10$ separately:
$$\frac{6}{3}\times10^{8-(-2)}=2\times10^{10}.$$

So the result is $2\times10^{10}$, answer **B**. Option A subtracts $8-2$ and ignores the negative sign.
:::

## Complex numbers

The number $i$ satisfies $i^2=-1$. Add complex numbers by adding real parts and imaginary parts; multiply them like binomials and then replace $i^2$ by $-1$. Powers of $i$ repeat with period $4$: $i,-1,-i,1$.

:::example Multiplying complex numbers
Which of the following is equal to $(3+2i)(1-i)$?
A. $5-i$
B. $3-2i$
C. $1+i$
D. $5+i$

**Solution.** We expand the product like two binomials:
$$(3+2i)(1-i)=3-3i+2i-2i^2.$$

Since $i^2=-1$, the term $-2i^2$ equals $+2$, so the product is $3+2-i=5-i$.

So $(3+2i)(1-i)=5-i$, answer **A**.
:::

:::warning Common traps
- **Adding percentages:** a $20\%$ rise followed by a $20\%$ fall is a $4\%$ fall, not $0\%$.
- **The base of a percentage:** "$25\%$ more than $x$" is $1.25x$, but "$x$ is $25\%$ more than $y$" means $x=1.25y$.
- **Unit conversions upside down:** check that the units cancel to what you want.
- **Exponent rules with different bases:** convert to a common base first.
- **Powers of $i$:** divide the exponent by $4$ and use the remainder.
:::

- percent increase / decrease | tăng / giảm phần trăm

- ratio, proportion | tỉ số, tỉ lệ thức
- scale | tỉ lệ bản đồ

- exponent, radical | số mũ, căn thức

:::

## Similar exercises

:::exercise
A pair of shoes costs $80$ dollars. It is on sale for $30\%$ off, and then a $10\%$ sales tax is added to the sale price. What is the final cost?

::hint
**What you need:** multipliers $0.7$ and $1.1$.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply $80$ by $0.7$.
2. Multiply the result by $1.1$.

::solution
The discount of $30\%$ multiplies the price by $0.7$, and the tax of $10\%$ multiplies the sale price by $1.1$.

So the final cost is
$$80\times0.7\times1.1=56\times1.1=61.6.$$

So the final cost of the shoes is $61.60$ dollars.

**Answer.** $61.60$ dollars.
:::

:::exercise
The ratio of boys to girls in a class is $3:5$. There are $32$ students in the class. How many boys are there?

::hint
**What you need:** write the numbers of boys and girls as $3k$ and $5k$.

::solution
Let the numbers of boys and girls be $3k$ and $5k$.

Then $3k+5k=32$, so $8k=32$ and $k=4$.

The number of boys is $3k=3\times4=12$, and the number of girls is $5k=20$. Check: $12+20=32$.

So there are $12$ boys in the class.

**Answer.** $12$.
:::

:::exercise
A car travels $300$ km in $3$ hours $45$ minutes. Find its average speed in km/h.

::hint
**What you need:** write $45$ minutes as a fraction of an hour.

::solution
Since $45$ minutes $=\dfrac{45}{60}=0.75$ hours, the travel time is $3.75$ hours.

The average speed is
$$\frac{300}{3.75}=80\text{ km/h}.$$

So the average speed of the car is $80$ km/h.

**Answer.** $80$ km/h.
:::

:::exercise
Simplify $\dfrac{\left(x^3y^{-2}\right)^2}{x^4y^{-3}}$, where $x,y\ne0$.

::hint
**What you need:** $\left(a^m\right)^n=a^{mn}$ and $\dfrac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}$.

::solution
First we expand the numerator: $\left(x^3y^{-2}\right)^2=x^6y^{-4}$.

Then we divide powers with the same base:
$$\frac{x^6y^{-4}}{x^4y^{-3}}=x^{6-4}y^{-4-(-3)}=x^2y^{-1}.$$

So the expression simplifies to $\dfrac{x^2}{y}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{x^2}{y}$.
:::

:::exercise
Simplify $i^{2027}$.

::hint
**What you need:** the powers of $i$ repeat every $4$; find the remainder when $2027$ is divided by $4$.

::solution
Dividing $2027$ by $4$ gives $2027=4\times506+3$.

Since $i^4=1$, we have $i^{2027}=\left(i^4\right)^{506}\cdot i^3=i^3$.

Also $i^3=i^2\cdot i=-i$.

So $i^{2027}=-i$.

**Answer.** $-i$.
:::

## Application exercises

:::exercise
A city has a population of $2.4\times10^6$. The population grows by $5\%$. Write the new population in scientific notation.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply by $1.05$, then write the result as $a\times10^n$ with $1\le a<10$.

::solution
A growth of $5\%$ multiplies the population by $1.05$.

So the new population is
$$2.4\times10^6\times1.05=2.52\times10^6.$$

So the new population is $2.52\times10^6$ people, that is $2{,}520{,}000$.

**Answer.** $2.52\times10^6$.
:::

:::exercise
A wall is $3.5$ m high and $4$ m wide. It needs two coats of paint, and $1$ liter of paint covers $10$ m². Paint is sold only in $1$-liter cans. How many cans are needed?

::hint
**What you need:** total area $=$ area of the wall $\times$ number of coats; round up at the end.

::solution
The area of the wall is $3.5\times4=14$ m², so two coats cover $2\times14=28$ m².

The paint needed is $\dfrac{28}{10}=2.8$ liters.

Paint is sold in whole $1$-liter cans, so $2$ cans are not enough and we must round up to $3$ cans.

So $3$ cans of paint are needed.

**Answer.** $3$ cans.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose that $1$ dollar is worth $25{,}000$ đồng. A hotel room costs $120$ dollars per night. How much is a stay of $3$ nights in đồng?

::hint
**What you need:** first the cost in dollars, then multiply by the exchange rate.

::solution
The cost of $3$ nights is $3\times120=360$ dollars.

At $25{,}000$ đồng per dollar, this is
$$360\times25{,}000=9{,}000{,}000\text{ đồng}.$$

So a stay of $3$ nights costs $9{,}000{,}000$ đồng.

**Answer.** $9{,}000{,}000$ đồng.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Let $A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{pmatrix}$ and let $I=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$. Find the matrix $2A-I$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply every entry by $2$, then subtract entry by entry.

::solution
Multiplying every entry of $A$ by $2$ gives $2A=\begin{pmatrix}2&4\\6&8\end{pmatrix}$.

Subtracting $I$ entry by entry, we get
$$2A-I=\begin{pmatrix}2-1&4-0\\6-0&8-1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&4\\6&7\end{pmatrix}.$$

So $2A-I=\begin{pmatrix}1&4\\6&7\end{pmatrix}$.

**Answer.** $\begin{pmatrix}1&4\\6&7\end{pmatrix}$.
:::

What is $15\%$ of $240$?
A. $24$
B. $16$
C. $36$
D. $3.6$
::key C
$0.15\times240=36$.

Which of the following is equal to $\sqrt{72}$?
A. $6\sqrt2$
B. $8\sqrt3$
C. $3\sqrt6$
D. $12\sqrt2$
::key A
$\sqrt{72}=\sqrt{36\cdot2}=6\sqrt2$. Option C is $\sqrt{54}$ and option D is $\sqrt{288}$.

::mc
What is $(2-i)+(3+4i)$?
A. $5+5i$
B. $5+3i$
C. $6-4i$
D. $-1+3i$
::key B
Add the real parts and the imaginary parts: $(2+3)+(-1+4)i$.

::mc
What is $\left(5\times10^3\right)\left(4\times10^{-5}\right)$ in scientific notation?
A. $20\times10^{-2}$
B. $2\times10^{-2}$
C. $2\times10^{8}$
D. $2\times10^{-1}$
::key D
$20\times10^{-2}=2\times10^{-1}$. Option A is the same value, but not in scientific notation.

::tf
A price rises by $50\%$ and then falls by $50\%$. Decide whether each statement is true or false.
a) The final price is $75\%$ of the original price.
b) The overall change is a decrease of $25\%$.
c) The final price is equal to the original price.
d) The final price would be the same if the fall came first.
::key T T F T
$1.5\times0.5=0.75$. Multiplication is commutative, so the order of the changes does not matter.
