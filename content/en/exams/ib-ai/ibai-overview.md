- Know the papers, timings and weightings of IB Maths AI at SL and HL.
- Know the five topics and the distinctive content of AI: finance, models, Voronoi diagrams, statistical tests.
- Understand how answers are marked, and the rounding rules.
- Plan your revision over the last twelve weeks.

Think of the questions people actually ask with numbers. How much will this loan really cost me? Which model fits the growth of this town? Where should a new hospital go so that it serves the people furthest from the old ones? IB Mathematics: Applications and Interpretation, usually called **Maths AI**, is built around questions like these. It is still real mathematics, but the starting point is almost always a situation, and the finishing point is an answer that makes sense in it.

That changes how you prepare. In AI the calculator does much of the heavy arithmetic, so marks go to choosing the right model, setting it up correctly, and explaining what the result means. If you have come from the Vietnamese curriculum, where speed with algebra by hand is prized, this can feel unusual at first. It soon becomes a strength: you already know the mathematics, and now you learn to use it.

## The papers

A **graphing display calculator (GDC)** is needed in **every** AI paper. The current syllabus is examined until November 2028.

| Level | Component | Time | Marks | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SL | Paper 1 (short questions) | 90 minutes | 80 | 40% |
| SL | Paper 2 (longer questions) | 90 minutes | 80 | 40% |
| SL | Internal assessment (exploration) | — | — | 20% |
| HL | Paper 1 | 120 minutes | 110 | 30% |
| HL | Paper 2 | 120 minutes | 110 | 30% |
| HL | Paper 3 (two extended problems) | 1 hour 15 minutes | 55 | 20% |
| HL | Internal assessment | — | — | 20% |

Final grades go from 1 to 7. A formula booklet is provided in every paper. Paper 3 at HL has been 1 hour 15 minutes since the 2025 exams; older materials may say 1 hour.

## The five topics

| Topic | What is special in AI |
|---|---|
| Number and algebra | approximation and errors, financial mathematics with the TVM solver; HL: matrices |
| Functions | building and choosing models: linear, quadratic, exponential, sinusoidal and more |
| Geometry and trigonometry | 3D measurement, trigonometry in context, Voronoi diagrams; HL: graph theory |
| Statistics and probability | regression, correlation, chi-squared tests and $t$-tests at IB level; HL: further distributions and tests |
| Calculus | rates of change, optimisation, areas with the trapezoidal rule; HL: differential equations |

## How answers are marked

IB examiners award marks for **method** as well as for the final answer, so always write down what you are doing, even when the GDC does the work. For example, write the equation you solved, or the inputs you gave the TVM solver, before the answer. A correct answer with no working may not receive full marks, and a wrong answer with a correct method often keeps most of them.

Two rounding rules matter in almost every question:

- Give approximate answers to **three significant figures** unless the question says otherwise.
- Money is usually given to **two decimal places**.

Keep full accuracy in the calculator until the end; rounding in the middle is one of the most common ways to lose a mark.

## Two small examples of the AI style

:::example Money that grows
You invest $5000$ dollars at $4\%$ per year, compounded monthly. What is the investment worth after $6$ years?

**Solution.** Each month the money is multiplied by $1+\dfrac{0.04}{12}$, and there are $6\times12=72$ months, so the value is
$$5000\left(1+\frac{0.04}{12}\right)^{72}\approx6353.71\text{ dollars}.$$

On the GDC you would enter $N=72$, $I\%=4$, $PV=-5000$, $PMT=0$, $P/Y=C/Y=12$ and solve for $FV$; write these inputs in your answer.

So the investment is worth about $6353.71$ dollars after $6$ years.
:::

:::example Choosing a model
The number of bacteria in a dish doubles roughly every $3$ hours. Which type of function is the most suitable model?
A. Linear
B. Quadratic
C. Exponential
D. Sinusoidal

**Solution.** Doubling over equal time intervals means the quantity is **multiplied** by the same factor each step, which is the defining property of exponential growth. A linear model adds the same amount each step, and a sinusoidal model repeats in cycles.

So the most suitable model is exponential, answer **C**.
:::

## A revision plan

:::note Twelve weeks before the exams
1. **Weeks 1–2:** [Number, approximation and financial maths](lesson:ibai-number-finance), then [Function models](lesson:ibai-function-models). Learn the TVM solver and regression menus on your own GDC.
2. **Weeks 3–4:** [Geometry, trigonometry and Voronoi diagrams](lesson:ibai-geometry-voronoi).
3. **Weeks 5–6:** [Statistics, correlation and hypothesis tests](lesson:ibai-statistics-tests).
4. **Weeks 7–8:** [Applied calculus](lesson:ibai-calculus); HL students add [Matrices and graph theory](lesson:ibai-hl-matrices-graphs) and [Further statistics and differential equations](lesson:ibai-hl-further-statistics).
5. **Weeks 9–12:** full timed papers, starting with the [SL practice paper](lesson:ibai-practice-paper-sl), then an error log: for each lost mark, write whether it was method, rounding or reading the question.
:::

:::warning Common traps
- **Answer only from the GDC:** write the equation, the regression type or the solver inputs first.
- **Rounding too early:** store values in the calculator.
- **Missing units and context:** "$6353.71$ dollars after $6$ years", not just "$6353.71$".
- **Wrong mode:** check degrees or radians before any trigonometry.
- **Models outside their domain:** say when a model stops making sense.
:::

Come back to the questions at the start: the cost of a loan, the best model, the best place for a hospital. Every one of them is answered by setting up mathematics carefully and then saying what the numbers mean. That is the whole of Maths AI in one sentence. Learn your calculator well, practise writing your method, and the rest will follow.
