AP Precalculus is a College Board course for students who are preparing for calculus or who want a solid final year of algebra and functions. Its exam is built around one idea: functions as models of how quantities change. Students from the Vietnamese curriculum know most of the algebra already; the new parts are the emphasis on **rates of change**, on **modeling with data**, and on explaining answers in words.

## The exam from May 2027

From May 2027 the exam is **hybrid**: you answer the multiple-choice section in the Bluebook app and write the free-response answers by hand.

| | Part | Questions | Time | Calculator | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Section I: multiple choice | A | 29 | 65 minutes | not allowed | 43.75% |
| | B | 13 | 40 minutes | graphing calculator required | 18.75% |
| Section II: free response | A | 2 | 35 minutes | graphing calculator required | |
| | B | 2 | 35 minutes | not allowed | |

In total, Section I has **42 questions in 1 hour 45 minutes** and is worth **62.5%** of the score; Section II has **4 questions in 1 hour 10 minutes**, each worth **6 points**, and is worth **37.5%**. Scores are reported on the AP scale from 1 to 5. Before 2027 the multiple-choice section had 40 questions, so older practice materials do not match the current format.

## What is tested

The course has four units, but **only Units 1, 2 and 3 are tested**:

| Unit | Content | Share of the multiple-choice section |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Polynomial and rational functions | rates of change, polynomials, rational functions, transformations, modeling | about 30–40% |
| 2. Exponential and logarithmic functions | sequences, exponential and logarithmic functions, inverses, semi-log plots, modeling | about 27–40% |
| 3. Trigonometric and polar functions | periodic phenomena, sine, cosine and tangent, trigonometric equations, polar coordinates | about 30–35% |

Unit 4 (parametric functions, vectors and matrices) is part of the course but is **not** on the exam.

## The four free-response questions

Each free-response question is worth 6 points and has a fixed type:

1. **Function concepts** (calculator): reading and combining functions given by graphs, tables or formulas; rates of change; composition and inverses.
2. **Modeling a non-periodic context** (calculator): building a model, often linear, quadratic, exponential or logarithmic, from a situation or data, using it, and judging its limitations.
3. **Modeling a periodic context** (no calculator): building a sinusoidal model from a description or a graph and interpreting it.
4. **Symbolic manipulations** (no calculator): rewriting expressions with exponent, logarithm and trigonometric identities, and solving equations.

The lesson "The four free-response types and how to score" in this section will show how each type is marked.

## Three sample questions

:::example Average rate of change
What is the average rate of change of $f(x)=x^2-3x$ on the interval $[1,4]$?
A. $2$
B. $6$
C. $-\dfrac23$
D. $4$

**Solution.** The average rate of change is $\dfrac{f(4)-f(1)}{4-1}$. We have $f(4)=16-12=4$ and $f(1)=1-3=-2$, so
$$\frac{4-(-2)}{3}=\frac63=2.$$

So the average rate of change is $2$, answer **A**. Option B forgets to divide by the length of the interval.
:::

:::example An exponential model
A sample of $80$ grams of a substance decays so that half of it remains after every $5$ days. Which function gives the mass, in grams, after $t$ days?
A. $M(t)=80-8t$
B. $M(t)=80\left(\dfrac12\right)^{5t}$
C. $M(t)=80\left(\dfrac12\right)^{t/5}$
D. $M(t)=40^{t/5}$

**Solution.** The mass is multiplied by $\dfrac12$ every $5$ days, so after $t$ days it has been halved $\dfrac t5$ times. Hence
$$M(t)=80\left(\frac12\right)^{t/5}.$$

Check: $M(5)=80\cdot\dfrac12=40$.

So the model is $M(t)=80\left(\dfrac12\right)^{t/5}$, answer **C**. Option B halves the mass $5$ times every day.
:::

:::example A periodic function
What are the amplitude and the period of $y=3\sin(2x)+1$?
A. Amplitude $1$, period $2\pi$
B. Amplitude $3$, period $\pi$
C. Amplitude $3$, period $4\pi$
D. Amplitude $2$, period $\pi$

**Solution.** For $y=a\sin(bx)+d$, the amplitude is $|a|=3$ and the period is $\dfrac{2\pi}{b}=\dfrac{2\pi}{2}=\pi$.

So the amplitude is $3$ and the period is $\pi$, answer **B**.
:::

## A ten-week plan

:::note Weeks before the exam
1. **Weeks 1 to 3, Unit 1:** [Rates of change and polynomial functions](lesson:apprecalc-rates-polynomials), then [Rational functions, transformations and modelling](lesson:apprecalc-rational-transformations).
2. **Weeks 4 to 6, Unit 2:** sequences, exponential and logarithmic functions, semi-log plots.
3. **Weeks 7 to 8, Unit 3:** trigonometric functions, equations and polar functions.
4. **Weeks 9 to 10:** the free-response types, then a full timed practice exam; review every mistake in an error log.
:::

:::warning Common traps
- **Old practice materials:** before 2027 there were 40 multiple-choice questions; Unit 4 topics are not tested.
- **Calculator parts:** Part A of Section I has no calculator; Part B requires a graphing calculator. Practice both.
- **Answers without explanation:** free-response questions often ask you to justify or interpret; a correct number alone may not earn the point.
- **Rates in words:** be ready to say whether a function is increasing or decreasing and at an increasing or decreasing rate.
:::
