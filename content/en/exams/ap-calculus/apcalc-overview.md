AP Calculus is one of the most popular Advanced Placement exams. A good score can earn college credit or placement at many universities in the United States and elsewhere, and it shows admissions officers that you can handle university-level mathematics. For students from Vietnam the content is familiar: limits, derivatives and integrals appear in grades 11 and 12. What is different is the depth of **justification**: AP asks you to explain your reasoning in words and with theorems, not only to compute.

## AB or BC?

| | Calculus AB | Calculus BC |
|---|---|---|
| Content | Units 1–8: limits, derivatives, integrals, differential equations and their applications | Everything in AB, plus Units 9–10 (parametric, polar and vector-valued functions; infinite series) and a few extra topics inside Units 6–8 |
| Equivalent to | about one semester of college calculus | about two semesters |
| Scores | one AP score, 1 to 5 | one AP score, plus an AB subscore |

If your school or university plan allows it and you are comfortable with the AB content, BC is usually the better choice: the AB subscore shows your performance on the AB part as well.

## The exam format (from May 2027)

Both exams have the same structure. The multiple-choice section is taken in the Bluebook app; free-response answers are handwritten in a booklet.

| Section | Part | Questions | Time | Calculator | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I. Multiple choice | A | 29 | 62 minutes | not permitted | 50% for Section I |
| | B | 13 | 38 minutes | graphing calculator required | |
| II. Free response | A | 2 | 30 minutes | graphing calculator required | 50% for Section II |
| | B | 4 | 60 minutes | not permitted | |

Multiple-choice questions have four options and there is no penalty for wrong answers. Each free-response question is worth 9 points. Older books and websites describe 30 + 15 multiple-choice questions; that format ended with the 2026 exam. The AB exam in 2027 is scheduled for 10 May; check AP Central for the final timetable.

## What free-response questions look like

Free-response questions mix several units, and each part is scored separately, so you can earn points on later parts even if an earlier part is wrong. Common types are:

- **Rates in context**, often with a table of values: estimating a derivative, a Riemann sum, or the total change $\displaystyle\int_a^b r(t)\,dt$.
- **Particle motion**: velocity, acceleration, speeding up or slowing down, total distance.
- **Analysis of a graph of $f'$**: where $f$ increases, has extrema or inflection points, with justification.
- **Differential equations**: a slope field, a tangent-line approximation, separation of variables.
- **Area and volume** of regions between curves.
- **BC only**: Taylor polynomials and error bounds, convergence of series, parametric or polar curves.

:::warning How free-response answers are marked
Readers look for the **setup** and the **justification**, not just the number. "The function has a relative maximum at $x=2$" earns little without the reason: "because $f'$ changes from positive to negative at $x=2$". In calculator parts, write the integral or equation you are solving before giving the decimal answer, correct to three decimal places.
:::

## Two sample questions

:::example Multiple choice, no calculator
What is $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to3}\frac{x^2-9}{x-3}$?
A. $0$
B. $3$
C. $6$
D. The limit does not exist.

**Solution.** For $x\ne3$, $\dfrac{x^2-9}{x-3}=x+3$, which approaches $6$. The answer is **C**. Option D is a common error: the function is undefined at $x=3$, but the limit exists.
:::

:::example A free-response part, no calculator
The function $f$ is twice differentiable, and $f'(x)=x^2-4$. On which intervals is $f$ increasing? Justify your answer.

**Solution.** $f'(x)=(x-2)(x+2)>0$ for $x<-2$ and for $x>2$. So $f$ is increasing on $(-\infty, -2]$ and $[2, \infty)$, **because $f'(x)>0$ on these intervals** (except at the endpoints). The justification sentence is what earns the point.
:::

## A study plan

1. **Weeks 1–2:** take a full official practice exam in Bluebook to find your level; review Units 1–3.
2. **Weeks 3–6:** Units 4–8 (and 9–10 for BC), one lesson at a time, with the exercises.
3. **Weeks 7–9:** past free-response questions from AP Central, timed: 15 minutes per question. Compare your work with the official scoring guidelines.
4. **Weeks 10–12:** full practice exams and your [error log](lesson:error-log).

Free official resources: AP Classroom (through your school), past free-response questions with scoring guidelines on AP Central, and the practice exams in Bluebook. For calculator skills, see [Using calculators and Desmos wisely](lesson:calculator-skills).
