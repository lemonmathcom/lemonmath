The SAT is an admission test run by College Board and accepted by universities in the United States and many other countries. Since 2024 it has been fully digital: you take it on a laptop or tablet in an app called Bluebook. The good news for students who learn math in Vietnam is that the math itself is rarely harder than grade 10. What is new is the language, the pace and the style of the questions. This lesson shows you exactly what to expect.

## The structure of the Math section

| Feature | Digital SAT Math |
|---|---|
| Modules | 2, each with 22 questions |
| Time | 35 minutes per module, 70 minutes in total |
| Questions | 44 in total |
| Question types | About 75% multiple choice with four options; about 25% student-produced response (you type the answer) |
| Calculator | Allowed on every question; the Desmos graphing calculator is built into Bluebook |
| Reference sheet | Common geometry formulas are available in the app |
| Score | 200–800 for Math |

Two details matter for your strategy.

**The test is adaptive by stage.** Everyone gets a first module with a mix of easy, medium and hard questions. How you do on it decides whether your second module is the harder or the easier version. A high score is only possible through the harder second module, so accuracy in module 1 is worth more than speed.

**There is no penalty for a wrong answer.** Never leave a question blank. Each module also contains 2 unscored trial questions, but you cannot tell which ones they are, so treat every question as real.

:::tip Pace yourself
35 minutes for 22 questions is about 95 seconds per question. Questions are arranged from easier to harder inside each module, so aim to finish the first 10 in about 12 minutes and keep the saved time for the last 6.
:::

## The four content domains

| Domain | Share of the section | Questions | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Algebra | about 35% | 13–15 | Linear equations, linear functions, systems of two linear equations, linear inequalities |
| Advanced Math | about 35% | 13–15 | Equivalent expressions, quadratic and exponential functions, nonlinear equations and systems |
| Problem-Solving and Data Analysis | about 15% | 5–7 | Ratios, rates, percentages, units, statistics, probability, data displays |
| Geometry and Trigonometry | about 15% | 5–7 | Area and volume, lines and angles, triangles, right-triangle trigonometry, circles |

About 30% of the questions are set in a real-life context, so reading carefully in English is part of the skill.

Start with Algebra, because it carries the most questions and everything else builds on it:

## If you study the Vietnamese curriculum

Most of SAT Math is covered by grades 8 to 10 in Vietnam, but the emphasis is different.

| You already know | You need to practice | Probably new to you |
|---|---|---|
| Solving linear and quadratic equations, systems, Viète's theorem | Reading word problems in English and writing the equation yourself | Interpreting what a number in a formula means in context |
| Graphs of $y=ax+b$ and $y=ax^2$ | The forms $y=mx+b$ and $y=a(x-h)^2+k$, slope as a rate of change | Exponential growth and decay models, such as $P=200(1.05)^t$ |
| Trigonometric ratios in right triangles, circle geometry | Circle equations $(x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2$, radians, arc length | Margin of error, random sampling and what a study can conclude |
| Mean, median, frequency tables | Reading scatterplots, lines of best fit and two-way tables | Units such as feet, miles, pounds and gallons |

Notation differs too. The SAT writes decimals with a point ($2.5$, not $2{,}5$), uses a comma to separate thousands ($12{,}000$), and writes slope as $m$ in $y=mx+b$.

## Three sample questions

These questions are written by Lemonmath in the style of the test.

:::example Algebra, multiple choice
If $3x+7=22$, what is the value of $6x+5$?  
A. $15$  
B. $30$  
C. $35$  
D. $40$

**Solution.** The question asks for $6x+5$, not for $x$. From $3x+7=22$ we get $3x=15$, so $6x=30$ and $6x+5=35$. The answer is **C**. Option B stops at $6x$; option A is $3x$.
:::

:::example Advanced Math, student-produced response
The function $f$ is defined by $f(x)=x^2-6x+11$. What is the minimum value of $f(x)$?

**Solution.** Complete the square: $f(x)=(x-3)^2+2$. A square is never negative, so the minimum value is $2$, reached when $x=3$. You type **2**. In Desmos, typing the function and clicking the lowest point of the parabola shows $(3, 2)$.
:::

:::example Problem-Solving and Data Analysis, multiple choice
The price of a jacket was 80 dollars. The price was increased by 25%, and the new price was later decreased by 25%. What is the final price, in dollars?  
A. $70$  
B. $75$  
C. $80$  
D. $85$

**Solution.** After the increase: $80\times1.25=100$. After the decrease: $100\times0.75=75$. The answer is **B**. Option C is the trap: the two changes are percentages of different amounts, so they do not cancel.
:::

## A 10-week study plan

| Weeks | Focus | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Diagnose | Take one full official practice test in Bluebook under real timing. Sort every mistake by domain. |
| 2–3 | Algebra | The two Algebra lessons, then mixed sets of 20 questions. |
| 4–5 | Advanced Math | Expressions, quadratics, exponentials. Learn to check answers with Desmos. |
| 6 | Problem-Solving and Data Analysis | Percentages, rates, units, statistics vocabulary. |
| 7 | Geometry and Trigonometry | Formulas, circles, right triangles, radians. |
| 8 | Second practice test | Full test, then two days only on your error log. |
| 9 | Weak spots | Mixed timed sets from your two weakest domains. |
| 10 | Final practice | One or two full tests; review, sleep well, do not learn new topics. |

Throughout, use the methods from [Revision that works](lesson:evidence-based-revision): test yourself instead of rereading, come back to old topics every week, and keep an error log.

:::note Free official resources
College Board publishes full-length digital practice tests inside the Bluebook app, and Khan Academy offers free Official Digital SAT Prep made with College Board. Use these for full tests; use Lemonmath lessons to learn and drill each topic. Test dates, fees and registration change every year, so check the College Board website before you register.
:::

## What to do next

1. Install Bluebook and take the first practice test this week, before studying anything.
2. Start the Algebra lessons and keep an error log from day one.
3. Spend ten minutes exploring Desmos: graph a line, a parabola and find where they cross.
