You are thinking about taking the SAT, and you have heard that the Math section is digital and in English. The first questions are usually: what will I face, and how should I prepare? The SAT is an admission test run by College Board and accepted by universities in the United States and many other countries. Since 2024 it has been fully digital: you take it on a laptop or tablet in an app called Bluebook. The good news for students who learn maths in Vietnam is that the maths itself is rarely harder than grade 10. What is new is the language, the pace and the style of the questions. So let us look at exactly what to expect.

## The structure of the Math section

| Feature | Digital SAT Math |
|---|---|
| Modules | 2, each with 22 questions |
| Time | 35 minutes per module, 70 minutes in total |
| Questions | 44 in total |
| Question types | About 75% multiple choice with four options; about 25% student-produced response (you type the answer) |
| Calculator | Allowed on every question; the Desmos graphing calculator is built into Bluebook |
| Reference sheet | Common geometry formulas are available in the app |
| Score | 200–800 for Math |

Beyond the numbers in the table, two details shape your strategy.

**The test is adaptive by stage.** Everyone gets a first module with a mix of easy, medium and hard questions. How you do on it decides whether your second module is the harder or the easier version. A high score is only possible through the harder second module. That is why accuracy in module 1 is worth more than speed.

**There is no penalty for a wrong answer.** So never leave a question blank: pick an option or type your best answer. Each module also contains 2 unscored trial questions. You cannot tell which ones they are, so treat every question as real.

:::tip Pace yourself
35 minutes for 22 questions is about 95 seconds per question. Questions are arranged from easier to harder inside each module, so the first ones can be quick. Aim to finish the first 10 in about 12 minutes and keep the saved time for the last 6.
:::

## The four content domains

| Domain | Share of the section | Questions | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Algebra | about 35% | 13–15 | Linear equations, linear functions, systems of two linear equations, linear inequalities |
| Advanced Math | about 35% | 13–15 | Equivalent expressions, quadratic and exponential functions, nonlinear equations and systems |
| Problem-Solving and Data Analysis | about 15% | 5–7 | Ratios, rates, percentages, units, statistics, probability, data displays |
| Geometry and Trigonometry | about 15% | 5–7 | Area and volume, lines and angles, triangles, right-triangle trigonometry, circles |

About 30% of the questions are set in a real-life context, so reading carefully in English is part of the skill. Do not worry if this feels unfamiliar at first: reading carefully and then writing the equation yourself is a habit you can train.

Start with Algebra, because it carries the most questions and everything else builds on it. These two lessons are the place to begin:

- [Linear equations and systems](lesson:sat-linear-equations-systems): solving equations, setting up equations from words, and solving systems
- [Linear functions and inequalities](lesson:sat-linear-functions-inequalities): lines, slope, models in context and inequalities

## If you study the Vietnamese curriculum

If you study the Vietnamese curriculum, you are not starting from zero. Most of SAT Math is covered by grades 8 to 10 in Vietnam, but the emphasis is different. The table shows what you already know, what you need to practise and what is probably new to you.

| You already know | You need to practice | Probably new to you |
|---|---|---|
| Solving linear and quadratic equations, systems, Viète's theorem | Reading word problems in English and writing the equation yourself | Interpreting what a number in a formula means in context |
| Graphs of $y=ax+b$ and $y=ax^2$ | The forms $y=mx+b$ and $y=a(x-h)^2+k$, slope as a rate of change | Exponential growth and decay models, such as $P=200(1.05)^t$ |
| Trigonometric ratios in right triangles, circle geometry | Circle equations $(x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2$, radians, arc length | Margin of error, random sampling and what a study can conclude |
| Mean, median, frequency tables | Reading scatterplots, lines of best fit and two-way tables | Units such as feet, miles, pounds and gallons |

Notation differs too, and it catches many students at first. The SAT writes decimals with a point ($2.5$, not $2{,}5$), uses a comma to separate thousands ($12{,}000$), and writes slope as $m$ in $y=mx+b$. Get used to it early and it will not trip you up in the test.

## Three sample questions

Before you start revising, try a few questions to get a feel for the test. These three questions are written by Lemonmath in the style of the test, one from each of three domains. Try them yourself before reading the solutions.

:::example Algebra, multiple choice
If $3x+7=22$, what is the value of $6x+5$?
A. $15$
B. $30$
C. $35$
D. $40$

**Solution.** The question asks for $6x+5$, not for $x$. From $3x+7=22$ we get $3x=15$, so $6x=30$ and $6x+5=35$. The answer is **C**. Option B stops at $6x$; option A is $3x$.
:::

:::example Advanced Math, student-produced response
The function $f$ is defined by $f(x)=x^2-6x+11$. What is the minimum value of $f(x)$?

**Solution.** Complete the square: $f(x)=(x-3)^2+2$. A square is never negative, so the minimum value is $2$, reached when $x=3$. You type **2**. In Desmos, typing the function and clicking the lowest point of the parabola shows $(3, 2)$.
:::

:::example Problem-Solving and Data Analysis, multiple choice
The price of a jacket was 80 dollars. The price was increased by 25%, and the new price was later decreased by 25%. What is the final price, in dollars?
A. $70$
B. $75$
C. $80$
D. $85$

**Solution.** After the increase: $80\times1.25=100$. After the decrease: $100\times0.75=75$. The answer is **B**. Option C is the trap: the two changes are percentages of different amounts, so they do not cancel.
:::

## A 10-week study plan

| Weeks | Focus | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Diagnose | Take one full official practice test in Bluebook under real timing. Sort every mistake by domain. |
| 2–3 | Algebra | The two Algebra lessons, then mixed sets of 20 questions. |
| 4–5 | Advanced Math | Expressions, quadratics, exponentials. Learn to check answers with Desmos. |
| 6 | Problem-Solving and Data Analysis | Percentages, rates, units, statistics vocabulary. |
| 7 | Geometry and Trigonometry | Formulas, circles, right triangles, radians. |
| 8 | Second practice test | Full test, then two days only on your error log. |
| 9 | Weak spots | Mixed timed sets from your two weakest domains. |
| 10 | Final practice | One or two full tests; review, sleep well, do not learn new topics. |

A plan only works if the way you study is sound too. Throughout, use the methods from [Revision that works](lesson:evidence-based-revision): test yourself instead of rereading, come back to old topics every week, and keep an error log. Your error log shows you what to revise next.

:::note Free official resources
Two official resources are free and well worth using. College Board publishes full-length digital practice tests inside the Bluebook app, and Khan Academy offers free Official Digital SAT Prep made with College Board. Use these for full tests; use Lemonmath lessons to learn and drill each topic. Test dates, fees and registration change every year, so check the College Board website before you register.
:::

## What to do next

1. Install Bluebook and take the first practice test this week, before studying anything. The result shows you where you stand.
2. Start the Algebra lessons and keep an error log from day one.
3. Spend ten minutes exploring Desmos: graph a line, a parabola and find where they cross.

So what does SAT Math test, and how do you prepare? The test has a clear structure, and so does the plan. Remember to take a real practice test early, to record your mistakes, and to return to old topics every week. The first item on the list is all you need to begin.
