- Split a quantity in a given ratio and solve proportions, including map scales.

- Solve multi-step problems with density, fuel use and mixtures.

Have you ever read a map to work out how far away a place is, or worked out what a jacket costs in a sale? That is ratios, rates and percentages at work. Problem-Solving and Data Analysis has about 5 to 7 questions on the test, and roughly half of them are about ratios, rates, percentages and units. These are the most "everyday" questions on the SAT: recipes, maps, prices, discounts and speeds. The maths is from grades 6 and 7, but careless reading costs many points. So the habit to build here is to write every quantity **with its unit**.

## Ratios and proportions

When a total is shared out in a ratio, the first job is to count the parts. A ratio $a:b$ compares two quantities. If a total is split in the ratio $a:b$, the total is made of $a+b$ equal parts. After that, the only question left is how big one part is.

:::example Splitting in a ratio
In a class of 36 students, the ratio of boys to girls is $4:5$. How many girls are in the class?
A. $16$
B. $20$
C. $9$
D. $25$

**Solution.** There are $4+5=9$ equal parts, so each part is $36\div9=4$ students. Girls: $5\cdot4=20$. The answer is **B**. Option A is the number of boys; option C is the number of parts.
:::

A **proportion** says that two ratios are equal: $\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{c}{d}$. If you know three of the numbers, you can find the fourth. Why? Because cross-multiplication gives $ad=bc$, which is an ordinary equation. A map scale is the most familiar example.

:::example A map scale
On a map, 1 centimeter represents 2.5 kilometers. Two towns are 7.2 centimeters apart on the map. What is the actual distance between the towns, in kilometers?

**Solution.** $\dfrac{1}{2.5}=\dfrac{7.2}{d}$, so $d=7.2\cdot2.5=18$. You type **18**.
:::

## Rates and unit conversion

Sometimes you are given a speed in one unit, but the question asks for another. A rate is a ratio with units, such as kilometres per hour or dollars per kilogram. To convert units, multiply by fractions equal to $1$, such as $\dfrac{5{,}280\text{ feet}}{1\text{ mile}}$. Multiplying by $1$ changes the units but not the value. Arrange the fractions so that the unwanted units cancel. The SAT always gives the conversion factors for US units.

:::example Converting a speed
A car travels at 60 miles per hour. What is its speed in feet per second? (1 mile = 5,280 feet)

**Solution.** Cancel miles and hours:

$$\frac{60\text{ miles}}{1\text{ hour}}\cdot\frac{5{,}280\text{ feet}}{1\text{ mile}}\cdot\frac{1\text{ hour}}{3{,}600\text{ seconds}}=\frac{316{,}800}{3{,}600}=88\text{ feet per second}.$$

You type **88**.
:::

## Percentages

:::theorem Three percent tools
- $p\%$ of $N$ is $\dfrac{p}{100}\cdot N$.
- Percent change $=\dfrac{\text{new}-\text{original}}{\text{original}}\cdot100\%$.
- An increase of $p\%$ multiplies by $1+\dfrac{p}{100}$; a decrease of $p\%$ multiplies by $1-\dfrac{p}{100}$.
:::

:::example Percent change
The price of a book rose from 80 dollars to 92 dollars. By what percent did the price increase?
A. $12\%$
B. $13\%$
C. $15\%$
D. $87\%$

**Solution.** $\dfrac{92-80}{80}=\dfrac{12}{80}=0.15$, an increase of $15\%$. The answer is **C**. Option A uses the change in dollars as a percent; option B divides by $92$.
:::

Many students slip here: the price has gone down, so surely you add the percent back? Not quite. The new price is the original multiplied by a factor, so to get back you undo that multiplication. To find an original value after a percent change, **divide** by the multiplier. Never subtract the percent from the new price.

:::example Reverse percentage
After a 20% discount, a jacket costs 64 dollars. What was the price before the discount, in dollars?
A. $76.80$
B. $80$
C. $84$
D. $77$

**Solution.** The sale price is $0.8$ of the original: $0.8P=64$, so $P=80$. The answer is **B**. Option A adds 20% of the sale price, which is the common trap: $64\cdot1.2=76.8$.
:::

:::example A percent of a percent
At a school, 30% of the students are in a sports club, and 40% of the club members are girls. What percent of all the students are girls in the sports club?

**Solution.** $0.4\cdot0.3=0.12$, so $12\%$. You type **12**.
:::

:::warning Common traps
- **Ratio of parts versus ratio to the whole.** In $4:5$, boys are $\dfrac49$ of the class, not $\dfrac45$.
- **Dividing by the wrong number in a percent change.** Always divide by the original value.
- **Undoing a percent by the same percent.** A 20% decrease followed by a 20% increase does not return to the start: $0.8\cdot1.2=0.96$.
- **Dropping units.** Write units at each step; they show whether to multiply or divide.
These are the places where questions like to set traps. Read slowly and you will keep clear of them.
:::

:::tip Fast percent checks
Here is a quick way to check whether an answer is sensible. $10\%$ of a number is easy (move the decimal point), and $5\%$ is half of that. So $15\%$ of $80$ is $8+4=12$. Estimate like this before you calculate properly.
:::

## Similar exercises

:::exercise Student-produced response
A sum of 150 dollars is shared between two people in the ratio $3:7$. How many dollars does the person with the larger share receive?

::hint
**What you need:** the total is made of $3+7$ equal parts.

**Steps:**
1. One part is $150\div10$.
2. Multiply by $7$.

::solution
Let the two shares be $3k$ dollars and $7k$ dollars, with $k>0$ (dollars per part), since the money is shared in the ratio $3:7$.

The total is $150$ dollars, so
$$3k+7k=150\iff10k=150\iff k=15.$$

Hence one part is $15$ dollars. The person with the larger share receives $7$ parts, that is,
$$7\cdot15=105\ \text{(dollars)}.$$

Check: the smaller share is $3\cdot15=45$ dollars and $45+105=150$ dollars (this agrees with the problem).

So the person with the larger share receives $105$ dollars.

**Answer.** $105$ dollars.
:::

:::exercise Student-produced response
Linh types at a rate of 45 words per minute. How many minutes does she need to type a 1,800-word essay?

::hint
**What you need:** time = amount ÷ rate.

**Steps:**
1. Divide $1{,}800$ by $45$.

::solution
Let $t$ (minutes) be the time Linh needs to type the essay ($t>0$).

Linh types $45$ words per minute and the essay has $1{,}800$ words, so the time equals the number of words divided by the typing rate:
$$t=\frac{1{,}800}{45}=40.$$

Check: $45\cdot40=1{,}800$ words (correct).

So Linh needs $40$ minutes to type the essay.

**Answer.** $40$ minutes.
:::

:::exercise Student-produced response
One inch is equal to 2.54 centimeters. What is the length, in centimeters, of a 30-inch screen diagonal?

::hint
**What you need:** multiply by the conversion factor $\dfrac{2.54\text{ cm}}{1\text{ inch}}$.

**Steps:**
1. $30\cdot2.54$.

::solution
One inch equals $2.54$ centimeters, so a length in centimeters equals the number of inches multiplied by $2.54$.

The screen diagonal is $30$ inches long, hence
$$30\cdot2.54=76.2\ \text{(centimeters)}.$$

So the screen diagonal is $76.2$ centimeters long.

**Answer.** $76.2$ centimeters.
:::

:::exercise Multiple choice
The number of visitors to a museum fell from 250 on Monday to 200 on Tuesday. By what percent did the number of visitors decrease?
A. $20\%$
B. $25\%$
C. $50\%$
D. $80\%$

::hint
**What you need:** percent change, divided by the original value.

**Steps:**
1. The change is $50$.
2. Divide by $250$.

::solution
The number of visitors fell from $250$ to $200$, so the decrease is
$$250-200=50\ \text{(visitors)}.$$

The percent decrease is measured against the original number of visitors (Monday), which is $250$:
$$\frac{50}{250}=0.2=20\%.$$

So the number of visitors decreased by $20\%$, and the answer is **A**.

*Remark.* Option B divides by $200$ (the later number) instead of the original number; option D is the ratio $\dfrac{200}{250}$, which is the percent remaining, not the percent decrease.

**Answer.** A.
:::

:::exercise Student-produced response
A total of 54 dollars includes an 8% sales tax. What was the price before tax, in dollars?

::hint
**What you need:** the total is $1.08$ times the price.

**Steps:**
1. Write $1.08P=54$.
2. Divide.

::solution
Let $P$ (dollars) be the price before tax ($P>0$).

The sales tax is $8\%$ of $P$, so the total amount paid is
$$P+0.08P=1.08P.$$

The total is $54$ dollars, so $1.08P=54$. It follows that
$$P=\frac{54}{1.08}=50.$$

Check: the tax is $8\%\cdot50=4$ dollars and $50+4=54$ dollars (correct).

So the price before tax was $50$ dollars.

**Answer.** $50$ dollars.
:::

## Application exercises

:::exercise Density
The density of gold is 19.3 grams per cubic centimeter. A gold block measures 2 centimeters by 3 centimeters by 5 centimeters. What is its mass, in grams?

::hint
**What you need:** mass = density × volume.

**Steps:**
1. Find the volume of the block.
2. Multiply by $19.3$.

::solution
The gold block is a rectangular box, so its volume is the product of its three dimensions:
$$V=2\cdot3\cdot5=30\ \text{(cubic centimeters)}.$$

Mass equals density times volume. The density of gold is $19.3$ grams per cubic centimeter, so
$$m=30\cdot19.3=579\ \text{(grams)}.$$

So the gold block has a mass of $579$ grams.

**Answer.** $579$ grams.
:::

:::exercise Fuel for a trip
A car uses 6.5 liters of fuel per 100 kilometers. Fuel costs 1.2 dollars per liter. How much does the fuel cost for a 340-kilometer trip?

::hint
**What you need:** two rates in a chain: liters per kilometer, then dollars per liter.

**Steps:**
1. Liters: $340\cdot\dfrac{6.5}{100}$.
2. Multiply by $1.2$.

::solution
The car uses $6.5$ liters of fuel per $100$ kilometers, that is, $\dfrac{6.5}{100}=0.065$ liters per kilometer.

For a trip of $340$ kilometers, the amount of fuel used is
$$340\cdot0.065=22.1\ \text{(liters)}.$$

Fuel costs $1.2$ dollars per liter, so the cost of the fuel is
$$22.1\cdot1.2=26.52\ \text{(dollars)}.$$

So the fuel for the $340$-kilometer trip costs $26.52$ dollars.

**Answer.** $26.52$ dollars.
:::

:::exercise Up and down
A town's population increased by 25% in one decade and then decreased by 20% in the next decade. What was the overall percent change over the two decades?

::hint
**What you need:** multiply the two multipliers.

**Steps:**
1. $1.25\cdot0.8$.
2. Compare with $1$.

::solution
Let $N$ be the town's original population ($N>0$).

After the first decade the population increases by $25\%$, so it becomes $N\cdot1.25$.

After the next decade it decreases by $20\%$, so $80\%$ of that population remains:
$$N\cdot1.25\cdot0.8=N\cdot1=N.$$

So the population after two decades is exactly the original population, which means a change of $0\%$.

Check with an example: $1{,}000\to1{,}000\cdot1.25=1{,}250\to1{,}250\cdot0.8=1{,}000$.

So the overall percent change over the two decades is $0\%$.

**Answer.** $0\%$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A container holds 40 liters of a salt solution that is 30% salt. How many liters of pure water must be added to make a solution that is 20% salt?

::hint
**What you need:** adding water does not change the amount of salt.

**Steps:**
1. Salt: $0.3\cdot40=12$ liters.
2. Solve $\dfrac{12}{40+w}=0.2$.

::solution
Let $w$ (liters) be the amount of pure water to be added ($w>0$).

The amount of salt in $40$ liters of a $30\%$ solution is
$$0.3\cdot40=12\ \text{(liters)}.$$

Adding pure water does not change the amount of salt (it stays $12$ liters), while the volume of the new solution is $40+w$ (liters). The new salt concentration is $20\%$, so
$$\begin{aligned}12&=0.2(40+w)\\60&=40+w\\w&=20.\end{aligned}$$

The value $w=20>0$ satisfies the condition.

Check: the new solution has $12$ liters of salt in $40+20=60$ liters, and $\dfrac{12}{60}=0.2=20\%$ (correct).

So $20$ liters of pure water must be added.

**Answer.** $20$ liters.
:::
