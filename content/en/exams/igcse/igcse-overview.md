Imagine opening an exam paper and finding that, for the next two hours, you are not allowed a calculator. For many students this is the moment IGCSE Mathematics becomes real. Since 2025, every candidate sits one paper without a calculator and one with, so fluent arithmetic by hand matters as much as knowing which formula to use.

The good news for students from the Vietnamese curriculum is that the mathematics is familiar: fractions, equations, triangles, probability. What may be new is the style. Questions are structured in parts, marks are shown for each part, and examiners want to see your method written out clearly. This guide explains how the exam works so that your revision is aimed at the right target.

## The papers

Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics (syllabus 0580) is offered at two tiers. The current syllabus is examined in 2025, 2026 and 2027, in June and November.

| Tier | Grades | Papers | Time | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core | C–G | Paper 1 (non-calculator) and Paper 3 (calculator) | 1 hour 30 minutes each | 50% each |
| Extended | A*–E | Paper 2 (non-calculator) and Paper 4 (calculator) | 2 hours each, 100 marks each | 50% each |

From 2025, each tier has exactly **one non-calculator paper**, and a list of formulae is printed in each paper (different for Core and Extended). In the calculator paper you use a scientific calculator. The lessons on Lemonmath are written for the **Extended** tier, which leads to the highest grades.

## The nine content areas

| Area | Examples of what is tested |
|---|---|
| Number | fractions, percentages, standard form, indices, surds, bounds |
| Algebra and graphs | equations, inequalities, sequences, functions, graphs |
| Coordinate geometry | gradients, equations of lines, midpoints, perpendicular lines |
| Geometry | angles, similarity, circle theorems, constructions |
| Mensuration | perimeter, area, surface area and volume |
| Trigonometry | right-angled triangles, sine and cosine rules, 3D problems |
| Transformations and vectors | reflections, rotations, enlargements, vector geometry |
| Probability | single and combined events, tree diagrams, conditional probability |
| Statistics | averages, charts, cumulative frequency, histograms |

## Accuracy and working

Unless a question says otherwise, give non-exact answers to **three significant figures**, and angles in degrees to **one decimal place**. Keep full accuracy in the middle of a calculation and round only at the end. Show your working: marks are given for correct method even when the final answer is wrong, and a correct answer with no working may lose marks in questions that ask you to show it.

On the non-calculator paper, leave answers such as $\dfrac{2}{3}$ or $5\sqrt2$ in exact form when they cannot be simplified to a neat decimal.

## Two small examples of the style

:::example Non-calculator arithmetic
Work out $2\dfrac34\div1\dfrac56$, giving your answer as a mixed number in its simplest form.

**Solution.** Change to improper fractions: $2\dfrac34=\dfrac{11}{4}$ and $1\dfrac56=\dfrac{11}{6}$.

Dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal: $\dfrac{11}{4}\times\dfrac{6}{11}=\dfrac{6}{4}=\dfrac32$.

So the answer is $1\dfrac12$.
:::

:::example Choosing the right rounding
A student calculates the length of a side as $7.34851$ cm. How should it be written in an IGCSE answer, unless the question says otherwise?
A. $7.3$ cm
B. $7.35$ cm
C. $7.348$ cm
D. $7$ cm

**Solution.** Lengths that are not exact are given to three significant figures. The first three significant figures of $7.34851$ are $7$, $3$ and $4$, and the next digit, $8$, rounds the $4$ up.

So the answer is $7.35$ cm, answer **B**.
:::

## A revision plan

:::note Ten weeks before the exam
1. **Weeks 1–2:** [Number](lesson:igcse-number) and [Algebra and graphs](lesson:igcse-algebra-graphs). Practise fraction and index work without a calculator every day.
2. **Weeks 3–4:** coordinate geometry, geometry and circle theorems.
3. **Weeks 5–6:** mensuration and trigonometry.
4. **Weeks 7–8:** transformations, vectors, probability and statistics.
5. **Weeks 9–10:** one non-calculator paper and one calculator paper each week, timed, then an error list: for each lost mark, write whether it came from method, accuracy or reading the question.
:::

:::warning Common traps
- **Rounding too early:** keep the full value in your calculator until the last step.
- **Wrong accuracy:** three significant figures for lengths and areas; one decimal place for angles.
- **No working on the non-calculator paper:** write each step; examiners cannot give method marks for an answer alone.
- **Calculator in the wrong mode:** IGCSE uses degrees.
- **Units:** check that the answer has the units the question asks for, such as $\text{cm}^2$ or litres.
:::

Think back to that first non-calculator paper. It feels hard only when hand methods are rusty. Ten minutes of mental and written arithmetic each day, alongside the topic lessons, will make it feel ordinary by the time of the exam. Steady practice beats last-minute cramming every time.
