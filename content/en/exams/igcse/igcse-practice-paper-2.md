Nothing prepares you for an exam quite like sitting one. Put your calculator in a drawer, set a timer for two hours, and work through this paper in one sitting. You will learn more about your speed, your arithmetic and your habits than from another evening of reading notes.

The paper follows the format of Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics (0580) Extended **Paper 2**: 2 hours, **no calculator**, 100 marks, with the number of marks shown after each part. All questions are written by Lemonmath; they are not real Cambridge questions, but they follow the Extended syllabus and the style of the papers. The numbers have been chosen so that everything can be done by hand, which is exactly what the real non-calculator paper expects.

**Instructions.** Answer all questions. Show all your working: on a non-calculator paper, the working is where most of the marks are. Give answers exactly where possible (fractions, surds, $\pi$), unless the question asks for something else. Each question has a short hint below it: cover it unless you are stuck, and open the solution only when you have finished.

:::exercise [3 marks]
(a) Write $0.000507$ in standard form. [1 mark]
(b) Work out $(6\times10^5)\times(4\times10^{-2})$, giving your answer in standard form. [2 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** standard form is $a\times10^n$ with $1\le a<10$.

::solution
(a) Move the decimal point $4$ places to the right to get $5.07$, so $0.000507=5.07\times10^{-4}$.

(b) Multiply the numbers and add the powers: $6\times4=24$ and $10^5\times10^{-2}=10^3$, so the product is $24\times10^3$. Since $24$ is not between $1$ and $10$, rewrite it: $24\times10^3=2.4\times10^4$.

So the answers are $5.07\times10^{-4}$ and $2.4\times10^4$.
:::

:::exercise [3 marks]
Without using a calculator, work out $2\tfrac34\div1\tfrac56$. Give your answer as a mixed number in its simplest form. [3 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** change to improper fractions, then multiply by the reciprocal.

::solution
As improper fractions, $2\tfrac34=\dfrac{11}{4}$ and $1\tfrac56=\dfrac{11}{6}$.

Dividing is multiplying by the reciprocal: $\dfrac{11}{4}\times\dfrac{6}{11}=\dfrac{6}{4}=\dfrac32$.

So the answer is $1\tfrac12$.
:::

:::exercise [4 marks]
(a) Find the value of $27^{\frac23}$. [2 marks]
(b) Simplify $(2x^3y)^4$. [2 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** $a^{\frac mn}=\left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m$; $(ab)^n=a^nb^n$ and $(x^m)^n=x^{mn}$.

::solution
(a) Take the cube root first, then square: $\sqrt[3]{27}=3$ and $3^2=9$.

(b) Raise each factor to the power $4$: $2^4=16$, $(x^3)^4=x^{12}$ and $y^4$.

So $27^{\frac23}=9$ and $(2x^3y)^4=16x^{12}y^4$.
:::

:::exercise [4 marks]
After a $20\%$ increase, the price of a bicycle is $300$ dollars.

(a) Find the price before the increase. [2 marks]
(b) In a sale, the new price of $300$ dollars is reduced by $15\%$. Find the sale price. [2 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** a $20\%$ increase multiplies by $1.2$; a $15\%$ decrease multiplies by $0.85$.

::solution
(a) The new price is $120\%$ of the original, so the original is $300\div1.2=250$ dollars. (Check: $250\times1.2=300$.)

(b) The sale price is $85\%$ of $300$: $300\times0.85=255$ dollars.

So the original price was $250$ dollars and the sale price is $255$ dollars.
:::

:::exercise [4 marks]
(a) Expand and simplify $(2x-3)(x+5)$. [2 marks]
(b) Factorise completely $6x^2-9xy$. [2 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** multiply every term by every term; take out the highest common factor.

::solution
(a) $(2x-3)(x+5)=2x^2+10x-3x-15=2x^2+7x-15$.

(b) The highest common factor of $6x^2$ and $9xy$ is $3x$, so $6x^2-9xy=3x(2x-3y)$.

So the answers are $2x^2+7x-15$ and $3x(2x-3y)$.
:::

:::exercise [5 marks]
(a) Solve the simultaneous equations. [3 marks]
$3x+2y=16$
$5x-2y=8$
(b) Solve the inequality $5-2x<11$. [2 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** add the equations to eliminate $y$; dividing an inequality by a negative number reverses the sign.

::solution
(a) Adding the equations removes $y$: $8x=24$, so $x=3$. Substituting into the first equation, $9+2y=16$, so $y=3.5$.

(b) Subtract $5$: $-2x<6$. Divide by $-2$ and reverse the sign: $x>-3$.

So $x=3$, $y=3.5$, and $x>-3$.
:::

:::exercise [5 marks]
$A$ is the point $(-2,\ 1)$ and $B$ is the point $(4,\ 13)$.

(a) Find the gradient of $AB$. [1 mark]
(b) Find the equation of the line $AB$ in the form $y=mx+c$. [2 marks]
(c) Find the coordinates of the midpoint of $AB$ and the gradient of the perpendicular bisector of $AB$. [2 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** gradient $=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$; perpendicular gradients multiply to $-1$.

::solution
(a) The gradient is $\dfrac{13-1}{4-(-2)}=\dfrac{12}{6}=2$.

(b) $y=2x+c$ passes through $(4,\ 13)$, so $13=8+c$ and $c=5$: $y=2x+5$.

(c) The midpoint is $\left(\dfrac{-2+4}{2},\ \dfrac{1+13}{2}\right)=(1,\ 7)$. The perpendicular gradient is $-\dfrac12$, because $2\times\left(-\dfrac12\right)=-1$.

So the gradient is $2$, the line is $y=2x+5$, the midpoint is $(1,\ 7)$ and the perpendicular gradient is $-\dfrac12$.
:::

:::exercise [6 marks]
(a) Factorise $x^2-x-12$. [2 marks]
(b) Hence solve $x^2-x-12=0$. [1 mark]
(c) Write $x^2-6x+11$ in the form $(x-a)^2+b$. [2 marks]
(d) Write down the coordinates of the minimum point of $y=x^2-6x+11$. [1 mark]

::hint
**What you need:** two numbers with product $-12$ and sum $-1$; half of $-6$ is $-3$.

::solution
(a) $-4$ and $3$ have product $-12$ and sum $-1$, so $x^2-x-12=(x-4)(x+3)$.

(b) One bracket must be zero, so $x=4$ or $x=-3$.

(c) $(x-3)^2=x^2-6x+9$, so $x^2-6x+11=(x-3)^2+2$.

(d) $(x-3)^2$ is smallest, $0$, when $x=3$, so the minimum point is $(3,\ 2)$.

So the solutions are $4$ and $-3$, and the minimum point is $(3,\ 2)$.
:::

:::exercise [5 marks]
(a) $A$, $B$ and $C$ lie on a circle with centre $O$, and $C$ is on the major arc $AB$. Angle $AOB=130^\circ$. Find angle $ACB$, giving a reason. [2 marks]
(b) $PQRS$ is a cyclic quadrilateral. Angle $PQR=3x^\circ$ and angle $PSR=(x+20)^\circ$. Find $x$, giving a reason. [3 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** angle at centre $=2\times$ angle at circumference; opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to $180^\circ$.

::solution
(a) Angle $ACB$ stands on the same arc as angle $AOB$, so angle $ACB=130^\circ\div2=65^\circ$ (angle at centre $=2\times$ angle at circumference).

(b) Angles $PQR$ and $PSR$ are opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral, so $3x+x+20=180$. Then $4x=160$ and $x=40$.

So angle $ACB=65^\circ$ and $x=40$.
:::

:::exercise [5 marks]
A bag contains $4$ green counters and $6$ yellow counters. Two counters are taken at random without replacement.

(a) Find the probability that both counters are green. [2 marks]
(b) Find the probability that the counters are different colours. [3 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** on the second pick there are $9$ counters left; "different colours" has two paths.

::solution
(a) $P(\text{green, green})=\dfrac{4}{10}\times\dfrac39=\dfrac{12}{90}=\dfrac{2}{15}$.

(b) Green then yellow: $\dfrac{4}{10}\times\dfrac69=\dfrac{24}{90}$. Yellow then green: $\dfrac{6}{10}\times\dfrac49=\dfrac{24}{90}$. Adding the two paths gives $\dfrac{48}{90}=\dfrac{8}{15}$.

So the probabilities are $\dfrac{2}{15}$ and $\dfrac{8}{15}$.
:::

:::exercise [5 marks]
(a) Find an expression for the $n$th term of the sequence $7,\ 11,\ 15,\ 19,\ \dots$ [2 marks]
(b) Find an expression for the $n$th term of the sequence $2,\ 6,\ 12,\ 20,\ 30,\ \dots$ [3 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** (a) a constant difference; (b) the second differences are constant, so the $n$th term contains $n^2$.

::solution
(a) The terms go up by $4$, so the $n$th term is $4n+c$. When $n=1$, $4+c=7$, so $c=3$: the $n$th term is $4n+3$.

(b) The differences are $4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10$ and the second differences are $2$, so the sequence starts with $n^2$. Subtracting $n^2=1,\ 4,\ 9,\ 16,\ 25$ leaves $1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 5$, which is $n$. So the $n$th term is $n^2+n$.

So the $n$th terms are $4n+3$ and $n^2+n$.
:::

:::exercise [5 marks]
$f(x)=2x-1$ and $g(x)=x^2+3$.

(a) Find $fg(2)$. [2 marks]
(b) Find $f^{-1}(x)$. [2 marks]
(c) Find $gf(x)$. You do not need to simplify. [1 mark]

::hint
**What you need:** $fg(2)$ means apply $g$ first, then $f$.

::solution
(a) $g(2)=4+3=7$, then $f(7)=14-1=13$.

(b) Write $y=2x-1$ and solve for $x$: $x=\dfrac{y+1}{2}$. So $f^{-1}(x)=\dfrac{x+1}{2}$.

(c) $gf(x)=g(2x-1)=(2x-1)^2+3$.

So $fg(2)=13$, $f^{-1}(x)=\dfrac{x+1}{2}$ and $gf(x)=(2x-1)^2+3$.
:::

:::exercise [5 marks]
A curve has equation $y=x^3-6x^2+5$.

(a) Find $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$. [2 marks]
(b) Find the coordinates of the two stationary points, and determine the nature of each. [3 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** stationary points have $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=0$; a positive cubic rises, falls, then rises again.

::solution
(a) $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}=3x^2-12x$.

(b) $3x^2-12x=3x(x-4)=0$ gives $x=0$ or $x=4$. When $x=0$, $y=5$; when $x=4$, $y=64-96+5=-27$. The gradient is positive before $x=0$, negative between $0$ and $4$, and positive after $4$.

So $(0,\ 5)$ is a maximum and $(4,\ -27)$ is a minimum.
:::

:::exercise [5 marks]
$\overrightarrow{OA}=\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB}=\mathbf{b}$. $P$ is the point on $AB$ with $AP:PB=3:1$, and $M$ is the midpoint of $OB$.

(a) Find $\overrightarrow{AB}$ in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$. [1 mark]
(b) Find $\overrightarrow{OP}$ in its simplest form. [2 marks]
(c) Find $\overrightarrow{PM}$ in its simplest form. [2 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** $P$ is $\tfrac34$ of the way from $A$ to $B$.

::solution
(a) $\overrightarrow{AB}=-\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}$.

(b) $\overrightarrow{OP}=\mathbf{a}+\tfrac34(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})=\tfrac14\mathbf{a}+\tfrac34\mathbf{b}$.

(c) $\overrightarrow{OM}=\tfrac12\mathbf{b}$, so $\overrightarrow{PM}=\overrightarrow{OM}-\overrightarrow{OP}=\tfrac12\mathbf{b}-\tfrac14\mathbf{a}-\tfrac34\mathbf{b}=-\tfrac14\mathbf{a}-\tfrac14\mathbf{b}$.

So $\overrightarrow{OP}=\tfrac14\mathbf{a}+\tfrac34\mathbf{b}$ and $\overrightarrow{PM}=-\tfrac14(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})$.
:::

:::exercise [6 marks]
(a) Simplify $\dfrac{x^2-9}{x^2+5x+6}$. [3 marks]
(b) Make $r$ the subject of $V=\dfrac{\pi r^2h}{3}$, where $r>0$. [3 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** factorise top and bottom; undo the operations on $r$ in reverse order.

::solution
(a) $x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3)$ is a difference of two squares, and $x^2+5x+6=(x+2)(x+3)$. Cancelling the common factor $x+3$ gives $\dfrac{x-3}{x+2}$.

(b) Multiply by $3$: $3V=\pi r^2h$. Divide by $\pi h$: $r^2=\dfrac{3V}{\pi h}$. Take the positive square root: $r=\sqrt{\dfrac{3V}{\pi h}}$.

So the fraction simplifies to $\dfrac{x-3}{x+2}$ and $r=\sqrt{\dfrac{3V}{\pi h}}$.
:::

:::exercise [5 marks]
A rectangle has length $12$ cm and width $8$ cm, both correct to the nearest centimetre.

(a) Write down the lower bound of the length. [1 mark]
(b) Calculate the upper bound of the area. [2 marks]
(c) Calculate the lower bound of the perimeter. [2 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** to the nearest centimetre, the bounds are $0.5$ cm either side.

::solution
(a) The length is between $11.5$ cm and $12.5$ cm, so the lower bound is $11.5$ cm.

(b) The largest possible area uses both upper bounds: $12.5\times8.5=106.25\text{ cm}^2$.

(c) The smallest possible perimeter uses both lower bounds: $2(11.5+7.5)=38$ cm.

So the bounds are $11.5$ cm, $106.25\text{ cm}^2$ and $38$ cm.
:::

:::exercise [5 marks]
The masses, $m$ grams, of $40$ apples are grouped below.

| Mass $m$ | $0<m\le20$ | $20<m\le30$ | $30<m\le60$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | $8$ | $20$ | $12$ |

(a) Calculate the frequency density of each class. [2 marks]
(b) Calculate an estimate of the mean mass. [3 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** frequency density $=\dfrac{\text{frequency}}{\text{class width}}$; use midpoints for the mean.

::solution
(a) The class widths are $20$, $10$ and $30$, so the densities are $\dfrac{8}{20}=0.4$, $\dfrac{20}{10}=2$ and $\dfrac{12}{30}=0.4$.

(b) The midpoints are $10$, $25$ and $45$. Then $8\times10+20\times25+12\times45=80+500+540=1120$, and the estimate of the mean is $\dfrac{1120}{40}=28$ g.

So the densities are $0.4$, $2$, $0.4$ and the estimated mean is $28$ g.
:::

:::exercise [5 marks]
(a) A triangle has sides $8$ cm and $10$ cm with an angle of $30^\circ$ between them. Find its area. [2 marks]
(b) Another triangle has sides $5$ cm and $8$ cm with an angle of $60^\circ$ between them. Find the length of the third side. [3 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** $\sin30^\circ=\tfrac12$ and $\cos60^\circ=\tfrac12$.

::solution
(a) Area $=\tfrac12\times8\times10\times\sin30^\circ=40\times\tfrac12=20\text{ cm}^2$.

(b) By the cosine rule, $c^2=5^2+8^2-2\times5\times8\times\cos60^\circ=25+64-40=49$, so $c=7$ cm.

So the area is $20\text{ cm}^2$ and the third side is $7$ cm.
:::

:::exercise [4 marks]
Two cones are mathematically similar. Their heights are $6$ cm and $9$ cm.

(a) The volume of the smaller cone is $80\text{ cm}^3$. Find the volume of the larger cone. [2 marks]
(b) The surface area of the larger cone is $180\text{ cm}^2$. Find the surface area of the smaller cone. [2 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** the length scale factor is $\tfrac32$; volumes use $k^3$, areas use $k^2$.

::solution
(a) $k=\dfrac96=\dfrac32$, so the volume is $80\times\left(\dfrac32\right)^3=80\times\dfrac{27}{8}=270\text{ cm}^3$.

(b) Going from large to small, the area is multiplied by $\left(\dfrac23\right)^2=\dfrac49$: $180\times\dfrac49=80\text{ cm}^2$.

So the larger volume is $270\text{ cm}^3$ and the smaller surface area is $80\text{ cm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise [4 marks]
$y$ is inversely proportional to the square of $x$. When $x=3$, $y=8$.

(a) Find a formula for $y$ in terms of $x$. [2 marks]
(b) Find $y$ when $x=6$. [1 mark]
(c) Find the positive value of $x$ when $y=18$. [1 mark]

::hint
**What you need:** $y=\dfrac{k}{x^2}$.

::solution
(a) $8=\dfrac{k}{9}$, so $k=72$ and $y=\dfrac{72}{x^2}$.

(b) $y=\dfrac{72}{36}=2$.

(c) $18=\dfrac{72}{x^2}$ gives $x^2=4$, so $x=2$.

So $y=\dfrac{72}{x^2}$, $y=2$ and $x=2$.
:::

:::exercise [7 marks]
A cyclist starts from rest and accelerates uniformly to $12$ m/s in $8$ seconds. She travels at $12$ m/s for $20$ seconds, then decelerates uniformly to rest in $4$ seconds.

(a) Find her acceleration in the first $8$ seconds. [1 mark]
(b) Find her deceleration in the last $4$ seconds. [1 mark]
(c) Find the total distance travelled. [3 marks]
(d) Find her average speed for the whole journey. [2 marks]

::hint
**What you need:** on a speed–time graph, the gradient is the acceleration and the area is the distance.

::solution
(a) Acceleration $=\dfrac{12}{8}=1.5\text{ m/s}^2$.

(b) Deceleration $=\dfrac{12}{4}=3\text{ m/s}^2$.

(c) The area under the graph is a triangle, a rectangle and a triangle: $\tfrac12\times8\times12+20\times12+\tfrac12\times4\times12=48+240+24=312$ m.

(d) The journey lasts $8+20+4=32$ seconds, so the average speed is $\dfrac{312}{32}=9.75$ m/s.

So the answers are $1.5\text{ m/s}^2$, $3\text{ m/s}^2$, $312$ m and $9.75$ m/s.
:::
