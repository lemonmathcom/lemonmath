Suppose 14 shoes are put into pairs, 2 shoes in each pair. You want to know how many pairs of shoes there are. In the next room, the long hand of a clock has passed 35 minutes, and you want to know which number it points to.

Is there a quick way to answer both questions with the times tables you have already learned?

## Division facts for 2

Remember the lesson on division: if $2\times7=14$, then $14\div2=7$. To work out $14\div2$, find the number that multiplied by 2 gives 14. You can also count on in 2s from 0 to 14, and count how many times you counted.

| From $2\div2$ to $10\div2$ | From $12\div2$ to $20\div2$ |
|---|---|

| 10 ÷ 2 = 5 | 20 ÷ 2 = 10 |

:::definition Division facts for 2
In the division facts for 2, the divisor is always 2. When the dividend goes up by **2**, the quotient goes up by **1**.
:::

Why is this so? When the dividend goes up by 2, there is one more pair, so the number of pairs goes up by 1. The division facts for 2 are just the 2 times table read backwards: $2\times7=14$, so $14\div2=7$.

:::example The number of pairs of shoes
14 shoes are put into pairs, 2 shoes in each pair. How many pairs of shoes are there?

**Solution.** We have $2\times7=14$, so $14\div2=7$. So there are 7 pairs of shoes.
:::

:::example Mental calculation with the facts for 2
Work out in your head: a) $8\div2$; b) $20\div2$.

**Solution.** Use the 2 times table:
$$2\times4=8\ \text{so}\ 8\div2=4,\qquad 2\times10=20\ \text{so}\ 20\div2=10.$$
So a) $4$; b) $10$.
:::

:::example Sharing cakes into boxes (Apply it)
Suppose 16 cakes are shared equally between 2 boxes. How many cakes are in each box?

**Understand.** We know there are 16 cakes shared equally between 2 boxes. We are asked for the number of cakes in each box.

**Plan.** Sharing equally means division, so we do $16\div2$, using the 2 times table.

**Solve.**

Solution

The number of cakes in each box is:
$$16\div2=8\ \text{(cakes)}.$$
Answer: 8 cakes.

**Look back.** Check with multiplication: $2\times8=16$, which is correct. What if 18 cakes were shared equally between 2 boxes? How many cakes would each box hold? Since $2\times9=18$, we get $18\div2=9$, which is 9 cakes.
:::

| From $5\div5$ to $25\div5$ | From $30\div5$ to $50\div5$ |
|---|---|
| 5 ÷ 5 = 1 | 30 ÷ 5 = 6 |
| 10 ÷ 5 = 2 | 35 ÷ 5 = 7 |
| 15 ÷ 5 = 3 | 40 ÷ 5 = 8 |
| 20 ÷ 5 = 4 | 45 ÷ 5 = 9 |
| 25 ÷ 5 = 5 | 50 ÷ 5 = 10 |

:::definition Division facts for 5
In the division facts for 5, the divisor is always 5. When the dividend goes up by **5**, the quotient goes up by **1**. The dividends all end in 0 or 5.
:::

**Connection.** In the lesson on hours and minutes, you learned that each number on a clock face is 5 minutes after the one before. So when you know how many minutes have passed, you divide by 5 to find which number the long hand points to. This is the 5 times table read backwards.

:::example Which number does the long hand point to?
The long hand of a clock has passed 35 minutes. Which number does the long hand point to?

**Solution.** Each number is 5 minutes after the one before, so we work out $35\div5$. Since $5\times7=35$:
$$35\div5=7.$$
So the long hand points to 7.
:::

:::example Putting apples into baskets
Suppose 45 apples are put into baskets, 5 apples in each basket. How many baskets are filled?

**Solution.** We divide the number of apples by the number of apples in each basket. Since $5\times9=45$:
$$45\div5=9.$$
So 9 baskets of apples are filled.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Mixing up division and subtraction.** Wrong: $14\div2=12$ because $14-2=12$. Why it is wrong: putting 14 shoes into pairs of 2 gives 7 pairs. Right: $14\div2=7$.
- **Using the wrong product from the times table.** Wrong: $35\div5=6$ because $5\times6=30$. Why it is wrong: $30$ is not $35$. Right: $5\times7=35$, so $35\div5=7$.
- **Forgetting the unit in a word problem.** Wrong: "Answer: 8". Right: "Answer: 8 cakes".
:::

## Summary

- Division facts for 2: $2\div2=1$ up to $20\div2=10$. When the dividend goes up by 2, the quotient goes up by 1.
- Division facts for 5: $5\div5=1$ up to $50\div5=10$. When the dividend goes up by 5, the quotient goes up by 1.
- The division facts are the times tables read backwards: if $2\times7=14$, then $14\div2=7$.
- The number of minutes that have passed, divided by 5, is the number the long hand points to.
