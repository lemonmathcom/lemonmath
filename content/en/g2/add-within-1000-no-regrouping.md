:::goals
- Set out and add two numbers within 1,000 when no regrouping is needed, for example $235+142$.
- Add with regrouping into the tens or the hundreds, for example $156+237$.
- Add round tens and round hundreds in your head.
- Solve word problems with one addition, giving the answer with its unit.
:::

A primary school library has $235$ picture story books and $142$ science books. The librarian wants to know how many books there are altogether in these two groups. Can you work it out for her?

You can already add numbers within 100. This lesson adds one new column: the hundreds.

## Addition within 1,000 (no regrouping)

### Let's explore

Write each number as hundreds, tens and ones:

| | Hundreds | Tens | Ones |
|---|---|---|---|
| $235$ | $2$ | $3$ | $5$ |
| $142$ | $1$ | $4$ | $2$ |
| Total | $3$ | $7$ | $7$ |

We add column by column: $5+2=7$ ones, $3+4=7$ tens, $2+1=3$ hundreds. That is $3$ hundreds, $7$ tens and $7$ ones, which is $377$. So the library has $377$ books.

Now set it out in columns, with digits of the same place in the same column:

$$\begin{array}{r} 235 \\ +\ 142 \\ \hline 377 \end{array}$$

:::theorem Adding numbers within 1,000 (no regrouping)
To add two three-digit numbers when no regrouping is needed:
1. Write one number under the other so that ones are under ones, tens under tens and hundreds under hundreds.
2. Add from right to left: the ones, then the tens, then the hundreds.
3. Write the result of each column under the line.
:::

Why must the digits line up? You can only add things of the same kind. $3$ tens and $2$ ones are different kinds, like $3$ bags of sweets and $2$ single sweets: together they do not make $5$ of anything.

### Examples

We start with an addition that needs no regrouping.

:::example Work out $324+155$
**Solution.** Set it out in columns and add from right to left:
$$\begin{array}{r} 324 \\ +\ 155 \\ \hline 479 \end{array}$$
Ones: $4+5=9$. Tens: $2+5=7$. Hundreds: $3+1=4$.

So $324+155=479$.
:::

With round tens and round hundreds you can add in your head, with no columns.

:::example Work out in your head: a) $600+200$; b) $340+50$
**Solution.** a) $6$ hundreds plus $2$ hundreds is $8$ hundreds, so $600+200=800$.

b) $34$ tens plus $5$ tens is $39$ tens, so $340+50=390$.
:::

Now use addition to solve a problem from everyday life.

:::example A shop selling biscuits
A shop sold $432$ boxes of biscuits in the first week and $356$ boxes in the second week. How many boxes of biscuits did the shop sell in the two weeks altogether?

**Solution.** "In the two weeks altogether" means putting the amounts together, so we add.

The number of boxes the shop sold in the two weeks is:
$$432+356=788\ \text{(boxes)}.$$
**Answer:** $788$ boxes of biscuits.
:::

## Addition within 1,000 (with regrouping)

### Let's explore

What if a column adds up to $10$ or more? A fruit shop sold $156$ oranges in the morning and $237$ in the afternoon. How many did it sell in the whole day?

| | Hundreds | Tens | Ones |
|---|---|---|---|
| $156$ | $1$ | $5$ | $6$ |
| $237$ | $2$ | $3$ | $7$ |

Ones: $6+7=13$ ones. $13$ ones is $1$ ten and $3$ ones, so we write $3$ in the ones column and **regroup** $1$ ten into the tens column. Tens: $5+3=8$ tens, and the $1$ ten we regrouped makes $9$ tens. Hundreds: $1+2=3$ hundreds.

$$\begin{array}{r} 156 \\ +\ 237 \\ \hline 393 \end{array}$$

So the shop sold $393$ oranges in the whole day.

:::theorem Adding numbers within 1,000 (with regrouping)
To add two three-digit numbers when regrouping is needed:
1. Set out the sum as you do when there is no regrouping.
2. Add from right to left. If a column adds up to $10$ or more, write the ones digit of that total and regroup $1$ into the column on the left.
3. In the next column, add the regrouped one as well.
:::

Why regroup $1$? Because $10$ ones make $1$ ten and $10$ tens make $1$ hundred. Each column holds only the digits $0$ to $9$, as $10$ sweets must be packed into $1$ bag.

### Examples

We start with a sum that regroups only in the tens column.

:::example Work out $360+470$
**Solution.** Set it out in columns and add from right to left:
$$\begin{array}{r} 360 \\ +\ 470 \\ \hline 830 \end{array}$$
Ones: $0+0=0$. Tens: $6+7=13$, write $3$ and regroup $1$. Hundreds: $3+4+1=8$.

So $360+470=830$.
:::

Sometimes you regroup twice in a row.

:::example Work out $275+168$
**Solution.** Set it out in columns:
$$\begin{array}{r} 275 \\ +\ 168 \\ \hline 443 \end{array}$$
Ones: $5+8=13$, write $3$ and regroup $1$. Tens: $7+6+1=14$, write $4$ and regroup $1$. Hundreds: $2+1+1=4$.

So $275+168=443$.
:::

The next problem is a little harder, so we use four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example Pupils in two year groups
A primary school has $187$ pupils in Year 2 and $164$ pupils in Year 3. How many pupils are there in the two year groups altogether?

**Understand.** We know that Year 2 has $187$ pupils and Year 3 has $164$ pupils. We are asked for the number of pupils in both year groups.

**Plan.** "In both year groups" means putting the numbers together, so we add $187+164$.

**Solve.**
$$\begin{array}{r} 187 \\ +\ 164 \\ \hline 351 \end{array}$$
Ones: $7+4=11$, write $1$ and regroup $1$. Tens: $8+6+1=15$, write $5$ and regroup $1$. Hundreds: $1+1+1=3$.

**Answer:** $351$ pupils.

**Look back.** The sum $351$ is greater than each addend, so it is sensible. Swapping the addends gives $164+187=351$, the same result. Why? Try to explain it using two groups of pupils.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Digits not lined up.** Wrong: $235+42=655$. Why it is wrong: $42$ was written too far to the left, so the digit $4$ sits in the hundreds column and the number becomes $420$. Right: put $42$ under the last two digits of $235$, which gives $235+42=277$.
- **Forgetting to regroup.** Wrong: $156+237=383$. Why it is wrong: $6+7=13$, but only $3$ was written and the $1$ ten was forgotten. Right: $156+237=393$.
- **No unit in a word problem.** Wrong: "Answer: $351$". Right: "Answer: $351$ pupils".
:::

**Connection.** In the next lesson you will use the same layout for subtraction, and addition will help you check it.

## Summary

- When you set out a sum, write digits of the same place in the same column, then add from right to left: ones, tens, hundreds.
- If a column adds up to $10$ or more, write the ones digit of the total and regroup $1$ into the column on the left.
- Round tens and round hundreds can be added in your head: $6$ hundreds $+\ 2$ hundreds $=8$ hundreds.
- Questions with "altogether" or "both" usually need addition; remember to give the answer with its unit.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Set out in columns and work out: $214+365$.

::hint
**What you need:** set out the sum with digits of the same place in the same column, then add from right to left.

**Steps:**
1. Write $365$ under $214$, ones under ones.
2. Add the ones, then the tens, then the hundreds.

::solution
$$\begin{array}{r} 214 \\ +\ 365 \\ \hline 579 \end{array}$$
Ones: $4+5=9$. Tens: $1+6=7$. Hundreds: $2+3=5$.

**Answer.** $579$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out in your head: $600+300$ and $520+70$.

::hint
**What you need:** add hundreds to hundreds and tens to tens.

**Steps:**
1. How many hundreds is $600$? How many hundreds is $300$?
2. How many tens is $520$? How many tens is $70$?

::solution
$6$ hundreds plus $3$ hundreds is $9$ hundreds, so $600+300=900$.

$52$ tens plus $7$ tens is $59$ tens, so $520+70=590$.

**Answer.** $900$ and $590$.
:::

:::exercise
Set out in columns and work out: $346+278$.

::hint
**What you need:** when a column adds up to $10$ or more, write the ones digit and regroup $1$ into the column on the left.

**Steps:**
1. Set out the sum in columns.
2. Add the ones: what is $6+8$? What do you write, and what do you regroup?

::solution
$$\begin{array}{r} 346 \\ +\ 278 \\ \hline 624 \end{array}$$
Ones: $6+8=14$, write $4$ and regroup $1$. Tens: $4+7+1=12$, write $2$ and regroup $1$. Hundreds: $3+2+1=6$.

**Answer.** $624$.
:::

:::exercise
Set out in columns and work out: $485+219$.

::hint
**What you need:** a column can add up to exactly $10$: write $0$ and regroup $1$.

**Steps:**
1. Add the ones: $5+9$.
2. In the tens column, add the regrouped one too: $8+1+1$. What is this total?

::solution
$$\begin{array}{r} 485 \\ +\ 219 \\ \hline 704 \end{array}$$
Ones: $5+9=14$, write $4$ and regroup $1$. Tens: $8+1+1=10$, write $0$ and regroup $1$. Hundreds: $4+2+1=7$.

**Answer.** $704$.
:::

:::exercise
A rice shop sold $235$ kg of rice on the first day and $187$ kg of rice on the second day. How many kilograms of rice did the shop sell in the two days altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** "in the two days altogether" means putting together: use addition.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. How do we solve it? Write the sum, then set it out in columns.

::solution
The number of kilograms of rice the shop sold in the two days is:
$$235+187=422\ \text{(kg)}.$$
Ones: $5+7=12$, write $2$ and regroup $1$. Tens: $3+8+1=12$, write $2$ and regroup $1$. Hundreds: $2+1+1=4$.

**Answer.** $422$ kg of rice.
:::

:::exercise
Remember adding within 100: $58+27=85$. Use this result to work out $158+27$ and $258+27$ in your head.

::hint
**What you need:** adding $1$ hundred to one addend adds $1$ hundred to the sum.

**Steps:**
1. How many hundreds more is $158$ than $58$?
2. So how many hundreds more is $158+27$ than $58+27$?

::solution
$158$ is $1$ hundred more than $58$, so $158+27=85+100=185$.

$258$ is $2$ hundreds more than $58$, so $258+27=85+200=285$.

**Remark.** The ones and the tens work out exactly as in $58+27$; only the hundreds change.

**Answer.** $185$ and $285$.
:::

:::exercise
It is $450$ m from Nam's house to school, and $380$ m from school to the library. How many metres is it from Nam's house, past the school, to the library?

::hint
**What you need:** a journey in two parts is as long as the two parts added together.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Write the sum $450+380$, then set it out in columns.

::solution
The distance from Nam's house past the school to the library is:
$$450+380=830\ \text{(m)}.$$
Ones: $0+0=0$. Tens: $5+8=13$, write $3$ and regroup $1$. Hundreds: $4+3+1=8$.

**Answer.** $830$ m.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Fill in the missing digit in each box:
$$\begin{array}{r} \square 4 \square \\ +\ 2 \square 5 \\ \hline 612 \end{array}$$

::hint
**What you need:** work from the ones column to the left, keeping track of what you regroup in each column.

**Steps:**
1. Ones: which digit added to $5$ gives a ones digit of $2$?
2. Remember to regroup $1$, then find the tens digit, and after that the hundreds digit.

::solution
Ones: $7+5=12$, write $2$ and regroup $1$, so the missing digit is $7$.

Tens: $4+6+1=11$, write $1$ and regroup $1$, so the missing digit is $6$.

Hundreds: $3+2+1=6$, so the missing digit is $3$.

We have $347+265=612$.

**Answer.** $347+265=612$.
:::

::mc

A. $383$.

::key A
$5+8=13$, write $3$ and regroup $1$; $4+3+1=8$; $1+2=3$, so the sum is $383$. A forgets to regroup into the tens, D regroups into the hundreds by mistake, B is the subtraction $238-145$.

A. $530$.
B. $630$.
C. $63$.

::key B

::mc

A. $73$ pens.
B. $413$ pens.
C. $423$ pens.
D. $323$ pens.
::key C
"Altogether" means putting together: $248+175=423$. B forgets to regroup in the ones, D in the tens, A is the subtraction $248-175$.

::mc
Which of these additions needs regrouping?
A. $312+245$.
B. $230+405$.
C. $421+354$.
D. $156+138$.
::key D
In $156+138$ the ones column gives $6+8=14$, which is $10$ or more, so we regroup. In the other three sums every column adds up to less than $10$.

::tf
Look at the sum $358+267$.
a) When set out in columns, the digit $6$ of $267$ is under the digit $8$ of $358$.
b) In the ones column, $8+7=15$, so we write $5$ and regroup $1$.
c) The sum is $625$.
d) The sum is less than $600$.
::key F T T F
a) False: the digit $6$ is in the tens column, under the digit $5$ of $358$; the digit $7$ is the one under $8$. b) True: $8+7=15$. c) True: $5+6+1=12$, write $2$ and regroup $1$; $3+2+1=6$, so the sum is $625$. d) False: $625>600$.
