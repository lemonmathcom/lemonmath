Suppose Nam has one $5\,000$ dong note and two $2\,000$ dong notes. Nam wants to buy a pen that costs $8\,000$ dong.

How much money does Nam have altogether, and is that enough to buy the pen?

## Let's explore

Vietnamese money is called the **dong** (đồng in Vietnamese). The State Bank of Vietnam issues the money. You often see paper banknotes of $1\,000$, $2\,000$ and $5\,000$ dong, and polymer banknotes of $10\,000$, $20\,000$, $50\,000$ and $100\,000$ dong.

| Banknote | How to say it | Number of thousands of dong |
|---|---|---|
| 1 000 dong | one thousand dong | 1 thousand |
| 2 000 dong | two thousand dong | 2 thousand |
| 5 000 dong | five thousand dong | 5 thousand |
| 10 000 dong | ten thousand dong | 10 thousand |
| 20 000 dong | twenty thousand dong | 20 thousand |
| 50 000 dong | fifty thousand dong | 50 thousand |
| 100 000 dong | one hundred thousand dong | 100 thousand |

To add or subtract amounts of money, count **one thousand dong as one unit**, just like counting objects of the same kind. For example, $2\,000$ dong is 2 thousand dong and $5\,000$ dong is 5 thousand dong.

:::definition Adding and subtracting money
To add or subtract amounts of money that are whole thousands, add or subtract the numbers of thousands, then write the unit thousand dong:
$$2\ \text{thousand}+5\ \text{thousand}=7\ \text{thousand},\qquad\text{that is}\ 2\,000+5\,000=7\,000\ \text{dong}.$$
:::

Why does this work? Adding 2 thousand dong to 5 thousand dong is just like adding 2 things to 5 things: the parts of the same kind are put together to make 7 parts, each part being 1 thousand dong.

**Connection.** You have learned addition, subtraction and multiplication with small numbers. With money you use exactly the same calculations, with the unit thousand dong added.

## Examples

We return to Nam's question.

:::example How much money does Nam have
Nam has one $5\,000$ dong note and two $2\,000$ dong notes. How much money does Nam have altogether? Is that enough to buy a pen that costs $8\,000$ dong?

**Solution.** Work in thousands of dong: 5 thousand plus 2 thousand plus 2 thousand:
$$5+2+2=9\ \text{(thousand dong)}.$$
So Nam has $9\,000$ dong. Since $9\,000>8\,000$, the money is enough to buy the pen.
:::

Now change some money.

:::example Changing money
How many $5\,000$ dong notes can you change one $10\,000$ dong note for?

**Solution.** We have $5\,000+5\,000=10\,000$, so one $10\,000$ dong note can be changed for 2 notes of $5\,000$ dong.
:::

Now compare two amounts.

:::example Comparing amounts of money
Which is more money: three $2\,000$ dong notes or one $5\,000$ dong note?

**Solution.** Three $2\,000$ dong notes make:
$$2+2+2=6\ \text{(thousand dong)},$$
which is $6\,000$ dong. Since $6\,000>5\,000$, three $2\,000$ dong notes are more money than one $5\,000$ dong note.
:::

Finally, solve a shopping problem.

:::example Buying an exercise book (Apply it)
Suppose Mai buys an exercise book that costs $6\,000$ dong and gives the shopkeeper one $10\,000$ dong note. How much change should the shopkeeper give Mai?

**Understand.** We know the exercise book costs $6\,000$ dong and Mai gives $10\,000$ dong. We are asked how much change the shopkeeper gives back.

**Plan.** The change is the money Mai gives, take away the price of the exercise book, so we subtract in thousands of dong.

**Solve.**

Solution

The change the shopkeeper gives back is:
$$10-6=4\ \text{(thousand dong)}.$$
That is $4\,000$ dong.

Answer: $4\,000$ dong.

**Look back.** Check with addition: $6+4=10$, that is $6\,000+4\,000=10\,000$, which is correct.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting the thousands when adding money.** Wrong: $2\,000$ dong $+\ 5\,000$ dong $=7$ dong. Why it is wrong: 2 thousand plus 5 thousand is 7 thousand, not 7. Right: $7\,000$ dong.
- **Adding the number of notes instead of the amounts.** Wrong: three $2\,000$ dong notes make $5\,000$ dong because $3+2=5$. Why it is wrong: each note is 2 thousand dong, and three notes make $2+2+2=6$ thousand dong. Right: $6\,000$ dong.
- **Forgetting the unit in the answer.** Wrong: "Answer: $4\,000$". Right: "Answer: $4\,000$ dong".
:::

## Summary

- Vietnamese money is called the dong. The banknotes you often meet are $1\,000$, $2\,000$, $5\,000$, $10\,000$, $20\,000$, $50\,000$ and $100\,000$ dong.
- To add or subtract amounts of money that are whole thousands, add or subtract the thousands, then write the unit thousand dong.
- Change is the money given, take away the price of the goods.
- Remember the unit dong in the answer.

## Practice

Write how to say these amounts of money: $2\,000$ dong, $10\,000$ dong, $50\,000$ dong.

::hint
**What you need:** say the number of thousands, then add "thousand dong".

**Steps:**
1. How many thousands is each amount?
2. Say the number of thousands, then add "thousand dong".

::solution
$2\,000$ dong is said "two thousand dong".
$10\,000$ dong is said "ten thousand dong".
$50\,000$ dong is said "fifty thousand dong".

**Answer.** Two thousand dong; ten thousand dong; fifty thousand dong.

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $1\,000+4\,000$

b) $5\,000+5\,000$

c) $20\,000+30\,000$

d) $9\,000-3\,000$

::hint
**What you need:** add or subtract the thousands, then write the unit.

**Steps:**
1. How many thousands is each amount?
2. Add or subtract the numbers of thousands.

::solution
a) $1+4=5$, so $1\,000+4\,000=5\,000$.

b) $5+5=10$, so $5\,000+5\,000=10\,000$.

c) $20+30=50$, so $20\,000+30\,000=50\,000$.

d) $9-3=6$, so $9\,000-3\,000=6\,000$.

**Answer.** a) $5\,000$; b) $10\,000$; c) $50\,000$; d) $6\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
Lan has two $5\,000$ dong notes and one $1\,000$ dong note. How much money does Lan have altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** "altogether" means putting together: use addition.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. How do we solve it? Add the thousands on each note.
3. Check: the total must be greater than each note.

::solution
Solution

The amount of money Lan has altogether is:
$$5+5+1=11\ \text{(thousand dong)}.$$
That is $11\,000$ dong.

Answer: $11\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
a) How many $10\,000$ dong notes can you change one $20\,000$ dong note for?

b) How many $2\,000$ dong notes can you change one $10\,000$ dong note for?

::hint
**What you need:** when you change money, the total amount stays the same.

**Steps:**
1. Part a: what is $10\,000+10\,000$?
2. Part b: count on in 2 thousands up to 10 thousand.

::solution
a) $10\,000+10\,000=20\,000$, so you get 2 notes.

b) $2+2+2+2+2=10$, so $2\,000+2\,000+2\,000+2\,000+2\,000=10\,000$, and you get 5 notes.

**Answer.** a) 2 notes; b) 5 notes.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in each box:
$$2\ \text{notes of}\ 5\,000\ \text{dong}\ \square\ 10\,000\ \text{dong},$$
$$3\,000\ \text{dong}+2\,000\ \text{dong}\ \square\ 6\,000\ \text{dong},$$
$$20\,000\ \text{dong}\ \square\ 10\,000\ \text{dong}+5\,000\ \text{dong}.$$

::hint
**What you need:** work out the amount on each side, then compare.

**Steps:**
1. Change each amount into thousands of dong.
2. Compare the numbers of thousands.

::solution
$5+5=10$ thousand, so two $5\,000$ dong notes equal $10\,000$ dong.
$3+2=5$ thousand, and $5<6$.
$10+5=15$ thousand, and $20>15$.

**Answer.** $=$; $<$; $>$.
:::

Remember the lesson on multiplication. Mai has 4 notes of $5\,000$ dong. How much money does Mai have? Write it as a multiplication.

::hint

:::exercise Challenge
Mai has three banknotes: one $10\,000$ dong note, one $5\,000$ dong note and one $2\,000$ dong note. Which notes can Mai use to pay exactly $12\,000$ dong? Which notes can she use to pay exactly $15\,000$ dong?

::hint
**What you need:** find the notes whose numbers of thousands add up to the amount to be paid.

**Steps:**
1. Change $12\,000$ dong and $15\,000$ dong into thousands of dong.
2. Try putting together 10, 5 and 2 thousand dong.

::solution
We have $10+2=12$, so Mai can pay exactly $12\,000$ dong with the $10\,000$ dong note and the $2\,000$ dong note.
We have $10+5=15$, so Mai can pay exactly $15\,000$ dong with the $10\,000$ dong note and the $5\,000$ dong note.

**Answer.** $12\,000$ dong: the $10\,000$ and $2\,000$ notes; $15\,000$ dong: the $10\,000$ and $5\,000$ notes.
:::
