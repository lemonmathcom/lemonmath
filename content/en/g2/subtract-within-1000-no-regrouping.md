:::goals
- Set out and subtract two numbers within 1,000 when no regrouping is needed, for example $468-235$.
- Subtract with regrouping from the tens or the hundreds, for example $351-127$.
- Subtract round tens and round hundreds in your head.
- Solve word problems with one subtraction and check the answer with addition.
:::

A stationery shop has $468$ exercise books. After one day of selling, the shop has sold $235$ of them. The owner wants to know how many exercise books are left in the storeroom. Can you work it out for her?

You can already set out and add numbers within 1,000. In this lesson you subtract with the same layout.

## Subtraction within 1,000 (no regrouping)

### Let's explore

Write each number as hundreds, tens and ones, then subtract column by column:

| | Hundreds | Tens | Ones |
|---|---|---|---|
| $468$ | $4$ | $6$ | $8$ |
| $235$ | $2$ | $3$ | $5$ |
| Difference | $2$ | $3$ | $3$ |

We have $8-5=3$ ones, $6-3=3$ tens, $4-2=2$ hundreds. That is $2$ hundreds, $3$ tens and $3$ ones, which is $233$. So there are $233$ exercise books left in the storeroom.

Now set it out in columns, with digits of the same place in the same column:

$$\begin{array}{r} 468 \\ -\ 235 \\ \hline 233 \end{array}$$

:::theorem Subtracting numbers within 1,000 (no regrouping)
To subtract two three-digit numbers when no regrouping is needed:
1. Write the larger number on top and the number you subtract underneath, with digits of the same place in the same column.
2. Subtract from right to left: the ones, then the tens, then the hundreds.
3. Write the difference of each column under the line.
:::

Why must the digits line up? You can only subtract things of the same kind. Taking $3$ tens away from $6$ tens makes sense, but taking $3$ tens away from $8$ ones does not.

### Examples

We start with a subtraction that needs no regrouping.

:::example Work out $749-326$
**Solution.** Set it out in columns and subtract from right to left:
$$\begin{array}{r} 749 \\ -\ 326 \\ \hline 423 \end{array}$$
Ones: $9-6=3$. Tens: $4-2=2$. Hundreds: $7-3=4$.

So $749-326=423$.
:::

With round tens and round hundreds you can subtract in your head.

:::example Work out in your head: a) $800-300$; b) $570-40$
**Solution.** a) $8$ hundreds minus $3$ hundreds is $5$ hundreds, so $800-300=500$.

b) $57$ tens minus $4$ tens is $53$ tens, so $570-40=530$.
:::

Now use subtraction to solve a problem from everyday life.

:::example A shop selling biscuits
A shop has $856$ boxes of biscuits and has sold $432$ boxes. How many boxes of biscuits are left?

**Solution.** "Are left" means taking away, so we subtract.

The number of boxes of biscuits left is:
$$856-432=424\ \text{(boxes)}.$$
**Answer:** $424$ boxes of biscuits.
:::

## Subtraction within 1,000 (with regrouping)

### Let's explore

What if a digit in the top number is smaller than the digit under it? A fruit shop has $351$ oranges and has sold $127$ of them. How many oranges are left?

Ones: $1$ cannot take away $7$. Exchange $1$ ten for $10$ ones, which gives $10+1=11$ ones, and $11-7=4$. The tens column now has only $4$ tens. Tens: $4-2=2$. Hundreds: $3-1=2$.

$$\begin{array}{r} 351 \\ -\ 127 \\ \hline 224 \end{array}$$

So the shop has $224$ oranges left. Check with addition: $224+127=351$.

:::theorem Subtracting numbers within 1,000 (with regrouping)
To subtract two three-digit numbers when regrouping is needed:
1. Set out the subtraction as you do when there is no regrouping.
2. Subtract from right to left. If a digit in the top number is smaller than the digit under it, exchange $1$ from the column on the left for $10$ in this column, then subtract.
3. Remember that the column on the left now has $1$ less.
:::

Why can we exchange? Because $1$ ten is $10$ ones and $1$ hundred is $10$ tens. The total does not change; only the way we count it does, like swapping one large note for several small ones.

### Examples

We start with a subtraction that exchanges from the hundreds.

:::example Work out $463-281$
**Solution.** Set it out in columns:
$$\begin{array}{r} 463 \\ -\ 281 \\ \hline 182 \end{array}$$
Ones: $3-1=2$. Tens: $6$ cannot take away $8$; exchange $1$ hundred for $10$ tens, so $16-8=8$. Hundreds: $3$ are left, so $3-2=1$.

So $463-281=182$.
:::

Sometimes you regroup twice in a row.

:::example Work out $534-278$
**Solution.** Set it out in columns:
$$\begin{array}{r} 534 \\ -\ 278 \\ \hline 256 \end{array}$$
Ones: $4$ cannot take away $8$, so $14-8=6$ and $2$ tens are left. Tens: $2$ cannot take away $7$, so $12-7=5$ and $4$ hundreds are left. Hundreds: $4-2=2$.

So $534-278=256$.
:::

The next problem is a little harder, so we use four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example Number of girls
A primary school has $421$ pupils, of whom $187$ are boys. How many girls are there?

**Understand.** We know there are $421$ pupils in the school and $187$ of them are boys. We are asked for the number of girls.

**Plan.** The girls are what is left after we take away the boys, so we subtract $421-187$.

**Solve.**
$$\begin{array}{r} 421 \\ -\ 187 \\ \hline 234 \end{array}$$
Ones: $11-7=4$ and $1$ ten is left. Tens: $11-8=3$ and $3$ hundreds are left. Hundreds: $3-1=2$.

**Answer:** $234$ girls.

**Look back.** Check with addition: $234+187=421$, exactly the number of pupils in the school. If the school had $10$ more girls, would the number of boys change?
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Subtracting the smaller digit from the larger in every column.** Wrong: $351-127=236$. Why it is wrong: in the ones column $7-1=6$ was worked out, but the top digit must be the one you subtract from. Right: $351-127=224$.
- **Forgetting that the left column has $1$ less.** Wrong: $463-281=282$. Why it is wrong: $1$ hundred was exchanged for $10$ tens, but $4-2$ was still worked out in the hundreds. Right: $463-281=182$.
- **No unit in a word problem.** Wrong: "Answer: $234$". Right: "Answer: $234$ girls".
:::

**Connection.** Subtraction is the opposite of addition: if $224+127=351$ then $351-127=224$. You can use addition to check any subtraction.

## Summary

- When you set out a subtraction, write digits of the same place in the same column, then subtract from right to left: ones, tens, hundreds.
- If a digit in the top number is smaller than the digit under it, exchange $1$ from the column on the left for $10$ in this column, and remember that column now has $1$ less.
- Questions with "are left" or "take away" usually need subtraction; remember to give the answer with its unit.
- Check a subtraction with addition: the difference plus the number subtracted gives the number you started with.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Set out in columns and work out: $786-354$.

::hint
**What you need:** set out in columns, then subtract from right to left.

**Steps:**
1. Write $354$ under $786$, ones under ones.
2. Subtract the ones, then the tens, then the hundreds.

::solution
$$\begin{array}{r} 786 \\ -\ 354 \\ \hline 432 \end{array}$$
Ones: $6-4=2$. Tens: $8-5=3$. Hundreds: $7-3=4$.

**Answer.** $432$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out in your head: $900-400$ and $640-30$.

::hint
**What you need:** subtract hundreds from hundreds and tens from tens.

**Steps:**
1. How many hundreds is $900$? How many hundreds is $400$?
2. How many tens is $640$? How many tens is $30$?

::solution
$9$ hundreds minus $4$ hundreds is $5$ hundreds, so $900-400=500$.

$64$ tens minus $3$ tens is $61$ tens, so $640-30=610$.

**Answer.** $500$ and $610$.
:::

:::exercise
Set out in columns and work out: $541-217$.

::hint
**What you need:** if a digit in the top number is smaller than the digit under it, exchange $1$ from the column on the left for $10$ in this column.

**Steps:**
1. Ones: $1$ cannot take away $7$, so work out $11-7$.
2. How many tens are left now?

::solution
$$\begin{array}{r} 541 \\ -\ 217 \\ \hline 324 \end{array}$$
Ones: $11-7=4$ and $3$ tens are left. Tens: $3-1=2$. Hundreds: $5-2=3$.

**Answer.** $324$.

Set out in columns and work out: $625-387$.

::hint
**What you need:** you may have to regroup twice in a row.

**Steps:**
1. Ones: work out $15-7$; $1$ ten is left.
2. Tens: $1$ cannot take away $8$, so work out $11-8$.

::solution
$$\begin{array}{r} 625 \\ -\ 387 \\ \hline 238 \end{array}$$
Ones: $15-7=8$ and $1$ ten is left. Tens: $11-8=3$ and $5$ hundreds are left. Hundreds: $5-3=2$.

:::exercise
A shop has $735$ kg of rice and sold $258$ kg on the first day. How many kilograms of rice are left?

::hint
**What you need:** "are left" means taking away: use subtraction.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Write the subtraction $735-258$, set it out, then check with addition.

::solution
The number of kilograms of rice left is:
$$735-258=477\ \text{(kg)}.$$
Ones: $15-8=7$ and $2$ tens are left. Tens: $12-5=7$ and $6$ hundreds are left. Hundreds: $6-2=4$.

**Answer.** $477$ kg of rice.
:::

:::exercise
Nam works out $612-345=277$. Check with addition to see whether Nam is right, and correct the answer if it is wrong.

::hint
**What you need:** the difference plus the number subtracted must equal the number you started with.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $277+345$.
2. Compare the result with $612$.

::solution
We have $277+345=622$, and $622\neq 612$, so Nam is wrong.

Doing it again: ones $12-5=7$ and $0$ tens are left; tens $10-4=6$ and $5$ hundreds are left; hundreds $5-3=2$. So $612-345=267$.

Check: $267+345=612$. Correct.

**Answer.** Nam is wrong; the correct result is $267$.
:::

:::exercise
Uncle An's water tank holds $650$ litres. After a week the tank has used $275$ litres. How many litres of water are left in the tank?

::hint
**What you need:** the water used is taken away from the water at the start.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Set out $650-275$: the ones digit is $0$, so exchange from the tens.

::solution
The number of litres of water left in the tank is:
$$650-275=375\ \text{(litres)}.$$
Ones: $10-5=5$ and $4$ tens are left. Tens: $14-7=7$ and $5$ hundreds are left. Hundreds: $5-2=3$.

**Answer.** $375$ litres of water.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Work out quickly: $400-138$. Hint: write $400=399+1$.

::hint
**What you need:** the number $399$ needs no exchanging when you subtract.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $399-138$.
2. Add $1$ to get the result of $400-138$.

::solution
We have $400-138=(399-138)+1$.

$$399-138=261.$$
So $400-138=261+1=262$.

Check: $262+138=400$. Correct.

**Answer.** $262$.
:::
