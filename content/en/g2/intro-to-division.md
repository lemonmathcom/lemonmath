:::goals
- Understand that division means sharing a quantity equally into equal parts or equal groups.
- Write and read a division with the division sign.
- Name the dividend, the divisor and the quotient correctly.
- Use the times tables to find a quotient and solve word problems.
:::

Suppose Mum has 6 oranges and shares them equally between 2 people. You wonder how many oranges each person gets. Mum also tries another way: she puts the 6 oranges on plates, 2 oranges on each plate, to see how many plates she can fill.

Which numbers do these two ways of sharing find, and which calculation do we write?

## Division

Share the 6 oranges equally between 2 people: each time, give 1 orange to each person until they are all gone. Each person gets 3 oranges.

:::graph 6 oranges shared equally between 2 people, 3 oranges each
alt: Two rows of round dots, each row with three dots, standing for two people who each receive three oranges
x: 0..4
y: 0..3
point: (1, 1)

We write the division $6\div2=3$ and read it as "6 divided by 2 equals 3".

If you put the 6 oranges on plates, 2 oranges on each plate, you fill 3 plates. Notice that this is also $6\div2=3$.

:::definition Division
**Division** is used to share a quantity equally into equal parts, or into equal groups. The sign $\div$ is the **division sign**. The division $6\div2=3$ is read "6 divided by 2 equals 3".
:::

Why does division connect to multiplication? If each person gets 3 oranges and there are 2 people, the total number of oranges is $2\times3=6$. From this multiplication we get two divisions:
$$6\div2=3,\qquad 6\div3=2.$$

:::example Sharing oranges
Mum shares 6 oranges equally between 2 people. How many oranges does each person get?

**Solution.** We remember $2\times3=6$, so $6\div2=3$. So each person gets 3 oranges.
:::

:::example Using the times table to divide
Work out $10\div5$.

**Solution.** Look in the 5 times table for the number that multiplied by 5 gives 10. We have $5\times2=10$, so:
$$10\div5=2.$$
So $10\div5=2$.
:::

:::example Sharing pens into pencil cases (Apply it)
Suppose 12 pens are shared equally between 2 pencil cases. How many pens are in each pencil case?

**Understand.** We know there are 12 pens, shared equally between 2 pencil cases. We are asked for the number of pens in each pencil case.

**Plan.** Sharing equally means division, so we do $12\div2$. We use the 2 times table: $2\times6=12$.

**Solve.**

Solution

The number of pens in each pencil case is:
$$12\div2=6\ \text{(pens)}.$$
Answer: 6 pens.

**Look back.** Check with multiplication: $2\times6=12$, which is correct. What if the 12 pens were shared equally between 6 pencil cases? How many pens would each one hold? Since $6\times2=12$, we get $12\div6=2$, which is 2 pens.
:::

## Dividend, divisor and quotient

In the division $6\div2=3$, each number has a name.

:::definition Dividend, divisor and quotient
In the division $6\div2=3$:
- $6$ is the **dividend**.
- $2$ is the **divisor**.
- $3$ is the **quotient**. We also say "the quotient of 6 and 2 is 3".
:::

**Connection.** In a multiplication, the factors give the product. In a division, the product becomes the dividend, one factor becomes the divisor, and the other factor is the quotient:
$$2\times3=6,\qquad 6\div2=3,\qquad 6\div3=2.$$

:::example Naming the numbers in a division
Name the numbers in the division $15\div5=3$.

**Solution.** $15$ is the dividend, $5$ is the divisor and $3$ is the quotient.
:::

:::example Two divisions from one multiplication
You know that $5\times2=10$. Write the two matching divisions.

**Solution.** Divide the product 10 by each factor in turn:
$$10\div5=2,\qquad 10\div2=5.$$
So the two divisions are $10\div5=2$ and $10\div2=5$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Naming the numbers wrongly.** Wrong: in $8\div2=4$, the number $4$ is the dividend. Why it is wrong: the dividend is the number at the start, the one being shared out, which is $8$. Right: $8$ is the dividend, $2$ is the divisor and $4$ is the quotient.
- **Mixing up division and subtraction.** Wrong: $6\div2=4$ because $6-2=4$. Why it is wrong: sharing 6 equally into 2 parts gives 3 in each part, not 4. Right: $6\div2=3$.
- **Sharing unequally.** Wrong: sharing 6 oranges between 2 people so that the first gets 4 and the second gets 2. Why it is wrong: to share equally, each person must get the same number of oranges. Right: each person gets 3 oranges.
:::

## Summary

- Division means sharing a quantity equally into equal parts or equal groups. For example $6\div2=3$.
- In the division $6\div2=3$: $6$ is the dividend, $2$ is the divisor and $3$ is the quotient.
- From the multiplication $2\times3=6$ we get two divisions, $6\div2=3$ and $6\div3=2$.
- To find a quotient, use the times tables.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
8 sweets are shared equally between 2 children. How many sweets does each child get?

::hint
**What you need:** sharing equally means division, using the 2 times table.

**Steps:**
1. Which number multiplied by 2 gives 8?
2. Write the division.

::solution
We have $2\times4=8$, so $8\div2=4$.

**Answer.** Each child gets 4 sweets.
:::

:::exercise
15 apples are put on plates, 5 apples on each plate. How many plates are filled?

::hint
**What you need:** sharing into equal groups also uses division.

**Steps:**
1. Write the division of 15 by 5.
2. Use the 5 times table.

::solution
We have $5\times3=15$, so $15\div5=3$.

**Answer.** 3 plates.
:::

:::exercise
You know that $2\times4=8$. Write the two matching divisions.

::hint
**What you need:** the product becomes the dividend, and each factor in turn becomes the divisor.

**Steps:**
1. Which number is the product?
2. Divide the product by each factor.

::solution
Divide the product 8 by each factor:
$$8\div2=4,\qquad 8\div4=2.$$

**Answer.** $8\div2=4$ and $8\div4=2$.
:::

:::exercise
Name the numbers in the division $20\div5=4$.

::hint
**What you need:** the dividend comes first, the divisor comes after the division sign, and the quotient is the result.

**Steps:**
1. Which number comes first?
2. Which number is the result?

::solution
$20$ is the dividend, $5$ is the divisor and $4$ is the quotient.

**Answer.** Dividend 20; divisor 5; quotient 4.
:::

:::exercise
Work out in your head, using the times tables:

a) $10\div2$

b) $20\div5$

c) $12\div2$

d) $14\div2$

::hint
**What you need:** find the number that multiplied by the divisor gives the dividend.

**Steps:**
1. The divisor is 2 or 5: which times table do you use?
2. Which number multiplied by the divisor gives the dividend?

::solution
a) $2\times5=10$, so $10\div2=5$.

b) $5\times4=20$, so $20\div5=4$.

c) $2\times6=12$, so $12\div2=6$.

d) $2\times7=14$, so $14\div2=7$.

**Answer.** a) 5; b) 4; c) 6; d) 7.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose 18 story books are shared equally between 2 children. How many story books does each child get?

::hint
**What you need:** sharing equally means division.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. How do we solve it? Which calculation do you use?
3. Check with multiplication.

::solution
Solution

The number of story books each child gets is:
$$18\div2=9\ \text{(books)}.$$
Answer: 9 story books.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on multiplication. There are 3 pencil cases, each with 5 pens.

a) How many pens are there altogether?

b) If those pens are shared equally between the 3 pencil cases, how many pens are in each pencil case?

::hint
**What you need:** multiply to find the total, and divide to share equally again.

**Steps:**
1. Part a: each pencil case has the same number of pens, so multiply.
2. Part b: divide, and compare with the question.

::solution
a) The number of pens altogether is $5\times3=15$ (pens).

b) The number of pens in each pencil case is $15\div3=5$ (pens).

**Answer.** a) 15 pens; b) 5 pens.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A division has dividend 16 and quotient 8. What is the divisor?

::hint
**What you need:** the divisor multiplied by the quotient gives the dividend.

**Steps:**
1. Which number multiplied by 8 gives 16?
2. Check with a division.

::solution
We have $2\times8=16$, so the divisor is 2.

**Check.** $16\div2=8$, which is correct.

**Answer.** 2.
:::
