:::goals
- Add two numbers whose total is more than 10, for example $8+5$.
- Use the "make 10" trick to add quickly, without counting one by one.
- Solve a word problem with one addition.
:::

Lan has $8$ sweets. Her mum gives her $5$ more. How many sweets does Lan have altogether?

You could count on one at a time: $9, 10, 11, 12, 13$. But counting like that is slow and easy to get wrong. This lesson shows you a much quicker trick.

## Let's explore

Lan has a sweet box with exactly $10$ spaces. She puts her $8$ sweets in, so $2$ spaces are still empty.

- Lan takes $2$ of the $5$ sweets from her mum to fill the box. Now the box has $10$.
- $3$ of mum's sweets are left outside the box.
- $10$ in the box and $3$ outside make $13$.

So $8+5=13$. You see: just **make 10** first, and the answer is easy.

:::theorem The "make 10" trick
To work out $8+5$:
1. Ask: how many does $8$ need to make $10$? It needs $2$.
2. Split $5$ into $2$ and $3$.
3. Add: $8+2=10$, then $10+3=13$.
:::

The trick works because no sweet is lost: you only move $2$ of them into the box to make $10$, and the rest are still counted.

## Examples

We start with a sum that has a $9$ in it, because $9$ needs only $1$ more to make $10$.

:::example Work out $9+4$
**Solution.** $9$ needs $1$ more to make $10$, so split $4$ into $1$ and $3$:
$$9+4=9+1+3=10+3=13.$$
So $9+4=13$.
:::

With a $7$, you need more to make $10$.

:::example Work out $7+6$
**Solution.** $7$ needs $3$ more to make $10$, so split $6$ into $3$ and $3$:
$$7+6=7+3+3=10+3=13.$$
So $7+6=13$.
:::

When the first number is the smaller one, you can swap the two numbers to make it easier.

:::example Work out $6+8$
**Solution.** Swapping the addends does not change the sum, so $6+8=8+6$. $8$ needs only $2$ more to make $10$, so split $6$ into $2$ and $4$:
$$8+6=8+2+4=10+4=14.$$
So $6+8=14$.
:::

Now use addition to answer a question from everyday life.

:::example Planting trees
Group One planted $9$ trees and Group Two planted $7$ trees. How many trees did the two groups plant altogether?

**Solution.** "Altogether" means putting the two groups' trees together, so we add. $9$ needs $1$ more to make $10$, so split $7$ into $1$ and $6$:
$$9+7=10+6=16.$$
So the two groups planted $16$ trees.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting what is left.** Wrong: $8+5=10$. Why it is wrong: you only added $2$ of the $5$ and forgot the other $3$. Right: $8+5=10+3=13$.
- **Splitting wrongly.** Wrong: splitting $5$ into $2$ and $2$. Why it is wrong: $2+2=4$, not $5$. Right: $5=2+3$.
- **No unit in a word problem.** Wrong: "Answer: $16$". Right: "Answer: $16$ trees".
:::

## Summary

- To add across $10$, **make 10**: split the second number into the part that makes $10$ and the part that is left.
- Example: $8+5=8+2+3=10+3=13$.
- Swapping the addends does not change the sum; add on to the bigger number to make it easier.
- Questions with "altogether" or "both" usually need addition; remember the unit in the answer.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out $9+3$.

::hint
**What you need:** make $10$: $9$ needs $1$ more.

**Steps:**
1. Split $3$ into $1$ and $2$.
2. Work out $9+1$, then add $2$.

::solution
$9+3=9+1+2=10+2=12$.

**Answer.** $12$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $8+4$.

::hint
**What you need:** $8$ needs $2$ more to make $10$.

**Steps:**
1. Split $4$ into $2$ and $2$.
2. Work out $8+2$, then add what is left.

::solution
$8+4=8+2+2=10+2=12$.

**Answer.** $12$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $7+5$.

::hint
**What you need:** $7$ needs $3$ more to make $10$.

**Steps:**
1. Split $5$ into $3$ and $2$.
2. Work out $7+3$, then add $2$.

::solution
$7+5=7+3+2=10+2=12$.

**Answer.** $12$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $5+9$.

::hint
**What you need:** swapping the two addends does not change the sum.

**Steps:**
1. Write $5+9$ as $9+5$.
2. $9$ needs $1$ more: split $5$ into $1$ and $4$.

::solution
$5+9=9+5=9+1+4=10+4=14$.

**Answer.** $14$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the box: $8+6 \;\square\; 15$.

::hint
**What you need:** work out the sum first, then compare the two numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $8+6$ with the make-10 trick.
2. Compare the result with $15$.

::solution
$8+6=8+2+4=14$. Since $14<15$, we have $8+6<15$.

**Answer.** The sign $<$.
:::

:::exercise
In a garden there are $9$ hens and $6$ cockerels. How many chickens are there altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** "altogether" means putting together: use addition.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? $9$ hens and $6$ cockerels. What is asked? How many chickens altogether.
2. Work out $9+6$ and write the answer with its unit.

::solution
The number of chickens altogether is:
$$9+6=9+1+5=10+5=15\ \text{(chickens)}.$$

**Answer.** $15$ chickens.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson: what number comes just after $12$? Then work out (the number just after $12$) $+\,7$.

::hint
**What you need:** the number just after a number is $1$ more than it.

**Steps:**
1. Find the number just after $12$.
2. Add $7$ to it (count on, or add the ones).

::solution
The number just after $12$ is $13$. Then $13+7=20$ (because $3+7=10$, and $10$ and $10$ make $20$).

**Answer.** $20$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find the missing number: $\square+8=15$.

::hint
**What you need:** $8$ needs $2$ more to make $10$; then how many more to reach $15$?

**Steps:**
1. From $8$ to $10$ you need $2$.
2. From $10$ to $15$ you need $5$.
3. Add the two parts: $2+5$.

::solution
From $8$ up to $10$ is $2$, and from $10$ up to $15$ is $5$, so the missing number is $2+5=7$.

**Check.** $7+8=15$. Correct.

**Answer.** $7$.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
$9+5$ equals
A. $13$.
B. $14$.
C. $15$.
D. $10$.
::key B
$9+5=9+1+4=10+4=14$. Option D is the mistake of forgetting the $4$ that is left.

::mc
To work out $8+7$ with the make-10 trick, you split $7$ into
A. $1$ and $6$.
B. $3$ and $4$.
C. $2$ and $5$.
D. $2$ and $4$.
::key C
$8$ needs $2$ more to make $10$, so split $7=2+5$. Option D is wrong because $2+4=6$, not $7$.

::mc
Which sum equals $13$?
A. $6+7$.
B. $6+8$.
C. $5+7$.
D. $9+5$.
::key A
$6+7=7+6=7+3+3=13$. The other sums are $14$, $12$ and $14$.

::mc
Mai has $8$ flowers and Ha has $6$ flowers. How many flowers do the two girls have altogether?
A. $2$ flowers.
B. $13$ flowers.
C. $14$ flowers.
D. $15$ flowers.
::key C
"Altogether" means putting together: $8+6=14$. Option A is $8-6$, a subtraction by mistake.

::tf
Look at the sum $7+8$.
a) It can be written as $8+7$ without changing the answer.
b) $7+8=7+3+5$.
c) $7+8=14$.
d) $7+8$ is greater than $14$.
::key T T F T
a) True: swapping the addends does not change the sum. b) True: $8=3+5$. c) False: $7+3+5=10+5=15$. d) True: $15>14$.

::sa
Work out $9+9$.
::key 18
$9+9=9+1+8=10+8=18$.

::sa

::key 11
