:::goals
- Recognise, read and write multiples of 10, for example $30$ and $80$.
- Recognise, read and write multiples of 100, for example $400$ and $900$.
- Count on in tens and in hundreds.
:::

Each bag holds $1$ ten sweets. Mai has $6$ such bags. How many sweets does Mai have altogether?

You already know that $1$ ten is $10$ ones. This lesson helps you name and write the number of sweets in many bags quickly.

## Let's explore

Count on one ten at a time: $1$ ten, $2$ tens, $3$ tens, ... Each time we add $1$ ten, we add $10$.

- $1$ ten is $10$, $2$ tens are $20$, $3$ tens are $30$.
- Keep going to $9$ tens, which is $90$.

Numbers like $10, 20, 30, \dots, 90$ are called **multiples of 10**. Their last digit is always $0$.

Do the same with hundreds: $1$ hundred is $100$, $2$ hundreds are $200$, ..., $9$ hundreds are $900$. These are called **multiples of 100**. Their last two digits are always $0$.

:::theorem Multiples of 10 and 100
- $n$ tens is written as $n$ followed by one $0$. Example: $4$ tens is $40$.
- $n$ hundreds is written as $n$ followed by two $0$s. Example: $4$ hundreds is $400$.
:::

This works because $4$ tens is $10+10+10+10=40$, and $4$ hundreds is $100+100+100+100=400$.

## Examples

We start with multiples of 10.

:::example Writing multiples of 10
Write the number for $3$ tens and for $8$ tens.

**Solution.** Write the number of tens, then add a $0$:
$$3\text{ tens}=30,\qquad 8\text{ tens}=80.$$
So $3$ tens is $30$ and $8$ tens is $80$.
:::

Now you count on in tens to fill in a missing number.

:::example Counting on in tens
Fill in the missing numbers: $20, 30, \square, 50, \square$.

**Solution.** Each number is $10$ more than the one before. After $30$ comes $40$, and after $50$ comes $60$:
$$20, 30, 40, 50, 60.$$
So the missing numbers are $40$ and $60$.
:::

Next come the multiples of 100.

:::example Writing multiples of 100
Write the number for $4$ hundreds. How many hundreds is $700$?

**Solution.** $4$ hundreds is $4$ followed by two $0$s, which is $400$. The other way round, $700$ is $7$ hundreds.

So $4$ hundreds is $400$ and $700$ is $7$ hundreds.
:::

Finally you use a multiple of 10 in a problem.

:::example Bags of sweets
Each bag holds $1$ ten sweets. Mai has $6$ bags. How many sweets does she have?

**Solution.** $6$ bags with $1$ ten each make $6$ tens of sweets. $6$ tens is $60$.

So Mai has $60$ sweets.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting the $0$.** Wrong: "$4$ tens is $4$". Why it is wrong: $4$ tens is four groups of $10$. Right: $4$ tens is $40$.
- **Mixing up tens and hundreds.** Wrong: "$6$ hundreds is $60$". Why it is wrong: $60$ is $6$ tens. Right: $6$ hundreds is $600$.
- **Thinking every number is a multiple of 10.** Wrong: "$35$ is a multiple of 10". Why it is wrong: the last digit of $35$ is $5$, not $0$. Right: $35$ is not a multiple of 10.
:::

## Summary

- Multiples of 10: $10, 20, 30, \dots, 90$ (the last digit is $0$).
- Multiples of 100: $100, 200, 300, \dots, 900$ (the last two digits are $0$).
- $4$ tens is $40$; $4$ hundreds is $400$.
- Counting on $10$ gives the next multiple of 10; counting on $100$ gives the next multiple of 100.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Write the number.

a) $3$ tens.

b) $8$ tens.

c) $5$ hundreds.

d) $9$ hundreds.

::hint
**What you need:** for $n$ tens add one $0$; for $n$ hundreds add two $0$s.

**Steps:**
1. Write the number of tens or hundreds.
2. Add the right number of $0$s.

::solution
$3$ tens is $30$, $8$ tens is $80$, $5$ hundreds is $500$, $9$ hundreds is $900$.

**Answer.** a) $30$; b) $80$; c) $500$; d) $900$.
:::

:::exercise
Fill in the missing numbers.

a) $10, 20, \square, 40, \square, 60$.

b) $100, 200, \square, 400, \square$.

::hint
**What you need:** count on $10$ or $100$ each time.

**Steps:**
1. See how much the first two numbers differ by.
2. Keep counting the same way.

::solution
a) Counting on in tens: $10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60$. b) Counting on in hundreds: $100, 200, 300, 400, 500$.

**Answer.** a) $30$ and $50$; b) $300$ and $500$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson and fill in the numbers.

a) $10$ tens $=\square$ hundred.

b) $1$ hundred $=\square$ tens.

::hint
**What you need:** $10$ tens make $1$ hundred.

**Steps:**
1. Remember the rule for exchanging tens and hundreds.
2. Fill in each box.

::solution
Ten tens make one hundred, and one hundred has ten tens.

**Answer.** a) $1$; b) $10$.
:::

:::exercise
Which of the numbers $25, 40, 70, 56, 90$ are multiples of 10?

::hint
**What you need:** a multiple of 10 ends in $0$.

**Steps:**
1. Look at the last digit of each number.
2. Choose the numbers that end in $0$.

::solution
$40$, $70$ and $90$ end in $0$. $25$ and $56$ end in $5$ and $6$.

**Answer.** $40$, $70$, $90$.
:::

:::exercise
Write the number.

a) Seventy.

b) Seven hundred.

How are the two numbers different? Why?

::hint
**What you need:** "seventy" is $7$ tens; "seven hundred" is $7$ hundreds.

**Steps:**
1. Write $7$ tens.
2. Write $7$ hundreds and compare the number of digits.

::solution
$7$ tens is $70$. $7$ hundreds is $700$. The number $700$ has one more $0$ because a hundred is bigger than a ten.

**Answer.** a) $70$; b) $700$.
:::

:::exercise
Lan writes "$6$ hundreds is $60$". What is wrong with what Lan writes? Correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** $6$ hundreds has two $0$s at the end.

**Steps:**
1. How many tens is $60$?
2. Write $6$ hundreds again.

::solution
$60$ is $6$ tens, not $6$ hundreds. $6$ hundreds needs two $0$s.

**Answer.** $6$ hundreds is $600$.
:::

:::exercise
Each box holds $10$ pens. There are $8$ boxes. How many pens are there?

::hint
**What you need:** each box is $1$ ten pens; $8$ tens is $80$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? Each box has $1$ ten and there are $8$ boxes. What is asked? The number of pens.
2. Write $8$ tens and give the answer with its unit.

::solution
$8$ boxes make $8$ tens of pens, which is $80$ pens.

**Answer.** $80$ pens.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
There are $10$ boxes, each with $100$ marbles. People take away $4$ boxes. How many marbles are left?

::hint
**What you need:** $10$ boxes take away $4$ boxes leaves $6$ boxes; each box is $1$ hundred.

**Steps:**
1. Work out how many boxes are left.
2. Change the boxes left into hundreds of marbles.

::solution
The boxes left: $10-4=6$. Each box has $100$ marbles, so $6$ boxes are $6$ hundreds, which is $600$ marbles.

**Check.** $600+400=1000$. Correct.

**Answer.** $600$ marbles.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
Which number is $5$ tens?
A. $5$.
B. $15$.
C. $500$.
D. $50$.
::key D
$5$ tens is $50$. Option A forgets the $0$, and option C is $5$ hundreds.

::mc
Which number is a multiple of 100?
A. $300$.
B. $150$.
C. $330$.
D. $30$.
::key A
$300$ is $3$ hundreds. $30$ is a multiple of 10, and $150$ and $330$ are not multiples of 100.

A. $71$.
B. $90$.
C. $100$.
D. $78$.
::key B
After $80$, add $10$ to get $90$. Option A counts on $1$.
