:::goals
- Compare two multiples of 10 and two multiples of 100.
- Use the signs $>$, $<$ and $=$ correctly.
- Put multiples of 10 and 100 in order, smallest first and largest first.
:::

Lan has $30$ marbles. Nam has $50$ marbles. Who has more marbles?

You know that $30$ is $3$ tens and $50$ is $5$ tens. This lesson helps you compare numbers like these quickly and surely.

## Let's explore

Lan has $3$ tens of marbles and Nam has $5$ tens. We compare the tens: $5$ is greater than $3$, so $5$ tens is greater than $3$ tens.

You can also see this on a number line: the number further to the right is greater.

:::graph On the number line: 70 is to the right of 30, so 70 is greater than 30

:::

We use signs to write the result of a comparison:

- $50>30$ is read "fifty is greater than thirty".
- $30<50$ is read "thirty is less than fifty".
- $50=50$ is read "fifty is equal to fifty".

The pointed end of $>$ or $<$ always points to the smaller number.

:::theorem How to compare
- Two multiples of 10: the one with more tens is greater.
- Two multiples of 100: the one with more hundreds is greater.
- If the number of tens (or hundreds) is the same, the numbers are equal.
:::

This works because each ten has $10$ ones, so more tens means more ones. The same is true for hundreds.

## Examples

We start with two multiples of 10.

:::example Comparing multiples of 10
Compare $30$ and $50$.

**Solution.** $30$ is $3$ tens and $50$ is $5$ tens. Since $3<5$, $3$ tens is less than $5$ tens:
$$30<50.$$
So $30<50$, which means Nam has more marbles than Lan.
:::

Now we compare two multiples of 100.

:::example Comparing multiples of 100
Compare $600$ and $400$.

**Solution.** $600$ is $6$ hundreds and $400$ is $4$ hundreds. Since $6>4$, $6$ hundreds is greater than $4$ hundreds:
$$600>400.$$
So $600>400$.
:::

Next we put several numbers in order.

:::example Smallest first
Put the numbers $80, 20, 50$ in order, smallest first.

**Solution.** Change to tens: $8$ tens, $2$ tens, $5$ tens. The tens in order from smallest are $2, 5, 8$:
$$20<50<80.$$
So the order from smallest to largest is $20, 50, 80$.
:::

Finally you use comparing in a problem.

:::example Boxes of pens
Box A has $40$ pens and box B has $70$ pens. Which box has more pens?

**Solution.** Compare $40$ and $70$. $4$ tens is less than $7$ tens, so $40<70$.

So box B has more pens.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting that the pointed end faces the smaller number.** Wrong: $30>50$. Why it is wrong: $30$ is less than $50$. Right: $30<50$.
- **Comparing the first digits of a multiple of 10 and a multiple of 100.** Wrong: "$90>100$ because $9>1$". Why it is wrong: $100$ is $10$ tens, which is more than $9$ tens. Right: $90<100$.
- **Thinking $50$ equals $500$ because both have a $5$.** Wrong: $50=500$. Why it is wrong: $50$ is $5$ tens and $500$ is $5$ hundreds. Right: $50<500$.
:::

## Summary

- Multiples of 10: compare the tens. Multiples of 100: compare the hundreds.
- $>$ is read greater than, $<$ is read less than, $=$ is read equal to; the pointed end faces the smaller number.
- On a number line, the number further to the right is greater.
- To order smallest first, start with the smallest number.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the box.

a) $20\;\square\;40$.

b) $70\;\square\;30$.

c) $50\;\square\;50$.

::hint
**What you need:** compare the tens; the pointed end faces the smaller number.

**Steps:**
1. Change each number to tens.
2. Compare the tens and choose the sign.

::solution
a) $2$ tens is less than $4$ tens. b) $7$ tens is greater than $3$ tens. c) The two numbers are equal.

**Answer.** a) $<$; b) $>$; c) $=$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the box.

a) $300\;\square\;800$.

b) $900\;\square\;600$.

c) $400\;\square\;400$.

::hint
**What you need:** compare the hundreds.

**Steps:**
1. Change each number to hundreds.
2. Compare the hundreds and choose the sign.

::solution
a) $3$ hundreds is less than $8$ hundreds. b) $9$ hundreds is greater than $6$ hundreds. c) The two numbers are equal.

**Answer.** a) $<$; b) $>$; c) $=$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson. Write $6$ tens and $4$ hundreds as numbers, then write a sign: $6$ tens $\;\square\;$ $4$ hundreds.

::hint
**What you need:** $n$ tens is $n$ followed by one $0$; $n$ hundreds is $n$ followed by two $0$s.

**Steps:**
1. Write $6$ tens and $4$ hundreds.
2. Remember that $10$ tens make $1$ hundred, then compare.

::solution
$6$ tens is $60$ and $4$ hundreds is $400$. $60$ is not even $1$ hundred, so it is less than $400$.

**Answer.** $60<400$.
:::

:::exercise
Put the numbers in order.

a) Smallest first: $60, 10, 90, 30$.

b) Largest first: $200, 700, 100, 500$.

::hint
**What you need:** pick the smallest (or largest) number first, then the next.

**Steps:**
1. Change to tens or hundreds.
2. Pick the numbers one at a time.

::solution
a) Tens: $6, 1, 9, 3$. Smallest first: $1, 3, 6, 9$ tens. b) Hundreds: $2, 7, 1, 5$. Largest first: $7, 5, 2, 1$ hundreds.

**Answer.** a) $10, 30, 60, 90$; b) $700, 500, 200, 100$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$: $50\;\square\;500$. Why?

::hint
**What you need:** $50$ is $5$ tens and $500$ is $5$ hundreds.

**Steps:**
1. Change both numbers to tens.
2. How many tens are in $5$ hundreds?

::solution
$500$ is $5$ hundreds, which is $50$ tens. $50$ tens is greater than $5$ tens.

**Answer.** $50<500$.
:::

:::exercise
Nam says: "$90>100$ because $9>1$." What is wrong with what Nam says? Correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** $100$ is $10$ tens.

**Steps:**
1. How many tens is $100$?
2. Compare $9$ tens and $10$ tens.

::solution
$100$ is $10$ tens, but $90$ has only $9$ tens. Nam must not compare the first digits.

**Answer.** $90<100$.
:::

:::exercise
A supermarket has $60$ tins of milk and $40$ cans of fizzy drink. Which has more?

::hint
**What you need:** more tens means a greater number.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? $60$ tins of milk and $40$ cans of drink. What is asked? Which is more.
2. Compare $60$ and $40$.

::solution
$6$ tens is greater than $4$ tens, so $60>40$.

**Answer.** There are more tins of milk.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find all the multiples of 10 that are greater than $30$ and less than $70$.

::hint
**What you need:** the multiples of 10 are $10, 20, 30, \dots$

**Steps:**
1. List the multiples of 10 after $30$.
2. Stop before $70$.

::solution
After $30$ come $40, 50, 60$, and then $70$ (we must not include $70$).

**Check.** $30<40<50<60<70$. Correct.

**Answer.** $40$, $50$, $60$.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
Which sign goes in $40\;\square\;70$?
A. $>$.
B. $=$.
C. $<$.
D. They cannot be compared.
::key C
$4$ tens is less than $7$ tens, so $40<70$. Option A turns the pointed end the wrong way.

::mc
Which is the greatest of $300, 700, 500, 100$?
A. $300$.
B. $100$.
C. $500$.
D. $700$.
::key D
$7$ hundreds is the greatest number of hundreds.

::mc
Which is the order from smallest to largest of $30, 10, 50$?
A. $10, 30, 50$.
B. $50, 30, 10$.
C. $30, 10, 50$.
D. $10, 50, 30$.
::key A
The tens are $3, 1, 5$; from smallest they are $1, 3, 5$. Option B is largest first.

::mc
Which sign goes in $90\;\square\;100$?
A. $>$.
B. $<$.
C. $=$.
D. They cannot be compared.
::key B
$100$ is $10$ tens, which is more than $9$ tens. Option A comes from looking only at the first digit.

::tf
Compare the numbers.
a) $50<80$.
b) $600>400$.
c) $70=700$.
d) $100<90$.
::key T T F F
a) True. b) True. c) False: $70$ is $7$ tens and $700$ is $7$ hundreds, so $70<700$. d) False: $100>90$.

::key 80
$8$ tens is the greatest number of tens.

::sa

::key 300
$3$ hundreds is the smallest number of hundreds.
