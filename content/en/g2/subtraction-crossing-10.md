- Subtract within 20 by bridging through 10, splitting the number you take away.
- Build and remember the subtraction facts that bridge through 10.
- Use the link between addition and subtraction to find two subtractions from one addition.
- Solve word problems with subtraction that bridges through 10.
:::

Suppose Nam has 14 marbles and gives 6 of them to Ha. Nam could count back one marble at a time: 13, 12, 11, 10, 9, 8. But counting back over and over is slow and easy to get wrong.

Is there a way to subtract quickly without counting back? How many marbles does Nam have left?

## Subtraction bridging through 10 (within 20)

Notice that taking 4 away from 14 gives 10 straight away, and taking a small number away from 10 is very easy. The 6 you must take away is made of $4+2$. Take away the 4 first to reach 10, then take away the other 2.

:::graph On the number line: from 14 jump 4 to the left to reach 10, then jump 2 more to reach 8
alt: A number line from 7 to 15; the stretch from 8 to 14 is highlighted, with the points 14, 10 and 8 marked
x: 7..15
y: -1..1
segment: (8, 0) (14, 0)
point: (14, 0) "14" below
point: (10, 0) "10" above
point: (8, 0) "8" below
:::

So $14-6=8$, and Nam has 8 marbles left.

:::theorem Subtracting through 10
To work out $a-b$ when $a$ is from 11 to 18 and the number $b$ you take away is greater than the ones digit of $a$:
1. Split $b$ into two parts, one part equal to the ones digit of $a$, so that taking it from $a$ gives 10.
2. Take the other part away from 10.
:::

Why does this work? Taking away 6 is the same as taking away 4 and then taking away 2 more, so $14-6=14-4-2$. We reach 10 first, because taking a small number from 10 is easy:
$$14-6=14-4-2=10-2=8.$$

:::example Work out $13-5$
**Solution.** The ones digit of 13 is 3, so split $5=3+2$ so that $13-3=10$:
$$13-5=13-3-2=10-2=8.$$
So $13-5=8$.
:::

:::example Work out $15-7$
**Solution.** The ones digit of 15 is 5, so split $7=5+2$ so that $15-5=10$:
$$15-7=15-5-2=10-2=8.$$
So $15-7=8$.
:::

Now use this method to solve a word problem.

:::example Nam's box of marbles (Apply it)
Suppose there are 15 marbles in a box. Nam takes out 8 marbles to give to friends. How many marbles are left in the box?

**Understand.** We know the box has 15 marbles and Nam takes out 8. We are asked how many marbles are left.

**Plan.** "Takes out" means taking away, so we subtract $15-8$, splitting the number we take away to reach 10.

**Solve.**

Solution

The number of marbles left in the box is:
$$15-8=15-5-3=10-3=7\ \text{(marbles)}.$$
Answer: 7 marbles.

**Look back.** Check with addition: $7+8=15$, which is correct. There is another way too: split the 15 as $15=10+5$, then work out $10-8=2$ and $2+5=7$, which also gives 7.
:::

## Subtraction facts bridging through 10

You already know the addition facts that bridge through 10, for example $9+5=14$. From one addition you can write **two matching subtractions**:
$$14-9=5,\qquad 14-5=9.$$

:::definition The link between addition and subtraction
If $a+b=c$, then $c-a=b$ and $c-b=a$.
:::

Why? The sum $c$ is made of the two addends $a$ and $b$. If you take one addend away from the sum, the other addend is left. So you can use the addition facts you already know to remember the **subtraction facts that bridge through 10**.

| Minuend | − 2 | − 3 | − 4 | − 5 | − 6 | − 7 | − 8 | − 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | 9 | 8 | 7 | 6 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 |
| 12 | | 9 | 8 | 7 | 6 | 5 | 4 | 3 |
| 13 | | | 9 | 8 | 7 | 6 | 5 | 4 |
| 14 | | | | 9 | 8 | 7 | 6 | 5 |
| 15 | | | | | 9 | 8 | 7 | 6 |
| 16 | | | | | | 9 | 8 | 7 |
| 17 | | | | | | | 9 | 8 |
| 18 | | | | | | | | 9 |

Read the table like this: the minuend is in the first column, the number you take away is in the top row, and the difference is where they meet. For example, $13-6=7$. Along each row, when the number you take away goes up by 1, the difference goes down by 1.

**Connection.** This table uses exactly the numbers of the addition facts that bridge through 10, and it will help with larger subtractions with regrouping in later lessons.

:::example Writing two subtractions from one addition
You know that $8+6=14$. Write the two matching subtractions.

**Solution.** Take each addend away from the sum 14 in turn:
$$14-8=6,\qquad 14-6=8.$$
So the two subtractions are $14-8=6$ and $14-6=8$.
:::

:::example Using the subtraction facts
Work out $16-9$.

**Solution.** Look at the row for the minuend 16 and the column $-9$ in the table, and you find the difference 7. Check with addition: $9+7=16$, which is correct.

So $16-9=7$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Splitting the number you take away wrongly.** Wrong: $14-6=14-4-3=7$. Why it is wrong: $4+3=7$, not $6$, so you took away 1 too many. Right: $6=4+2$, so $14-6=14-4-2=8$.
- **Forgetting to take away the rest.** Wrong: $13-5=13-3=10$. Why it is wrong: you have only taken away 3, and you still have to take away 2 more. Right: $10-2=8$.
- **Forgetting the unit in the answer.** Wrong: "Answer: 7". Right: "Answer: 7 marbles".
:::

## Summary

- To subtract through 10, split the number you take away so that taking part of it from the minuend gives 10, then take the rest from 10. For example $14-6=14-4-2=8$.
- From the addition $a+b=c$ you get two subtractions: $c-a=b$ and $c-b=a$.
- The subtraction facts that bridge through 10 give you the difference straight away, and you can check them with the addition facts.
- In the subtraction table, when the number you take away goes up by 1, the difference goes down by 1.

a) $12-5$

b) $14-8$

::hint

**Steps:**

:::exercise Challenge
In the subtraction facts that bridge through 10, which subtractions have a difference of 9? What do you notice about the minuend and the number taken away in those subtractions?

::hint
**What you need:** if $a-b=9$, then $b+9=a$.

**Steps:**
1. Find the 9s in the subtraction table.
2. Compare the minuend and the number taken away in two neighbouring subtractions.

::solution
The subtractions with a difference of 9 are:
$$11-2,\ 12-3,\ 13-4,\ 14-5,\ 15-6,\ 16-7,\ 17-8,\ 18-9.$$
From one of these subtractions to the next, the minuend goes up by 1 and the number taken away also goes up by 1, so the difference stays the same.

**Answer.** There are 8 subtractions; when the minuend and the number taken away both go up by 1, the difference is still 9.
:::
