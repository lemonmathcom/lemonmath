:::goals
- Recognise a broken line and name its line segments.
- Find the length of a broken line by adding the lengths of its line segments.
- Recognise a quadrilateral and name its vertices and sides.
- Solve word problems about the length of a broken line.
:::

Suppose an ant crawls from point A to point B, which is 3 cm, turns towards point C and crawls another 4 cm, then turns towards point D and crawls another 5 cm. The ant's path is not straight but bends at each point.

What do we call this path? How many centimetres does the ant crawl altogether? And if the ant carried on from D back to A, what shape would we get?

## Let's explore

Look at the ant's path. The lengths on the picture are only there to give you the measurements; the picture is not drawn to scale.

This path is made of three line segments AB, BC and CD that are **joined end to end**. We call it the **broken line ABCD**.

:::definition Broken line
A **broken line** is made of line segments joined end to end, where the end of one segment is also the end of the next.

The broken line ABCD is made of three line segments AB, BC and CD. The **length of a broken line** is the sum of the lengths of its line segments.
:::

Why is the length a sum? The ant must crawl all of AB, then all of BC, then all of CD. The distance it crawls is the three parts put together, so we add.

Now let the ant carry on from D back to A. We get four line segments AB, BC, CD and DA that close up.

:::definition Quadrilateral
A **quadrilateral** has **4 sides** and **4 vertices**. The quadrilateral ABCD has sides AB, BC, CD and DA and vertices A, B, C and D.
:::

**Connection.** A quadrilateral is a broken line ABCD with the extra segment DA joining the last vertex back to the first, so that the segments close up.

## Examples

First, we name the line segments.

:::example Naming the line segments
Which line segments make up the broken line ABCD in the first picture?

**Solution.** The points A, B, C and D are joined in order, so the broken line is made of three line segments: AB, BC and CD.
:::

Now work out the distance the ant crawls.

:::example Length of a broken line
The broken line ABCD has $AB=3$ cm, $BC=4$ cm and $CD=5$ cm. Find the length of the broken line ABCD.

**Solution.** The length of a broken line is the sum of the lengths of its line segments:
$$3\ \text{cm}+4\ \text{cm}+5\ \text{cm}=12\ \text{cm}.$$
So the broken line ABCD is 12 cm long, and the ant crawls 12 cm altogether.
:::

Now to quadrilaterals.

:::example Vertices and sides of a quadrilateral
Which vertices and sides does the quadrilateral MNPQ have?

**Solution.** A quadrilateral has 4 vertices and 4 sides. The vertices are M, N, P and Q. The sides are MN, NP, PQ and QM.
:::

:::example Is a rectangle a quadrilateral?
Is a rectangle a quadrilateral?

**Solution.** A rectangle has 4 sides and 4 vertices, so a rectangle is a quadrilateral. A square also has 4 sides and 4 vertices, so it is a quadrilateral too.
:::

Finally, a word problem.

:::example The piece of wire (Apply it)
Suppose a piece of wire is bent into a broken line made of three line segments that are 6 cm, 5 cm and 7 cm long. How many centimetres long is the wire?

**Understand.** We know the three line segments are 6 cm, 5 cm and 7 cm long. We are asked for the length of the whole wire.

**Plan.** The length of a broken line is the sum of the lengths of its line segments, so we add.

**Solve.**

Solution

The length of the wire in centimetres is:
$$6+5+7=18\ \text{(cm)}.$$
Answer: 18 cm.

**Look back.** If we add in a different order, does the result change? Try it: $7+5+6=18$, which is still 18, because swapping the addends does not change the sum.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Taking the length of just one line segment.** Wrong: the broken line ABCD is 5 cm long because CD is the longest. Why it is wrong: the length of a broken line is the sum of all three line segments. Right: $3+4+5=12$ cm.
- **Counting the line segments wrongly.** Wrong: the broken line ABCD has 4 line segments because there are 4 points. Why it is wrong: 4 points joined in order make only 3 line segments. Right: there are 3 line segments AB, BC and CD.
- **Forgetting the unit in the answer.** Wrong: "Answer: 18". Right: "Answer: 18 cm".
:::

## Summary

- A broken line is made of line segments joined end to end. The broken line ABCD has 3 line segments AB, BC and CD.
- The length of a broken line is the sum of the lengths of its line segments.
- A quadrilateral has 4 sides and 4 vertices. Rectangles and squares are quadrilaterals.
- Remember to write the unit cm in the answer.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Which line segments make up the broken line MNPQ?

::hint
**What you need:** the points are joined in order to make the line segments.

**Steps:**
1. Join M to N, then N to P.
2. Then join P to Q.

::solution
The broken line MNPQ is made of three line segments: MN, NP and PQ.

**Answer.** MN, NP, PQ.
:::

:::exercise
The broken line EFGH has $EF=4$ cm, $FG=6$ cm and $GH=3$ cm. Find the length of the broken line EFGH.

::hint
**What you need:** the length of a broken line is the sum of the lengths of its line segments.

**Steps:**
1. How many line segments does the broken line EFGH have?
2. Add their lengths, and remember to write cm.

::solution
The length of the broken line EFGH is:
$$4\ \text{cm}+6\ \text{cm}+3\ \text{cm}=13\ \text{cm}.$$

**Answer.** 13 cm.
:::

:::exercise
The broken line ABC has $AB=8$ cm and $BC=7$ cm. Find the length of the broken line ABC.

::hint
**What you need:** the broken line ABC is made of two line segments, AB and BC.

**Steps:**
1. Write the addition of the lengths of the two line segments.
2. Bridge through 10: $8+7=8+2+5$.

::solution
$$8\ \text{cm}+7\ \text{cm}=15\ \text{cm}.$$

**Answer.** 15 cm.
:::

:::exercise
How many vertices and how many sides does the quadrilateral ABCD have? Name the vertices and the sides.

::hint
**What you need:** a quadrilateral has 4 sides and 4 vertices.

**Steps:**
1. The vertices are the letters in the name of the shape.
2. Join neighbouring letters, and remember to join the last letter back to the first.

::solution
The quadrilateral ABCD has 4 vertices, A, B, C and D, and 4 sides, AB, BC, CD and DA.

**Answer.** 4 vertices, 4 sides.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a piece of wire is bent into a broken line made of three line segments that are 9 cm, 4 cm and 6 cm long. How many centimetres long is the wire?

::hint
**What you need:** the length of a broken line is the sum of the lengths of its line segments.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. How do we solve it? Which calculation do you use?
3. Check: the sum must be greater than each addend.

::solution
Solution

The length of the wire in centimetres is:
$$9+4+6=19\ \text{(cm)}.$$
Answer: 19 cm.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose Mai uses a piece of wool to make a quadrilateral ABCD with sides $AB=4$ cm, $BC=5$ cm, $CD=4$ cm and $DA=5$ cm. How many centimetres long is the wool?

::hint
**What you need:** the wool makes four sides, so its length is the sum of the lengths of the four sides.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. Add the four numbers one at a time from left to right.
3. Check by adding in a different order.

::solution
Solution

The length of the wool in centimetres is:
$$4+5+4+5=18\ \text{(cm)}.$$
Answer: 18 cm.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on measuring length. You measure with a ruler and find $AB=6$ cm and $BC=8$ cm. Find the length of the broken line ABC. Which line segment is longer, and by how many centimetres?

::hint
**What you need:** to find the total, add. To find how many more, subtract the smaller number from the larger.

**Steps:**
1. Add the lengths of the two line segments.
2. Compare 6 and 8, then subtract the smaller from the larger.

::solution
The length of the broken line ABC is:
$$6\ \text{cm}+8\ \text{cm}=14\ \text{cm}.$$
We have $8>6$, so BC is longer than AB. BC is longer than AB by:
$$8\ \text{cm}-6\ \text{cm}=2\ \text{cm}.$$

**Answer.** 14 cm; BC is longer by 2 cm.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
The broken line ABCD is 18 cm long. You know that $AB=7$ cm and $BC=5$ cm. How many centimetres long is the line segment CD?

::hint
**What you need:** the length of a broken line is the sum of its three line segments. What is left after taking away the two known segments is CD.

**Steps:**
1. How long are AB and BC together?
2. Take that length away from 18.

::solution
AB and BC together are:
$$7+5=12\ \text{(cm)}.$$
So CD is:
$$18-12=6\ \text{(cm)}.$$

**Check.** $7+5+6=18$, which is correct.

**Answer.** 6 cm.
:::

:::quiz
::mc
The broken line ABCD is made of the line segments
A. AB, BC.
B. AB, BC, CD.
C. AC, BD.
D. AB, BC, CD, DA.
::key B
The points A, D, C and B are joined in order to make three line segments AB, BC and CD. Option D has the extra segment DA, which makes a quadrilateral.

::mc
The broken line ABC has $AB=6$ cm and $BC=7$ cm. The length of the broken line ABC is
A. $1$ cm.
B. $14$ cm.
C. $13$ cm.
D. $67$ cm.
::key C
$6+7=13$ (cm). Option A is a subtraction, and option D writes the two numbers side by side.

::mc
A quadrilateral has
A. 5 sides and 5 vertices.
B. 3 sides and 3 vertices.
C. 4 sides and 3 vertices.
D. 4 sides and 4 vertices.
::key D
A quadrilateral has 4 sides and 4 vertices. Option B is a triangle.

::mc
Which of these is a quadrilateral?
A. A rectangle.
B. The line segment AB.
C. The broken line ABC.
D. A point.
::key A
A rectangle has 4 sides and 4 vertices, so it is a quadrilateral. A line segment, a point and the broken line ABC do not close up with 4 sides.

::tf
The broken line ABCD has $AB=3$ cm, $BC=4$ cm and $CD=5$ cm.
a) The broken line ABCD is made of 3 line segments.
b) The length of the broken line ABCD is 12 cm.
c) The length of the broken line ABCD is greater than 10 cm.
d) The length of the broken line ABCD is equal to the length of the line segment CD.
::key T T T F
a) True: there are AB, BC and CD. b) True: $3+4+5=12$. c) True: $12>10$. d) False: the length of a broken line is the sum of the three segments, which is 12 cm and not 5 cm.

::sa
The broken line ABC has $AB=9$ cm and $BC=8$ cm. How many centimetres long is the broken line ABC?
::key 17
$9+8=17$ (cm).

::sa
An ant crawls along a broken line made of three line segments that are 4 cm, 6 cm and 5 cm long. How many centimetres does the ant crawl altogether?
::key 15
$4+6+5=15$ (cm).
:::
