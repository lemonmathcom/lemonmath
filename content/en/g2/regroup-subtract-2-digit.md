:::goals
- Set out a column and subtract two two-digit numbers, for example $52-27$.
- Remember to take away the $1$ ten you borrowed in the tens place.
- Solve a word problem with one subtraction that needs regrouping.
:::

The class library has $52$ story books. The children have borrowed $27$ of them. How many story books are left in the library?

In the last lesson you subtracted a one-digit number and borrowed $1$ ten. This lesson uses the same method, and also takes away some tens.

## Let's explore

We split $52$ into $5$ tens and $2$ ones, and $27$ into $2$ tens and $7$ ones.

- Ones: $2$ is too small to take away $7$. We exchange $1$ ten for $10$ ones to get $12$ ones, then work out $12-7=5$.
- Tens: $5$ tens take away the $1$ ten we borrowed leaves $4$ tens. Then take away $2$ tens: $4-2=2$.

So there are $2$ tens and $5$ ones, which is $25$.

$$\begin{array}{r} 52\\ -\ 27\\ \hline 25 \end{array}$$

You can also jump back on a number line: from $52$ jump back $20$ to reach $32$, then jump back $7$ to reach $25$.

:::theorem Subtracting two two-digit numbers with regrouping
To work out $52-27$:
1. Set out the column: ones under ones, tens under tens.
2. Ones: $2$ is too small to take away $7$, so borrow $1$ ten. Work out $12-7=5$ and write $5$.
3. Tens: $5-1-2=2$ (take away the borrowed ten, then take away $2$ tens). Write $2$.
:::

This works because $1$ ten is the same as $10$ ones. We only change how the number is written; the number of books stays the same.

## Examples

We start with two small numbers where the ones are only a little too small.

:::example Work out $41-15$
**Solution.** Ones: $1$ is too small to take away $5$, so borrow $1$ ten to make $11$. Work out $11-5=6$. Tens: $4-1-1=2$:
$$\begin{array}{r} 41\\ -\ 15\\ \hline 26 \end{array}$$
So $41-15=26$.
:::

The next subtraction is done in exactly the same way, with different numbers.

:::example Work out $63-28$
**Solution.** Ones: borrow $1$ ten, $13-8=5$. Tens: $6-1-2=3$:
$$\begin{array}{r} 63\\ -\ 28\\ \hline 35 \end{array}$$
So $63-28=35$.

**Check.** Check with addition: $35+28=63$. Correct.
:::

Now the ones digit of the first number is $0$, so we must be more careful.

:::example Work out $80-34$
**Solution.** Ones: $0$ is too small to take away $4$, so borrow $1$ ten to make $10$. Work out $10-4=6$. Tens: $8-1-3=4$:
$$\begin{array}{r} 80\\ -\ 34\\ \hline 46 \end{array}$$
So $80-34=46$.
:::

Now use subtraction to solve a problem from everyday life.

:::example The library
A library has $72$ books. Children borrow $45$ of them. How many books are left in the library?

**Solution.** "Borrow" and "left" mean taking away, so we subtract. Ones: borrow $1$ ten, $12-5=7$. Tens: $7-1-4=2$:
$$72-45=27\ \text{(books)}.$$
So there are $27$ books left in the library.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Taking the smaller digit from the bigger one.** Wrong: $41-15=34$. Why it is wrong: you worked out $5-1=4$ instead of borrowing a ten to work out $11-5$. Right: $41-15=26$.
- **Borrowing a ten but not reducing the tens.** Wrong: $41-15=36$. Why it is wrong: you worked out $11-5=6$ but in the tens you only did $4-1$ and forgot to take away the borrowed ten. Right: $41-15=26$.
- **No unit in a word problem.** Wrong: "Answer: $27$". Right: "Answer: $27$ books".
:::

## Summary

- Set out the column and subtract from the ones to the tens.
- If the ones are too small, borrow $1$ ten and exchange it for $10$ ones, then subtract.
- In the tens: take away the borrowed ten, then take away the tens of the number you are subtracting.
- Example: $52-27$: $12-7=5$; $5-1-2=2$; the answer is $25$. Check: $25+27=52$.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out $74-36$.

::hint
**What you need:** if the ones are too small, borrow $1$ ten.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $14-6$.
2. Tens: $7$ take away $1$, then take away $3$.

::solution
Ones: $4$ is too small to take away $6$, so borrow $1$ ten, $14-6=8$. Tens: $7-1-3=3$. So $74-36=38$.

**Answer.** $38$.
:::

:::exercise
Set out the column and work out $85-29$.

::hint
**What you need:** put tens under tens and ones under ones.

**Steps:**
1. Write the two numbers in columns.
2. Subtract the ones (borrow a ten), then the tens.

::solution
$15-9=6$. Tens: $8-1-2=5$. So $85-29=56$.

**Answer.** $56$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson. Work out $61-8$.

::hint
**What you need:** a one-digit number has only ones; you still borrow $1$ ten when the ones are too small.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $11-8$.
2. Tens: $6$ take away $1$.

::solution
$11-8=3$. Tens: $6-1=5$. So $61-8=53$.

**Answer.** $53$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out.

a) $93-47$.

b) $60-25$.

::hint
**What you need:** the same method works for both subtractions.

**Steps:**
1. Borrow $1$ ten in the ones place, then subtract.
2. Tens: take away the borrowed ten, then take away the tens of the other number.

::solution
a) $13-7=6$; $9-1-4=4$. So $93-47=46$.

b) $10-5=5$; $6-1-2=3$. So $60-25=35$.

**Answer.** a) $46$; b) $35$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the box. Say why.

a) $71-34 \;\square\; 36$.

b) $56-19 \;\square\; 38$.

::hint
**What you need:** work out the difference first, then compare the two numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the difference on the left.
2. Compare the tens, then the ones.

::solution
a) $71-34=37$. Since $37>36$, we have $71-34>36$.

b) $56-19=37$. Since $37<38$, we have $56-19<38$.

**Answer.** a) The sign $>$; b) the sign $<$.
:::

:::exercise
Phuong wrote: $63-27=44$. Where did Phuong go wrong? Correct the answer.

::hint
**What you need:** if the ones are too small, borrow $1$ ten; do not take the smaller digit from the bigger one.

**Steps:**
1. Can you work out $3-7$?
2. Try again with $13-7$.

::solution
Phuong worked out $7-3=4$, which is wrong because you must take $7$ away from $3$. Borrow $1$ ten: $13-7=6$, tens $6-1-2=3$. So $63-27=36$.

**Answer.** Phuong took the smaller digit from the bigger one; the right answer is $36$.
:::

:::exercise
There are $82$ children playing in the school yard. Then $37$ children go back to class. How many children are left in the yard?

::hint
**What you need:** "go back" and "left" mean taking away: use subtraction.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? $82$ children, and $37$ go back. What is asked?
2. Work out $82-37$ and write the answer with its unit.

::solution
Ones: borrow $1$ ten, $12-7=5$. Tens: $8-1-3=4$. The number of children left is:
$$82-37=45\ \text{(children)}.$$

**Answer.** $45$ children.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find the missing digit: $7\square-28=45$.

::hint
**What you need:** the ones digit of the difference is $5$. If you borrow $1$ ten, the ones are $10+\square$.

**Steps:**
1. $(10+\square)-8$ must equal $5$.
2. Check with the subtraction.

::solution
$(10+\square)-8=5$, so $10+\square=13$, which gives $\square=3$. Check: $73-28=45$ (because $13-8=5$ and $7-1-2=4$).

**Answer.** The missing digit is $3$.
:::
