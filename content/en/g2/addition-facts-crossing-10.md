:::goals
- Add two numbers whose sum is greater than 10 by splitting a number to make 10.
- Build and remember the addition facts that bridge through 10 within 20.
- Use the commutative property $a+b=b+a$ to calculate in your head faster.
- Solve word problems with addition that bridges through 10.
:::

Suppose Lan has 9 sweets and her mum gives her 5 more. Lan could count on one sweet at a time: 10, 11, 12, 13, 14. But if she has to do many sums like this, that takes a long time.

Is there a way to add quickly without counting every sweet? How many sweets does Lan have altogether?

## Let's explore

Notice that 9 needs just 1 more to make 10, and 10 is very easy to add to. Split the number 5 into $1+4$. Give 1 to the 9 to make 10, then add the 4 that is left.

:::graph On the number line: from 9 jump 1 to reach 10, then jump 4 more to reach 14
alt: A number line from 8 to 15; the stretch from 9 to 14 is highlighted, with the points 9, 10 and 14 marked
x: 8..15
y: -1..1
segment: (9, 0) (14, 0)
point: (9, 0) "9" below
point: (10, 0) "10" above
point: (14, 0) "14" below
:::

So $9+5=14$, and Lan has 14 sweets.

:::theorem Bridging through 10
To work out $a+b$ when the sum is greater than 10:
1. Split the number $b$ into two parts, so that one part makes 10 together with $a$.
2. Add 10 and the other part.
:::

Why does this work? Adding 5 is the same as adding 1 and then adding 4 more, so $9+5=9+1+4$. We make 10 first, because adding a small number to 10 is easy:
$$9+5=9+1+4=10+4=14.$$

Here are the **addition facts that bridge through 10**. Notice that in each column, when the second addend goes up by 1, the sum goes up by 1 too.

| Second addend | 9 + … | 8 + … | 7 + … | 6 + … |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 9 + 2 = 11 | | | |
| 3 | 9 + 3 = 12 | 8 + 3 = 11 | | |
| 4 | 9 + 4 = 13 | 8 + 4 = 12 | 7 + 4 = 11 | |
| 5 | 9 + 5 = 14 | 8 + 5 = 13 | 7 + 5 = 12 | 6 + 5 = 11 |
| 6 | 9 + 6 = 15 | 8 + 6 = 14 | 7 + 6 = 13 | 6 + 6 = 12 |
| 7 | 9 + 7 = 16 | 8 + 7 = 15 | 7 + 7 = 14 | 6 + 7 = 13 |
| 8 | 9 + 8 = 17 | 8 + 8 = 16 | 7 + 8 = 15 | 6 + 8 = 14 |
| 9 | 9 + 9 = 18 | 8 + 9 = 17 | 7 + 9 = 16 | 6 + 9 = 15 |

To work out the other sums, swap the two addends: $a+b=b+a$. For example, $5+9=9+5=14$.

**Connection.** This lesson uses the number line and the addition within 10 that you already know. The addition facts that bridge through 10 will help you with subtraction that bridges through 10 in later lessons.

## Examples

We start with an addend that can be split as $1$ plus another number.

:::example Work out $9+6$
**Solution.** Split $6=1+5$, so that $9$ plus $1$ makes $10$:
$$9+6=9+1+5=10+5=15.$$
So $9+6=15$.
:::

When the first number is 8, you need 2 more to make 10.

:::example Work out $8+5$
**Solution.** Split $5=2+3$, so that $8$ plus $2$ makes $10$:
$$8+5=8+2+3=10+3=13.$$
So $8+5=13$.
:::

With the number 7, you need 3 more.

:::example Work out $7+6$
**Solution.** Split $6=3+3$, so that $7$ plus $3$ makes $10$:
$$7+6=7+3+3=10+3=13.$$
So $7+6=13$.
:::

If the larger number comes second, swap them to make it easier.

:::example Swapping to calculate faster
Work out $5+9$.

**Solution.** Swap the two addends, putting the larger number first:
$$5+9=9+5=14.$$
So $5+9=14$.
:::

Finally, use the addition facts to solve a problem.

:::example The pencil case (Apply it)
Suppose a pencil case holds 8 red pens and 7 blue pens. How many pens are in the pencil case altogether?

**Understand.** We know there are 8 red pens and 7 blue pens. We are asked how many pens there are.

**Plan.** "Altogether" means putting together, so we add $8+7$, splitting a number to make 10.

**Solve.**

Solution

The number of pens in the pencil case altogether is:
$$8+7=8+2+5=15\ \text{(pens)}.$$
Answer: 15 pens.

**Look back.** Is there another way to split? You can split the 8 to make 10 with the 7: $8=3+5$, so $7+3+5=10+5=15$, which also gives 15.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Splitting a number wrongly.** Wrong: $9+6=9+2+3=14$. Why it is wrong: $2+3=5$, not $6$, so 1 is missing. Right: $6=1+5$, so $9+6=9+1+5=15$.
- **Forgetting to add the remaining part.** Wrong: $8+5=8+2+3=10$. Why it is wrong: after reaching 10 you still have to add 3. Right: $10+3=13$.
- **Forgetting the unit in the answer.** Wrong: "Answer: 15". Right: "Answer: 15 pens".
:::

## Summary

- To add by bridging through 10, split the second addend so that part of it makes 10 with the first addend, then add the rest. For example $9+5=9+1+4=14$.
- The number 9 needs 1 more, 8 needs 2 more and 7 needs 3 more to make 10.
- Swapping the two addends does not change the sum: $a+b=b+a$.
- In the addition facts, when the second addend goes up by 1, the sum goes up by 1.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out, splitting a number to make 10:

a) $9+4$

b) $8+6$

::hint
**What you need:** split the second addend so that the first addend makes 10.

**Steps:**
1. How much does the first addend need to make 10?
2. Add 10 and the remaining part.

::solution
a) $9+4=9+1+3=10+3=13$.

b) $8+6=8+2+4=10+4=14$.

**Answer.** a) 13; b) 14.
:::

:::exercise
Work out in your head:

a) $7+5$

b) $6+8$

c) $9+9$

::hint
**What you need:** split a number, or use the addition facts that bridge through 10.

**Steps:**
1. Part a: $7$ needs 3 more to make 10.
2. Part b: swap to $8+6$ and then split.

::solution
a) $7+5=7+3+2=10+2=12$.

b) $6+8=8+6=8+2+4=14$.

c) $9+9=9+1+8=10+8=18$.

**Answer.** a) 12; b) 14; c) 18.
:::

:::exercise
Work out in your head by swapping the two addends:

a) $4+9$

b) $6+7$

::hint
**What you need:** $a+b=b+a$. Put the larger number first.

**Steps:**
1. Rewrite the addition with the larger number first.
2. Split a number to make 10.

::solution
a) $4+9=9+4=9+1+3=13$.

b) $6+7=7+6=7+3+3=13$.

**Answer.** a) 13; b) 13.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in each box:
$$8+5\ \square\ 14,\qquad 7+6\ \square\ 13,\qquad 9+4\ \square\ 12.$$

::hint
**What you need:** work out each addition first, then compare the two numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $8+5$, $7+6$ and $9+4$.
2. Compare each result with the number on the right.

::solution
We have $8+5=13$ and $13<14$.
We have $7+6=13$ and $13=13$.
We have $9+4=13$ and $13>12$.

**Answer.** $8+5<14$; $7+6=13$; $9+4>12$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose that in the morning Lan folds 6 paper boats and in the afternoon she folds 7. How many paper boats does she fold in the two parts of the day?

::hint
**What you need:** "in the two parts of the day" means putting together: use addition.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. How do we solve it? Which calculation do you use?
3. Check: the sum must be greater than each addend.

::solution
Solution

The number of paper boats Lan folds in the two parts of the day is:
$$6+7=7+6=13\ \text{(boats)}.$$
Answer: 13 boats.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose that in the class library 8 children are reading story books and 9 children are reading science books. How many children are reading altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** "altogether" means putting together: use addition that bridges through 10.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. Split $9=2+7$ so that $8$ plus $2$ makes $10$.
3. Check by swapping: $9+8$.

::solution
Solution

The number of children reading altogether is:
$$8+9=8+2+7=10+7=17\ \text{(children)}.$$

:::

:::exercise Challenge
Among the sums $9+2$, $9+3$, $9+4$, …, $9+9$, which ones have a sum greater than 14 and less than 18?

::hint
**What you need:** when the second addend goes up by 1, the sum goes up by 1.

**Steps:**
1. What is the sum of $9+5$?
2. How does the sum change as the second addend goes up?

::solution
We have $9+5=14$, $9+6=15$, $9+7=16$, $9+8=17$, $9+9=18$. The sums greater than 14 and less than 18 are 15, 16 and 17.
