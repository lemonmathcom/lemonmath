:::goals
- Compare two three-digit numbers by comparing the places from left to right.
- Use the signs $>$, $<$ and $=$ correctly.
- Put three-digit numbers in order, smallest first and largest first.
:::

School A has $428$ pupils. School B has $482$ pupils. Which school has more pupils?

You already know how to write a three-digit number as a sum of hundreds, tens and ones. That is just what you need to compare two of them. The only question is where to look first, and we will see why the answer is the hundreds.

## Let's explore

We write both numbers as sums:
$$428=400+20+8,\qquad 482=400+80+2.$$

Both have $4$ hundreds, so the hundreds cannot settle it. We compare the tens next: $2$ tens is less than $8$ tens. So $428<482$, and School B has more pupils.

The number line shows this too. The number further to the right is greater:

:::theorem How to compare two three-digit numbers
Compare the places from left to right:
1. Compare the hundreds. The number with more hundreds is greater.
2. If the hundreds are equal, compare the tens.
3. If the tens are also equal, compare the ones.
4. If all three places are equal, the numbers are equal.
:::

Why do we start with the hundreds? Because they count for the most. Just $1$ hundred is $10$ tens, which is more than $9$ tens. So once the hundreds differ, the tens and ones that go with them cannot change the answer.

## Examples

First, two numbers that differ in the hundreds. Looking at the hundreds is enough.

:::example Comparing the hundreds
Compare $476$ and $534$.

**Solution.** Hundreds: $4$ and $5$. Since $4<5$, the first number is less, and we do not need the tens or ones:
$$476<534.$$
So $476<534$.
:::

What if the hundreds are the same? Then we move on to the tens.

:::example Comparing the tens
Compare $352$ and $378$.

**Solution.** Hundreds: $3$ and $3$, equal. Tens: $5$ and $7$. Since $5<7$:
$$352<378.$$
So $352<378$.
:::

And if the hundreds and the tens are both the same, we look at the ones.

:::example Comparing the ones
Compare $641$ and $647$.

**Solution.** Hundreds: $6$ and $6$. Tens: $4$ and $4$. Ones: $1$ and $7$. Since $1<7$:
$$641<647.$$
So $641<647$.
:::

With several numbers, we compare place by place in the same way to put them in order.

:::example Putting in order
Put the numbers $325, 235, 253$ in order, smallest first.

**Solution.** Hundreds: $3, 2, 2$. The number $325$ has the most hundreds. The other two have equal hundreds, so compare the tens: $3$ and $5$, so $235<253$:
$$235<253<325.$$
So the order from smallest to largest is $235, 253, 325$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Comparing the ones first.** Wrong: "$349>351$ because $9>1$". Why it is wrong: we always start with the hundreds. The hundreds $3$ and $3$ are equal, so we go on to the tens: $4<5$. Right: $349<351$.
- **Not comparing the next place when the first are equal.** Wrong: "$352=358$ because both start with $35$". Why it is wrong: the first two places match, but the ones are different: $2<8$. Right: $352<358$.
- **Turning the sign the wrong way.** Wrong: $476>534$. Why it is wrong: the first number is the smaller one, and the pointed end faces the smaller number. Right: $476<534$.
:::

## Summary

- Compare place by place from left to right: hundreds, then tens, then ones.
- The first place that differs decides which number is greater.
- The pointed end of $>$ or $<$ faces the smaller number.
- To order smallest first, start with the smallest number.
Back to the opening question: which school has more pupils? Comparing place by place, hundreds first, gives the answer. Remember that the first place that differs decides, and try the practice questions.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need to know and a hint for how to start. Try it yourself first. If an answer is not right, that is fine: it shows you what to look at again. Then open the solution.

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the box.

a) $243\;\square\;351$.

b) $780\;\square\;620$.

c) $505\;\square\;505$.

::hint
**What you need:** compare the hundreds first.

**Steps:**
1. Look at the hundreds digit of both numbers.
2. If they differ, choose the sign straight away.

::solution
a) Compare $243$ and $351$: the hundreds $2<3$, so $243<351$.

b) Compare $780$ and $620$: the hundreds $7>6$, so $780>620$.

c) The numbers $505$ and $505$ have the same hundreds, tens and ones, so $505=505$.

So we write: $243<351$; $780>620$; $505=505$.

**Answer.** a) $<$; b) $>$; c) $=$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the box.

a) $352\;\square\;358$.

b) $910\;\square\;190$.

c) $467\;\square\;427$.

::hint
**What you need:** the first place that differs (from the left) decides.

**Steps:**
1. Compare the hundreds.
2. If equal, compare the tens, then the ones.

::solution
a) Compare $352$ and $358$: the hundreds $3=3$, the tens $5=5$, the ones $2<8$, so $352<358$.

b) Compare $910$ and $190$: the hundreds $9>1$, so $910>190$.

c) Compare $467$ and $427$: the hundreds $4=4$, the tens $6>2$, so $467>427$.

So we write: $352<358$; $910>190$; $467>427$.

**Answer.** a) $<$; b) $>$; c) $>$.
:::

:::exercise
Put the numbers in order.

a) Smallest first: $450, 405, 540, 504$.

b) Largest first: $899, 989, 998$.

::hint
**What you need:** compare the hundreds, then the tens, then the ones.

**Steps:**
1. Pick the smallest (or largest) number first.
2. Pick the rest one at a time.

::solution
a) The hundreds digits of the four numbers are $4$ or $5$.

The numbers with $4$ hundreds are $450$ and $405$. The tens $0<5$, so $405<450$.

The numbers with $5$ hundreds are $540$ and $504$. The tens $0<4$, so $504<540$.

Since $4<5$, we get $405<450<504<540$.

b) The hundreds of $899$, $989$, $998$ are $8$, $9$, $9$. The number $899$ has the fewest hundreds, so $899$ is the smallest.

The numbers $989$ and $998$ have equal hundreds ($9$), so we compare the tens: $8<9$, so $989<998$.

So from largest to smallest we have $998>989>899$.

**Answer.** a) $405, 450, 504, 540$; b) $998, 989, 899$.
:::

:::exercise
Which is the greatest of $376, 367, 673$? Why?

::hint
**What you need:** compare the hundreds first.

**Steps:**
1. Write the hundreds of each number.
2. Choose the number with the most hundreds.

::solution
We compare the hundreds of the three numbers.

The hundreds of $376$, $367$, $673$ are $3$, $3$, $6$.

Since $6>3$, the number $673$ is greater than both of the others.

So the greatest number is $673$, because it has the most hundreds.

**Answer.** $673$.
:::

:::exercise
Nam says: "$349>351$ because $9>1$." What is wrong with what Nam says? Correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** we must compare the hundreds first.

**Steps:**
1. Compare the hundreds of the two numbers.
2. If equal, compare the tens.

::solution
We compare $349$ and $351$ starting from the highest place, the hundreds.

Hundreds: $3=3$.

Tens: $4<5$.

Therefore $349<351$.

Nam is wrong because he compared the ones ($9>1$) first. To compare three-digit numbers, we compare the hundreds first, then the tens, and the ones last.

So Nam is wrong; the correct statement is $349<351$.

**Answer.** $349<351$.
:::

:::exercise
School A has $428$ pupils and school B has $482$ pupils. Which school has more pupils?

::hint
**What you need:** the greater number means more.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? $428$ pupils and $482$ pupils. What is asked? Which school has more.
2. Compare $428$ and $482$.

::solution
Solution

To find which school has more pupils, we compare $428$ and $482$.

The hundreds are equal ($4$ hundreds) and the tens $2<8$, so $428<482$.

So school B ($482$ pupils) has more pupils.

**Answer.** School B has more pupils.

:::exercise Challenge
Find the digit to put in the box so that $475<4\square5<495$.

::hint
**What you need:** the hundreds are both $4$; look at the tens.

**Steps:**
1. The tens digit must be big enough, but not too big; check the equal cases too.
2. Try the digits $7, 8, 9$.

::solution
The number $4\square5$ must be greater than $475$ and less than $495$. These numbers all have $4$ hundreds and $5$ ones, so we try the digits in the tens place.

Try $\square=7$: $475$ is not greater than $475$, so it does not fit.

Try $\square=8$: $485$ is greater than $475$ and less than $495$, so it fits.

Try $\square=9$: $495$ is not less than $495$, so it does not fit.

Digits less than $7$ give numbers less than $475$, so they do not fit either.

Check: $475<485<495$. Correct.

So the right digit is $8$.

**Answer.** $\square=8$.
:::
