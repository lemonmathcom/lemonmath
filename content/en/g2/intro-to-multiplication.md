:::goals
- Understand that multiplication is a short way to write a sum of equal addends.
- Write a sum of equal addends as a multiplication, and the other way round.
- Name the factors and the product in a multiplication.
- Solve word problems with multiplication.
:::

Suppose Mum puts oranges on 4 plates, 3 oranges on each plate. To find how many oranges there are altogether, you could add $3+3+3+3$.

If there were more plates, the addition would get very long. Is there a shorter way to write it? And what are the numbers in that short way called?

## Multiplication

Look at the picture: there are 4 plates, with 3 oranges on each plate.

The number $3$ appears 4 times in the sum. We say that **3 is taken 4 times** and write it briefly as $3\times4$:
$$3+3+3+3=12,\qquad 3\times4=12.$$

:::definition Multiplication
A sum of equal addends can be written as a **multiplication**. If the number $a$ is taken $b$ times, then
$$a+a+\dots+a=a\times b.$$
The sign $\times$ is the **multiplication sign**. The multiplication $3\times4$ is read "3 times 4".
:::

Why can we write it like this? Multiplication is only a short way to write addition: the number before the sign is the addend that is repeated, and the number after the sign says how many times it is taken. (In Vietnam the first number is the one that is repeated, and the second says how many times.)

## Factors and products

In the multiplication $3\times4=12$, each number has a name.

:::definition Factors and product
In the multiplication $3\times4=12$:
- $3$ and $4$ are the **factors**.
- $12$ is the **product**. We also say "the product of 3 and 4 is 12".
:::

**Connection.** In an addition, the addends give the sum. In a multiplication, the factors give the product. The product is simply the sum of the equal addends.

## Examples

We start with writing a sum as a multiplication.

:::example From addition to multiplication
Write the sum $5+5+5$ as a multiplication and work it out.

**Solution.** The number 5 appears 3 times, so 5 is taken 3 times:
$$5+5+5=5\times3=15.$$
So $5\times3=15$.
:::

Now do it the other way round.

:::example From multiplication to addition
Write the multiplication $4\times2$ as a sum of equal addends and work it out.

**Solution.** The number 4 is taken 2 times:
$$4\times2=4+4=8.$$
So $4\times2=8$.
:::

:::example Naming the factors and the product
Name the numbers in the multiplication $2\times6=12$.

**Solution.** $2$ and $6$ are the factors, and $12$ is the product. We also have $2\times6=2+2+2+2+2+2=12$.
:::

:::example Working out a product
Work out $2\times4$.

**Solution.** The number 2 is taken 4 times, so we add:
$$2\times4=2+2+2+2=8.$$
So the product of 2 and 4 is 8.
:::

Finally, use multiplication to solve a word problem.

:::example The pencil cases (Apply it)
Suppose each pencil case holds 5 pens. There are 3 pencil cases like this. How many pens are there altogether?

**Understand.** We know each pencil case holds 5 pens and there are 3 of them. We are asked for the total number of pens.

**Plan.** Each pencil case has the same number of pens, so the number of pens is 5 taken 3 times: we do the multiplication $5\times3$.

**Solve.**

Solution

The number of pens altogether is:
$$5\times3=15\ \text{(pens)}.$$
Answer: 15 pens.

**Look back.** Check with addition: $5+5+5=15$, which is correct. What if there were 4 pencil cases? We work out $5\times4=5+5+5+5=20$, which is 20 pens.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Naming the product wrongly.** Wrong: in $2\times6=12$, the number $12$ is a factor. Why it is wrong: $12$ is the result of the multiplication. Right: $12$ is the product, and $2$ and $6$ are the factors.
- **Writing the wrong number of times.** Wrong: $3+3+3+3=3\times3$. Why it is wrong: the number 3 appears 4 times, not 3 times. Right: $3+3+3+3=3\times4$.
- **Mixing up multiplication and addition.** Wrong: $4\times2=6$. Why it is wrong: $4\times2$ means $4+4$, not $4+2$. Right: $4\times2=8$.
:::

## Key terms

- sum | tổng

:::

## Summary

- A sum of equal addends can be written briefly as a multiplication: $a+a+\dots+a=a\times b$ if $a$ is taken $b$ times.
- For example, $3+3+3+3=3\times4=12$.
- In a multiplication, the numbers being multiplied are called **factors** and the result is called the **product**.
- In a word problem, equal groups call for multiplication. Remember to write the unit.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Write each sum as a multiplication:

a) $2+2+2+2+2$

b) $4+4+4$

c) $5+5$

::hint
**What you need:** the repeated addend is the number before the sign, and the number of times is the number after it.

**Steps:**
1. Count how many times the addend appears.
2. Write the addend times the number of times.

::solution
a) The number 2 appears 5 times: $2+2+2+2+2=2\times5$.

b) The number 4 appears 3 times: $4+4+4=4\times3$.

c) The number 5 appears 2 times: $5+5=5\times2$.

**Answer.** a) $2\times5$; b) $4\times3$; c) $5\times2$.
:::

:::exercise
Write each multiplication as a sum of equal addends and work it out:

a) $3\times2$

b) $2\times6$

c) $5\times4$

::hint
**What you need:** $a\times b$ means $a$ taken $b$ times.

**Steps:**
1. Which number is repeated, and how many times?
2. Add the addends.

::solution
a) $3\times2=3+3=6$.

b) $2\times6=2+2+2+2+2+2=12$.

c) $5\times4=5+5+5+5=20$.

**Answer.** a) 6; b) 12; c) 20.
:::

:::exercise
In the multiplication $4\times3=12$, which numbers are the factors and which is the product?

::hint
**What you need:** the numbers being multiplied are the factors, and the result is the product.

**Steps:**
1. Which number is the result of the multiplication?
2. What are the other two numbers called?

::solution
The numbers $4$ and $3$ are the factors. The number $12$ is the product.

**Answer.** Factors: 4 and 3; product: 12.
:::

:::exercise
Work out:

a) The product of 2 and 8.

b) The product of 5 and 3.

::hint
**What you need:** the product of two numbers is the result of multiplying them.

**Steps:**
1. Write the multiplication.
2. Write it as a sum of equal addends and add.

::solution
a) $2\times8=2+2+2+2+2+2+2+2=16$.

b) $5\times3=5+5+5=15$.

**Answer.** a) 16; b) 15.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose each bicycle has 2 wheels. There are 6 bicycles like this. How many wheels are there altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** each bicycle has the same number of wheels: use multiplication.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. How do we solve it? Which calculation do you use?
3. Check with addition.

::solution
Solution

The number of wheels altogether is:
$$2\times6=12\ \text{(wheels)}.$$
Answer: 12 wheels.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose each child has 3 sweets. There are 5 children like this. How many sweets are there altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** each child has the same number of sweets: use multiplication.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. How many times is 3 taken?
3. Check: does $3+3+3+3+3$ equal your answer?

::solution
Solution

The number of sweets altogether is:
$$3\times5=15\ \text{(sweets)}.$$
Answer: 15 sweets.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the parts of an addition. You have the addition $4+4+4+4=16$.

a) Write it as a multiplication.

b) Name the addends, the sum, the factors and the product.

::hint
**What you need:** an addition has addends and a sum, and a multiplication has factors and a product.

**Steps:**
1. How many times is 4 taken?
2. Which number is the result of both ways of writing it?

::solution
a) The number 4 is taken 4 times, so $4+4+4+4=4\times4=16$.

b) In the addition: the addends are $4$, $4$, $4$ and $4$, and the sum is $16$. In the multiplication $4\times4=16$: the factors are $4$ and $4$, and the product is $16$.

**Answer.** $4\times4=16$; see the naming above.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Write the number 12 as a sum of equal addends in at least three different ways, then write each way as a multiplication.

::hint
**What you need:** if the addend $a$ taken $b$ times gives a sum of 12, then $a\times b=12$.

**Steps:**
1. Try the addends 2, 3, 4 and 6.
2. For each addend, how many times must it be taken to make 12?

::solution
The ways are:
$$6+6=6\times2,\qquad 4+4+4=4\times3,\qquad 3+3+3+3=3\times4,$$
$$2+2+2+2+2+2=2\times6.$$
Each sum is equal to 12.

**Answer.** For example $6\times2$, $4\times3$, $3\times4$ and $2\times6$ all equal 12.
:::
