Suppose each pair of shoes has 2 shoes, and Mai's family has 7 pairs of shoes. Mai also remembers that each hand has 5 fingers, and 6 hands make quite a lot of fingers.

To find how many shoes there are in 7 pairs, or how many fingers there are on 6 hands, is there a quick way that does not mean adding from the start every time?

## The 2 times table

Remember that $2\times3$ means 2 taken 3 times, which is $2+2+2=6$. Build the table by **counting on in 2s**: each time you take one more 2, the product goes up by 2.

| From $2\times1$ to $2\times5$ | From $2\times6$ to $2\times10$ |
|---|---|
| 2 × 1 = 2 | 2 × 6 = 12 |
| 2 × 2 = 4 | 2 × 7 = 14 |
| 2 × 3 = 6 | 2 × 8 = 16 |
| 2 × 4 = 8 | 2 × 9 = 18 |
| 2 × 5 = 10 | 2 × 10 = 20 |

:::definition The 2 times table
In the 2 times table, the first factor is always 2. When the second factor goes up by 1, the product goes up by **2**.
:::

Why is this so? Adding 1 to the second factor means 2 is taken one more time, so the total goes up by 2. For example $2\times6=2\times5+2=10+2=12$.

:::example Work out $2\times6$
**Solution.** We know $2\times5=10$. The second factor goes up by 1, so the product goes up by 2:
$$2\times6=10+2=12.$$
So $2\times6=12$.
:::

:::example Pairs of shoes (Apply it)
Suppose each pair of shoes has 2 shoes. There are 7 pairs like this. How many shoes are there altogether?

**Solution.** Each pair has the same number of shoes, so we multiply:

Solution

The number of shoes altogether is:
$$2\times7=14\ \text{(shoes)}.$$
Answer: 14 shoes.
:::

## The 5 times table

Now **count on in 5s**: each time you take one more 5, the product goes up by 5.

:::

| From $5\times1$ to $5\times5$ | From $5\times6$ to $5\times10$ |
|---|---|
| 5 × 1 = 5 | 5 × 6 = 30 |
| 5 × 2 = 10 | 5 × 7 = 35 |
| 5 × 3 = 15 | 5 × 8 = 40 |
| 5 × 4 = 20 | 5 × 9 = 45 |
| 5 × 5 = 25 | 5 × 10 = 50 |

:::definition The 5 times table
In the 5 times table, the first factor is always 5. When the second factor goes up by 1, the product goes up by **5**. The products all end in 0 or 5.
:::

**Connection.** You have learned that each number on a clock face is 5 minutes after the one before. So the long hand at 3 is $5\times3=15$ minutes, and at 6 it is $5\times6=30$ minutes. The 5 times table helps you read minutes very quickly.

:::example Work out $5\times4$
**Solution.** We know $5\times3=15$. The second factor goes up by 1, so the product goes up by 5:
$$5\times4=15+5=20.$$
So $5\times4=20$.
:::

:::example Hands and fingers (Apply it)
Suppose 6 children each hold up one hand, and each hand has 5 fingers. How many fingers are there altogether?

**Understand.** We know each hand has 5 fingers and there are 6 hands. We are asked for the total number of fingers.

**Plan.** Each hand has the same number of fingers, so the number of fingers is 5 taken 6 times: we do the multiplication $5\times6$.

**Solve.**

Solution

The number of fingers altogether is:
$$5\times6=30\ \text{(fingers)}.$$
Answer: 30 fingers.

**Look back.** Check by counting on in 5s: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, which is exactly 6 counts. What if there were 8 children? We work out $5\times8=40$, which is 40 fingers.
:::

:::example The long hand at 7
The long hand of a clock points to 7. How many minutes have passed?

**Solution.** Each number is 5 minutes after the one before, so the number of minutes is 5 taken 7 times:
$$5\times7=35.$$
So 35 minutes have passed.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting to go up when moving to the next product.** Wrong: $2\times6=10$ because $2\times5=10$. Why it is wrong: $2\times6$ is exactly 2 more than $2\times5$. Right: $2\times6=12$.
- **Mixing up the next product in the 5 times table.** Wrong: $5\times4=25$. Why it is wrong: $5\times4$ is 5 taken 4 times, and 25 is $5\times5$. Right: $5\times4=20$.
- **Forgetting the unit in a word problem.** Wrong: "Answer: 14". Right: "Answer: 14 shoes".
:::

## Summary

- The 2 times table: $2\times1=2$ up to $2\times10=20$. When the second factor goes up by 1, the product goes up by 2.
- The 5 times table: $5\times1=5$ up to $5\times10=50$. When the second factor goes up by 1, the product goes up by 5, and the products end in 0 or 5.
- To find a product in the table, count on in 2s or in 5s.
- The number the long hand of a clock points to, times 5, gives the number of minutes.
