:::goals
- Pair up objects to compare the sizes of two groups.
- Know that "how many more" and "how many fewer" are found by subtraction.
- Solve word problems about how many more or fewer.
- Write the solution and the answer with units.
:::

Suppose class 2A plants flowers in the school playground: 7 pots of roses and 4 pots of daisies. Nam wants to know how many more pots of roses there are than pots of daisies.

Does Nam have to count all the pots again from the start? Is there a way to see straight away how many more there are?

## Let's explore

Pair each pot of daisies with a pot of roses, using each pot only once.

There are 4 pairs. That leaves 3 pots of roses with no partner. So there are 3 more pots of roses than daisies, and 3 fewer pots of daisies than roses.

The number left over is exactly $7-4=3$.

:::definition How many more or fewer
To find how many more there are in one group than in another, **subtract the smaller number from the larger**.
- "A has how many more than B" and "B has how many fewer than A" are **the same subtraction**.
- The result of the subtraction is the **difference**: the number by which the two amounts differ.
:::

Why subtraction? After pairing up, what is left without a partner is the extra amount. If you take away from the larger number a part equal to the smaller number, exactly that extra amount remains.

**Connection.** You met the difference in the last lesson. On a number line, the difference between two numbers is also the number of marks between them.

## Examples

We start with two friends playing marbles.

:::example Ha and Nam play marbles
Ha has 15 marbles and Nam has 9 marbles. Who has more marbles? How many more? Who has fewer, and how many fewer?

**Solution.** We have $15>9$, so Ha has more marbles and Nam has fewer. The difference is:
$$15-9=6\ \text{(marbles)}.$$
So Ha has 6 more marbles than Nam, and Nam has 6 fewer marbles than Ha.
:::

When a question says "fewer", you still subtract the smaller number from the larger.

:::example Oranges and tangerines
Suppose Mum buys 16 oranges and 11 tangerines. How many fewer tangerines than oranges are there?

**Solution.** There are more oranges, so we subtract the number of tangerines from the number of oranges:
$$16-11=5\ \text{(fruits)}.$$
So there are 5 fewer tangerines than oranges.
:::

Now compare two numbers on a number line.

:::example Two marks on a number line
By how much do the numbers 6 and 14 differ? On a number line, how many marks apart are the marks labelled 6 and 14?

**Solution.** The larger number is 14 and the smaller number is 6, so:
$$14-6=8.$$
So 14 is 8 more than 6, and the marks labelled 6 and 14 are 8 marks apart.
:::

Finally, a problem set in a library.

:::example Books in the library (Apply it)
Suppose a library has 38 story books and 25 science books. How many more story books than science books are there?

**Understand.** We know there are 38 story books and 25 science books. We are asked how many more story books there are.

**Plan.** "How many more" means the larger number minus the smaller number.

**Solve.**

Solution

The number of story books is greater than the number of science books by:
$$38-25=13\ \text{(books)}.$$
Answer: 13 books.

**Look back.** Check with addition: $13+25=38$, which is correct. What if the question asked how many fewer science books there are than story books? That is still the subtraction $38-25$, so the answer is still 13 books.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Adding instead of subtracting.** Wrong: Ha has 15 marbles and Nam has 9, so Ha has $15+9=24$ more. Why it is wrong: more and fewer describe the gap between two amounts, so we subtract. Right: $15-9=6$ marbles.
- **Mixing up who has more and who has fewer.** Wrong: Nam has 6 more marbles than Ha. Why it is wrong: Nam has 9, which is fewer than Ha's 15. Right: Nam has 6 fewer marbles than Ha.
- **Forgetting the unit.** Wrong: "Answer: 6". Right: "Answer: 6 marbles".
:::

## Summary

- Pair each object in one group with an object in the other group. What is left without a partner shows how many more or fewer.
- To find how many more or fewer, **subtract the smaller number from the larger**.
- "A has more than B" and "B has fewer than A" give the same number and the same subtraction.
- Remember the unit in the answer.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
There are 9 plates and 6 cups. How many more plates than cups are there? How many fewer cups than plates are there?

::hint
**What you need:** to find how many more or fewer, subtract the smaller number from the larger.

**Steps:**
1. Which number is larger?
2. Are the two questions the same subtraction or two different ones?

::solution
We have $9>6$, so there are more plates than cups. The difference is:
$$9-6=3\ \text{(items)}.$$

**Answer.** There are 3 more plates than cups, and 3 fewer cups than plates.
:::

:::exercise
Fill in the missing numbers.

a) The number 17 is $\square$ more than 9.

b) The number 9 is $\square$ less than 17.

::hint
**What you need:** subtract the smaller number from the larger.

**Steps:**
1. Which number is larger and which is smaller?
2. Do parts a and b have the same answer?

::solution
a) $17-9=8$.

b) $17-9=8$.

**Answer.** a) 8; b) 8.
:::

:::exercise
On a number line, how many marks apart are the marks labelled 5 and 12?

::hint
**What you need:** the number of marks between two numbers is the difference of the two numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Which is the larger number?
2. Subtract the smaller number from the larger.

::solution
$$12-5=7.$$

**Answer.** 7 marks.
:::

:::exercise
Lan has 14 paper flowers and Mai has 8 paper flowers. Who has more flowers? How many more?

::hint
**What you need:** to find how many more, subtract the smaller number from the larger.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. How do we solve it? Which calculation do you use?
3. Check: does $8+6$ equal 14?

::solution
Solution

The number of flowers Lan has more than Mai is:
$$14-8=6\ \text{(flowers)}.$$
Answer: Lan has more, by 6 flowers.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose on the first day a shop sells 36 cakes and on the second day it sells 25 cakes. On which day does it sell more? How many more cakes?

::hint
**What you need:** subtract the smaller number from the larger, and write the unit cakes.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. How do we solve it? Which calculation do you use?
3. Check with addition.

::solution
Solution

We have $36>25$, so more cakes are sold on the first day. The number of extra cakes is:
$$36-25=11\ \text{(cakes)}.$$
Answer: the first day, by 11 cakes.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose the school playground has 28 flame trees and 15 banyan trees. How many fewer banyan trees than flame trees are there?

::hint
**What you need:** "how many fewer" is also the larger number minus the smaller number.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. Which number is larger and which is smaller?
3. Check: does $15+13$ equal 28?

::solution
Solution

The number of banyan trees is fewer than the number of flame trees by:
$$28-15=13\ \text{(trees)}.$$
Answer: 13 trees.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the names of the parts of a subtraction. Nam has 9 marbles and Ha has 15 marbles, so Ha has 6 more marbles than Nam because $15-9=6$. In this subtraction, which number is the minuend, which is the subtrahend and which is the difference?

::hint
**What you need:** in a subtraction, the number at the start is the minuend, the number taken away is the subtrahend, and the result is the difference.

**Steps:**
1. Read the subtraction $15-9=6$ from left to right.
2. Name each number.

::solution
In the subtraction $15-9=6$: $15$ is the minuend, $9$ is the subtrahend and $6$ is the difference.

**Answer.** The minuend is 15, the subtrahend is 9 and the difference is 6.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Lan, Mai and Ha have 9, 14 and 6 paper flowers respectively. How many more flowers does the child with the most flowers have than the child with the fewest?

::hint
**What you need:** find the largest and the smallest numbers, then subtract the smallest from the largest.

**Steps:**
1. Among 9, 14 and 6, which is the largest? Which is the smallest?
2. Work out the difference between those two numbers.

::solution
The largest number is 14 (Mai) and the smallest is 6 (Ha). We have:
$$14-6=8.$$

**Answer.** 8 flowers.
:::
