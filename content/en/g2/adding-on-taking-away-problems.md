Suppose there are 9 oranges in a basket. Mum puts in 4 more, and then the family eats 3. On another day, An has 7 tangerines and Binh has 3 more than An.

When you hear "puts in 4 more", "eats 3" and "3 more than", do you add or subtract? How can you avoid getting mixed up?

## Word problems: adding on and taking away

There are 9 oranges in the basket and Mum puts in 4 more. The number of oranges goes up, so we add: $9+4=13$. Then the family eats 3. The number goes down, so we subtract: $13-3=10$.

:::definition Adding on and taking away
- **Adding on** (put in more, buy more, more arrive, …): the amount goes up, so we **add**.
- **Taking away** (give away, eat, sell, leave, …): the amount goes down, so we **subtract**.
:::

Why is this so? If you add on, you have more than you started with, and to find a bigger amount we put the amounts together. If you take away, you have less than you started with, and to find what is left we take away.

:::example Adding oranges
There are 9 oranges in a basket. Mum puts in 4 more oranges. How many oranges are in the basket altogether?

**Solution.** Mum puts in more, so the number of oranges goes up and we add. Split $4=1+3$ so that $9$ plus $1$ makes $10$:

Solution

The number of oranges in the basket altogether is:
$$9+4=13\ \text{(oranges)}.$$
Answer: 13 oranges.
:::

:::example Giving away exercise books
Nam has 17 exercise books. He gives 5 exercise books to a friend. How many exercise books does Nam have left?

**Solution.** Nam gives some away, so the number of books goes down and we subtract:

Solution

The number of exercise books Nam has left is:
$$17-5=12\ \text{(books)}.$$
Answer: 12 exercise books.
:::

## Word problems: more than and fewer than

Now we compare two people. Ha has 7 marbles. Nam has 4 more marbles than Ha. Nam's number of marbles is Ha's number plus another 4.

:::definition More than, fewer than
You are given one child's amount and told by how many the other child has more or fewer.
- The other child has so many **more**: we **add**.
- The other child has so many **fewer**: we **subtract**.
:::

Why? In the diagram, Nam's segment is Ha's segment plus 4 more parts, so Nam's number is $7+4$. If Nam had 4 fewer marbles than Ha, his segment would be 4 parts shorter than Ha's, so his number would be $7-4$.

**Connection.** In the lesson "How many more or fewer?", you knew both numbers and found the difference. Here it is the other way round: you know one number and the difference, and you find the other number.

:::example More than
Ha has 7 marbles. Nam has 4 more marbles than Ha. How many marbles does Nam have?

**Solution.** Nam has more than Ha, so we add:

Solution

The number of marbles Nam has is:
$$7+4=11\ \text{(marbles)}.$$
Answer: 11 marbles.
:::

:::example Fewer than
Lan has 8 flowers. Mai has 3 fewer flowers than Lan. How many flowers does Mai have?

**Solution.** Mai has fewer than Lan, so we subtract:

Solution

The number of flowers Mai has is:
$$8-3=5\ \text{(flowers)}.$$
Answer: 5 flowers.
:::

:::example The boys in the class (Apply it)
Suppose class 2A has 14 girls. There are 3 more boys than girls. How many boys are in class 2A?

**Understand.** We know there are 14 girls, and that the number of boys is 3 more. We are asked for the number of boys.

**Plan.** "More" means the number of boys is the number of girls plus 3, so we add.

**Solve.**

Solution

The number of boys in class 2A is:
$$14+3=17\ \text{(boys)}.$$
Answer: 17 boys.

**Look back.** The number of boys must be greater than the number of girls, and $17>14$, so it makes sense. What if there were 3 fewer boys than girls? Then we subtract: $14-3=11$ boys.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Seeing "more than" and subtracting.** Wrong: Ha has 7 marbles and Nam has 4 more than Ha, so Nam has $7-4=3$ marbles. Why it is wrong: Nam has more than Ha, so he must have more than 7 marbles. Right: $7+4=11$ marbles.
- **Mixing up adding on and taking away.** Wrong: Nam has 17 exercise books and gives 5 away, so he has $17+5=22$ left. Why it is wrong: giving away makes the number go down. Right: $17-5=12$ books.
- **Forgetting the unit in the answer.** Wrong: "Answer: 11". Right: "Answer: 11 marbles".
:::

## Summary

- In an **adding on** problem we add, and in a **taking away** problem we subtract.
- Given one child's amount, if the other child has so many **more**, add; if so many **fewer**, subtract.
- Read the question carefully: what do we know, what are we asked, then choose the calculation.
- Set out the solution: a sentence, the calculation with its unit, and the answer.

:::exercise
Remember the lesson "How many more or fewer?". An has 12 marbles and Binh has 16 marbles.

a) How many more marbles does Binh have than An?

b) Check: if you add that number to An's marbles, do you get Binh's marbles?

::hint
**What you need:** when you know both numbers, subtract the smaller from the larger to find the difference.

**Steps:**
1. Part a: subtract the smaller number from the larger.
2. Part b: add An's marbles and the answer to part a.

::solution
a) Binh has $16-12=4$ (marbles) more than An.

b) We have $12+4=16$, which is exactly Binh's number of marbles.

**Answer.** a) 4 marbles; b) yes.
:::

:::exercise Challenge

::solution
The number of marbles Nam has is:
