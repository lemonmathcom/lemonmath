## How to use it

1. Choose **Countdown** or **Stopwatch** (counts up from zero).
2. For a countdown, press a quick time (30 seconds, 1, 3, 5, 10 or 15 minutes) or type your own minutes and seconds and press **Set time**.
3. Press **Start**. While it runs you can press **+1 min**, **−30 s** and so on to adjust it without starting again.
4. Press **Full screen** when you project it. When time is up the digits turn red, the alarm rings (if it is on) and the clock keeps counting the extra time, so you can see how far over the class has gone.

Shortcuts while projecting: **Space** start or pause, **R** reset, **F** full screen, **+** and **−** add or take away one minute.

## Ideas for maths lessons

- **A 3-minute starter:** put a mental maths question or a recap question on the board and set 3 minutes. The lesson starts on time without chasing anyone.
- **Think, then pair:** 5 minutes working alone, then 2 minutes comparing with a partner. Short, clear blocks of time help students keep their focus.
- **Group work:** use it with the [Random Group Maker](../random-group-maker/), set 10 or 15 minutes and leave the clock on the screen so the groups can pace themselves.
- **Exam pace:** divide the time of the paper by the number of questions and time each question (a 45-minute test with 30 multiple-choice questions gives about 1 minute 30 seconds each) so students get used to the rhythm.

## Questions teachers ask

**Does it go wrong if I switch to another tab?** No. The timer does not count ticks; it remembers when it started and works from the real clock, so switching tabs, a sleeping laptop or an accidental reload does not change the time.

**Why is there no sound?** Browsers only play sound after you have clicked on the page at least once, and the projector's speaker must be on. Ticking **Ring when time is up** plays a short test sound.

**Will the screen turn off while it counts?** In browsers that support it, the page keeps the screen awake while the timer runs.
