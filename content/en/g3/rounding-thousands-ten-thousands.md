:::goals
- Round a number to the nearest thousand.
- Round a number to the nearest ten thousand.
- Look at the right digit: $5$ or more rounds up, less than $5$ rounds down.
- Use rounding to say "about how many".
:::

The city stadium had $4\,732$ spectators at this afternoon's football match. A reporter wants to write it briefly: "About how many thousand spectators were there?" Help her choose a whole thousand close to $4\,732$.

In the last lesson you learned to compare numbers up to $100\,000$. You also know how to round to the nearest ten and hundred. This lesson uses the same thinking for thousands and ten thousands.

## Let's explore

We place the whole thousands $4\,000$ and $5\,000$ on a number line. The number $4\,732$ lies between them. The point exactly halfway between them is $4\,500$.

:::

Ask yourself: which number is $4\,732$ closer to?

- From $4\,000$ to $4\,732$ is $4\,732-4\,000=732$.
- From $4\,732$ to $5\,000$ is $5\,000-4\,732=268$.
- Since $268<732$, $4\,732$ is closer to $5\,000$.

We say: $4\,732$ rounded to the nearest thousand is $5\,000$, and we write $4\,732\approx5\,000$. The sign $\approx$ is read "is approximately equal to".

Notice: $4\,732$ is greater than the halfway point $4\,500$, so it is closer to $5\,000$. Any number less than $4\,500$ is closer to $4\,000$. The hundreds digit of $4\,500$ is $5$, so the hundreds digit tells you which half a number is in.

:::theorem How to round
**Rounding to the nearest thousand:** look at the hundreds digit.
- If that digit is **less than $5$** ($0$ to $4$): keep the thousands digit the same and change the digits to its right to $0$.
- If that digit is **$5$ or more** ($5$ to $9$): add $1$ to the thousands digit and change the digits to its right to $0$.

**Rounding to the nearest ten thousand:** do the same, but look at the thousands digit.
:::

This works because you choose the nearest whole number of that size. A hundreds digit of $5$ or more means the number is in the upper half, closer to the bigger whole thousand.

## Examples

We start with a number whose hundreds digit is small.

:::example Round $3\,214$ to the nearest thousand
**Solution.** To round to the nearest thousand, look at the hundreds digit. The hundreds digit of $3\,214$ is $2$, less than $5$, so keep $3$ thousands and change the digits to the right to $0$:
$$3\,214\approx3\,000.$$
So $3\,214$ rounded to the nearest thousand is $3\,000$.
:::

Now the hundreds digit is bigger.

:::example Round $6\,750$ to the nearest thousand
**Solution.** The hundreds digit of $6\,750$ is $7$, which is $5$ or more, so the thousands digit goes up from $6$ to $7$ and the digits to the right become $0$:
$$6\,750\approx7\,000.$$
So $6\,750$ rounded to the nearest thousand is $7\,000$.
:::

A five-digit number can be rounded in two different ways.

:::example Round $47\,328$ to the nearest thousand and to the nearest ten thousand
**Solution.** To the nearest thousand: the hundreds digit is $3$, less than $5$, so keep $47$ thousands:
$$47\,328\approx47\,000.$$
To the nearest ten thousand: look at the thousands digit, which is $7$, so $5$ or more. The $4$ ten thousands go up to $5$ ten thousands:
$$47\,328\approx50\,000.$$
So $47\,328$ rounded to the nearest thousand is $47\,000$, and to the nearest ten thousand is $50\,000$.
:::

One case often confuses pupils: the digit that goes up is a $9$.

:::example Round $9\,640$ to the nearest thousand
**Solution.** The hundreds digit of $9\,640$ is $6$, which is $5$ or more, so you add $1$ thousand to $9$ thousands. $9$ thousands and $1$ more thousand make $10$ thousands:
$$9\,640\approx10\,000.$$
So $9\,640$ rounded to the nearest thousand is $10\,000$.
:::

Now use rounding to answer a question from everyday life.

:::example Concert tickets
A concert sold $23\,480$ tickets. A newspaper asks: "About how many thousand tickets were sold?" Answer the question.

**Solution.** "About how many thousand" means rounding to the nearest thousand. The hundreds digit of $23\,480$ is $4$, less than $5$, so:
$$23\,480\approx23\,000.$$
So about $23$ thousand tickets were sold.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Looking at the wrong digit.** Wrong: rounding $4\,372$ to the nearest thousand by looking at the $2$ in the ones place. Why it is wrong: you must look at the place just to the right of the place you are rounding to, which is the hundreds, the digit $3$. Right: $4\,372\approx4\,000$.
- **Forgetting to change the digits on the right to $0$.** Wrong: $6\,750\approx7\,750$. Why it is wrong: a number rounded to the nearest thousand must be a whole thousand. Right: $6\,750\approx7\,000$.
- **Not daring to round a $9$ up.** Wrong: $9\,640\approx9\,000$. Why it is wrong: the hundreds digit is $6$, so you round up, and $9$ thousands plus $1$ thousand is $10$ thousands. Right: $9\,640\approx10\,000$.
:::

## Summary

- To round to the nearest thousand, look at the hundreds digit. Less than $5$: keep the thousands; $5$ or more: add $1$ to the thousands.
- To round to the nearest ten thousand, look at the thousands digit and do the same.
- After rounding, all the digits to the right become $0$.
- Examples: $4\,732\approx5\,000$ (nearest thousand); $47\,328\approx50\,000$ (nearest ten thousand).
- You can check on a number line: the rounded number must be the nearest one of that size.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Round each number to the nearest thousand:

a) $2\,148$.

b) $6\,905$.

::hint
**What you need:** look at the hundreds digit: less than $5$ keeps the thousands, $5$ or more adds $1$ to the thousands.

**Steps:**
1. Find the hundreds digit of the number.
2. Compare it with $5$, then change the digits on the right to $0$.

::solution
a) The hundreds digit of $2\,148$ is $1$, less than $5$, so $2\,148\approx2\,000$.

b) The hundreds digit of $6\,905$ is $9$, which is $5$ or more, so $6\,905\approx7\,000$.

**Answer.** a) $2\,000$; b) $7\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
Round:

a) $4\,372$ to the nearest thousand.

b) $8\,650$ to the nearest thousand.

c) $35\,480$ to the nearest ten thousand.

::hint
**What you need:** to round to the nearest thousand, look at the hundreds; to round to the nearest ten thousand, look at the thousands.

**Steps:**
1. Decide which place you round to and which place you look at.
2. Follow the method from the examples.

::solution
a) The hundreds digit is $3$, less than $5$: $4\,372\approx4\,000$.

b) The hundreds digit is $6$, which is $5$ or more: $8\,650\approx9\,000$.

c) The thousands digit is $5$, which is $5$ or more, so the $3$ ten thousands go up to $4$ ten thousands: $35\,480\approx40\,000$.

**Answer.** a) $4\,000$; b) $9\,000$; c) $40\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson (comparing numbers up to $100\,000$).

a) Write $>$, $<$ or $=$: $45\,802\;\square\;45\,820$.

b) Round the greater of the two numbers in part a) to the nearest thousand.

::hint
**What you need:** compare from the highest place down; the number with the bigger digit first is the greater number.

**Steps:**
1. Compare the places in turn until you find two different digits.
2. Find the greater number, then look at its hundreds digit.

::solution
a) The two numbers have the same ten-thousands, thousands and hundreds digits ($4$, $5$, $8$). In the tens: $0<2$. So $45\,802<45\,820$.

b) The greater number is $45\,820$. Its hundreds digit is $8$, which is $5$ or more, so $45\,820\approx46\,000$.

**Answer.** a) The sign $<$; b) $46\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
Of the numbers $5\,499$, $5\,500$, $6\,499$, $6\,500$, $6\,501$, which round to $6\,000$ to the nearest thousand?

::hint
**What you need:** a number rounds to $6\,000$ if its thousands digit is $5$ and its hundreds digit is $5$ or more, or if its thousands digit is $6$ and its hundreds digit is less than $5$.

**Steps:**
1. Round each number to the nearest thousand.
2. Choose the numbers that give $6\,000$.

::solution
$5\,499\approx5\,000$ (hundreds digit $4$). $5\,500\approx6\,000$ (hundreds digit $5$). $6\,499\approx6\,000$ (hundreds digit $4$). $6\,500\approx7\,000$ (hundreds digit $5$). $6\,501\approx7\,000$ (hundreds digit $5$).

**Answer.** $5\,500$ and $6\,499$.
:::

:::exercise
Nam's house is $7\,480$ m from the market. Nam says: "About $7\,000$ m." Lan says: "About $7\,500$ m."

a) Who rounded to the nearest thousand? Why?

b) To which place did the other pupil round?

::hint
**What you need:** rounding to the nearest thousand gives a whole thousand.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Round $7\,480$ to the nearest thousand and compare with what the two pupils said.

::solution
a) The hundreds digit of $7\,480$ is $4$, less than $5$, so $7\,480\approx7\,000$. So Nam rounded to the nearest thousand.

b) $7\,500$ is the whole hundred nearest to $7\,480$ ($80$ rounds up to $100$), so Lan rounded to the nearest hundred.

**Answer.** a) Nam; b) the nearest hundred.
:::

:::exercise
Find the mistake in each pupil's work and correct it.

a) Ha: "Rounding $3\,700$ to the nearest thousand: the hundreds digit is $7$, so I keep the thousands and get $3\,000$."

b) Tu: "Rounding $27\,450$ to the nearest ten thousand: I look at the hundreds digit, $4$, which is less than $5$, so I get $27\,000$."

::hint
**What you need:** to round to a place, look at the place just to its right; the result has only $0$s in the places to the right.

**Steps:**
1. Ha: is $7$ compared with $5$ a reason to round up or down?
2. Tu: to round to the nearest ten thousand, which place do you look at?

::solution
a) The hundreds digit is $7$, which is $5$ or more, so you must round up: $3\,700\approx4\,000$.

b) To round to the nearest ten thousand you look at the thousands digit, which is $7$, so $5$ or more, and $27\,450\approx30\,000$. Tu looked at the wrong place, and $27\,000$ is not a whole ten thousand.

**Answer.** a) $4\,000$; b) $30\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
The table shows the number of spectators at three football matches.

| Match | Spectators |
|---|---|
| Match 1 | $12\,380$ |
| Match 2 | $15\,720$ |
| Match 3 | $9\,560$ |

a) Round the number of spectators at each match to the nearest thousand.

b) Using the rounded numbers, which match had the most spectators?

::hint
**What you need:** to round to the nearest thousand, look at the hundreds digit.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Round each number in the table.
3. Compare the three rounded numbers.

::solution
a) $12\,380\approx12\,000$ (hundreds digit $3$). $15\,720\approx16\,000$ (hundreds digit $7$). $9\,560\approx10\,000$ (hundreds digit $5$).

b) Compare $12\,000$, $16\,000$, $10\,000$: the greatest is $16\,000$, for match $2$.

**Answer.** a) $12\,000$; $16\,000$; $10\,000$. b) Match $2$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A four-digit number has $4$ as its thousands digit. Rounded to the nearest thousand, it becomes $5\,000$.

a) What can its hundreds digit be?

b) What are the least and the greatest such numbers?

::hint
**What you need:** to go up from $4$ thousands to $5$ thousands, the hundreds digit must be $5$ or more.

**Steps:**
1. What condition must the hundreds digit meet?
2. Least number: choose the smallest possible digits. Greatest number: choose the biggest possible digits.

::solution
a) The hundreds digit must be $5$ or more, so it is $5$, $6$, $7$, $8$ or $9$.

b) Least number: hundreds digit $5$ and the other places $0$, giving $4\,500$. Greatest number: hundreds digit $9$ and the other places $9$, giving $4\,999$.

**Check.** $4\,500\approx5\,000$ and $4\,999\approx5\,000$.

**Answer.** a) $5$, $6$, $7$, $8$, $9$; b) $4\,500$ and $4\,999$.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
$6\,284$ rounded to the nearest thousand is
A. $6\,000$.
B. $7\,000$.
C. $6\,300$.
D. $6\,280$.
::key A
The hundreds digit is $2$, less than $5$, so keep $6$ thousands: $6\,000$. Option B always rounds up; options C and D round to the nearest hundred and the nearest ten.

::mc
$38\,700$ rounded to the nearest ten thousand is
A. $30\,000$.
B. $39\,000$.
C. $40\,000$.
D. $38\,000$.
::key C
The thousands digit is $8$, which is $5$ or more, so the $3$ ten thousands go up to $4$ ten thousands: $40\,000$. Option A always rounds down; option B rounds to the nearest thousand, and option D drops the end of the number without rounding.

::mc
Which number rounds to $9\,000$ to the nearest thousand?
A. $8\,490$.
B. $9\,500$.
C. $9\,600$.
D. $9\,400$.
::key D
$9\,400$ has hundreds digit $4$, so it rounds down to $9\,000$. $8\,490\approx8\,000$, while $9\,500$ and $9\,600$ round up to $10\,000$.

::mc
A town has $7\,960$ people. Rounded to the nearest thousand, the town has about
A. $7\,000$ people.
B. $8\,000$ people.
C. $7\,960$ people.
D. $10\,000$ people.
::key B
The hundreds digit is $9$, which is $5$ or more, so $7\,960\approx8\,000$. Option D rounds to the nearest ten thousand, not the nearest thousand.

::tf
Look at the number $24\,673$.
a) Rounded to the nearest thousand, it is $25\,000$.
b) Rounded to the nearest ten thousand, it is $20\,000$.
c) Rounded to the nearest ten thousand, it is $30\,000$.
d) To round to the nearest thousand, you look at the hundreds digit.
::key T T F T
a) True: the hundreds digit is $6$, so round up. b) True: the thousands digit is $4$, less than $5$, so $24\,673\approx20\,000$. c) False: the thousands digit is $4$, so you do not round up. d) True: look at the place just to the right of the thousands.

::sa
Round $5\,600$ to the nearest thousand. Write the answer (without spaces).
::key 6000
The hundreds digit is $6$, which is $5$ or more, so $5\,600\approx6\,000$.

::sa
A stadium has $15\,280$ seats. Rounded to the nearest thousand, about how many thousand seats does it have?
::key 15
The hundreds digit is $2$, less than $5$, so $15\,280\approx15\,000$, which is about $15$ thousand seats.
:::
