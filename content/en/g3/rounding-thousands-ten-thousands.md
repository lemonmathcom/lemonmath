:::goals
- Round a number to the nearest thousand.
- Round a number to the nearest ten thousand.
- Look at the right digit: $5$ or more rounds up, less than $5$ rounds down.
- Use rounding to say "about how many".
:::

This afternoon, $4\,732$ people watched the football match at the city stadium. A reporter wants to write one short line: "About how many thousand spectators were there?" Readers only need a rough idea, not an exact head count. Help her choose a whole thousand close to $4\,732$.

In the last lesson you compared numbers up to $100\,000$, and you have already rounded to the nearest ten and hundred. This time we use the very same idea for thousands and ten thousands. Then you can say "about so many thousand" without reading out every digit.

## Let's explore

To see which whole thousand $4\,732$ is nearest to, we put it on a number line. The whole thousands on either side are $4\,000$ and $5\,000$. The number $4\,732$ lies between them, and the point exactly halfway is $4\,500$.

:::graph The number $4\,732$ lies between $4\,000$ and $5\,000$
alt: A number line from 3800 to 5200 with the points 4000, 4500, 4732 and 5000 marked; 4732 is closer to 5000

y: -1..1
segment: (4000, 0) (5000, 0)

point: (4500, 0) "4500" above

point: (5000, 0) "5000" below
:::

Here is the question: on this line, which whole thousand is $4\,732$ closer to? We can find out by measuring the gaps.

- Going from $4\,000$ up to $4\,732$ is a gap of $4\,732-4\,000=732$.
- Going from $4\,732$ up to $5\,000$ is a gap of $5\,000-4\,732=268$.
- Since $268<732$, $4\,732$ is closer to $5\,000$.
The shorter gap points to the nearer whole thousand.

So we say $4\,732$ rounded to the nearest thousand is $5\,000$, and we write $4\,732\approx5\,000$. The sign $\approx$ is read "is approximately equal to". It reminds us that the number is no longer exact, but the nearest round one.

There is a quicker way than measuring each gap. The number $4\,732$ is greater than the halfway point $4\,500$, so it sits in the half nearer $5\,000$. Any number less than $4\,500$ sits in the half nearer $4\,000$. The hundreds digit of $4\,500$ is $5$, so you only need to look at the hundreds digit to know which half a number is in.

:::theorem How to round
**Rounding to the nearest thousand:** look at the hundreds digit.
- If that digit is **less than $5$** ($0$ to $4$): keep the thousands digit the same and change the digits to its right to $0$.
- If that digit is **$5$ or more** ($5$ to $9$): add $1$ to the thousands digit and change the digits to its right to $0$.

**Rounding to the nearest ten thousand:** do the same, but look at the thousands digit.
:::

Why does this work? Rounding means choosing the nearest whole number of that size. A hundreds digit of $5$ or more puts the number in the upper half, so the bigger whole thousand is the nearer one. A smaller hundreds digit puts it in the lower half.

## Examples

We start with a number whose hundreds digit is small, so the thousands stay the same.

:::example Round $3\,214$ to the nearest thousand
**Solution.** To round to the nearest thousand, look at the hundreds digit. The hundreds digit of $3\,214$ is $2$, less than $5$, so keep $3$ thousands and change the digits to the right to $0$:
$$3\,214\approx3\,000.$$
So $3\,214$ rounded to the nearest thousand is $3\,000$.
:::

Now a number whose hundreds digit is bigger. This time the thousands go up.

:::example Round $6\,750$ to the nearest thousand
**Solution.** The hundreds digit of $6\,750$ is $7$, which is $5$ or more, so the thousands digit goes up from $6$ to $7$ and the digits to the right become $0$:
$$6\,750\approx7\,000.$$
So $6\,750$ rounded to the nearest thousand is $7\,000$.
:::

A five-digit number can be rounded in two ways: to the nearest thousand or to the nearest ten thousand. Watch how the answer changes with the place.

:::example Round $47\,328$ to the nearest thousand and to the nearest ten thousand
**Solution.** To the nearest thousand: the hundreds digit is $3$, less than $5$, so keep $47$ thousands:
$$47\,328\approx47\,000.$$
To the nearest ten thousand: look at the thousands digit, which is $7$, so $5$ or more. The $4$ ten thousands go up to $5$ ten thousands:
$$47\,328\approx50\,000.$$
So $47\,328$ rounded to the nearest thousand is $47\,000$, and to the nearest ten thousand is $50\,000$.
:::

Here is a spot where many pupils hesitate: the digit that has to go up is a $9$. Let us see what happens.

:::example Round $9\,640$ to the nearest thousand
**Solution.** The hundreds digit of $9\,640$ is $6$, which is $5$ or more, so you add $1$ thousand to $9$ thousands. $9$ thousands and $1$ more thousand make $10$ thousands:
$$9\,640\approx10\,000.$$
So $9\,640$ rounded to the nearest thousand is $10\,000$.
:::

Rounding is most useful when we want to say "about how many". Try it on a number from real life.

:::example Concert tickets
A concert sold $23\,480$ tickets. A newspaper asks: "About how many thousand tickets were sold?" Answer the question.

**Solution.** "About how many thousand" means rounding to the nearest thousand. The hundreds digit of $23\,480$ is $4$, less than $5$, so:
$$23\,480\approx23\,000.$$
So about $23$ thousand tickets were sold.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Looking at the wrong digit.** Wrong: rounding $4\,372$ to the nearest thousand by looking at the $2$ in the ones place. Why it is wrong: the digit to check sits just to the right of the place you are rounding to, which is the hundreds, the digit $3$. Right: $4\,372\approx4\,000$.
- **Forgetting to change the digits on the right to $0$.** Wrong: $6\,750\approx7\,750$. Why it is wrong: rounding to the nearest thousand must give a whole thousand. Right: $6\,750\approx7\,000$.
- **Not daring to round a $9$ up.** Wrong: $9\,640\approx9\,000$. Why it is wrong: the hundreds digit is $6$, so you round up, and $9$ thousands plus $1$ thousand is $10$ thousands. Right: $9\,640\approx10\,000$.
Do not worry if you have made one of these slips. Meeting it once is how you learn to avoid it.
:::

## Summary

- To round to the nearest thousand, look at the hundreds digit. Less than $5$: keep the thousands; $5$ or more: add $1$ to the thousands.
- To round to the nearest ten thousand, look at the thousands digit and do the same.
- After rounding, all the digits to the right become $0$.
- Examples: $4\,732\approx5\,000$ (nearest thousand); $47\,328\approx50\,000$ (nearest ten thousand).
- You can check on a number line: the rounded number must be the nearest one of that size.
Back to the reporter's question: the first example already answers it, since $4\,732\approx5\,000$ gives her a whole thousand to write. The one thing to remember is to look at the digit just to the right. The practice below will make it feel natural.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need to remember and a hint to start you off. Try it yourself first, and open the solution only when you are stuck. A slip along the way is fine, because that is how the method sticks.

:::exercise
Round each number to the nearest thousand:

a) $2\,148$.

b) $6\,905$.

::hint
**What you need:** look at the hundreds digit: less than $5$ keeps the thousands, $5$ or more adds $1$ to the thousands.

**Steps:**
1. Find the hundreds digit of the number.
2. Compare it with $5$, then change the digits on the right to $0$.

::solution
When rounding to the nearest thousand, we look at the digit just to the right, that is the hundreds digit. If it is less than $5$, we keep the thousands digit; if it is $5$ or more, we increase the thousands digit by $1$. All the places to the right become $0$.

a) The number $2\,148$ has hundreds digit $1$, which is less than $5$, so we keep $2$ thousands. Hence $2\,148\approx2\,000$.

b) The number $6\,905$ has hundreds digit $9$, which is $5$ or more, so we increase $6$ thousands to $7$ thousands. Hence $6\,905\approx7\,000$.

So $2\,148$ rounded to the nearest thousand is $2\,000$ and $6\,905$ rounded to the nearest thousand is $7\,000$.

**Answer.** a) $2\,000$; b) $7\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
Round:

a) $4\,372$ to the nearest thousand.

b) $8\,650$ to the nearest thousand.

c) $35\,480$ to the nearest ten thousand.

::hint
**What you need:** to round to the nearest thousand, look at the hundreds; to round to the nearest ten thousand, look at the thousands.

**Steps:**
1. Decide which place you round to and which place you look at.
2. Follow the method from the examples.

::solution
To round to a given place, we look at the digit just to the right of that place: if it is less than $5$ we round down, and if it is $5$ or more we round up.

a) Rounding $4\,372$ to the nearest thousand: the hundreds digit is $3$, less than $5$, so we keep $4$ thousands. Hence $4\,372\approx4\,000$.

b) Rounding $8\,650$ to the nearest thousand: the hundreds digit is $6$, which is $5$ or more, so we increase $8$ thousands to $9$ thousands. Hence $8\,650\approx9\,000$.

c) Rounding $35\,480$ to the nearest ten thousand: we look at the thousands digit, which is $5$, so $5$ or more, and the $3$ ten thousands go up to $4$ ten thousands. Hence $35\,480\approx40\,000$.

So $4\,372\approx4\,000$; $8\,650\approx9\,000$; $35\,480\approx40\,000$.

**Answer.** a) $4\,000$; b) $9\,000$; c) $40\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson (comparing numbers up to $100\,000$).

a) Write $>$, $<$ or $=$: $45\,802\;\square\;45\,820$.

b) Round the greater of the two numbers in part a) to the nearest thousand.

::hint
**What you need:** compare from the highest place down; the number with the bigger digit first is the greater number.

**Steps:**
1. Compare the places in turn until you find two different digits.
2. Find the greater number, then look at its hundreds digit.

::solution
a) The two numbers have the same ten-thousands, thousands and hundreds digits ($4$, $5$, $8$). In the tens: $0<2$. So $45\,802<45\,820$.

b) The greater number is $45\,820$. Its hundreds digit is $8$, which is $5$ or more, so $45\,820\approx46\,000$.

**Answer.** a) The sign $<$; b) $46\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
Of the numbers $5\,499$, $5\,500$, $6\,499$, $6\,500$, $6\,501$, which round to $6\,000$ to the nearest thousand?

::hint
**What you need:** a number rounds to $6\,000$ if its thousands digit is $5$ and its hundreds digit is $5$ or more, or if its thousands digit is $6$ and its hundreds digit is less than $5$.

**Steps:**
1. Round each number to the nearest thousand.
2. Choose the numbers that give $6\,000$.

::solution
To round a number to the nearest thousand, we look at the hundreds digit. We round each number:

- $5\,499$: the hundreds digit is $4$, less than $5$, so $5\,499\approx5\,000$.
- $5\,500$: the hundreds digit is $5$, which is $5$ or more, so $5\,500\approx6\,000$.
- $6\,499$: the hundreds digit is $4$, less than $5$, so $6\,499\approx6\,000$.
- $6\,500$: the hundreds digit is $5$, which is $5$ or more, so $6\,500\approx7\,000$.
- $6\,501$: the hundreds digit is $5$, which is $5$ or more, so $6\,501\approx7\,000$.

Among the given numbers, only $5\,500$ and $6\,499$ round to $6\,000$.

So the numbers that round to $6\,000$ to the nearest thousand are $5\,500$ and $6\,499$.

**Answer.** $5\,500$ and $6\,499$.
:::

:::exercise
Nam's house is $7\,480$ m from the market. Nam says: "About $7\,000$ m." Lan says: "About $7\,500$ m."

a) Who rounded to the nearest thousand? Why?

b) To which place did the other pupil round?

::hint
**What you need:** rounding to the nearest thousand gives a whole thousand.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Round $7\,480$ to the nearest thousand and compare with what the two pupils said.

::solution
a) The hundreds digit of $7\,480$ is $4$, less than $5$, so $7\,480\approx7\,000$. So Nam rounded to the nearest thousand.

b) $7\,500$ is the whole hundred nearest to $7\,480$ ($80$ rounds up to $100$), so Lan rounded to the nearest hundred.

**Answer.** a) Nam; b) the nearest hundred.
:::

:::exercise
Find the mistake in each pupil's work and correct it.

a) Ha: "Rounding $3\,700$ to the nearest thousand: the hundreds digit is $7$, so I keep the thousands and get $3\,000$."

b) Tu: "Rounding $27\,450$ to the nearest ten thousand: I look at the hundreds digit, $4$, which is less than $5$, so I get $27\,000$."

::hint
**What you need:** to round to a place, look at the place just to its right; the result has only $0$s in the places to the right.

**Steps:**
1. Ha: is $7$ compared with $5$ a reason to round up or down?
2. Tu: to round to the nearest ten thousand, which place do you look at?

::solution
a) Ha is wrong. To round $3\,700$ to the nearest thousand we look at the hundreds digit. The hundreds digit is $7$, which is $5$ or more, so we must round up, that is increase $3$ thousands to $4$ thousands, not keep it. The correct work is
$$3\,700\approx4\,000.$$

b) Tu is wrong. To round $27\,450$ to the nearest ten thousand we must look at the thousands digit, not the hundreds digit. The thousands digit is $7$, which is $5$ or more, so we increase $2$ ten thousands to $3$ ten thousands. The correct work is
$$27\,450\approx30\,000.$$
Tu's result $27\,000$ is also not a whole number of ten thousands.

So Ha kept the thousands when the number had to be rounded up, and the correct result is $4\,000$; Tu looked at the wrong place, and the correct result is $30\,000$.

**Answer.** a) $4\,000$; b) $30\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
The table shows the number of spectators at three football matches.

| Match | Spectators |
|---|---|
| Match 1 | $12\,380$ |
| Match 2 | $15\,720$ |
| Match 3 | $9\,560$ |

a) Round the number of spectators at each match to the nearest thousand.

b) Using the rounded numbers, which match had the most spectators?

::hint
**What you need:** to round to the nearest thousand, look at the hundreds digit.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Round each number in the table.
3. Compare the three rounded numbers.

::solution
a) We round the number of spectators at each match to the nearest thousand by looking at the hundreds digit.

Match $1$: $12\,380$ has hundreds digit $3$, less than $5$, so $12\,380\approx12\,000$.

Match $2$: $15\,720$ has hundreds digit $7$, which is $5$ or more, so $15\,720\approx16\,000$.

Match $3$: $9\,560$ has hundreds digit $5$, which is $5$ or more, so $9\,560\approx10\,000$.

b) We compare the rounded numbers: $12\,000$, $16\,000$, $10\,000$. Since $10\,000<12\,000<16\,000$, the greatest is $16\,000$, for match $2$.

So the rounded numbers of spectators are $12\,000$, $16\,000$, $10\,000$; match $2$ had the most spectators.

**Answer.** a) $12\,000$; $16\,000$; $10\,000$. b) Match $2$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A four-digit number has $4$ as its thousands digit. Rounded to the nearest thousand, it becomes $5\,000$.

a) What can its hundreds digit be?

b) What are the least and the greatest such numbers?

::hint
**What you need:** to go up from $4$ thousands to $5$ thousands, the hundreds digit must be $5$ or more.

**Steps:**
1. What condition must the hundreds digit meet?
2. Least number: choose the smallest possible digits. Greatest number: choose the biggest possible digits.

::solution
The given number has four digits and thousands digit $4$. Rounding to the nearest thousand gives $5\,000$, which means we must round up from $4$ thousands to $5$ thousands.

a) To round up, the hundreds digit must be $5$ or more. So the hundreds digit can be $5$, $6$, $7$, $8$ or $9$.

b) Least number: take the smallest hundreds digit $5$ and the other places $0$, giving $4\,500$. Greatest number: take the largest hundreds digit $9$ and the other places $9$, giving $4\,999$.

Check: $4\,500$ has hundreds digit $5$, so $4\,500\approx5\,000$; $4\,999$ has hundreds digit $9$, so $4\,999\approx5\,000$.

So the hundreds digit can be $5$, $6$, $7$, $8$, $9$; the least number is $4\,500$ and the greatest number is $4\,999$.

**Answer.** a) $5$, $6$, $7$, $8$, $9$; b) $4\,500$ and $4\,999$.
:::

::mc TH

A. $6\,000$.
B. $7\,000$.
C. $6\,300$.
D. $6\,280$.
::key A
The hundreds digit is $2$, less than $5$, so keep $6$ thousands: $6\,000$. Option B always rounds up; options C and D round to the nearest hundred and the nearest ten.

::mc TH
$38\,700$ rounded to the nearest ten thousand is
A. $30\,000$.
B. $39\,000$.
C. $40\,000$.
D. $38\,000$.
::key C
The thousands digit is $8$, which is $5$ or more, so the $3$ ten thousands go up to $4$ ten thousands: $40\,000$. Option A always rounds down; option B rounds to the nearest thousand, and option D drops the end of the number without rounding.

::key D
$9\,400$ has hundreds digit $4$, so it rounds down to $9\,000$. $8\,490\approx8\,000$, while $9\,500$ and $9\,600$ round up to $10\,000$.
