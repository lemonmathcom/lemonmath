- Tell exact division from division with a remainder.
- Know that the remainder is always less than the divisor, and check by multiplying and adding the remainder.
- Divide a two-digit number by a one-digit number, for example $72\div3$.
- Solve word problems with a remainder and understand what the remainder tells you.

A shop has $17$ biscuits to pack into boxes, $5$ biscuits in each box. How many full boxes can it pack, and how many biscuits are left over? Does this division come out exactly?

Some divisions come out exactly and some leave a little over. This lesson shows you how to tell the two apart and how to divide larger numbers.

## Exact division and division with remainders

### Let's explore

You put $12$ biscuits into boxes, $4$ in each box. You fill exactly $3$ boxes with none left over, because $4\times 3=12$:
$$12\div4=3.$$
This is an **exact division**.

Now you share $17$ biscuits, $5$ in each box. We have $5\times 3=15$, so you fill $3$ boxes, and $17-15=2$ biscuits are left over. You write:
$$17\div5=3\ (\text{remainder }2).$$
This is a **division with a remainder**, and $2$ is the **remainder**. Check: $5\times 3+2=17$. Correct.

:::theorem Exact division and division with a remainder
- An exact division is a division with remainder $0$.
- In a division with a remainder, **the remainder is always less than the divisor**.
- To check: quotient $\times$ divisor $+$ remainder $=$ dividend.
:::

Why must the remainder be less than the divisor? If the remainder were equal to or greater than the divisor, you could still fill at least one more box, so the division would not be finished. For example, $5$ biscuits left over with $5$ biscuits to a box means one more box can be filled.

### Examples

We start with an exact division.

:::example Work out $18\div3$
**Solution.** Remember the $3$ times table: $3\times 6=18$, so
$$18\div3=6.$$
The remainder is $0$, so this is an exact division.
:::

Now a division with a remainder.

:::example Work out $23\div5$
**Solution.** Find the largest number that $5$ can be multiplied by without going over $23$. We have $5\times 4=20$ and $23-20=3$, so
$$23\div5=4\ (\text{remainder }3).$$
The remainder $3$ is less than the divisor $5$. Check: $5\times 4+3=23$.
:::

The remainder tells you how much is left over.

:::example Packing exercise books into bags
A teacher has $34$ exercise books to put into bags, $8$ books in each bag. How many full bags can she fill, and how many books are left over?

**Solution.** Divide the number of books by the number of books in each bag. We have $8\times 4=32$ and $34-32=2$, so
$$34\div8=4\ (\text{remainder }2).$$
**Answer:** $4$ full bags, with $2$ exercise books left over.
:::

## Dividing a two-digit number by a one-digit number

### Let's explore

You share $72$ storybooks equally among $3$ children. Divide place by place, from left to right:

- Tens: $7$ tens divided by $3$ is $2$ tens, because $3\times 2=6$; $7-6=1$ ten is left over.
- Exchange the $1$ leftover ten for $10$ ones, then bring down the $2$ ones to make $12$ ones.
- Ones: $12$ divided by $3$ is $4$, with remainder $0$.

So
$$72\div3=24.$$
Check: $24\times 3=72$. Correct.

:::theorem Dividing a two-digit number by a one-digit number
To divide a two-digit number by a one-digit number:
1. Divide the tens first, and find the remainder of the tens.
2. Bring down the ones digit, join it to the remainder to make a new number, and keep dividing.
3. The final remainder must be less than the divisor; check by multiplying and adding the remainder.
:::

Why start with the tens? Because the tens are the larger part, so divide them first and then the ones. The leftover tens are exchanged for ones, like swapping one large note for several small ones so you can keep sharing.

### Examples

We start with an exact division.

:::example Work out $84\div4$
**Solution.** Tens: $8$ tens divided by $4$ is $2$ tens, remainder $0$. Bring down $4$ ones: $4$ divided by $4$ is $1$.

So $84\div4=21$. Check: $21\times 4=84$.
:::

Sometimes there is a remainder.

:::example Work out $59\div4$
**Solution.** Tens: $5$ tens divided by $4$ is $1$ ten, with $1$ ten left over. Exchange $1$ ten for $10$ ones and bring down $9$ to make $19$ ones. $19$ divided by $4$ is $4$, because $4\times 4=16$, with remainder $19-16=3$.

So
$$59\div4=14\ (\text{remainder }3).$$
The remainder $3$ is less than the divisor $4$. Check: $14\times 4+3=59$.
:::

The next problem is a little harder, so we use four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example Standing in rows
Class $3$A has $47$ pupils standing in rows of $6$. What is the least number of rows needed for every pupil to be in a row?

**Understand.** The class has $47$ pupils, and each row has $6$ pupils. We are asked for the least number of rows so that no pupil is left out.

**Plan.** Divide $47$ by $6$ to find how many full rows there are and how many pupils are left.

**Solve.** We have $6\times 7=42$ and $47-42=5$, so
$$47\div6=7\ (\text{remainder }5).$$
There are $7$ full rows and $5$ pupils have no place. These $5$ pupils need $1$ more row, so we need $7+1=8$ rows.

**Answer:** $8$ rows.

**Look back.** Check: $6\times 7+5=47$. With only $7$ rows we could seat $42$ pupils, leaving $5$ without a place, so $8$ rows makes sense.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **A remainder equal to or greater than the divisor.** Wrong: $17\div5=2$ (remainder $7$). Why it is wrong: $7>5$, so you can still divide $1$ more time. Right: $17\div5=3$ (remainder $2$).
- **Forgetting to bring down the ones digit.** Wrong: $84\div4=2$. Why it is wrong: only $8$ tens were divided and the $4$ ones were never brought down. Right: $84\div4=21$.
- **Ignoring the remainder in a word problem.** Wrong: $47$ pupils, $6$ in a row, "we need $7$ rows". Why it is wrong: $7$ rows seat only $42$ pupils, leaving $5$ without a place. Right: we need $8$ rows.
:::

**Connection.** Division is the opposite of multiplication, and you check a division by multiplying and adding the remainder. In the next lesson you will divide three-digit numbers by one-digit numbers in the same way.

## Summary

- An exact division has remainder $0$; a division with a remainder has a remainder greater than $0$ that is always less than the divisor.
- Check: quotient $\times$ divisor $+$ remainder $=$ dividend.
- To divide a two-digit number: divide the tens first, bring down the ones digit, then keep dividing.
- In a word problem, think about what the remainder means: something left over, or one more group needed.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out and say whether each division is exact or has a remainder: a) $24\div6$; b) $26\div6$.

::hint
**What you need:** use the $6$ times table to find the quotient and the remainder.

**Steps:**
1. What is $6\times 4$?

::solution
a) $6\times 4=24$, so $24\div6=4$. This is an exact division.

b) $6\times 4=24$ and $26-24=2$, so $26\div6=4$ (remainder $2$). This is a division with a remainder.

**Answer.** a) $4$, exact; b) $4$ remainder $2$, with a remainder.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $31\div7$ and check.

::hint
**What you need:** find the largest number that $7$ can be multiplied by without going over $31$.

**Steps:**
1. What is $7\times 4$? What is $7\times 5$?

::solution
$7\times 4=28$ and $31-28=3$, so $31\div7=4$ (remainder $3$).

Check: $7\times 4+3=31$. Correct.

**Answer.** $4$ remainder $3$.
:::

:::exercise
When you divide a number by $8$, what is the largest possible remainder? Why?

::hint
**What you need:** the remainder is always less than the divisor.

**Steps:**
1. The remainder must be less than what number?

::solution
The remainder must be less than $8$, so it can be $0$, $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, $5$, $6$ or $7$. The largest remainder is $7$.

**Answer.** $7$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on making a number several times smaller. Make $96$ become $3$ times smaller.

::hint
**What you need:** to make a number $n$ times smaller, divide it by $n$.

**Steps:**
1. Divide $9$ tens by $3$, then bring down $6$ ones.

::solution
$9$ tens divided by $3$ is $3$ tens, remainder $0$. Bring down $6$: $6$ divided by $3$ is $2$. So
$$96\div3=32.$$
Check: $32\times 3=96$.

**Answer.** $32$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $85\div5$.

::hint
**What you need:** if the tens do not divide exactly, exchange the remainder for ones and bring down the next digit.

**Steps:**
1. $8$ tens divided by $5$ is how many tens, with how many tens left over?

::solution
$8$ tens divided by $5$ is $1$ ten, with $3$ tens left over. Exchange $3$ tens for $30$ ones and bring down $5$ to make $35$. $35$ divided by $5$ is $7$. So
$$85\div5=17.$$
Check: $17\times 5=85$.

**Answer.** $17$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $73\div6$ and check.

::hint
**What you need:** this division has a remainder, and the remainder must be less than $6$.

**Steps:**
1. $7$ tens divided by $6$ is $1$ ten, with $1$ ten left over; exchange it and bring down $3$.

::solution
Bring down $3$ to make $13$ ones. $13$ divided by $6$ is $2$, because $6\times 2=12$, with remainder $1$. So
$$73\div6=12\ (\text{remainder }1).$$
Check: $12\times 6+1=73$. Correct.

**Answer.** $12$ remainder $1$.
:::

:::exercise
There are $50$ oranges to pack into bags, $6$ oranges in each bag. a) How many full bags can be filled, and how many oranges are left over? b) What is the least number of bags needed to hold all the oranges?

::hint
**What you need:** the remainder tells you how many oranges are left over; those oranges still need a bag.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Divide $50$ by $6$, then think about how many more bags the remainder needs.

::solution
a) We have $6\times 8=48$ and $50-48=2$, so
$$50\div6=8\ (\text{remainder }2).$$
We can fill $8$ full bags, with $2$ oranges left over.

b) The $2$ leftover oranges need $1$ more bag, so we need at least $8+1=9$ bags.

**Answer.** a) $8$ full bags, $2$ oranges left over; b) $9$ bags.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A division has divisor $7$, quotient $9$ and remainder $5$. Find the dividend.

::hint
**What you need:** dividend $=$ quotient $\times$ divisor $+$ remainder.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $9\times 7$, then add the remainder.

::solution
The dividend is:
$$9\times 7+5=63+5=68.$$
Check: $68\div7=9$ (remainder $5$), because $7\times 9=63$ and $68-63=5$. Correct.

**Answer.** $68$.
:::

::mc

A. $3$ remainder $8$.
B. $4$ remainder $3$.
C. $5$ remainder $3$.
D. $18$.
::key B
$5\times 4=20$ and $23-20=3$, so $23\div5=4$ (remainder $3$). B has a remainder greater than the divisor, C rounds up, D is $23-5$.

A. $2$.
B. $20$.
C. $21$.
D. $12$.
::key C

::key D
$59\div4=14$ (remainder $3$), because $14\times 4=56$ and $59-56=3$. A and B have remainders that are not less than the divisor, C ignores the remainder.
