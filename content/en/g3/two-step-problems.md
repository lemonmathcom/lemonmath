:::goals
- Recognise when a problem needs two steps.
- Find the result of the first step and use it in the second step.
- Solve word problems in two steps using addition, subtraction, multiplication and division.
- Set out the solution neatly with an answer and its unit, and check it.
:::

Mum buys $3$ bags of ordinary rice, $5$ kg in each bag, and $8$ kg of sticky rice. How many kilograms of rice does she buy altogether?

The problem only gives the number of bags, the kilograms in each bag and the sticky rice. You do not yet know how much ordinary rice Mum buys, so you cannot add straight away. This lesson shows you what to do in a situation like this.

## Let's explore

To find the total, you first need the amount of ordinary rice. You work in two steps:

- **Step 1:** The ordinary rice Mum buys is $5\times 3=15$ (kg).
- **Step 2:** The rice Mum buys altogether is $15+8=23$ (kg).

So Mum buys $23$ kg of rice altogether. The result of step $1$ is the number $15$, and you use it in step $2$.

:::theorem Solving a problem in two steps
To solve a problem in two steps:
1. Read the problem carefully and decide what you know and what you are asked.
2. Work out which number you need first, and do the first step to find it.
3. Use the result of the first step to do the second step and find the answer.
4. Write the answer with its unit and check it.
:::

Why two steps? Because the final question needs a number that the problem does not give. You must find that number first, just as to cross two bridges you must cross the first one first.

## Examples

We start with a problem that uses addition and subtraction.

:::example Oranges in a shop
A shop has $25$ oranges, gets $18$ more, then sells $30$. How many oranges are left in the shop?

**Solution.** First find the number of oranges after the shop gets more.

The number of oranges after getting more is:
$$25+18=43\ \text{(oranges)}.$$
The number of oranges left is:
$$43-30=13\ \text{(oranges)}.$$
**Answer:** $13$ oranges.
:::

Now a problem that multiplies and then adds.

:::example Boxes of pens
Each box holds $8$ pens. A teacher has $4$ boxes of blue pens and $6$ loose red pens. How many pens does she have altogether?

**Solution.** First find the number of blue pens.

The number of blue pens is:
$$8\times 4=32\ \text{(pens)}.$$
The number of pens she has altogether is:
$$32+6=38\ \text{(pens)}.$$
**Answer:** $38$ pens.
:::

Some problems divide in the first step.

:::example Sharing exercise books
A teacher has $48$ exercise books to share equally among $6$ children. Each child has already used $3$ books. How many books does each child have left?

**Solution.** First find how many books each child gets.

The number of books each child gets is:
$$48\div6=8\ \text{(books)}.$$
The number of books each child has left is:
$$8-3=5\ \text{(books)}.$$
**Answer:** $5$ books.
:::

There are also problems that multiply and then subtract.

:::example Bottles of water
A shop has $5$ crates of water with $12$ bottles in each crate. It has sold $37$ bottles. How many bottles of water are left?

**Solution.** First find the total number of bottles at the start.

The number of bottles at the start is:
$$12\times 5=60\ \text{(bottles)}.$$
The number of bottles left is:
$$60-37=23\ \text{(bottles)}.$$
**Answer:** $23$ bottles of water.
:::

The next problem is a little harder, so we use four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example Hens and ducks
Lan's family keeps $8$ hens. They keep $3$ times as many ducks as hens. How many hens and ducks do they keep altogether?

**Understand.** Lan's family has $8$ hens, and the number of ducks is $3$ times the number of hens. We are asked for the total number of hens and ducks.

**Plan.** We do not know the number of ducks, so step one is to find it by multiplying. Step two is to add the hens and the ducks.

**Solve.** The number of ducks is:
$$8\times 3=24\ \text{(ducks)}.$$
The number of hens and ducks altogether is:
$$8+24=32\ \text{(birds)}.$$
**Answer:** $32$ birds.

**Look back.** The hens are $1$ part and the ducks are $3$ parts, so there are $4$ equal parts altogether, each of $8$ birds: $8\times 4=32$. The result matches the first method. Can you think why the two methods give the same number?
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Doing only one step.** Wrong: Mum buys $3$ bags of rice with $5$ kg in each, and $8$ kg of sticky rice: $5+8=13$ kg. Why it is wrong: the $3$ bags were forgotten. Right: $5\times 3+8=23$ kg.
- **Not using the result of the first step.** Wrong: $48\div6=8$, then $48-3=45$. Why it is wrong: the second step must use the $8$ just found. Right: $8-3=5$.
- **Wrong unit in the first step.** Wrong: "$12\times 5=60$ (crates)". Why it is wrong: $60$ is the number of bottles, not crates. Right: "$12\times 5=60$ (bottles)".
:::

**Connection.** This lesson reuses the four operations and the earlier lessons on making numbers several times larger and smaller. Later you will meet problems with more steps, and the method is still to find each number you need first.

## Summary

- A two-step problem needs a number that is not given, found first, and then used to find the answer.
- Before calculating, ask: "Which number do I need first?"
- The second step must use the result of the first step.
- Write the answer with its unit, and check that the result is sensible.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
A tank holds $36$ litres of oil. Another $25$ litres are poured in, then $40$ litres are used. How many litres of oil are left in the tank?

::hint
**What you need:** first find the litres of oil after pouring in more.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Add first, then subtract.

::solution
The number of litres after pouring in more is:
$$36+25=61\ \text{(litres)}.$$
The number of litres left is:
$$61-40=21\ \text{(litres)}.$$
**Answer.** $21$ litres of oil.
:::

:::exercise
A teacher buys $4$ boxes of pens with $6$ pens in each box, and $5$ more loose pens. How many pens does she buy altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** the pens in the boxes are found by multiplying.

**Steps:**
1. Find the number of pens in $4$ boxes.
2. Add the loose pens.

::solution
The number of pens in $4$ boxes is:
$$6\times 4=24\ \text{(pens)}.$$
The number of pens she buys altogether is:
$$24+5=29\ \text{(pens)}.$$
**Answer.** $29$ pens.
:::

:::exercise
$36$ oranges are shared equally onto $4$ plates, then each plate gets $2$ more oranges. How many oranges are on each plate now?

::hint
**What you need:** the number of oranges on each plate at first is found by dividing.

**Steps:**
1. Divide $36$ by $4$.
2. Add $2$.

::solution
The number of oranges on each plate at first is:
$$36\div4=9\ \text{(oranges)}.$$
The number of oranges on each plate now is:
$$9+2=11\ \text{(oranges)}.$$
**Answer.** $11$ oranges.
:::

:::exercise
A garden has $7$ rows of trees with $8$ trees in each row. A farmer has watered $20$ trees. How many trees have not been watered?

::hint
**What you need:** first find the total number of trees in the garden.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply the number of rows by the trees in each row.
2. Subtract the trees already watered.

::solution
The number of trees in the garden is:
$$8\times 7=56\ \text{(trees)}.$$
The number of trees not watered is:
$$56-20=36\ \text{(trees)}.$$
**Answer.** $36$ trees.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on making a number several times smaller. Nam has $56$ marbles. Hoa has Nam's number made $7$ times smaller. How many fewer marbles does Hoa have than Nam?

::hint
**What you need:** making $7$ times smaller means dividing by $7$.

**Steps:**
1. Find Hoa's number of marbles.
2. "How many fewer" is a subtraction.

::solution
The number of marbles Hoa has is:
$$56\div7=8\ \text{(marbles)}.$$
The number of marbles Hoa has fewer than Nam is:
$$56-8=48\ \text{(marbles)}.$$
**Answer.** $48$ marbles.
:::

:::exercise
$38$ pupils go on a trip by coach, and each coach carries at most $7$ pupils. What is the least number of coaches needed to carry all the pupils?

::hint
**What you need:** remember division with a remainder: the leftover pupils still need one more coach.

**Steps:**
1. Divide $38$ by $7$.
2. How many more coaches do the leftover pupils need?

::solution
We have $38\div7=5$ (remainder $3$). There are $5$ full coaches and $3$ pupils have no place, so we need $1$ more coach.

The least number of coaches is:
$$5+1=6\ \text{(coaches)}.$$
**Answer.** $6$ coaches.
:::

:::exercise
A sack holds $50$ kg of rice. A seller has sold $3$ bags of $8$ kg each. How many kilograms of rice are left in the sack?

::hint
**What you need:** first find how much rice has been sold.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply the number of bags by the kilograms in each bag.
2. Subtract the rice sold from the rice at the start.

::solution
The number of kilograms of rice sold is:
$$8\times 3=24\ \text{(kg)}.$$
The number of kilograms of rice left is:
$$50-24=26\ \text{(kg)}.$$
**Answer.** $26$ kg of rice.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Lan has $5$ bags of sweets with $6$ sweets in each bag. She gives Mai $12$ sweets, then shares the rest equally among $3$ other friends. How many sweets does each friend get?

::hint
**What you need:** this problem needs three steps, but the method is still to find each number you need first.

**Steps:**
1. Find the total number of Lan's sweets.
2. Take away the sweets given to Mai, then share among $3$ friends.

::solution
The number of sweets Lan has is:
$$6\times 5=30\ \text{(sweets)}.$$
The number of sweets left after giving some to Mai is:
$$30-12=18\ \text{(sweets)}.$$
The number of sweets each friend gets is:
$$18\div3=6\ \text{(sweets)}.$$
**Answer.** $6$ sweets.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
Mum buys $3$ bags of rice with $5$ kg in each bag, and $8$ kg of sticky rice. She buys altogether
A. $23$ kg.
B. $16$ kg.
C. $13$ kg.
D. $120$ kg.
::key A
The rice in $3$ bags is $5\times 3=15$ (kg), then $15+8=23$ (kg). A adds $3+5+8$, C forgets there are $3$ bags, D multiplies all the numbers.

::mc
A teacher has $48$ exercise books to share equally among $6$ children. Each child has used $3$ books. Each child has left
A. $45$ books.
B. $5$ books.
C. $51$ books.
D. $8$ books.
::key B
Each child gets $48\div6=8$ books, then $8-3=5$ books. A does not divide first, D stops after the first step, C adds instead of subtracting.

::mc
A shop has $5$ crates with $12$ bottles of water in each, and has sold $37$ bottles. How many are left?
A. $97$ bottles.
B. $60$ bottles.
C. $23$ bottles.
D. $49$ bottles.
::key C
The total is $12\times 5=60$, then $60-37=23$. B stops after the first step, A adds instead of subtracting, D works out $12+37$.

::mc
Lan's family keeps $8$ hens. They keep $3$ times as many ducks as hens. They keep altogether
A. $24$ birds.
B. $11$ birds.
C. $16$ birds.
D. $32$ birds.
::key D
The ducks number $8\times 3=24$, then $8+24=32$. A counts only the ducks, B works out $8+3$, C takes the hens from the ducks.

::tf
Look at this problem: $36$ oranges are shared equally onto $4$ plates, then each plate gets $2$ more oranges. How many oranges are on each plate now?
a) Step one is $36\div4=9$.
b) Step two is $9-2=7$.
c) Each plate has $38$ oranges.
d) The answer is $11$ oranges.
::key T F F T
a) True: each plate first has $9$ oranges. b) False: oranges are added, so $9+2=11$. c) False: $38$ comes from wrongly adding $36+2$. d) True: $9+2=11$ oranges.

::sa
A shop has $5$ crates of milk with $12$ cartons in each, and has sold $25$ cartons. How many cartons of milk are left?
::key 35
$12\times 5=60$ (cartons), then $60-25=35$ (cartons).

::sa
Lan has $6$ bags of sweets with $4$ sweets in each, and gives a friend $9$ sweets. How many sweets does Lan have left?
::key 15
$4\times 6=24$ (sweets), then $24-9=15$ (sweets).
:::
