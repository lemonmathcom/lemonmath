:::goals
- Tell the time in hours and minutes, including saying "minutes to".
- Change hours to minutes: $1$ hour $=60$ minutes.
- Work out how long it is between two times.
- State the number of days in each month and in a year, and find the number of days in several months in a row.
:::

Nam's class (made-up numbers) starts lessons at $30$ minutes past $7$. This morning Nam arrives at school at $15$ minutes past $7$. How many minutes early is Nam?

To answer, you need to read a clock with confidence and know how to find how long it is between two times. After that you will meet larger units of time: months and years.

## Telling the time

### Let's explore

A clock face has $12$ numbers. The short hand shows the hour and the long hand shows the minutes. Look at the clock when Nam arrives at school.

The short hand has passed $7$, so the hour is $7$. The long hand points at $3$. The long hand takes $5$ minutes to move from one number to the next, so at $3$ it is $3\times5=15$ minutes.

So the clock shows $15$ minutes past $7$. Lessons start at $30$ minutes past $7$. From $15$ minutes past $7$ to $30$ minutes past $7$ is $30-15=15$ minutes, so Nam is $15$ minutes early.

### How to read a clock

:::theorem Telling the time on a clock
1. The short hand shows the hours and the long hand shows the minutes.
2. If the long hand points at $n$, the clock shows $n\times5$ minutes. If it points at $12$, it is an exact hour.
3. $1$ hour $=60$ minutes.
:::

Why are the numbers $5$ minutes apart? Because the long hand makes one full turn through the $12$ numbers in $1$ hour, and $12\times5=60$ minutes.

When the long hand has passed $6$, you can also say the time with "to": $50$ minutes past $8$ is $10$ minutes to $9$, because from $50$ minutes past $8$ to $9$ o'clock there are $60-50=10$ minutes.

:::example What time does the clock show?
What time does the clock in the picture show? Read it in two ways.

**Solution.** The short hand is between $4$ and $5$, and it has passed $4$, so the hour is $4$. The long hand points at $7$, and $7\times5=35$ minutes.

So the clock shows $35$ minutes past $4$. From $35$ minutes past $4$ to $5$ o'clock there are $60-35=25$ minutes, so we can also say $25$ minutes to $5$.
:::

Now find how long it is between two times.

:::example A film
A film (made-up numbers) is shown from $45$ minutes past $8$ to $20$ minutes past $10$. How long is the film?

**Solution.** We split the time into three parts: from $45$ minutes past $8$ to $9$ o'clock is $60-45=15$ minutes; from $9$ o'clock to $10$ o'clock is $1$ hour; from $10$ o'clock to $20$ minutes past $10$ is $20$ minutes.

The minutes add up to $15+20=35$ minutes.

**Answer.** $1$ hour $35$ minutes.
:::

Finally, a time problem that needs some thinking. We work in four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example The first lesson
The first lesson of Nam's class (made-up numbers) starts at $30$ minutes past $7$ and lasts $35$ minutes. At what time does the lesson end?

**Understand.** We are given the start time and the length of the lesson. We are asked for the end time.

**Plan.** The end time is the start time plus the length of the lesson. We add the minutes first, then change to hours if there are $60$ minutes or more.

**Solve.**

Solution

The minutes at the end are $30+35=65$ minutes, which is $1$ hour $5$ minutes.

The lesson ends at $7$ o'clock $+$ $1$ hour $5$ minutes, which is $5$ minutes past $8$.

Answer: $5$ minutes past $8$.

**Look back.** Check with the inverse operation: from $30$ minutes past $7$ to $5$ minutes past $8$ is $30+5=35$ minutes. Correct. If the lesson lasted $45$ minutes, we would have $30+45=75$ minutes, which is $1$ hour $15$ minutes, and the lesson would end at $15$ minutes past $8$.
:::

## Months and years

A year has $12$ months. The months do not all have the same number of days. Use this table to remember them:

| Number of days | Months |
|:-:|:--|
| $31$ days | months $1$, $3$, $5$, $7$, $8$, $10$, $12$ (January, March, May, July, August, October, December) |
| $30$ days | months $4$, $6$, $9$, $11$ (April, June, September, November) |
| $28$ or $29$ days | month $2$ (February) |

:::note Leap years
Month $2$ has $28$ days, but in some years month $2$ has $29$ days. Such a year is called a leap year. A year usually has $365$ days, and a leap year has $366$ days.
:::

Use the table to answer questions about the number of days.

:::example Months with $30$ days
Which of the months $3$, $6$, $8$, $11$ have $30$ days?

**Solution.** From the table, month $3$ and month $8$ have $31$ days; month $6$ and month $11$ have $30$ days.

So month $6$ (June) and month $11$ (November) have $30$ days.
:::

We add to find the number of days in several months in a row.

:::example The summer holiday
The summer holiday of a school (made-up numbers) starts on day $1$ of month $6$ (June) and ends at the end of day $31$ of month $8$ (August). How many days is the summer holiday?

**Solution.** Month $6$ has $30$ days, month $7$ has $31$ days, month $8$ has $31$ days. The number of days of the holiday is:
$$30+31+31=92\ \text{(days)}.$$
**Answer.** $92$ days.
:::

**Connection.** When you work with time, you use all four operations: addition and subtraction to find how long it is between two times, multiplication to change to minutes ($n\times5$) and division to change days into weeks. These are operations you have learned in earlier lessons.

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Reading the long hand at $3$ as $3$ minutes.** Wrong: the long hand at $3$ is $3$ minutes. Why it is wrong: the numbers are $5$ minutes apart. Right: the long hand at $3$ is $3\times5=15$ minutes.
- **Saying "minutes to" with the wrong hour.** Wrong: $50$ minutes past $8$ is $10$ minutes to $8$. Why it is wrong: after $50$ minutes past $8$, there are $10$ minutes more before it is $9$ o'clock. Right: $50$ minutes past $8$ is $10$ minutes to $9$.
- **Subtracting times the wrong way.** Wrong: from $40$ minutes past $7$ to $15$ minutes past $9$ is $2$ hours $25$ minutes. Why it is wrong: $9-7=2$ and $40-15=25$ were worked out, but $15$ minutes cannot have $40$ minutes taken away. Right: $1$ hour $35$ minutes.
:::

## Summary

- The short hand shows the hours and the long hand shows the minutes. If the long hand points at $n$, it is $n\times5$ minutes; $1$ hour $=60$ minutes.
- "Minutes to": $50$ minutes past $8$ is $10$ minutes to $9$, because $60-50=10$.
- To find how long it is between two times, split it into parts (to the next hour, then the whole hours, then the extra minutes) and add them up.
- A year has $12$ months: months with $31$ days, with $30$ days, and month $2$ with $28$ or $29$ days. A year has $365$ days, and a leap year has $366$ days.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
The short hand of a clock is between $5$ and $6$, and the long hand points at $6$. What time does the clock show?

::hint
**What you need:** if the long hand points at $n$, it is $n\times5$ minutes; the short hand shows the hour of the last number it has passed.

**Steps:**
1. Which number has the short hand passed?
2. Work out the minutes from the number the long hand points at.

::solution
The short hand has passed $5$, so the hour is $5$. The long hand points at $6$, and $6\times5=30$ minutes.

**Answer.** $30$ minutes past $5$.
:::

:::exercise
The short hand of a clock is close to $8$ but has not reached $8$, and the long hand points at $9$. What time does the clock show? Say it using "to".

::hint
**What you need:** the short hand has not reached $8$, so it is not yet $8$ o'clock.

**Steps:**
1. What hour and how many minutes does the clock show?
2. How many minutes are there until $8$ o'clock?

::solution
The short hand has not reached $8$, so the hour is $7$. The long hand points at $9$, and $9\times5=45$ minutes. The clock shows $45$ minutes past $7$. There are $60-45=15$ minutes until $8$ o'clock.

**Answer.** $45$ minutes past $7$, which is $15$ minutes to $8$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $40$ minutes past $6$ using "to".

::hint
**What you need:** from $40$ minutes past $6$ to the next hour there are $60-40$ minutes.

**Steps:**
1. What is the next hour?
2. How many minutes are there until that hour?

::solution
The next hour is $7$ o'clock. There are $60-40=20$ minutes until then.

**Answer.** $20$ minutes to $7$.
:::

:::exercise
Change: $1$ hour $25$ minutes $=\ ?$ minutes.

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ hour $=60$ minutes.

**Steps:**
1. Change $1$ hour to minutes.
2. Add $25$ minutes.

::solution
We have $1$ hour $=60$ minutes. So $1$ hour $25$ minutes $=60+25=85$ minutes.

**Answer.** $85$ minutes.
:::

:::exercise
A film (made-up numbers) is shown from $10$ minutes past $9$ to $55$ minutes past $10$. How long is the film?

::hint
**What you need:** split the time into parts that are easy to work out.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. How long is it from $10$ minutes past $9$ to $10$ minutes past $10$? How many more minutes to $55$ minutes past $10$?
3. Check by working in minutes.

::solution
Solution

From $10$ minutes past $9$ to $10$ minutes past $10$ is $1$ hour. From $10$ minutes past $10$ to $55$ minutes past $10$ is $55-10=45$ minutes.

The film is $1$ hour $45$ minutes long.

**Check.** $1$ hour $45$ minutes $=60+45=105$ minutes. Correct.

**Answer.** $1$ hour $45$ minutes.
:::

:::exercise
Which of the months $4$, $7$, $9$, $12$ have $31$ days?

::hint
**What you need:** use the table of the number of days in each month.

**Steps:**
1. Find each month in the table.
2. Choose the months with $31$ days.

::solution
Month $4$ and month $9$ have $30$ days. Month $7$ and month $12$ have $31$ days.

**Answer.** Month $7$ and month $12$.
:::

:::exercise
The first term of a school (made-up numbers) starts on day $1$ of month $9$ and ends at the end of day $31$ of month $12$. How many days is the first term?

::hint
**What you need:** add the days in months $9$, $10$, $11$, $12$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Find the days in each month from the table.
3. Add them up.

::solution
Solution

Month $9$ has $30$ days, month $10$ has $31$ days, month $11$ has $30$ days, month $12$ has $31$ days. The number of days is:
$$30+31+30+31=122\ \text{(days)}.$$

**Answer.** $122$ days.
:::

:::exercise
Every day Mai practises the piano for $35$ minutes. How many hours and minutes does Mai practise altogether in $7$ days?

::hint
**What you need:** multiply the minutes each day by the number of days, then change to hours.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the total minutes: $35\times7$.
2. Change to hours and minutes: $60$ minutes $=1$ hour.

::solution
Solution

The minutes Mai practises in $7$ days are:
$$35\times7=245\ \text{(minutes)}.$$
We have $245=4\times60+5$, so $245$ minutes $=4$ hours $5$ minutes.

**Answer.** $4$ hours $5$ minutes.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on dividing by a one-digit number. A year usually has $365$ days, and a week has $7$ days. How many weeks is $365$ days, and how many days are left over?

::hint
**What you need:** divide $365$ by $7$; the quotient is the number of weeks and the remainder is the days left over.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $365\div7$.
2. Check: quotient $\times7+$ remainder.

::solution
We have $365\div7=52$ (remainder $1$).

**Check.** $52\times7=364$ and $364+1=365$. Correct.

**Answer.** $52$ weeks and $1$ day.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Day $1$ of month $3$ (March) is a Monday. What day of the week is day $1$ of month $4$ (April) in the same year?

::hint
**What you need:** month $3$ has $31$ days; the days of the week repeat every $7$ days.

**Steps:**
1. How many days are there from day $1$ of month $3$ to day $1$ of month $4$?
2. Divide that number of days by $7$ and look at the remainder.

::solution
From day $1$ of month $3$ to day $1$ of month $4$ there are $31$ days. We have $31\div7=4$ (remainder $3$). The days of the week repeat every $7$ days, so we count $3$ more days after Monday: Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday.

**Answer.** Thursday.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
The short hand of a clock is between $2$ and $3$, and the long hand points at $8$. The clock shows
A. $8$ minutes past $2$.
B. $40$ minutes past $3$.
C. $10$ minutes past $8$.
D. $40$ minutes past $2$.
::key D
The short hand has passed $2$, so the hour is $2$. The long hand points at $8$, and $8\times5=40$ minutes. Option A reads the $8$ as $8$ minutes, option B gets the hour wrong, and option C swaps the two hands.

::mc
$10$ minutes to $8$ is
A. $50$ minutes past $7$.
B. $10$ minutes past $7$.
C. $50$ minutes past $8$.
D. $10$ minutes past $8$.
::key A
There are $10$ minutes still to go until $8$ o'clock, so it is $50$ minutes past $7$. Options D and C move on to the hour after $8$, and option B uses $10$ minutes instead of $60-10=50$ minutes.

::mc
Which of these months has exactly $30$ days?
A. Month $8$.
B. Month $9$.
C. Month $10$.
D. Month $12$.
::key B
Month $9$ has $30$ days. Months $8$, $10$ and $12$ all have $31$ days.

::mc
A film is shown from $40$ minutes past $7$ to $15$ minutes past $9$. The film is
A. $2$ hours $25$ minutes long.
B. $1$ hour $25$ minutes long.
C. $1$ hour $35$ minutes long.
D. $2$ hours $55$ minutes long.
::key C
From $40$ minutes past $7$ to $40$ minutes past $8$ is $1$ hour, and to $15$ minutes past $9$ is $35$ more minutes. Option A takes $40-15$ and $9-7$, option B subtracts the minutes wrongly, and option D adds $40+15$.

::tf
Look at these statements.
a) $1$ hour $=60$ minutes.
b) Month $2$ always has $29$ days.
c) $45$ minutes past $7$ is $15$ minutes to $8$.
d) Month $4$ has $31$ days.
::key T F T F
a) True: $1$ hour $=60$ minutes. b) False: month $2$ has $28$ days, and only a leap year has $29$. c) True: there are $60-45=15$ minutes until $8$ o'clock. d) False: month $4$ has $30$ days.

::sa
How many days are there in the first three months (month $1$, month $2$, month $3$) of a year that is not a leap year?
::key 90
Month $1$ has $31$ days, month $2$ has $28$ days, month $3$ has $31$ days. We have $31+28+31=90$ (days).

::sa
How many minutes are in $1$ hour $20$ minutes?
::key 80
$1$ hour $=60$ minutes, so $1$ hour $20$ minutes $=60+20=80$ minutes.
