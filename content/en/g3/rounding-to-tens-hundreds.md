:::goals
- Round numbers to the nearest ten and to the nearest hundred.
- Look at the digit to the right to decide whether to round up or down.
- Use the sign $\approx$ to write a rounded value.
- Use rounding to estimate the answer to a calculation.
:::

A librarian counts $238$ books in the storeroom. When she reports to the school, she says "about $240$ books". How did she get from $238$ to $240$? If she had said "about $200$ books", which place would she have rounded to?

You do not always need an exact number. Often an approximate number, a round ten or a round hundred, is enough and easier to remember. This lesson teaches you how to round.

## Let's explore

Look at the number $34$. The number $34$ lies between the two round tens $30$ and $40$. See which one $34$ is closer to:

- $34$ is $4$ away from $30$.
- $34$ is $6$ away from $40$.

Since $4<6$, $34$ is closer to $30$. You say: rounding $34$ to the nearest ten gives $30$, and write $34\approx 30$.

:::graph On the number line: 34 lies between 30 and 40, closer to 30
alt: A number line from 30 to 40 with the three points 30, 34 and 40 marked; the point 34 is closer to 30 than to 40
x: 30..40
y: -1..1

point: (30, 0) "30" below

:::

In the same way, $36$ is $6$ away from $30$ and $4$ away from $40$, so $36$ is closer to $40$: $36\approx 40$. And $35$ is exactly halfway between $30$ and $40$ ($5$ each way). By agreement, we round up: $35\approx 40$.

:::theorem Rounding numbers
- **Rounding to the nearest ten:** look at the ones digit. If it is $0$, $1$, $2$, $3$ or $4$, keep the tens digit (round down). If it is $5$, $6$, $7$, $8$ or $9$, increase the tens digit by $1$ (round up). The ones digit of the rounded value is $0$.
- **Rounding to the nearest hundred:** look at the tens digit. If it is $0$ to $4$, round down; if it is $5$ to $9$, round up. The tens and ones digits of the rounded value are both $0$.
:::

Why look at the digit to the right? Because that digit tells you whether your number is in the near half or the far half of the gap between two round numbers. The rounded value is the nearest round number.

## Examples

We start with rounding down to the nearest ten.

:::example Round $82$ to the nearest ten
**Solution.** The ones digit of $82$ is $2$, which is less than $5$, so round down: keep $8$ tens.

So $82\approx 80$.
:::

Now rounding up.

:::example Round $47$ to the nearest ten
**Solution.** The ones digit of $47$ is $7$, which is greater than $5$, so round up: $4$ tens becomes $5$ tens.

So $47\approx 50$.
:::

The digit $5$ also rounds up.

:::example Round $365$ to the nearest ten
**Solution.** The ones digit of $365$ is $5$, so round up: $36$ tens becomes $37$ tens.

So $365\approx 370$.
:::

For the nearest hundred, look at the tens digit.

:::example Rounding to the nearest hundred
Round $438$ and $750$ to the nearest hundred.

**Solution.** The tens digit of $438$ is $3$, which is less than $5$, so round down: $438\approx 400$.

The tens digit of $750$ is $5$, so round up: $750\approx 800$.
:::

Now use rounding to estimate.

:::example The librarian's report
The storeroom has $238$ books. Round the number of books to the nearest ten and to the nearest hundred.

**Solution.** To the nearest ten: the ones digit is $8$, so round up, $238\approx 240$.

To the nearest hundred: the tens digit is $3$, so round down, $238\approx 200$.

So the librarian can say "about $240$ books" or "about $200$ books".
:::

The next problem is a little harder, so we use four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example Estimating marbles
Lan has $48$ marbles and Nam has $63$ marbles. Estimate the total number of marbles by rounding to the nearest ten, then work out the exact total.

**Understand.** Lan has $48$ marbles and Nam has $63$ marbles. We are asked to estimate the total, then find the exact total.

**Plan.** Round each number to the nearest ten and add the two round numbers to estimate. Then add the two given numbers.

**Solve.** $48\approx 50$ and $63\approx 60$, so the estimated total is:
$$50+60=110.$$
The exact total is:
$$48+63=111\ \text{(marbles)}.$$
**Answer:** an estimate of about $110$ marbles; the exact total is $111$ marbles.

**Look back.** The estimate $110$ is very close to the exact answer $111$, so the result is sensible. You can use this method to check an addition for big mistakes.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Rounding the digit $5$ down.** Wrong: $35\approx 30$. Why it is wrong: the digit $5$ must be rounded up. Right: $35\approx 40$.
- **Looking at the wrong digit.** Wrong: to round $438$ to the nearest hundred, looking at the digit $8$ and getting $500$. Why it is wrong: for the nearest hundred you look at the tens digit, which is $3$. Right: $438\approx 400$.
- **Forgetting to turn the later digits into $0$.** Wrong: $365\approx 37$. Why it is wrong: a number rounded to the nearest ten must have ones digit $0$. Right: $365\approx 370$.
:::

**Connection.** You have learned to compare numbers and three-digit numbers. Rounding uses the same idea as comparing the distances between numbers. In later years you will round larger numbers and use rounding to estimate many calculations.

## Summary

- Rounding to the nearest ten: look at the ones digit; $0$ to $4$ round down, $5$ to $9$ round up.
- Rounding to the nearest hundred: look at the tens digit, with the same rule: $0$ to $4$ down, $5$ to $9$ up.
- A rounded value has $0$ in the places to the right; write $34\approx 30$.
- Rounding helps you estimate answers, for example $48+63\approx 50+60=110$.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Round to the nearest ten: a) $23$; b) $68$; c) $95$.

::hint
**What you need:** look at the ones digit: $0$ to $4$ down, $5$ to $9$ up.

**Steps:**
1. What is the ones digit of each number?

::solution
a) $23$ has ones digit $3$, so $23\approx 20$.

b) $68$ has ones digit $8$, so $68\approx 70$.

c) $95$ has ones digit $5$, so $95\approx 100$.

**Answer.** a) $20$; b) $70$; c) $100$.
:::

:::exercise
Round to the nearest ten: a) $154$; b) $281$; c) $399$.

::hint
**What you need:** you still look at the ones digit.

**Steps:**
1. For $399$, rounding up turns $39$ tens into how many tens?

::solution
a) $154$: digit $4$, round down, $154\approx 150$.

b) $281$: digit $1$, round down, $281\approx 280$.

c) $399$: digit $9$, round up, $39$ tens becomes $40$ tens, $399\approx 400$.

**Answer.** a) $150$; b) $280$; c) $400$.
:::

:::exercise
Round to the nearest hundred: a) $326$; b) $750$; c) $899$.

::hint
**What you need:** look at the tens digit: $0$ to $4$ down, $5$ to $9$ up.

**Steps:**
1. What is the tens digit of each number?

::solution
a) $326$: the tens digit is $2$, so $326\approx 300$.

b) $750$: the tens digit is $5$, so $750\approx 800$.

c) $899$: the tens digit is $9$, so $899\approx 900$.

**Answer.** a) $300$; b) $800$; c) $900$.
:::

:::exercise
A primary school has $637$ pupils. Round the number of pupils to the nearest ten and to the nearest hundred.

::hint
**What you need:** for the nearest ten look at the ones digit; for the nearest hundred look at the tens digit.

**Steps:**
1. Which two digits of $637$ do you need to look at?

::solution
To the nearest ten: the ones digit is $7$, round up, $637\approx 640$.

To the nearest hundred: the tens digit is $3$, round down, $637\approx 600$.

**Answer.** About $640$ pupils (nearest ten) and about $600$ pupils (nearest hundred).
:::

:::exercise
Estimate $38+41$ by rounding each number to the nearest ten, then work out the exact total and compare.

::hint
**What you need:** round each number to the nearest ten, then add the round numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Round $38$ and $41$ to the nearest ten.

::solution
$38\approx 40$ and $41\approx 40$, so the estimated total is $40+40=80$.

The exact total is $38+41=79$. The numbers $80$ and $79$ are very close.

**Answer.** Estimate $80$; exact total $79$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember four-digit numbers. Round $998$ to the nearest ten and to the nearest hundred.

::hint
**What you need:** when you round up, you may have to carry into a higher place.

**Steps:**
1. For the nearest ten: $99$ tens plus $1$ ten makes how many tens?

::solution
To the nearest ten: the ones digit is $8$, round up, $99$ tens becomes $100$ tens, so $998\approx 1000$.

To the nearest hundred: the tens digit is $9$, round up, $9$ hundreds becomes $10$ hundreds, so $998\approx 1000$.

**Answer.** Both are $1000$.
:::

:::exercise
Find all the two-digit numbers that round to $60$ when rounded to the nearest ten.

::hint
**What you need:** a number that rounds down to $60$ has tens digit $6$; a number that rounds up to $60$ has tens digit $5$.

**Steps:**
1. List the numbers that round down to $60$, then those that round up to $60$.

::solution
Numbers with tens digit $6$ and ones digit from $0$ to $4$: $60$, $61$, $62$, $63$, $64$. Numbers with tens digit $5$ and ones digit from $5$ to $9$: $55$, $56$, $57$, $58$, $59$.

There are $10$ numbers: $55$, $56$, $57$, $58$, $59$, $60$, $61$, $62$, $63$, $64$.

**Answer.** $55$, $56$, $57$, $58$, $59$, $60$, $61$, $62$, $63$, $64$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
What are the smallest and the largest three-digit numbers that round to $500$ when rounded to the nearest hundred?

::hint
**What you need:** a number that rounds down to $500$ has tens digit $0$ to $4$; a number that rounds up to $500$ has hundreds digit $4$ and tens digit $5$ to $9$.

**Steps:**
1. Find the smallest number that rounds up to $500$.
2. Find the largest number that rounds down to $500$.

::solution
The smallest number: $450$, because its tens digit is $5$ so it rounds up to $500$ ($449\approx 400$).

The largest number: $549$, because its tens digit is $4$ so it rounds down to $500$ ($550\approx 600$).

**Answer.** The smallest number is $450$; the largest number is $549$.
:::
