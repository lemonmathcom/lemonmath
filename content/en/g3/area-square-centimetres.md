- Recognise the square centimetre ($\text{cm}^2$) as a unit of area.
- Find the area of rectangles and squares.
- Tell area from perimeter and solve word problems about area.

Lan wants to stick coloured paper on a rectangular piece of card $6$ cm long and $4$ cm wide. Each piece of paper is a square with sides of $1$ cm. How many pieces of paper does Lan need to cover the card completely?

The number of pieces tells you how much surface the card has. This lesson teaches you to measure how much surface a shape has, using area.

## The area of a shape

### Let's explore

Every shape covers part of a flat surface, as a piece of card covers part of a table. The part of a flat surface that a shape covers is called the **area** of the shape.

You compare two shapes by counting how many equal squares each is made of. Shape $A$ has $2$ rows with $3$ squares in each row:

```
■ ■ ■
■ ■ ■
```

Shape $B$ has $2$ rows with $4$ squares in each row:

```
■ ■ ■ ■
■ ■ ■ ■
```

Shape $A$ has $6$ squares and shape $B$ has $8$ squares. Since $6<8$, shape $B$ has a greater area than shape $A$.

:::theorem Area
- The area of a shape is the part of a flat surface that the shape covers.
- To compare the areas of two shapes, count the equal squares each is made of.
- The shape made of more squares has the greater area.
:::

Why use equal squares? If the squares were different sizes, the number of squares would no longer tell you which shape covers more. With the same kind of square, comparing the numbers of squares compares the areas.

## Square centimetres

### Let's explore

To say how big an area is, you need a unit of measurement. Choose a square whose side is $1$ cm long. The area of that square is **$1$ square centimetre**, written $1\ \text{cm}^2$.

Shape $A$ above is made of $6$ such squares, so its area is $6\ \text{cm}^2$. Shape $B$ is made of $8$ squares, so its area is $8\ \text{cm}^2$.

:::theorem Square centimetre
- The square centimetre is a unit of area.
- A square with sides of $1$ cm has an area of $1\ \text{cm}^2$.
- A shape made of $n$ such squares has an area of $n\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

Why is it called "square"? Because this unit is the area of a square, and it is different from the centimetre, which measures length. When you write an area, you must write $\text{cm}^2$, not cm.

### Examples

:::example Counting squares
A shape is made of two rows of $1\ \text{cm}^2$ squares: the top row has $4$ squares and the bottom row has $3$ squares. Find the area of the shape.

**Solution.** The shape has $4+3=7$ squares, and each square has an area of $1\ \text{cm}^2$.

So the area of the shape is $7\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

## Area of rectangles and squares

### Let's explore

Back to Lan's card: $6$ cm long and $4$ cm wide. Lay the $1\ \text{cm}^2$ pieces of paper in $4$ rows with $6$ pieces in each row. The number of pieces is
$$6\times 4=24.$$
So the card has an area of $24\ \text{cm}^2$, and Lan needs $24$ pieces of paper.

:::theorem Area of rectangles and squares
- The area of a rectangle is its length times its width (in the same unit).
- The area of a square is its side times its side.
:::

Why multiply? Because a rectangle is made of rows of equal squares: the number of squares in a row is the length, the number of rows is the width, and multiplying is a quick way to count equal rows. A square is a rectangle whose length equals its width, so its area is side times side.

### Examples

We start with a rectangle.

:::example Area of a rectangle
Find the area of a rectangle $5$ cm long and $3$ cm wide.

**Solution.** The area of the rectangle is:
$$5\times 3=15\ (\text{cm}^2).$$
**Answer:** $15\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

Now a square.

:::example Area of a square
Find the area of a square with sides of $6$ cm.

**Solution.** The area of the square is:
$$6\times 6=36\ (\text{cm}^2).$$
**Answer:** $36\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

Use the rules to solve a problem from everyday life.

:::example A greetings card
A rectangular greetings card is $12$ cm long and $8$ cm wide. What is the area of the card in square centimetres?

**Solution.** Multiply the length by the width.

The area of the card is:
$$12\times 8=96\ (\text{cm}^2).$$
**Answer:** $96\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

Compare the areas of two shapes.

:::example Comparing areas
A rectangle is $8$ cm long and $3$ cm wide. A square has sides of $5$ cm. Which shape has the greater area?

**Solution.** The area of the rectangle is $8\times 3=24\ (\text{cm}^2)$. The area of the square is $5\times 5=25\ (\text{cm}^2)$.

Since $24<25$, the square has the greater area.
:::

The next problem is a little harder, so we use four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example A sheet with a piece cut out
A rectangular sheet of paper is $9$ cm long and $5$ cm wide. Ha cuts out a square with sides of $3$ cm. What is the area of the paper that is left, in square centimetres?

**Understand.** The sheet is $9$ cm long and $5$ cm wide. Ha cuts out a square with sides of $3$ cm. We are asked for the area of the paper left.

**Plan.** Find the area of the sheet, then the area of the square cut out, then subtract.

**Solve.** The area of the sheet is:
$$9\times 5=45\ (\text{cm}^2).$$
The area of the square cut out is:
$$3\times 3=9\ (\text{cm}^2).$$
The area of the paper left is:
$$45-9=36\ (\text{cm}^2).$$
**Answer:** $36\ \text{cm}^2$.

**Look back.** The area left, $36\ \text{cm}^2$, is less than the area of the sheet, $45\ \text{cm}^2$, so it is sensible. Think about what would be left if Ha cut out a square with sides of $5$ cm.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Mixing up area and perimeter.** Wrong: a rectangle $6$ cm long and $4$ cm wide has area $6+4+6+4=20\ \text{cm}^2$. Why it is wrong: $20$ is the perimeter, the length around the shape. Right: $6\times 4=24\ \text{cm}^2$.
- **Writing the wrong unit for area.** Wrong: "Area: $24$ cm". Why it is wrong: area is measured in square centimetres, not centimetres. Right: "Area: $24\ \text{cm}^2$".
- **Multiplying measurements in different units.** Wrong: length $1$ dm, width $5$ cm: $1\times 5=5\ \text{cm}^2$. Why it is wrong: $1$ dm $=10$ cm, so you must convert to the same unit. Right: $10\times 5=50\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

**Connection.** You have already learned the perimeter of rectangles and squares. Perimeter measures the length around a shape, while area measures how much surface is inside it. Multiplication lets you find area quickly, and it is the same multiplication you have used all year.

## Summary

- Area is the part of a flat surface a shape covers; a shape made of more squares has the greater area.
- The unit of area is the square centimetre ($\text{cm}^2$).
- Area of a rectangle $=$ length $\times$ width; area of a square $=$ side $\times$ side.
- Do not mix up area and perimeter; always convert to the same unit before multiplying.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
A shape is made of two rows of $1\ \text{cm}^2$ squares with $5$ squares in each row. Find the area of the shape.

::hint
**What you need:** the area equals the number of $1\ \text{cm}^2$ squares.

**Steps:**
1. How many squares are there altogether?

::solution
The number of squares is $5+5=10$, and each square has an area of $1\ \text{cm}^2$.

**Answer.** $10\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the area of a rectangle $8$ cm long and $3$ cm wide.

::hint
**What you need:** area of a rectangle $=$ length $\times$ width.

**Steps:**
1. Write the multiplication $8\times 3$.

::solution
The area of the rectangle is:
$$8\times 3=24\ (\text{cm}^2).$$
**Answer.** $24\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the area of a square with sides of $7$ cm.

::hint
**What you need:** area of a square $=$ side $\times$ side.

**Steps:**
1. Write the multiplication $7\times 7$.

::solution
The area of the square is:
$$7\times 7=49\ (\text{cm}^2).$$
**Answer.** $49\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
The lid of a biscuit tin is a rectangle $15$ cm long and $9$ cm wide. What is the area of the lid in square centimetres?

::hint
**What you need:** multiply a two-digit number by a one-digit number.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Write the multiplication $15\times 9$.

::solution
The area of the lid is:
$$15\times 9=135\ (\text{cm}^2).$$
**Answer.** $135\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
Shape $A$ is a rectangle $9$ cm long and $4$ cm wide. Shape $B$ is a square with sides of $6$ cm. Which shape has the greater area?

::hint
**What you need:** find the area of each shape, then compare.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $9\times 4$ and $6\times 6$.

::solution
The area of shape $A$ is $9\times 4=36\ (\text{cm}^2)$. The area of shape $B$ is $6\times 6=36\ (\text{cm}^2)$.

The two areas are equal: $36=36$.

**Answer.** The two shapes have equal areas.
:::

:::exercise
A rectangular sheet of paper is $10$ cm long and $6$ cm wide. A square with sides of $2$ cm is cut out. What is the area of the paper that is left, in square centimetres?

::hint
**What you need:** this problem has two steps: find two areas, then subtract.

**Steps:**
1. Find the area of the sheet and the area of the square cut out.
2. Subtract the area of the square from the area of the sheet.

::solution
The area of the sheet is $10\times 6=60\ (\text{cm}^2)$. The area of the square cut out is $2\times 2=4\ (\text{cm}^2)$.

The area of the paper left is:
$$60-4=56\ (\text{cm}^2).$$
**Answer.** $56\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember numerical expressions. A piece of card is made of two rectangles joined together: the first is $5$ cm long and $3$ cm wide; the second is $4$ cm long and $3$ cm wide. Write an expression and work out the total area of the card.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply first, then add.

**Steps:**
1. Write the area of each rectangle, then add.

::solution
The total area of the card is:
$$5\times 3+4\times 3=15+12=27\ (\text{cm}^2).$$
**Answer.** $27\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A square has a perimeter of $24$ cm. Find the area of the square.

::hint
**What you need:** a square has $4$ equal sides.

**Steps:**
1. Find the length of one side from the perimeter.
2. Find the area.

::solution
The length of one side of the square is:
$$24\div4=6\ (\text{cm}).$$
The area of the square is:
$$6\times 6=36\ (\text{cm}^2).$$
**Answer.** $36\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::
