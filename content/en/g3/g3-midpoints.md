:::goals
- Recognise three points in a straight line and a point between two points.
- Recognise the midpoint of a line segment: it lies between the ends and is the same distance from both.
- Work out half of a segment, or the whole segment, when you know the midpoint.
:::

An's house, the school and Binh's house are on the same straight road. From An's house to the school is $3$ km, and from the school to Binh's house is also $3$ km. What is special about where the school stands?

This lesson helps you name that position and use it to work out lengths.

## Let's explore

Points on the same straight line are called **three points in a straight line**. If $A$, $M$, $B$ are in a straight line and going from $A$ to $B$ means passing through $M$, then $M$ is **between** $A$ and $B$.

We draw An's house as point $A$, the school as point $M$ and Binh's house as point $B$:

:::diagram M is between A and B, and MA and MB are equal
alt: A horizontal line segment AB with the point M in the middle; AM and MB each carry the same small tick mark, so they are equal
point A 0,0 below
point M 3,0 below
point B 6,0 below

mark A M 1
mark M B 1
:::

Point $M$ is between $A$ and $B$, and $MA=MB=3$ km. Point $M$ splits the line segment $AB$ into two equal parts. We call $M$ the **midpoint** of the line segment $AB$.

:::theorem The midpoint of a line segment
Point $M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB$ when $M$ is between $A$ and $B$ and $MA=MB$.

Then $AB=MA+MB=2\times MA$, so $MA=AB\div2$.
:::

This is true because $MA$ and $MB$ are equal, so each one is $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of $AB$. In the last lesson you saw that half of a number is that number divided by $2$.

## Examples

We start by finding points between.

:::example Points in between
On a straight line there are four points $D$, $E$, $G$, $H$ in this order from left to right. Which points are between $D$ and $H$? Which point is between $E$ and $H$?

**Solution.** Going from $D$ to $H$ we pass $E$ and then $G$, so $E$ and $G$ are between $D$ and $H$. Going from $E$ to $H$ we pass only $G$, so only $G$ is between $E$ and $H$.

So $E$ and $G$ are between $D$ and $H$; $G$ is between $E$ and $H$.
:::

Now check whether a point is a midpoint.

:::example Is it a midpoint?
$M$ is between $A$ and $B$.

a) $AM=5$ cm and $MB=5$ cm. Is $M$ the midpoint of $AB$?

b) $AM=4$ cm and $MB=6$ cm. Is $M$ the midpoint of $AB$?

**Solution.** $M$ is already between $A$ and $B$, so we only compare $AM$ and $MB$.

a) $AM=MB=5$ cm, so $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$.

b) $AM=4$ cm is not equal to $MB=6$ cm, so $M$ is not the midpoint of $AB$.

So a) yes; b) no.
:::

Next, work out half of a segment.

:::example Find $AM$
$M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB$, which is $12$ cm long. Find $AM$.

**Solution.** Since $M$ is the midpoint, $AM$ is half of $AB$, so we divide $AB$ by $2$:
$$AM=12\div2=6\ \text{(cm)}.$$
**Check.** $MB=6$ cm, and $AM+MB=6+6=12$ cm. Correct.

So $AM=6$ cm.
:::

Now go the other way and find the whole segment.

:::example Find $AB$
$M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB$ and $MA=7$ cm. Find $AB$.

**Solution.** $AB$ is made of two equal parts $MA$ and $MB$, so we multiply $MA$ by $2$:
$$AB=7\times2=14\ \text{(cm)}.$$
**Check.** $14\div2=7$. Correct.

So $AB=14$ cm.
:::

Finally, the problem from the start, in a different setting.

:::example The road to school
An's house, the school and Binh's house are on a straight line. The school is the midpoint of the road from An's house to Binh's house, which is $8$ km long. How many kilometres is it from An's house to the school?

**Solution.** The school is the midpoint, so the distance from An's house to the school is half of $8$ km:
$$8\div2=4\ \text{(km)}.$$
**Check.** $4+4=8$. Correct.

So it is $4$ km from An's house to the school.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Between does not always mean midpoint.** Wrong: $M$ is between $A$ and $B$, so $M$ is the midpoint. Why it is wrong: if $AM=3$ cm and $MB=7$ cm, $M$ is still between $A$ and $B$ but the two parts are not equal. Right: we also need $MA=MB$.
- **Forgetting to halve.** Wrong: $M$ is the midpoint of $AB=12$ cm so $AM=12$ cm. Why it is wrong: $AM$ is only half of $AB$. Right: $AM=12\div2=6$ cm.
- **Forgetting to double.** Wrong: $MA=7$ cm so $AB=7$ cm. Why it is wrong: $AB$ is made of two parts, $MA$ and $MB$. Right: $AB=7\times2=14$ cm.
- **Forgetting the unit.** Wrong: "Answer: $6$". Right: "Answer: $6$ cm".
:::

## Summary

- Three points on the same straight line are in a straight line.
- Point $M$ is between $A$ and $B$ when going from $A$ to $B$ means passing through $M$.
- $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ when $M$ is between $A$ and $B$ and $MA=MB$.
- When $M$ is the midpoint: $MA=AB\div2$ and $AB=MA\times2$.
- Write the unit of length in the answer.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
On a straight line there are four points $P$, $Q$, $R$, $S$ in this order from left to right.

a) Which points are between $P$ and $S$?

b) Which point is between $Q$ and $S$?

c) Which point is between $P$ and $Q$?

::hint
**What you need:** $M$ is between $A$ and $B$ when going from $A$ to $B$ means passing through $M$.

**Steps:**
1. Go from the first point to the last and note the points you pass.
2. Do not count the first and last points.

::solution
a) Going from $P$ to $S$ we pass $Q$ and $R$, so $Q$ and $R$ are between.

b) Going from $Q$ to $S$ we pass only $R$, so $R$ is between.

c) Going from $P$ to $Q$ we pass no other point, so no point is between.

**Answer.** a) $Q$, $R$; b) $R$; c) no point.
:::

:::exercise
$M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB$. Find $AM$ when:

a) $AB=8$ cm.

b) $AB=20$ cm.

c) $AB=30$ cm.

::hint
**What you need:** $AM=AB\div2$.

**Steps:**
1. Take the length of $AB$.
2. Divide it by $2$, then check.

::solution
a) $AM=8\div2=4$ (cm).

b) $AM=20\div2=10$ (cm).

c) $AM=30\div2=15$ (cm).

**Answer.** a) $4$ cm; b) $10$ cm; c) $15$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson on unit fractions. Find $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of $18$. Then let $M$ be the midpoint of the line segment $AB=18$ cm, find $AM$ and compare.

::hint
**What you need:** one half of a number is that number divided by $2$; $AM=AB\div2$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $18\div2$.
2. Compare with $AM$.

::solution
$\dfrac{1}{2}$ of $18$ is $18\div2=9$. Since $M$ is the midpoint, $AM=18\div2=9$ cm, which is $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of $AB$.

**Answer.** $9$ and $AM=9$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
$M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB$. Find $AB$ when:

a) $MA=5$ cm.

b) $MA=9$ cm.

c) $MB=12$ cm.

::hint
**What you need:** $AB=MA\times2$ and $MA=MB$.

**Steps:**
1. Take the length of one half.
2. Multiply by $2$, then check.

::solution
a) $AB=5\times2=10$ (cm).

b) $AB=9\times2=18$ (cm).

c) $MA=MB=12$ cm, so $AB=12\times2=24$ (cm).

**Answer.** a) $10$ cm; b) $18$ cm; c) $24$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
$M$ is between $A$ and $B$. Is $M$ the midpoint of $AB$? Why?

a) $AM=3$ cm and $MB=3$ cm.

b) $AM=2$ cm and $MB=5$ cm.

c) Point $M$ is not on the line $AB$, but $MA=MB=4$ cm.

::hint
**What you need:** $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ when $M$ is between $A$ and $B$ and $MA=MB$.

**Steps:**
1. Is $M$ between $A$ and $B$?
2. Is $MA$ equal to $MB$?

::solution
a) $M$ is between and $AM=MB=3$ cm, so $M$ is the midpoint.

b) $AM=2$ cm is not equal to $MB=5$ cm, so $M$ is not the midpoint.

c) $MA=MB$ but $M$ is not between $A$ and $B$ (not in a straight line), so $M$ is not the midpoint.

**Answer.** a) Yes; b) no; c) no.
:::

:::exercise
Ha says: "$AB=10$ cm and $M$ is between $A$ and $B$, so $AM=5$ cm". Where did Ha go wrong? Give an example that shows Ha is wrong.

::hint
**What you need:** a point between two points is not always the midpoint.

**Steps:**
1. Can $AM$ be different from $5$ cm while $M$ is still between $A$ and $B$?
2. Work out $MB$ and compare.

::solution
Ha confused a point between with a midpoint. For example, if $AM=3$ cm then $MB=10-3=7$ cm. Then $M$ is between $A$ and $B$ but $AM\neq MB$, so $AM$ is not $5$ cm.

**Answer.** Ha forgot the condition $MA=MB$.
:::

:::exercise
a) A plank $16$ dm long is sawn at its midpoint into two pieces. How many decimetres long is each piece?

b) The school is the midpoint of the road from Mai's house to Lan's house. From Mai's house to the school is $6$ km. How many kilometres is it from Mai's house to Lan's house?

::hint
**What you need:** when $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$, $MA=AB\div2$ and $AB=MA\times2$.

**Steps:**
1. Are we given the whole segment or one half?
2. Divide or multiply by $2$ and write the answer with its unit.

::solution
a) Each piece is $16\div2=8$ (dm) long.

b) The whole road is $6\times2=12$ (km).

**Answer.** a) $8$ dm; b) $12$ km.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
The line segment $AB$ is $16$ cm long. $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $N$ is the midpoint of $AM$. Find $AM$, $AN$ and $NB$.

::hint
**What you need:** a midpoint splits a segment into two equal parts. $N$ is between $A$ and $M$, and $M$ is between $N$ and $B$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $AM$, then $AN$.
2. $NB=NM+MB$.

::solution
$AM=16\div2=8$ (cm). $N$ is the midpoint of $AM$, so $AN=NM=8\div2=4$ (cm). $M$ is between $N$ and $B$, so $NB=NM+MB=4+8=12$ (cm).

**Answer.** $AM=8$ cm; $AN=4$ cm; $NB=12$ cm.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
$M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB=14$ cm. The length of $AM$ is
A. $28$ cm.
B. $7$ cm.
C. $14$ cm.
D. $12$ cm.
::key B
$AM=14\div2=7$ cm. Option A is the mistake of multiplying $14\times2$, and option C forgets to halve.

::mc
$M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $CD$ and $MC=6$ cm. The length of $CD$ is
A. $3$ cm.
B. $6$ cm.
C. $12$ cm.
D. $36$ cm.
::key C
$CD=6\times2=12$ cm. Option A is the mistake of halving instead of doubling.

::mc
On a straight line there are four points $E$, $F$, $G$, $H$ in this order from left to right. Which points are between $E$ and $H$?
A. Only $F$.
B. $F$ and $G$.
C. Only $G$.
D. No point.
::key B
Going from $E$ to $H$ we pass $F$ and then $G$. Option A misses $G$.

::mc
Point $M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB$ when
A. $M$ is between $A$ and $B$.
B. $MA=MB$.
C. $M$ is between $A$ and $B$ and $MA=MB$.
D. $AB=2$ cm.
::key C
We need both conditions: between, and the same distance from both ends. Options A and B each give only one condition.

::tf
$M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB=18$ cm.
a) $AM=MB$.
b) $AM=9$ cm.
c) $AB=2\times AM$.
d) $MB=18$ cm.
::key T T T F
a) True: $M$ is the midpoint. b) True: $18\div2=9$. c) True: $2\times9=18$. d) False: $MB=9$ cm.

::sa
$M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB=26$ cm. Find $AM$ (unit: cm).
::key 13
$AM=26\div2=13$ (cm).

::sa
The school is the midpoint of the road from An's house to Binh's house, which is $18$ km long. How many kilometres is it from An's house to the school?
::key 9
$18\div2=9$ (km).
:::
