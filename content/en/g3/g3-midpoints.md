:::goals
- Recognise three points in a straight line and a point between two points.
- Recognise the midpoint of a line segment: it lies between the ends and is the same distance from both.
- Work out half of a segment, or the whole segment, when you know the midpoint.
:::

Picture a straight road with An's house, the school and Binh's house along it. From An's house to the school is $3$ km, and from the school to Binh's house is also $3$ km. Where does the school stand compared with the two houses? Is there anything special about that spot?

That spot has a name of its own. Once you know it, you can work out a length without measuring the whole road. We will name it and then put it to use.

## Let's explore

Think of points sitting along one straight line. Three such points are called **three points in a straight line**. Now take $A$, $M$, $B$ in a straight line. If you must pass through $M$ on the way from $A$ to $B$, then $M$ is **between** $A$ and $B$.

Let us draw the road. An's house is point $A$, the school is point $M$ and Binh's house is point $B$:

:::diagram M is between A and B, and MA and MB are equal
alt: A horizontal line segment AB with the point M in the middle; AM and MB each carry the same small tick mark, so they are equal
point A 0,0 below
point M 3,0 below

mark A M 1
mark M B 1
:::

Point $M$ is between $A$ and $B$, and $MA=MB=3$ km. So $M$ splits the line segment $AB$ into two equal parts. A point like that has its own name: we call $M$ the **midpoint** of the line segment $AB$.

:::theorem The midpoint of a line segment
Point $M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB$ when $M$ is between $A$ and $B$ and $MA=MB$.

Then $AB=MA+MB=2\times MA$, so $MA=AB\div2$.
:::

Why do we halve? Because $MA$ and $MB$ are equal, so each one is $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of $AB$. In the last lesson you saw that half of a number is that number divided by $2$. So finding half of a segment is just a division.

## Examples

First, practise spotting points between: walk from one point to another and see which points you pass.

:::example Points in between
On a straight line there are four points $D$, $E$, $G$, $H$ in this order from left to right. Which points are between $D$ and $H$? Which point is between $E$ and $H$?

**Solution.** Going from $D$ to $H$ we pass $E$ and then $G$, so $E$ and $G$ are between $D$ and $H$. Going from $E$ to $H$ we pass only $G$, so only $G$ is between $E$ and $H$.

So $E$ and $G$ are between $D$ and $H$; $G$ is between $E$ and $H$.
:::

Being between is not enough to make a midpoint. Let us check whether a point is a midpoint.

:::example Is it a midpoint?
$M$ is between $A$ and $B$.

a) $AM=5$ cm and $MB=5$ cm. Is $M$ the midpoint of $AB$?

b) $AM=4$ cm and $MB=6$ cm. Is $M$ the midpoint of $AB$?

**Solution.** $M$ is already between $A$ and $B$, so we only compare $AM$ and $MB$.

a) $AM=MB=5$ cm, so $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$.

b) $AM=4$ cm is not equal to $MB=6$ cm, so $M$ is not the midpoint of $AB$.

So a) yes; b) no.
:::

Once we know the midpoint, we can work out half of a segment. Let us try it.

:::example Find $AM$
$M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB$, which is $12$ cm long. Find $AM$.

**Solution.** Since $M$ is the midpoint, $AM$ is half of $AB$, so we divide $AB$ by $2$:
$$AM=12\div2=6\ \text{(cm)}.$$
**Check.** $MB=6$ cm, and $AM+MB=6+6=12$ cm. Correct.

So $AM=6$ cm.
:::

Now go the other way: you know the half, and you find the whole segment.

:::example Find $AB$
$M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB$ and $MA=7$ cm. Find $AB$.

**Solution.** $AB$ is made of two equal parts $MA$ and $MB$, so we multiply $MA$ by $2$:
$$AB=7\times2=14\ \text{(cm)}.$$
**Check.** $14\div2=7$. Correct.

So $AB=14$ cm.
:::

Back to An, Binh and the school, this time with different numbers.

:::example The road to school
An's house, the school and Binh's house are on a straight line. The school is the midpoint of the road from An's house to Binh's house, which is $8$ km long. How many kilometres is it from An's house to the school?

**Solution.** The school is the midpoint, so the distance from An's house to the school is half of $8$ km:
$$8\div2=4\ \text{(km)}.$$
**Check.** $4+4=8$. Correct.

So it is $4$ km from An's house to the school.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Between does not always mean midpoint.** Wrong: $M$ is between $A$ and $B$, so $M$ is the midpoint. Why it is wrong: suppose $AM=3$ cm and $MB=7$ cm. Then $M$ is still between $A$ and $B$, but the two parts are not equal. Right: we also need $MA=MB$.
- **Forgetting to halve.** Wrong: $M$ is the midpoint of $AB=12$ cm so $AM=12$ cm. Why it is wrong: $AM$ is only half of $AB$. Right: $AM=12\div2=6$ cm.
- **Forgetting to double.** Wrong: $MA=7$ cm so $AB=7$ cm. Why it is wrong: $AB$ is made of two parts, $MA$ and $MB$. Right: $AB=7\times2=14$ cm.
- **Forgetting the unit.** Wrong: "Answer: $6$". Right: "Answer: $6$ cm".
Many people slip up in these places, so don't worry if you do. Check your answer by putting the two parts back together and you will see it straight away.
:::

## Summary

- Three points on the same straight line are in a straight line.
- Point $M$ is between $A$ and $B$ when going from $A$ to $B$ means passing through $M$.
- $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ when $M$ is between $A$ and $B$ and $MA=MB$.
- When $M$ is the midpoint: $MA=AB\div2$ and $AB=MA\times2$.
- Write the unit of length in the answer.
So what about the question at the start? The school is between the two houses and the same distance from both, so it is the midpoint of the road. Remember: to find half, halve; to find the whole, double. A few practice questions will make it feel natural.

## Practice

Every exercise gives you the idea you need and a hint for getting started. Have a go on your own first. If you get stuck, read the hint, and only then open the solution to compare.

:::exercise
On a straight line there are four points $P$, $Q$, $R$, $S$ in this order from left to right.

a) Which points are between $P$ and $S$?

b) Which point is between $Q$ and $S$?

c) Which point is between $P$ and $Q$?

::hint
**What you need:** $M$ is between $A$ and $B$ when going from $A$ to $B$ means passing through $M$.

**Steps:**
1. Go from the first point to the last and note the points you pass.
2. Do not count the first and last points.

::solution
Solution

The four points $P$, $Q$, $R$, $S$ lie on a straight line in this order from left to right.

a) Going from $P$ to $S$ we pass $Q$ and then $R$. So $Q$ and $R$ are between $P$ and $S$.

b) Going from $Q$ to $S$ we pass only $R$. So $R$ is between $Q$ and $S$.

c) Going from $P$ to $Q$ we pass no other point. So no point is between $P$ and $Q$.

So a) $Q$ and $R$ are between $P$ and $S$; b) $R$ is between $Q$ and $S$; c) no point is between $P$ and $Q$.

**Answer.** a) $Q$, $R$; b) $R$; c) no point.
:::

:::exercise
$M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB$. Find $AM$ when:

a) $AB=8$ cm.

b) $AB=20$ cm.

c) $AB=30$ cm.

::hint
**What you need:** $AM=AB\div2$.

**Steps:**
1. Take the length of $AB$.
2. Divide it by $2$, then check.

::solution
Solution

Since $M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB$, it splits $AB$ into two equal parts, that is, $AM$ is half of $AB$. So we calculate $AM=AB\div2$.

a) $AM=8\div2=4$ (cm).

b) $AM=20\div2=10$ (cm).

c) $AM=30\div2=15$ (cm).

So $AM$ is $4$ cm, $10$ cm and $15$ cm respectively.

**Answer.** a) $4$ cm; b) $10$ cm; c) $15$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson on unit fractions. Find $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of $18$. Then let $M$ be the midpoint of the line segment $AB=18$ cm, find $AM$ and compare.

::hint
**What you need:** one half of a number is that number divided by $2$; $AM=AB\div2$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $18\div2$.
2. Compare with $AM$.

::solution
Solution

One half of $18$ is:
$$18\div2=9$$

Since $M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB$, $AM$ is half of $AB$. The length of $AM$ is:
$$AM=18\div2=9\ \text{(cm)}$$

Comparing: $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of $18$ is $9$ and $AM=9$ cm, so $AM$ is $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of $AB$.

So $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of $18$ is $9$, and $AM=9$ cm, which is $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of $AB$.

**Answer.** $9$ and $AM=9$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
$M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB$. Find $AB$ when:

a) $MA=5$ cm.

b) $MA=9$ cm.

c) $MB=12$ cm.

::hint
**What you need:** $AB=MA\times2$ and $MA=MB$.

**Steps:**
1. Take the length of one half.
2. Multiply by $2$, then check.

::solution
Solution

Since $M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB$, we have $MA=MB$ and $AB$ is twice $MA$. So we calculate $AB=MA\times2$.

a) $AB=5\times2=10$ (cm).

b) $AB=9\times2=18$ (cm).

c) Since $M$ is the midpoint, $MA=MB=12$ cm. Hence $AB=12\times2=24$ (cm).

So $AB$ is $10$ cm, $18$ cm and $24$ cm respectively.

**Answer.** a) $10$ cm; b) $18$ cm; c) $24$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
$M$ is between $A$ and $B$. Is $M$ the midpoint of $AB$? Why?

a) $AM=3$ cm and $MB=3$ cm.

b) $AM=2$ cm and $MB=5$ cm.

c) Point $M$ is not on the line $AB$, but $MA=MB=4$ cm.

::hint
**What you need:** $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ when $M$ is between $A$ and $B$ and $MA=MB$.

**Steps:**
1. Is $M$ between $A$ and $B$?
2. Is $MA$ equal to $MB$?

::solution
Solution

The point $M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $AB$ when $M$ is between $A$ and $B$ and $MA=MB$. We check these two conditions.

a) $M$ is between $A$ and $B$, and $AM=MB=3$ cm. Both conditions hold, so $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$.

b) $M$ is between $A$ and $B$ but $AM=2$ cm is not equal to $MB=5$ cm. The condition $MA=MB$ fails, so $M$ is not the midpoint of $AB$.

c) $M$ is not on the line $AB$, so $M$ is not between $A$ and $B$. Although $MA=MB=4$ cm, the condition of being between fails, so $M$ is not the midpoint of $AB$.

So $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ in part a); it is not the midpoint in parts b) and c).

**Answer.** a) Yes; b) no; c) no.
:::

:::exercise
Ha says: "$AB=10$ cm and $M$ is between $A$ and $B$, so $AM=5$ cm". Where did Ha go wrong? Give an example that shows Ha is wrong.

::hint
**What you need:** a point between two points is not always the midpoint.

**Steps:**
1. Can $AM$ be different from $5$ cm while $M$ is still between $A$ and $B$?
2. Work out $MB$ and compare.

::solution
Solution

Ha thinks that if $M$ is between $A$ and $B$ then $AM=5$ cm, that is, $AM$ is half of $AB=10$ cm. But for $AM$ to be half of $AB$, $M$ must be the midpoint, which means that besides being between $A$ and $B$ we also need $MA=MB$. Ha confused a point between with a midpoint.

For example, take $AM=3$ cm. Since $M$ is between $A$ and $B$,
$$MB=AB-AM=10-3=7\ \text{(cm)}.$$

Then $M$ is between $A$ and $B$ but $AM=3$ cm is not equal to $MB=7$ cm, so $M$ is not the midpoint and $AM$ is not $5$ cm.

So Ha is wrong because the condition $MA=MB$ is missing.

**Answer.** Ha forgot the condition $MA=MB$.
:::

:::exercise
a) A plank $16$ dm long is sawn at its midpoint into two pieces. How many decimetres long is each piece?

b) The school is the midpoint of the road from Mai's house to Lan's house. From Mai's house to the school is $6$ km. How many kilometres is it from Mai's house to Lan's house?

::hint
**What you need:** when $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$, $MA=AB\div2$ and $AB=MA\times2$.

**Steps:**
1. Are we given the whole segment or one half?
2. Divide or multiply by $2$ and write the answer with its unit.

::solution
Solution

a) The plank is sawn at its midpoint, so the two pieces are equal and each is half of the plank. Each piece is:
$$16\div2=8\ \text{(dm)}$$

b) The school is the midpoint of the road from Mai's house to Lan's house, so the distance from Mai's house to the school is half of the distance from Mai's house to Lan's house. The distance from Mai's house to Lan's house is:
$$6\times2=12\ \text{(km)}$$

So each piece of the plank is $8$ dm long and it is $12$ km from Mai's house to Lan's house.

**Answer.** a) $8$ dm; b) $12$ km.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
The line segment $AB$ is $16$ cm long. $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $N$ is the midpoint of $AM$. Find $AM$, $AN$ and $NB$.

::hint
**What you need:** a midpoint splits a segment into two equal parts. $N$ is between $A$ and $M$, and $M$ is between $N$ and $B$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $AM$, then $AN$.
2. $NB=NM+MB$.

::solution
Solution

Since $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$, $AM$ is half of $AB$:
$$AM=16\div2=8\ \text{(cm)}$$

Since $N$ is the midpoint of $AM$, $AN=NM$ and each is half of $AM$:
$$AN=NM=8\div2=4\ \text{(cm)}$$

Since $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$, $MB=AM=8$ cm. The point $M$ is between $N$ and $B$, so
$$NB=NM+MB=4+8=12\ \text{(cm)}.$$

Check: $NB=AB-AN=16-4=12$ (cm). This matches the result above.

So $AM=8$ cm, $AN=4$ cm and $NB=12$ cm.

**Answer.** $AM=8$ cm; $AN=4$ cm; $NB=12$ cm.
:::
