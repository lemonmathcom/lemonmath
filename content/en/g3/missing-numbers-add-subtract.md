:::goals
- Name the parts of an addition and a subtraction.
- Find a missing addend, minuend or subtrahend with the inverse operation.
- Check the answer and solve word problems that ask for the starting number.
:::

Lan has some stickers in a box. Her mum gives her $7$ more, and now Lan has $15$ stickers. How many stickers were in the box at the start?

We do not know the starting number, so we write $\square+7=15$. This lesson shows you how to find the number in the box for sure, without guessing.

## Let's explore

Remember the last lesson: in the addition $8+7=15$, the numbers $8$ and $7$ are called **addends** and $15$ is called the **sum**.

Look again at Lan's story:

- The box holds $15$ stickers after the new ones are added.
- Mum gave $7$ stickers.
- The stickers Lan had at the start are the rest, so we take $7$ away from $15$.

So $15-7=8$. Lan had $8$ stickers at the start.

You see: if we know the sum and one addend, we subtract to find the other addend.

:::theorem Finding a missing addend
The missing addend is the sum minus the known addend.

If $\square+7=15$, then $\square=15-7=8$.
:::

Now look at subtraction. In $15-7=8$, the number $15$ is the **minuend**, $7$ is the **subtrahend** and $8$ is the **difference**.

Think of the story: there are $15$ stickers, we take away $7$, and $8$ are left. If we know $8$ are left and $7$ were taken away, then at the start there were $8+7=15$. If we know there were $15$ and $8$ are left, then $15-8=7$ were taken away.

:::theorem Finding a missing minuend or subtrahend
The minuend is the difference plus the subtrahend.

The subtrahend is the minuend minus the difference.

If $\square-7=8$, then $\square=8+7=15$. If $15-\square=8$, then $\square=15-8=7$.
:::

This works because addition and subtraction undo each other: add a number and then subtract it, and you are back where you started.

## Examples

We start with the missing addend in the first place.

:::example Find $\square$ when $\square+6=14$
**Solution.** $\square$ is an addend, so we subtract the known addend from the sum:
$$\square=14-6=8.$$
**Check.** $8+6=14$. Correct.

So $\square=8$.
:::

The missing addend can also be in the second place. The method is the same.

:::example Find $\square$ when $9+\square=17$
**Solution.** Subtract the known addend, $9$, from the sum:
$$\square=17-9=8.$$
**Check.** $9+8=17$. Correct.

So $\square=8$.
:::

Now we find a missing minuend.

:::example Find $\square$ when $\square-5=12$
**Solution.** $\square$ is the minuend, so we add the subtrahend to the difference:
$$\square=12+5=17.$$
**Check.** $17-5=12$. Correct.

So $\square=17$.
:::

Next, a missing subtrahend.

:::example Find $\square$ when $20-\square=13$
**Solution.** $\square$ is the subtrahend, so we subtract the difference from the minuend:
$$\square=20-13=7.$$
**Check.** $20-7=13$. Correct.

So $\square=7$.
:::

Finally, use this method for a problem from everyday life.

:::example Marbles
Binh has some marbles. Nam gives him $12$ more, and now Binh has $30$ marbles. How many marbles did Binh have at the start?

**Solution.** Let the starting number be $\square$. The story says $\square+12=30$. The missing addend is the sum minus the known addend:
$$\square=30-12=18\ \text{(marbles)}.$$
**Check.** $18+12=30$. Correct.

So Binh had $18$ marbles at the start.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Adding instead of subtracting in an addition.** Wrong: $\square+6=14$ so $\square=14+6=20$. Why it is wrong: $20+6=26$, not $14$. Right: $\square=14-6=8$.
- **Subtracting instead of adding for a minuend.** Wrong: $\square-5=12$ so $\square=12-5=7$. Why it is wrong: $7-5=2$, not $12$. Right: $\square=12+5=17$.
- **Mixing up the subtrahend.** Wrong: $20-\square=13$ so $\square=20+13=33$. Why it is wrong: $20-33$ cannot be done here. Right: $\square=20-13=7$.
:::

## Key terms

:::terms
- addend | số hạng
- sum (total) | tổng
- minuend | số bị trừ
- subtrahend | số trừ
- difference | hiệu
- missing number | số chưa biết
- inverse operation | phép tính ngược
- check the answer | thử lại
:::

## Summary

- Addition: addend + addend = sum. Subtraction: minuend − subtrahend = difference.
- Missing addend = sum − known addend.
- Missing minuend = difference + subtrahend.
- Missing subtrahend = minuend − difference.
- Always check by putting your answer in the box; write the unit in word problems.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Find $\square$ when $\square+8=15$.

::hint
**What you need:** the missing addend is the sum minus the known addend.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? The sum is $15$ and one addend is $8$.
2. Work out $15-8$.

::solution
$\square=15-8=7$.

**Check.** $7+8=15$. Correct.

**Answer.** $7$.
:::

:::exercise
Find $\square$ when $24+\square=40$.

::hint
**What you need:** the missing addend is the sum minus the known addend.

**Steps:**
1. The sum is $40$ and the known addend is $24$.
2. Work out $40-24$, then check.

::solution
$\square=40-24=16$.

**Check.** $24+16=40$. Correct.

**Answer.** $16$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember addition with regrouping: work out $46+28$. Then find $\square$ when $\square+28=74$.

::hint
**What you need:** add the ones first and regroup $1$ ten when you reach $10$ or more. The missing addend is the sum minus the known addend.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $46+28$.
2. Compare the result with $74$ and find $\square$.

::solution
$46+28=74$ (because $6+8=14$, write $4$ and regroup $1$; $4+2+1=7$). From $\square+28=74$ we get $\square=74-28=46$.

**Answer.** $46+28=74$ and $\square=46$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the minuend when the subtrahend is $9$ and the difference is $30$, that is $\square-9=30$.

::hint
**What you need:** the minuend is the difference plus the subtrahend.

**Steps:**
1. The difference is $30$ and the subtrahend is $9$.
2. Work out $30+9$, then check.

::solution
$\square=30+9=39$.

**Check.** $39-9=30$. Correct.

**Answer.** $39$.
:::

:::exercise
For each sum, do you add or subtract to find $\square$? Why? Then find $\square$.

a) $\square+15=40$.

b) $\square-15=40$.

c) $40-\square=15$.

::hint
**What you need:** decide whether $\square$ is an addend, a minuend or a subtrahend.

**Steps:**
1. Name $\square$ in each sum.
2. Use the matching rule.

::solution
a) $\square$ is an addend: sum minus addend, $\square=40-15=25$.

b) $\square$ is the minuend: difference plus subtrahend, $\square=40+15=55$.

c) $\square$ is the subtrahend: minuend minus difference, $\square=40-15=25$.

**Answer.** a) $25$; b) $55$; c) $25$.
:::

:::exercise
Mai solved $63-\square=45$ like this: "$\square=63+45=108$". Where did Mai go wrong? Correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** in $63-\square=45$, $\square$ is the subtrahend.

**Steps:**
1. Which operation finds a missing subtrahend?
2. Check Mai's answer: can $63-108$ be done?

::solution
Mai added, but $\square$ is the subtrahend, so we subtract the difference from the minuend: $\square=63-45=18$.

**Check.** $63-18=45$. Correct.

**Answer.** Mai used the wrong operation; $\square=18$.
:::

:::exercise
a) A teacher has some exercise books. She gives $35$ to the class and has $18$ left. How many books did she have at the start?

b) A shop sold $27$ kg of rice in the morning. It sold $60$ kg in the whole day. How many kilograms of rice did it sell in the afternoon?

::hint
**What you need:** read the story and decide whether the missing number is an addend, a minuend or a subtrahend.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Write a sum with a box, find the box, then write the answer with its unit.

::solution
a) Let the starting number be $\square$: $\square-35=18$, so $\square=18+35=53$ (books).

b) Let the afternoon amount be $\square$: $27+\square=60$, so $\square=60-27=33$ (kg).

**Answer.** a) $53$ books; b) $33$ kg of rice.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find $\square$.

a) $\square+28=100-35$.

b) $90-\square=47+16$.

::hint
**What you need:** work out the side you already know first, then find the missing number.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $100-35$ and $47+16$.
2. Find $\square$ with the matching rule and check.

::solution
a) $100-35=65$, so $\square+28=65$ and $\square=65-28=37$. Check: $37+28=65$.

b) $47+16=63$, so $90-\square=63$ and $\square=90-63=27$. Check: $90-27=63$.

**Answer.** a) $37$; b) $27$.
:::

Look at the sum $\square+24=70$.
a) The missing number equals $70-24$.
b) The missing number equals $46$.
c) The missing number equals $94$.

::key T T F T
a) True: the missing addend is the sum minus the known addend. b) True: $70-24=46$. c) False: $94$ is $70+24$, and $94+24=118$. d) True: $46+24=70$.
