- Use the 7 times table to divide by 7.

A week has $7$ days. How many days are there in $5$ weeks?

You could add $7+7+7+7+7$, but adding many times is easy to get wrong. This lesson helps you work it out quickly with the $7$ times table.

## Let's explore

Each week is a group of $7$ days. We add one week at a time:

- $1$ week: $7$ days.
- $2$ weeks: $7+7=14$ days.
- $3$ weeks: $14+7=21$ days.
- $4$ weeks: $21+7=28$ days.
- $5$ weeks: $28+7=35$ days.

So $5$ weeks have $35$ days, that is $7\times5=35$. Each new week adds $7$ to the product.

Here is one more trick: $7=5+2$, so $7\times4=5\times4+2\times4=20+8=28$.

:::theorem The 7 times table
To work out $7\times n$, take $7\times(n-1)$ and add $7$. You can also split $7=5+2$ and add $5\times n$ and $2\times n$.

| $7\times1$ | $7\times2$ | $7\times3$ | $7\times4$ | $7\times5$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $7$ | $14$ | $21$ | $28$ | $35$ |

| $7\times6$ | $7\times7$ | $7\times8$ | $7\times9$ | $7\times10$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $42$ | $49$ | $56$ | $63$ | $70$ |
:::

The division facts for $7$ come from the $7$ times table. Since $7\times5=35$, we have $35\div7=5$. If you split $35$ days into weeks of $7$ days, you get $5$ weeks.

## Examples

We start by taking the product before and adding $7$.

:::example Work out $7\times6$
**Solution.** You know $7\times5=35$. Add one more group of $7$:
$$7\times6=7\times5+7=35+7=42.$$
So $7\times6=42$.
:::

Now use the trick of splitting $7=5+2$.

:::example Work out $7\times8$ by splitting $7=5+2$
**Solution.** $8$ groups of $7$ are $8$ groups of $5$ and $8$ groups of $2$:
$$7\times8=5\times8+2\times8=40+16=56.$$
So $7\times8=56$.
:::

Sometimes it is easier to take the next product and subtract $7$.

:::example Work out $7\times9$
**Solution.** You know $7\times10=70$. Take away one group of $7$:
$$7\times9=7\times10-7=70-7=63.$$
So $7\times9=63$.
:::

Next, division.

:::example Work out $56\div7$
**Solution.** Ask: which number times $7$ makes $56$? In the $7$ times table, $7\times8=56$, so
$$56\div7=8.$$
**Check.** $7\times8=56$. Correct.

So $56\div7=8$.
:::

Finally, a word problem.

:::example Days into weeks
A trip lasts $49$ days. How many weeks is that?

**Solution.** A week has $7$ days, and we split $49$ days into groups of $7$, so we divide. The number of weeks is:
$$49\div7=7\ \text{(weeks)}.$$
**Check.** $7\times7=49$. Correct.

So the trip lasts $7$ weeks.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Taking one group too few.** Wrong: $7\times6=36$. Why it is wrong: $36$ is $6\times6$, so you have only $6$ groups. Right: $7\times6=35+7=42$.
- **Adding instead of multiplying.** Wrong: $7\times3=10$. Why it is wrong: $7+3$ is an addition, but $7\times3$ means $3$ groups of $7$. Right: $7\times3=21$.
- **Dividing wrongly.** Wrong: $56\div7=7$. Why it is wrong: $7\times7=49$, not $56$. Right: $7\times8=56$, so $56\div7=8$.
- **Forgetting the unit.** Wrong: "Answer: $7$". Right: "Answer: $7$ weeks".
:::

## Summary

- The $7$ times table: $7,\ 14,\ 21,\ 28,\ 35,\ 42,\ 49,\ 56,\ 63,\ 70$.
- Each new group adds $7$ to the product: $7\times n=7\times(n-1)+7$.
- Trick: $7=5+2$, so $7\times n=5\times n+2\times n$.
- From $7\times a=b$ we get $b\div7=a$ and $b\div a=7$.
- A week has $7$ days; write the unit in the answer and check a division with a multiplication.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $7\times3$.

b) $7\times5$.

c) $7\times6$.

::hint
**What you need:** the $7$ times table: each new group adds $7$ to the product.

**Steps:**
1. Count in sevens: $7,\ 14,\ 21,\ \dots$
2. Stop at the number you need.

::solution
a) $7\times3=21$.

b) $7\times5=35$.

c) $7\times6=42$.

**Answer.** a) $21$; b) $35$; c) $42$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $7\times7$.

b) $7\times9$.

c) $7\times10$.

::hint
**What you need:** $7\times n=7\times(n-1)+7$.

**Steps:**
1. Remember the product before.
2. Add $7$.

::solution
a) $7\times7=42+7=49$.

b) $7\times9=56+7=63$.

c) $7\times10=63+7=70$.

**Answer.** a) $49$; b) $63$; c) $70$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the $6$ times table. Work out and compare:

a) $6\times7$.

b) $7\times6$.

c) $6\times9$ and $7\times8$.

::hint
**What you need:** swapping the two factors does not change the product.

**Steps:**
1. Work out each product with the $6$ or $7$ times table.
2. Compare the results.

::solution
a) $6\times7=42$.

b) $7\times6=42$, the same as $6\times7$.

c) $6\times9=54$ and $7\times8=56$, so $6\times9<7\times8$.

**Answer.** a) $42$; b) $42$; c) $54$ and $56$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $21\div7$.

b) $49\div7$.

c) $70\div7$.

::hint
**What you need:** from $7\times a=b$ we get $b\div7=a$.

**Steps:**
1. Find the product equal to the dividend in the $7$ times table.
2. The other number is the quotient.

::solution
a) $7\times3=21$, so $21\div7=3$.

b) $7\times7=49$, so $49\div7=7$.

c) $7\times10=70$, so $70\div7=10$.

**Answer.** a) $3$; b) $7$; c) $10$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the box. Why?

a) $7\times6 \;\square\; 40$.

b) $56\div7 \;\square\; 7\times1$.

c) $7\times9 \;\square\; 8\times8$.

::hint
**What you need:** work out each side first, then compare the two numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Work out each side with the times table and division facts.
2. Compare the results.

::solution
a) $7\times6=42$ and $42>40$, so $7\times6>40$.

b) $56\div7=8$ and $7\times1=7$; $8>7$, so $56\div7>7\times1$.

c) $7\times9=63$ and $8\times8=64$; $63<64$, so $7\times9<8\times8$.

**Answer.** a) $>$; b) $>$; c) $<$.
:::

:::exercise
Lam wrote: "$7\times8=54$". Where did Lam go wrong? Correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** $7\times8=7\times7+7$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $7\times8$ again from the product before.
2. Check with a division.

::solution
$7\times8=7\times7+7=49+7=56$, not $54$.

**Check.** $56\div7=8$. Correct.

**Answer.** Lam remembered the wrong product; $7\times8=56$.
:::

:::exercise
a) How many days are there in $6$ weeks?

b) A journey lasts $63$ days. How many weeks is that?

::hint
**What you need:** a week has $7$ days; to find days, multiply; to find weeks, divide.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Choose the operation, work it out and write the answer with its unit.

::solution
a) The number of days is $7\times6=42$ (days).

b) The number of weeks is $63\div7=9$ (weeks).

**Answer.** a) $42$ days; b) $9$ weeks.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A library puts $70$ storybooks on shelves, $7$ books on each shelf. It has already filled $4$ shelves. Find:

a) How many shelves are needed altogether?

b) How many shelves are still to fill, and how many books are still to be shelved?

::hint
**What you need:** the number of shelves is $70\div7$; the books already shelved are $7\times4$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the total number of shelves.
2. Subtract the shelves filled, then work out the books left.

::solution
a) The number of shelves needed is $70\div7=10$ (shelves).

b) The shelves still to fill: $10-4=6$ (shelves). The books already shelved: $7\times4=28$, so the books left are $70-28=42$. Check: $7\times6=42$.

**Answer.** a) $10$ shelves; b) $6$ shelves and $42$ books.
:::
