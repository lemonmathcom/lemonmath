:::goals
- Make a number several times larger using multiplication, for example making $4$ become $3$ times larger.
- Make a number several times smaller using division, for example making $12$ become $3$ times smaller.
- Tell the difference between "several times larger" and "several more".
- Solve word problems about making numbers several times larger or smaller.
:::

Lan has $4$ marbles. Nam has $3$ times as many marbles as Lan. How many marbles does Nam have? Then, if you know that Nam has $12$ marbles, how can you find Lan's number again?

These two questions are two sides of the same idea: making larger and then making smaller. This lesson teaches you both.

## Making a number several times larger

### Let's explore

Lan has $4$ marbles. "Nam has $3$ times as many marbles as Lan" means that Nam's marbles make $3$ groups, each group having $4$ marbles like Lan's.

Add the $3$ groups: $4+4+4=12$. Adding equal numbers is done by multiplication:
$$4\times 3=12.$$
So Nam has $12$ marbles.

:::theorem Making a number several times larger
To make a number $n$ times larger, multiply that number by $n$.
:::

Why multiply? Because "$n$ times as many" means taking $n$ equal groups, each equal to the number, and adding $n$ equal groups is exactly multiplication.

### Examples

We start with a simple one.

:::example Making $7$ become $5$ times larger
**Solution.** To make $7$ become $5$ times larger, multiply $7$ by $5$:
$$7\times 5=35.$$
So $7$ made $5$ times larger is $35$.
:::

The number being made larger can also have two digits.

:::example Making $12$ become $4$ times larger
**Solution.** Multiply $12$ by $4$:
$$12\times 4=48.$$
So $12$ made $4$ times larger is $48$.
:::

Now use this to solve a word problem.

:::example Hoa's garden
Hoa plants $6$ orange trees. She plants $4$ times as many grapefruit trees as orange trees. How many grapefruit trees does Hoa plant?

**Solution.** "$4$ times as many" means multiply by $4$.

The number of grapefruit trees Hoa plants is:
$$6\times 4=24\ \text{(trees)}.$$
**Answer:** $24$ grapefruit trees.
:::

## Making a number several times smaller

### Let's explore

Above, $4$ made $3$ times larger gives $12$. Now go the other way: Nam has $12$ marbles, and Lan's number of marbles is Nam's number made $3$ times smaller. How many marbles does Lan have?

"Made $3$ times smaller" means sharing the $12$ marbles into $3$ equal groups and taking one group:
$$12\div3=4.$$
So Lan has $4$ marbles. Check: $4\times 3=12$. Correct.

:::theorem Making a number several times smaller
To make a number $n$ times smaller, divide that number by $n$.
:::

Why divide? Because making a number $n$ times smaller undoes making it $n$ times larger. Making larger multiplies by $n$, so making smaller divides by $n$.

### Examples

We start with a simple one.

:::example Making $35$ become $5$ times smaller
**Solution.** To make $35$ become $5$ times smaller, divide $35$ by $5$:
$$35\div5=7.$$
Check: $7\times 5=35$. So $35$ made $5$ times smaller is $7$.
:::

Now try another number.

:::example Making $63$ become $9$ times smaller
**Solution.** Divide $63$ by $9$:
$$63\div9=7.$$
Check: $7\times 9=63$. So $63$ made $9$ times smaller is $7$.
:::

The next problem is a little harder, so we use four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example Lan's and Mai's counters
Lan has $48$ counters. Mai has Lan's number of counters made $6$ times smaller. How many counters does Mai have? How many counters do the two girls have altogether?

**Understand.** Lan has $48$ counters. Mai's number is Lan's number made $6$ times smaller. We are asked for Mai's number and for the number the two girls have altogether.

**Plan.** Making $6$ times smaller means dividing by $6$. Then "altogether" means putting together, so we add.

**Solve.** The number of counters Mai has is:
$$48\div6=8\ \text{(counters)}.$$
The number of counters the two girls have is:
$$48+8=56\ \text{(counters)}.$$
**Answer:** Mai has $8$ counters; the two girls have $56$ counters.

**Look back.** Check: $8\times 6=48$, exactly Lan's number. If Mai had $6$ times as many counters as Lan, which calculation would you do?
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Adding instead of multiplying.** Wrong: $4$ made $3$ times larger is $7$. Why it is wrong: $4+3$ was worked out, but "$3$ times larger" means $3$ groups of $4$. Right: $4\times 3=12$.
- **Subtracting instead of dividing.** Wrong: $24$ made $4$ times smaller is $20$. Why it is wrong: $24-4$ was worked out, but making $4$ times smaller means sharing into $4$ equal parts. Right: $24\div4=6$.
- **No unit in a word problem.** Wrong: "Answer: $24$". Right: "Answer: $24$ grapefruit trees".
:::

**Connection.** Making larger is multiplying, making smaller is dividing, and these two operations undo each other. In the next lesson you will use this idea to find how many times larger one number is than another.

## Summary

- To make a number $n$ times larger, multiply it by $n$.
- To make a number $n$ times smaller, divide it by $n$.
- "Several times larger" is not adding, and "several times smaller" is not subtracting.
- Check: if $12$ made $3$ times smaller is $4$, then $4\times 3$ must equal $12$.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Make $6$ become $3$ times larger.

::hint
**What you need:** to make a number $n$ times larger, multiply it by $n$.

**Steps:**
1. Write the multiplication $6\times 3$.

::solution
$$6\times 3=18.$$
**Answer.** $18$.
:::

:::exercise
Make $15$ become $5$ times larger.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply a two-digit number by a one-digit number.

**Steps:**
1. Write the multiplication $15\times 5$, multiplying the ones first.

::solution
$$15\times 5=75.$$
**Answer.** $75$.
:::

:::exercise
Make $32$ become $4$ times smaller.

::hint
**What you need:** to make a number $n$ times smaller, divide it by $n$.

**Steps:**
1. Write the division $32\div4$.

::solution
$$32\div4=8.$$
Check: $8\times 4=32$.

**Answer.** $8$.
:::

:::exercise
Make $54$ become $6$ times smaller.

::hint
**What you need:** use the $6$ times table to divide.

**Steps:**
1. Which number multiplied by $6$ gives $54$?

::solution
$$54\div6=9.$$
Check: $9\times 6=54$.

**Answer.** $9$.
:::

:::exercise
Lan's family keeps $9$ hens. They keep $4$ times as many ducks as hens. How many ducks does Lan's family keep?

::hint
**What you need:** "$4$ times as many" means multiply by $4$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Write the calculation, then answer.

::solution
The number of ducks Lan's family keeps is:
$$9\times 4=36\ \text{(ducks)}.$$
**Answer.** $36$ ducks.
:::

:::exercise
In the morning a shop sold $72$ pens. In the afternoon it sold the morning's number made $8$ times smaller. How many pens did the shop sell in the afternoon? How many pens did it sell in the whole day?

::hint
**What you need:** making $8$ times smaller means dividing by $8$; "the whole day" means putting together.

**Steps:**
1. Find the afternoon's number with a division.
2. Add the pens from the two parts of the day.

::solution
The number of pens sold in the afternoon is:
$$72\div8=9\ \text{(pens)}.$$
The number of pens sold in the whole day is:
$$72+9=81\ \text{(pens)}.$$
**Answer.** $9$ pens in the afternoon; $81$ pens in the whole day.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the $6$ times table: you know that $6\times 7=42$. Make $42$ become $6$ times smaller and make $42$ become $7$ times smaller.

::hint
**What you need:** from one multiplication fact you can find two division facts.

**Steps:**
1. Write the two divisions $42\div6$ and $42\div7$.

::solution
Since $6\times 7=42$, we have $42\div6=7$ and $42\div7=6$.

**Answer.** $42$ made $6$ times smaller is $7$; $42$ made $7$ times smaller is $6$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A number is made $4$ times larger and then $2$ times smaller, and the result is $18$. Find the number.

::hint
**What you need:** work backwards step by step: undoing "$2$ times smaller" means making $2$ times larger.

**Steps:**
1. What was the number before it was made $2$ times smaller?

::solution
Before being made $2$ times smaller, the number was $18\times 2=36$.

Before being made $4$ times larger, the number was $36\div4=9$.

Check: $9\times 4=36$, then $36\div2=18$. Correct.

**Answer.** $9$.
:::

D. $86$.
::key A

A. $63$.
B. $8$.
C. $392$.
D. $49$.
::key B
$56\div7=8$. A adds, D subtracts, C multiplies.

::key C

::key D
"Made $6$ times smaller" means divide: $42\div6=7$ (m). A subtracts, C adds, B multiplies.
