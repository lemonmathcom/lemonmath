:::goals
- Read, write and break down five-digit numbers, for example $45\,327$.
- Name the places: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten-thousands.
- Know that $100\,000$ is the number after $99\,999$ and has six digits.
- Compare numbers within $100\,000$ and put them in order.
:::

A small city (made-up numbers) has $45\,327$ people, and the city next to it has $45\,273$ people. How do you read the number $45\,327$? Which city has more people?

Five-digit numbers are much bigger than the numbers you are used to. To read, write and compare them with confidence, you need to know which place each digit is in.

## Five-digit numbers

### Let's explore

Count on: $9\,999$ plus $1$ is $10\,000$. The number $10\,000$ is made of $10$ thousands, which is $1$ ten-thousand. So after the thousands place there is a new place, the ten-thousands place.

Put the number $45\,327$ into a table of places:

| Ten-thousands | Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| $4$ | $5$ | $3$ | $2$ | $7$ |

Read the table: the number $45\,327$ is made of $4$ ten-thousands, $5$ thousands, $3$ hundreds, $2$ tens and $7$ ones. We write:
$$45\,327=40\,000+5\,000+300+20+7.$$

You read this number as "forty-five thousand, three hundred and twenty-seven". We read the thousands part first, then the rest like a three-digit number.

### The places of a number

:::theorem The places of a five-digit number
A five-digit number has five places. From right to left they are: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten-thousands.

Every $10$ ones make $1$ ten, $10$ tens make $1$ hundred, $10$ hundreds make $1$ thousand, and $10$ thousands make $1$ ten-thousand.
:::

Why is the smallest five-digit number $10\,000$? Because the digit in the ten-thousands place cannot be $0$, so the smallest digit there is $1$, and every other place is $0$. The largest five-digit number is $99\,999$, where every place holds the largest digit.

:::note Reading numbers
If the tens digit is $0$ and the ones digit is not $0$, we do not say a tens word: $50\,206$ is "fifty thousand, two hundred and six". In British English we always say "and" before the last two digits.
:::

We start by reading and writing numbers.

:::example Reading and writing numbers
a) Read the number $50\,206$.
b) Write the number "thirty-two thousand, four hundred and fifteen".

**Solution.** a) The number $50\,206$ has $50$ thousands, $2$ hundreds, $0$ tens and $6$ ones. We read it as "fifty thousand, two hundred and six".

b) "Thirty-two thousand" is $32\,000$ and "four hundred and fifteen" is $415$. We write $32\,000+415=32\,415$.

So the number is $32\,415$.
:::

Next, break a number down by places.

:::example Breaking down the number $67\,804$
Write $67\,804$ as a sum. Which place is the digit $7$ in, and what is its value?

**Solution.** The number $67\,804$ has $6$ ten-thousands, $7$ thousands, $8$ hundreds, $0$ tens and $4$ ones. We have:
$$67\,804=60\,000+7\,000+800+4.$$
The digit $7$ is in the thousands place, so its value is $7\,000$.
:::

Now find the number before and the number after.

:::example The number before and the number after
Find the number after $49\,999$ and the number before $70\,000$.

**Solution.** The number after is $1$ more than the given number, and the number before is $1$ less. We have:
$$49\,999+1=50\,000,\qquad 70\,000-1=69\,999.$$
So the number after $49\,999$ is $50\,000$ and the number before $70\,000$ is $69\,999$.
:::

## The number 100,000

The largest five-digit number is $99\,999$. Add $1$ and you get $99\,999+1=100\,000$. The number $100\,000$ is made of $10$ ten-thousands, which is $1$ hundred-thousand. You read it as "one hundred thousand".

The number $100\,000$ has six digits: one digit $1$ and five digits $0$. It is the smallest six-digit number, and it is the number after $99\,999$.

You meet this number in daily life when you count money.

:::example Money in a wallet
Nam has three $20\,000$ dong notes and four $10\,000$ dong notes. Does Nam have $100\,000$ dong?

**Solution.** The money in the $20\,000$ dong notes is $3\times20\,000=60\,000$ (dong). The money in the $10\,000$ dong notes is $4\times10\,000=40\,000$ (dong).

The total is $60\,000+40\,000=100\,000$ (dong), because $6$ ten-thousands plus $4$ ten-thousands is $10$ ten-thousands.

**Answer.** Nam has exactly $100\,000$ dong.
:::

## Comparing numbers within 100,000

:::theorem Comparing two numbers
To compare two numbers:
1. The number with more digits is greater.
2. If both numbers have the same number of digits, compare pairs of digits in the same place, from left to right. The first pair that differs decides: the larger digit gives the greater number.
3. If every pair of digits is equal, the numbers are equal.
:::

Why compare from the left? Because a digit in a higher place is worth much more: a digit in the ten-thousands place is worth $10$ thousands, more than all the digits to its right added together.

:::example Comparing two numbers
Compare: a) $9\,876$ and $10\,000$; b) $45\,327$ and $45\,273$.

**Solution.** a) $9\,876$ has four digits and $10\,000$ has five digits. So $9\,876<10\,000$.

b) Both numbers have five digits. Ten-thousands: $4=4$. Thousands: $5=5$. Hundreds: $3>2$. So $45\,327>45\,273$.

So the city with $45\,327$ people has more people.
:::

When there are many numbers, compare them two at a time and then put them in order.

:::example Putting numbers in order
Four villages (made-up numbers) have populations of $62\,480$, $62\,408$, $59\,999$ and $62\,840$ people. Put the numbers in order from smallest to largest.

**Solution.** The number $59\,999$ has $5$ in the ten-thousands place, less than the $6$ of the other three. The other three all start with $62$, so we compare the hundreds: $4$, $4$ and $8$. The number $62\,840$ has the largest hundreds digit. The numbers $62\,480$ and $62\,408$ have equal hundreds digits, and their tens are $8>0$.

We have $59\,999<62\,408<62\,480<62\,840$.

So the order from smallest to largest is $59\,999$; $62\,408$; $62\,480$; $62\,840$.
:::

Finally, a problem that needs some thinking. We work in four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example Making numbers from five digits
Using the five digits $0$, $3$, $5$, $7$, $9$, make the largest and the smallest five-digit number, using each digit exactly once.

**Understand.** We have five different digits and must use all of them. We need the largest and the smallest number.

**Plan.** The largest number has the largest digit in the highest place. The smallest number has the smallest digit in the highest place, but the digit $0$ cannot go first.

**Solve.** Arranging the digits from largest to smallest gives the largest number, $97\,530$. For the smallest number, the smallest digit for the ten-thousands place is $3$ (not $0$), then we arrange $0$, $5$, $7$, $9$ from smallest to largest, giving $30\,579$.

So the largest number is $97\,530$ and the smallest is $30\,579$.

**Look back.** If we wrote $03\,579$, it would have only four digits, because a $0$ at the front means nothing. Try changing the digit $0$ to $1$: the smallest number is then $13\,579$, because the digit $1$ is now the smallest and is allowed to go first.
:::

**Connection.** You have used places to read, write and compare numbers. The same idea is used to add and subtract larger numbers and to round numbers in later lessons.

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Leaving out a $0$ when writing.** Wrong: "forty thousand, three hundred" written as $4\,300$. Why it is wrong: "forty thousand" is $40\,000$, so the number has five digits. Right: $40\,300$.
- **Comparing only the first digit.** Wrong: $9\,876>10\,000$ because $9>1$. Why it is wrong: $10\,000$ has five digits, more than the four digits of $9\,876$. Right: $9\,876<10\,000$.
- **Putting $0$ at the front.** Wrong: the smallest number made from $0$, $3$, $5$, $7$, $9$ is $03\,579$. Why it is wrong: a $0$ at the front does not make a five-digit number. Right: $30\,579$.
:::

## Summary

- A five-digit number has the places: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten-thousands. The smallest is $10\,000$ and the largest is $99\,999$.
- Break a number down by places: $45\,327=40\,000+5\,000+300+20+7$.
- The number $100\,000=99\,999+1$ is read "one hundred thousand" and has six digits.
- To compare two numbers: more digits means greater; with the same number of digits, compare from left to right, and the first pair of digits that differs decides.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Write the number: sixty thousand, seven hundred and thirty-two.

::hint
**What you need:** write the thousands part first, then the rest like a three-digit number.

**Steps:**
1. What number is "sixty thousand"?
2. What number is "seven hundred and thirty-two"? Put the two parts together.

::solution
"Sixty thousand" is $60\,000$ and "seven hundred and thirty-two" is $732$. We have $60\,000+732=60\,732$.

**Answer.** $60\,732$.
:::

:::exercise
Read the number $80\,019$.

::hint
**What you need:** if the hundreds digit is $0$, we do not say hundreds.

**Steps:**
1. Read the thousands part: $80$ thousand.
2. Read the rest: $019$.

::solution
The number $80\,019$ has $80$ thousands, $0$ hundreds, $1$ ten and $9$ ones.

**Answer.** Eighty thousand and nineteen.
:::

:::exercise
Write $53\,092$ as a sum of ten-thousands, thousands, hundreds, tens and ones.

::hint
**What you need:** each digit has a value that depends on its place.

**Steps:**
1. Make a table of places for $53\,092$.
2. Leave out the place with digit $0$.

::solution
The number $53\,092$ has $5$ ten-thousands, $3$ thousands, $0$ hundreds, $9$ tens and $2$ ones. We have:
$$53\,092=50\,000+3\,000+90+2.$$

**Answer.** $50\,000+3\,000+90+2$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the number before and the number after $20\,000$.

::hint
**What you need:** the number before is $1$ less, the number after is $1$ more.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $20\,000-1$.
2. Work out $20\,000+1$.

::solution
The number before is $20\,000-1=19\,999$. The number after is $20\,000+1=20\,001$.

**Answer.** $19\,999$ and $20\,001$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the gap: $74\,215\ \ldots\ 74\,251$.

::hint
**What you need:** compare pairs of digits from left to right.

**Steps:**
1. Do the numbers have the same number of digits?
2. Find the first pair of digits that differs.

::solution
Both numbers have five digits. The ten-thousands, thousands and hundreds are equal. Tens: $1<5$. So $74\,215<74\,251$.

**Answer.** The sign $<$.
:::

:::exercise
Four districts (made-up numbers) have populations of $38\,540$, $38\,450$, $40\,125$ and $38\,504$ people. Which district has the most people, and which has the fewest?

::hint
**What you need:** put the numbers in order, then pick the largest and the smallest.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Compare the numbers, starting with the ten-thousands.
3. For the three numbers that start with $38$, compare the hundreds.

::solution
The number $40\,125$ has $4$ in the ten-thousands place, more than the $3$ of the other three. The other three start with $38$; comparing the hundreds: $5$, $4$ and $5$. The number $38\,450$ has the smallest hundreds digit. The numbers $38\,540$ and $38\,504$ have equal hundreds digits, and their tens are $4>0$. We have $38\,450<38\,504<38\,540<40\,125$.

**Answer.** The district with $40\,125$ people has the most, and the district with $38\,450$ people has the fewest.
:::

:::exercise
Mai has saved five $10\,000$ dong notes and two $20\,000$ dong notes. How many more dong does Mai need to have $100\,000$ dong?

::hint
**What you need:** the money still needed is $100\,000$ minus the money she has.

**Steps:**
1. Work out how much money Mai has.
2. Subtract that from $100\,000$.

::solution
Solution

The money Mai has is:
$$5\times10\,000+2\times20\,000=50\,000+40\,000=90\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
The money Mai still needs is:
$$100\,000-90\,000=10\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$

**Answer.** $10\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on addition within $10\,000$. Work out $6\,482+3\,519$, then compare the result with $10\,000$.

::hint
**What you need:** set out the sum and add with regrouping from right to left.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the sum $6\,482+3\,519$.
2. Compare the sum with $10\,000$: which number has more digits?

::solution
We have $6\,482+3\,519=10\,001$. The sum has five digits and so does $10\,000$; comparing place by place, only the ones differ: $1>0$. So $10\,001>10\,000$.

**Answer.** $6\,482+3\,519=10\,001$, which is greater than $10\,000$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find the largest and the smallest five-digit number whose digits add up to $5$.

::hint
**What you need:** the largest number has the largest digit in the ten-thousands place; the smallest has the smallest digit there ($1$) and the smallest digits elsewhere.

**Steps:**
1. Largest number: put the whole total $5$ in the ten-thousands place.
2. Smallest number: the ten-thousands digit is $1$; put the rest in the ones place.

::solution
Largest number: the ten-thousands digit is $5$ and all the other digits are $0$, giving $50\,000$. Smallest number: the ten-thousands digit is $1$, and the other places need a total of $4$. To make the number smallest, the higher places are all $0$ and $4$ goes in the ones place, giving $10\,004$.

**Answer.** $50\,000$ and $10\,004$.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
The number "thirty thousand, two hundred" is written
A. $3\,200$.
B. $30\,020$.
C. $32\,000$.
D. $30\,200$.
::key D
"Thirty thousand" is $30\,000$ and "two hundred" is $200$, so the number is $30\,200$. Option A is missing a $0$, option B puts the $2$ in the tens place, and option C puts the $2$ in the thousands place.

::mc
The number after $79\,999$ is
A. $80\,000$.
B. $79\,998$.
C. $70\,000$.
D. $100\,000$.
::key A
$79\,999+1=80\,000$. Option B is the number before, option C forgets to change the thousands, and option D is the number after $99\,999$.

::mc
The largest of these numbers is
A. $56\,209$.
B. $56\,290$.
C. $56\,029$.
D. $56\,092$.
::key B
All four numbers start with $56$. Hundreds: $2$, $2$, $0$, $0$, so the largest is $56\,290$ or $56\,209$. Tens: $9>0$, so $56\,290>56\,209$.

::mc
Five $20\,000$ dong notes make
A. $10\,000$ dong.
B. $25\,000$ dong.
C. $100\,000$ dong.
D. $50\,000$ dong.
::key C
$5\times20\,000=100\,000$ (dong). Option A works out $5\times2$ and forgets the $0$, option B adds $5+20$ instead of multiplying, and option D multiplies by $10\,000$ instead of $20\,000$.

::tf
Look at the number $70\,508$.
a) The digit $5$ is in the thousands place.
b) The digit $7$ has a value of $70\,000$.
c) $70\,508=70\,000+5\,000+8$.
d) $70\,508$ is greater than $70\,058$.
::key F T F T
a) False: the digit $5$ is in the hundreds place. b) True: the digit $7$ is in the ten-thousands place. c) False: $70\,000+5\,000+8=75\,008$; the right sum is $70\,000+500+8$. d) True: both numbers start with $70$ and the hundreds digits are $5>0$.

How many digits $0$ does the number $100\,000$ have?
::key 5
$100\,000$ is made of the digit $1$ and five digits $0$.

::sa
Work out the sum of the digits of the number before $40\,000$.
::key 39
The number before $40\,000$ is $39\,999$. The sum of its digits is $3+9+9+9+9=39$.
