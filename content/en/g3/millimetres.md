:::goals
- Recognise the millimetre (mm) and know that $1\ \text{cm}=10\ \text{mm}$ and $1\ \text{m}=1000\ \text{mm}$.
- Recognise the gram (g) and know that $1\ \text{kg}=1000\ \text{g}$.
- Convert measurements between units, for example $4\ \text{cm}\ 5\ \text{mm}=45\ \text{mm}$.
- Add and subtract measurements in the same unit and solve word problems.
:::

A pencil is $120$ mm long and a rubber is $35$ mm long. How many millimetres longer is the pencil than the rubber? Mum buys $1$ kg of sticky rice and uses $650$ g of it to cook rice for a meal. How many grams of sticky rice are left?

To answer, you need two new units: the millimetre to measure very short things, and the gram to weigh very light things.

## Millimetres

### Let's explore

Look at a ruler. Between two neighbouring numbers there is $1$ centimetre (cm). Each centimetre is divided into $10$ equal small spaces, and each small space is $1$ **millimetre** long, written mm.

So $1\ \text{cm}=10\ \text{mm}$. You already know that $1\ \text{m}=100\ \text{cm}$, so
$$1\ \text{m}=100\times 10\ \text{mm}=1000\ \text{mm}.$$
A millimetre is very small, so we use it to measure very short things.

:::theorem Millimetre
- The millimetre is written mm.
- $1\ \text{cm}=10\ \text{mm}$.
- $1\ \text{m}=1000\ \text{mm}$.
:::

Why is $1$ cm equal to $10$ mm and not $100$ mm? Because on a ruler each centimetre has only $10$ small marks. You can count them on your own ruler.

### Examples

We start with converting from centimetres to millimetres.

:::example Converting to millimetres
Convert: a) $3\ \text{cm}$ to millimetres; b) $4\ \text{cm}\ 5\ \text{mm}$ to millimetres.

**Solution.** a) $1\ \text{cm}=10\ \text{mm}$, so $3\ \text{cm}=3\times 10=30\ \text{mm}$.

b) $4\ \text{cm}=40\ \text{mm}$, so $4\ \text{cm}\ 5\ \text{mm}=40\ \text{mm}+5\ \text{mm}=45\ \text{mm}$.
:::

Now convert the other way.

:::example Converting to centimetres and millimetres
Convert $52\ \text{mm}$ to centimetres and millimetres.

**Solution.** $52\ \text{mm}=50\ \text{mm}+2\ \text{mm}$. Since $50\ \text{mm}=5\ \text{cm}$,
$$52\ \text{mm}=5\ \text{cm}\ 2\ \text{mm}.$$
:::

Use millimetres to solve a problem from everyday life.

:::example A pencil and a rubber
A pencil is $120$ mm long and a rubber is $35$ mm long. How many millimetres longer is the pencil than the rubber?

**Solution.** "How much longer" is a subtraction, and both lengths are in millimetres.

The number of millimetres by which the pencil is longer than the rubber is:
$$120-35=85\ \text{(mm)}.$$
**Answer:** $85$ mm.
:::

## Grams

### Let's explore

The kilogram (kg) is used to weigh heavy things such as a sack of rice. For lighter things, such as a packet of flour, you use a smaller unit, the **gram**, written g.

You have $1\ \text{kg}=1000\ \text{g}$. If you weigh $1$ kg of sugar and divide it into $1000$ equal parts, each part weighs $1$ g.

:::theorem Gram
- The gram is written g.
- $1\ \text{kg}=1000\ \text{g}$.
- To add or subtract masses, convert them to the same unit.
:::

Why do we need grams? Because the kilogram is too big for light things. Suppose a packet of flour weighs $60$ g: that is much easier to say than that it weighs a small part of a kilogram.

### Examples

We start with converting from kilograms to grams.

:::example Converting to grams
Convert: a) $3\ \text{kg}$ to grams; b) $2\ \text{kg}\ 300\ \text{g}$ to grams.

**Solution.** a) $1\ \text{kg}=1000\ \text{g}$, so $3\ \text{kg}=3\times 1000=3000\ \text{g}$.

b) $2\ \text{kg}=2000\ \text{g}$, so $2\ \text{kg}\ 300\ \text{g}=2000\ \text{g}+300\ \text{g}=2300\ \text{g}$.
:::

And convert the other way.

:::example Converting to kilograms and grams
Convert $4050\ \text{g}$ to kilograms and grams.

**Solution.** $4050\ \text{g}=4000\ \text{g}+50\ \text{g}$. Since $4000\ \text{g}=4\ \text{kg}$,
$$4050\ \text{g}=4\ \text{kg}\ 50\ \text{g}.$$
:::

The next problem is a little harder, so we use four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example Cooking sticky rice
Mum buys $1$ kg of sticky rice. She uses $650$ g of it to cook a meal. How many grams of sticky rice are left?

**Understand.** Mum has $1$ kg of sticky rice and uses $650$ g. We are asked for the number of grams left.

**Plan.** The two measurements are in different units, so first convert $1$ kg to grams. Then "left" is a subtraction.

**Solve.** $1\ \text{kg}=1000\ \text{g}$. The number of grams of sticky rice left is:
$$1000-650=350\ \text{(g)}.$$
**Answer:** $350$ g of sticky rice.

**Look back.** Check by adding: $350+650=1000$, which is exactly $1$ kg. If Mum shared $1$ kg equally among $4$ homes, how many grams would each home get?
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Thinking $1$ cm is $100$ mm.** Wrong: $3\ \text{cm}=300\ \text{mm}$. Why it is wrong: each centimetre has only $10$ millimetres. Right: $3\ \text{cm}=30\ \text{mm}$.
- **Thinking $1$ kg is $100$ g.** Wrong: $2\ \text{kg}=200\ \text{g}$. Why it is wrong: $1$ kilogram is $1000$ grams. Right: $2\ \text{kg}=2000\ \text{g}$.
- **Adding measurements in different units.** Wrong: $4\ \text{cm}+15\ \text{mm}=19$. Why it is wrong: one length is in centimetres and the other in millimetres. Right: $40\ \text{mm}+15\ \text{mm}=55\ \text{mm}$.
:::

**Connection.** You can now convert units of length and mass by multiplying or dividing by $10$, $100$ and $1000$. In the next lesson you will learn millilitres for capacity and degrees for temperature.

## Summary

- $1\ \text{cm}=10\ \text{mm}$ and $1\ \text{m}=1000\ \text{mm}$.
- $1\ \text{kg}=1000\ \text{g}$.
- To add or subtract measurements, convert them to the same unit.
- Always write the unit in the answer.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Convert to millimetres: a) $6\ \text{cm}$; b) $7\ \text{cm}\ 3\ \text{mm}$.

::hint
**What you need:** $1\ \text{cm}=10\ \text{mm}$.

**Steps:**
1. Convert the centimetres to millimetres, then add the extra millimetres.

::solution
a) $6\ \text{cm}=6\times 10=60\ \text{mm}$.

b) $7\ \text{cm}=70\ \text{mm}$, so $7\ \text{cm}\ 3\ \text{mm}=70\ \text{mm}+3\ \text{mm}=73\ \text{mm}$.

**Answer.** a) $60$ mm; b) $73$ mm.
:::

:::exercise
Convert $86\ \text{mm}$ to centimetres and millimetres.

::hint
**What you need:** $80\ \text{mm}=8\ \text{cm}$.

**Steps:**
1. Split $86\ \text{mm}$ into $80\ \text{mm}$ and $6\ \text{mm}$.

::solution
$86\ \text{mm}=80\ \text{mm}+6\ \text{mm}=8\ \text{cm}\ 6\ \text{mm}$.

**Answer.** $8\ \text{cm}\ 6\ \text{mm}$.
:::

:::exercise
Fill in $>$, $<$ or $=$: $5\ \text{cm}\ 4\ \text{mm}\ \ldots\ 56\ \text{mm}$.

::hint
**What you need:** convert both measurements to the same unit, then compare.

**Steps:**
1. Convert $5\ \text{cm}\ 4\ \text{mm}$ to millimetres.

::solution
$5\ \text{cm}\ 4\ \text{mm}=50\ \text{mm}+4\ \text{mm}=54\ \text{mm}$. Since $54<56$, we have $5\ \text{cm}\ 4\ \text{mm}<56\ \text{mm}$.

**Answer.** $<$.
:::

:::exercise
A piece of string is $150$ mm long. $68$ mm is cut off. How many millimetres long is the string that is left?

::hint
**What you need:** "cut off" and "left" mean subtraction.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Write the subtraction, set it out in columns, then check.

::solution
The length of string left is:
$$150-68=82\ \text{(mm)}.$$
Check: $82+68=150$. Correct.

**Answer.** $82$ mm.
:::

:::exercise
Convert to grams: a) $4\ \text{kg}$; b) $2\ \text{kg}\ 50\ \text{g}$.

::hint
**What you need:** $1\ \text{kg}=1000\ \text{g}$.

**Steps:**
1. Convert the kilograms to grams, then add the extra grams.

::solution
a) $4\ \text{kg}=4\times 1000=4000\ \text{g}$.

b) $2\ \text{kg}=2000\ \text{g}$, so $2\ \text{kg}\ 50\ \text{g}=2000\ \text{g}+50\ \text{g}=2050\ \text{g}$.

**Answer.** a) $4000$ g; b) $2050$ g.
:::

:::exercise
Mum buys $800$ g of meat and $700$ g of fish. How many grams does she buy altogether? Write the result in kilograms and grams.

::hint
**What you need:** "altogether" is an addition; $1000\ \text{g}=1\ \text{kg}$.

**Steps:**
1. Add the two masses.
2. Split the result into $1000\ \text{g}$ and the rest.

::solution
The number of grams of meat and fish Mum buys altogether is:
$$800+700=1500\ \text{(g)}.$$
Since $1500\ \text{g}=1000\ \text{g}+500\ \text{g}$, we have $1500\ \text{g}=1\ \text{kg}\ 500\ \text{g}$.

**Answer.** $1500$ g, which is $1\ \text{kg}\ 500\ \text{g}$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on making a number several times larger. A packet of salt weighs $200$ g. A packet of sugar weighs $3$ times as much as the packet of salt. How many grams do the two packets weigh altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** $3$ times as much means multiply by $3$; work out $2$ hundreds times $3$ in your head.

**Steps:**
1. Find the mass of the packet of sugar.
2. Add the masses of the two packets.

::solution
The packet of sugar weighs:
$$200\times 3=600\ \text{(g)}.$$
The two packets weigh:
$$200+600=800\ \text{(g)}.$$
**Answer.** $800$ g.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A ruler is $1$ m long. A piece $250$ mm long and a piece $400$ mm long are cut from it. How many millimetres long is the part of the ruler that is left?

::hint
**What you need:** first convert $1$ m to millimetres.

**Steps:**
1. How many millimetres long are the two pieces cut off altogether?
2. Subtract that from $1000$.

::solution
We have $1\ \text{m}=1000\ \text{mm}$. The two pieces cut off are altogether:
$$250+400=650\ \text{(mm)}.$$
The part of the ruler that is left is:
$$1000-650=350\ \text{(mm)}.$$
**Answer.** $350$ mm.
:::
