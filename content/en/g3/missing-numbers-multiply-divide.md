:::goals
- Name the parts of a multiplication and a division.
- Find a missing factor, dividend or divisor with the inverse operation.
- Check the answer and solve word problems that ask for the number of groups or the starting number.
:::

Each box holds $6$ pens. A teacher has some such boxes, with $24$ pens altogether. How many boxes of pens does the teacher have?

We do not know the number of boxes, so we write $6\times\square=24$. This lesson shows you how to find the number in the box for sure.

## Let's explore

Remember the $6$ times table: $6\times4=24$. In this multiplication, $6$ and $4$ are **factors** and $24$ is the **product**.

From $6\times4=24$ we also get $24\div6=4$ and $24\div4=6$. In the division $24\div6=4$, the number $24$ is the **dividend**, $6$ is the **divisor** and $4$ is the **quotient**.

In the teacher's story: there are $24$ pens altogether and $6$ in each box, so the number of boxes is $24\div6=4$.

:::theorem Finding a missing factor
The missing factor is the product divided by the known factor.

If $6\times\square=24$, then $\square=24\div6=4$.
:::

Now look at the division $24\div6=4$ when one part is missing.

- We know the divisor $6$ and the quotient $4$: each group is $6$, there are $4$ groups, so the dividend is $4\times6=24$.
- We know the dividend $24$ and the quotient $4$: split $24$ into $4$ equal groups, each group has $24\div4=6$, so the divisor is $6$.

:::theorem Finding a missing dividend or divisor
The dividend is the quotient times the divisor.

The divisor is the dividend divided by the quotient.

If $\square\div6=4$, then $\square=4\times6=24$. If $24\div\square=4$, then $\square=24\div4=6$.
:::

This works because multiplication and division undo each other: multiply by a number and then divide by it, and you are back where you started.

## Examples

We start with the missing factor in the first place.

:::example Find $\square$ when $\square\times7=35$
**Solution.** $\square$ is a missing factor, so we divide the product by the known factor:
$$\square=35\div7=5.$$
**Check.** $5\times7=35$. Correct.

So $\square=5$.
:::

The missing factor can also be in the second place.

:::example Find $\square$ when $8\times\square=56$
**Solution.** Divide the product by the known factor, $8$:
$$\square=56\div8=7.$$
**Check.** $8\times7=56$. Correct.

So $\square=7$.
:::

Now we find a missing dividend.

:::example Find $\square$ when $\square\div4=9$
**Solution.** $\square$ is the dividend, so we multiply the quotient by the divisor:
$$\square=9\times4=36.$$
**Check.** $36\div4=9$. Correct.

So $\square=36$.
:::

Next, a missing divisor.

:::example Find $\square$ when $42\div\square=6$
**Solution.** $\square$ is the divisor, so we divide the dividend by the quotient:
$$\square=42\div6=7.$$
**Check.** $42\div7=6$. Correct.

So $\square=7$.
:::

Finally, use this method for a problem from everyday life.

:::example Standing in rows
$45$ pupils stand in equal rows of $9$. How many rows are there?

**Solution.** Let the number of rows be $\square$. The story says $9\times\square=45$. The missing factor is the product divided by the known factor:
$$\square=45\div9=5\ \text{(rows)}.$$
**Check.** $9\times5=45$. Correct.

So there are $5$ rows.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Multiplying instead of dividing for a factor.** Wrong: $\square\times7=35$ so $\square=35\times7=245$. Why it is wrong: $245\times7$ is far bigger than $35$. Right: $\square=35\div7=5$.
- **Dividing instead of multiplying for a dividend.** Wrong: $\square\div4=9$ so $\square=9\div4$. Why it is wrong: $9$ does not divide by $4$ exactly, and $9\div4$ is not the number we want. Right: $\square=9\times4=36$.
- **Multiplying for a divisor.** Wrong: $42\div\square=6$ so $\square=42\times6$. Why it is wrong: $42\div252$ does not equal $6$. Right: $\square=42\div6=7$.
:::

## Summary

- Multiplication: factor $\times$ factor $=$ product. Division: dividend $\div$ divisor $=$ quotient.
- Missing factor $=$ product $\div$ known factor.
- Missing dividend $=$ quotient $\times$ divisor.
- Missing divisor $=$ dividend $\div$ quotient.
- Use the times tables and division facts; always check by putting your answer in the box.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Find $\square$:

a) $\square\times3=18$.

b) $4\times\square=32$.

c) $\square\times9=81$.

::hint
**What you need:** the missing factor is the product divided by the known factor.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? The product and one factor.
2. Divide the product by the known factor, then check.

::solution
a) $\square=18\div3=6$. Check: $6\times3=18$.

b) $\square=32\div4=8$. Check: $4\times8=32$.

c) $\square=81\div9=9$. Check: $9\times9=81$.

**Answer.** a) $6$; b) $8$; c) $9$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the missing dividend $\square$:

a) $\square\div5=6$.

b) $\square\div8=4$.

c) $\square\div9=7$.

::hint
**What you need:** the dividend is the quotient times the divisor.

**Steps:**
1. Which numbers are the quotient and the divisor?
2. Multiply them, then check.

::solution
a) $\square=6\times5=30$. Check: $30\div5=6$.

b) $\square=4\times8=32$. Check: $32\div8=4$.

c) $\square=7\times9=63$. Check: $63\div9=7$.

**Answer.** a) $30$; b) $32$; c) $63$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the $9$ times table. Work out $9\times6$. Then find $\square$ when $\square\times9=54$ and work out $54\div9$.

::hint
**What you need:** $9\times n=10\times n-n$. The missing factor is the product divided by the known factor.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $9\times6$.
2. Compare with $\square\times9=54$.

::solution
$9\times6=60-6=54$. From $\square\times9=54$ we get $\square=54\div9=6$.

**Answer.** $9\times6=54$; $\square=6$ and $54\div9=6$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the missing divisor $\square$:

a) $24\div\square=3$.

b) $56\div\square=7$.

c) $90\div\square=9$.

::hint
**What you need:** the divisor is the dividend divided by the quotient.

**Steps:**
1. Which numbers are the dividend and the quotient?
2. Divide the dividend by the quotient, then check.

::solution
a) $\square=24\div3=8$. Check: $24\div8=3$.

b) $\square=56\div7=8$. Check: $56\div8=7$.

c) $\square=90\div9=10$. Check: $90\div10=9$.

**Answer.** a) $8$; b) $8$; c) $10$.
:::

:::exercise
For each sum, do you multiply or divide to find $\square$? Why? Then find $\square$.

a) $\square\times6=48$.

b) $\square\div6=8$.

c) $48\div\square=6$.

::hint
**What you need:** decide whether $\square$ is a factor, a dividend or a divisor.

**Steps:**
1. Name $\square$ in each sum.
2. Use the matching rule.

::solution
a) $\square$ is a factor: product divided by factor, $\square=48\div6=8$.

b) $\square$ is the dividend: quotient times divisor, $\square=8\times6=48$.

c) $\square$ is the divisor: dividend divided by quotient, $\square=48\div6=8$.

**Answer.** a) $8$; b) $48$; c) $8$.
:::

:::exercise
Lan solved $36\div\square=4$ like this: "$\square=36\times4=144$". Where did Lan go wrong? Correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** in $36\div\square=4$, $\square$ is the divisor.

**Steps:**
1. Which operation finds a missing divisor?
2. Check Lan's answer: does $36\div144$ equal $4$?

::solution
Lan multiplied, but $\square$ is the divisor, so we divide the dividend by the quotient: $\square=36\div4=9$.

**Check.** $36\div9=4$. Correct.

**Answer.** Lan used the wrong operation; $\square=9$.
:::

:::exercise
a) $72$ cakes are put into boxes, $8$ cakes in each box. How many boxes are filled?

b) Some pens are shared equally among $7$ children, $6$ pens each. How many pens are there altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** read the story and decide whether the missing number is a factor, a dividend or a divisor.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Write a sum with a box, find the box, then write the answer with its unit.

::solution
a) Let the number of boxes be $\square$: $8\times\square=72$, so $\square=72\div8=9$ (boxes).

b) Let the number of pens be $\square$: $\square\div7=6$, so $\square=6\times7=42$ (pens).

**Answer.** a) $9$ boxes; b) $42$ pens.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find $\square$.

a) $\square\times6=4\times9$.

b) $63\div\square=3\times3$.

::hint
**What you need:** work out the side you already know first, then find the missing number.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $4\times9$ and $3\times3$.
2. Find $\square$ with the matching rule and check.

::solution
a) $4\times9=36$, so $\square\times6=36$ and $\square=36\div6=6$. Check: $6\times6=36$.

b) $3\times3=9$, so $63\div\square=9$ and $\square=63\div9=7$. Check: $63\div7=9$.

**Answer.** a) $6$; b) $7$.
:::

## Mini test

::mc

A. $49$.
B. $6$.
C. $35$.
D. $7$.
::key B
$\square=42\div7=6$. Option A is $42+7$ and option C is $42-7$.

A. $14$.
B. $48$.

D. $8$.
::key B

B. $35$.

D. $45$.
::key C
$\square$ is the divisor, so $\square=40\div5=8$. Option A is the mistake of multiplying $40\times5$.

::key C
We have $9\times\square=54$, so $\square=54\div9=6$. Option D is the mistake of multiplying $54\times9$.

::tf
Look at the sum $\square\times8=56$.
a) $\square=56\div8$.
b) $\square=7$.
c) $\square=64$.
d) Check: $7\times8=56$.
::key T T F T
a) True: the missing factor is the product divided by the known factor. b) True: $56\div8=7$. c) False: $64$ is $56+8$, and $64\times8=512$. d) True: $7\times8=56$.
