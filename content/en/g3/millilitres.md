:::goals
- Recognise the millilitre (ml) and know that $1\ \text{l}=1000\ \text{ml}$.
- Convert measurements of capacity, for example $2\ \text{l}\ 400\ \text{ml}=2400\ \text{ml}$.
- Know that a thermometer measures temperature and that the unit is the degree Celsius ($^\circ\text{C}$).
- Read temperatures, compare warm and cold, and solve word problems.
:::

A bottle holds $1$ l of water. You pour water into cups, $200$ ml in each cup. How many full cups can you fill? Outside in the yard a thermometer shows $30\ ^\circ\text{C}$, and inside the fridge a thermometer shows $5\ ^\circ\text{C}$. Which place is colder?

To answer, you need to learn two things: the millilitre for measuring amounts of water, and the degree Celsius for measuring how warm or cold something is.

## Millilitres

### Let's explore

You already know that the litre (l) measures capacity, for example a bottle holds $1$ l. For smaller amounts of water, such as a cup, you use the **millilitre**, written ml.

Just as $1\ \text{m}=1000\ \text{mm}$ and $1\ \text{kg}=1000\ \text{g}$, you have
$$1\ \text{l}=1000\ \text{ml}.$$
Sharing a $1$ l bottle into cups of $200$ ml gives $1000\div200=5$ cups, because $5\times 200=1000$. You have answered the first question.

:::theorem Millilitre
- The millilitre is written ml.
- $1\ \text{l}=1000\ \text{ml}$.
- To add or subtract measurements of capacity, convert them to the same unit.
:::

Why do we need millilitres? Because the litre is too big to measure a cup of water or a spoonful of medicine. Small amounts are easier to read in millilitres.

### Examples

We start with converting from litres to millilitres.

:::example Converting to millilitres
Convert: a) $3\ \text{l}$ to millilitres; b) $2\ \text{l}\ 400\ \text{ml}$ to millilitres.

**Solution.** a) $1\ \text{l}=1000\ \text{ml}$, so $3\ \text{l}=3\times 1000=3000\ \text{ml}$.

b) $2\ \text{l}=2000\ \text{ml}$, so $2\ \text{l}\ 400\ \text{ml}=2000\ \text{ml}+400\ \text{ml}=2400\ \text{ml}$.
:::

And convert the other way.

:::example Converting to litres and millilitres
Convert $1500\ \text{ml}$ to litres and millilitres.

**Solution.** $1500\ \text{ml}=1000\ \text{ml}+500\ \text{ml}$. Since $1000\ \text{ml}=1\ \text{l}$,
$$1500\ \text{ml}=1\ \text{l}\ 500\ \text{ml}.$$
:::

Now use millilitres to solve a word problem.

:::example Pouring water into a jug
A jug holds $750$ ml of water, and you pour in $250$ ml more. How many millilitres of water are in the jug altogether? How many litres is that?

**Solution.** "Pour in more" is an addition, and both measurements are in millilitres.

The number of millilitres of water in the jug is:
$$750+250=1000\ \text{(ml)}.$$
Since $1000\ \text{ml}=1\ \text{l}$, the jug holds $1$ l of water.

**Answer:** $1000$ ml, which is $1$ l.
:::

The next problem is a little harder, so we use four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example Pouring water into cups
A bottle holds $1$ l of water. You fill $4$ cups, $200$ ml in each cup. How many millilitres of water are left in the bottle?

**Understand.** The bottle holds $1$ l of water, and you fill $4$ cups of $200$ ml each. We are asked for the number of millilitres left in the bottle.

**Plan.** The units differ, so convert $1$ l to millilitres. Step one finds the water poured out by multiplying; step two subtracts.

**Solve.** $1\ \text{l}=1000\ \text{ml}$. The number of millilitres poured out is:
$$200\times 4=800\ \text{(ml)}.$$
The number of millilitres left is:
$$1000-800=200\ \text{(ml)}.$$
**Answer:** $200$ ml.

**Look back.** Check by adding: $800+200=1000$, exactly $1$ l. The $200$ ml left is enough to fill exactly $1$ more cup.
:::

## Temperature and units of temperature

### Let's explore

To find out whether today is warm or cold, you use a **thermometer**. A thermometer shows the **temperature**. Temperature is measured in the unit **degree Celsius**, written $^\circ\text{C}$. The measurement $30\ ^\circ\text{C}$ is read "thirty degrees Celsius".

Remember two marks for water under normal conditions: water starts to freeze at $0\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and boils at $100\ ^\circ\text{C}$. The higher the temperature, the warmer it is.

Outside in the yard the thermometer shows $30\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and in the fridge it shows $5\ ^\circ\text{C}$. Since $5<30$, it is colder in the fridge than in the yard.

:::theorem Temperature
- A thermometer measures temperature.
- The unit of temperature is the degree Celsius, written $^\circ\text{C}$.
- The higher the temperature, the warmer it is; the lower the temperature, the colder it is.
:::

Why can you compare the numbers? Because a thermometer has a scale of numbers, like a ruler: the bigger the number, the higher the temperature.

### Examples

We start with comparing warm and cold.

:::example Comparing temperatures
Yesterday the thermometer showed $25\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and today it shows $18\ ^\circ\text{C}$. Which day was warmer?

**Solution.** Compare the two numbers: $25>18$.

So yesterday was warmer than today.
:::

The temperature can rise or fall, and you use addition and subtraction.

:::example Temperature during a day
In the morning the temperature is $24\ ^\circ\text{C}$. At noon it rises by $9\ ^\circ\text{C}$, and in the afternoon it falls by $6\ ^\circ\text{C}$. What is the temperature in the afternoon, in degrees Celsius?

**Solution.** "Rises by" is an addition and "falls by" is a subtraction.

The temperature at noon is:
$$24+9=33\ (^\circ\text{C}).$$
The temperature in the afternoon is:
$$33-6=27\ (^\circ\text{C}).$$
**Answer:** $27\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Thinking $1$ l is $100$ ml.** Wrong: $2\ \text{l}=200\ \text{ml}$. Why it is wrong: $1$ litre is $1000$ millilitres. Right: $2\ \text{l}=2000\ \text{ml}$.
- **Adding measurements in different units.** Wrong: $1\ \text{l}+300\ \text{ml}=301$. Why it is wrong: one measurement is in litres and the other in millilitres. Right: $1000\ \text{ml}+300\ \text{ml}=1300\ \text{ml}$.
- **Thinking the smaller number is warmer.** Wrong: $5\ ^\circ\text{C}$ is warmer than $30\ ^\circ\text{C}$. Why it is wrong: the higher the temperature, the warmer it is. Right: $30\ ^\circ\text{C}$ is warmer than $5\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
:::

**Connection.** The millilitre is like the millimetre and the gram: each is a small unit, one thousandth of a larger one. The degree Celsius is a new unit, but you still compare measurements as you compare other numbers.

## Summary

- $1\ \text{l}=1000\ \text{ml}$; to add or subtract measurements of capacity, convert them to the same unit.
- A thermometer measures temperature; the unit is the degree Celsius ($^\circ\text{C}$).
- The higher the temperature, the warmer it is; water freezes at $0\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and boils at $100\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
- Always write the unit in the answer.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Convert to millilitres: a) $5\ \text{l}$; b) $3\ \text{l}\ 200\ \text{ml}$.

::hint
**What you need:** $1\ \text{l}=1000\ \text{ml}$.

**Steps:**
1. Convert the litres to millilitres, then add the extra millilitres.

::solution
a) $5\ \text{l}=5\times 1000=5000\ \text{ml}$.

b) $3\ \text{l}=3000\ \text{ml}$, so $3\ \text{l}\ 200\ \text{ml}=3000\ \text{ml}+200\ \text{ml}=3200\ \text{ml}$.

**Answer.** a) $5000$ ml; b) $3200$ ml.
:::

:::exercise
Convert $2500\ \text{ml}$ to litres and millilitres.

::hint
**What you need:** $2000\ \text{ml}=2\ \text{l}$.

**Steps:**
1. Split $2500\ \text{ml}$ into $2000\ \text{ml}$ and $500\ \text{ml}$.

::solution
$2500\ \text{ml}=2000\ \text{ml}+500\ \text{ml}=2\ \text{l}\ 500\ \text{ml}$.

**Answer.** $2\ \text{l}\ 500\ \text{ml}$.
:::

:::exercise
Fill in $>$, $<$ or $=$: $1\ \text{l}\ 300\ \text{ml}\ \ldots\ 1200\ \text{ml}$.

::hint
**What you need:** convert both measurements to the same unit, then compare.

**Steps:**
1. Convert $1\ \text{l}\ 300\ \text{ml}$ to millilitres.

::solution
$1\ \text{l}\ 300\ \text{ml}=1000\ \text{ml}+300\ \text{ml}=1300\ \text{ml}$. Since $1300>1200$, we have $1\ \text{l}\ 300\ \text{ml}>1200\ \text{ml}$.

**Answer.** $>$.
:::

:::exercise
The first jug holds $600$ ml of water and the second jug holds $800$ ml. How many millilitres of water do the two jugs hold altogether? Write the result in litres and millilitres.

::hint
**What you need:** "altogether" is an addition; $1000\ \text{ml}=1\ \text{l}$.

**Steps:**
1. Add the two measurements.
2. Split the result into $1000\ \text{ml}$ and the rest.

::solution
The number of millilitres of water in the two jugs is:
$$600+800=1400\ \text{(ml)}.$$
Since $1400\ \text{ml}=1000\ \text{ml}+400\ \text{ml}$, we have $1400\ \text{ml}=1\ \text{l}\ 400\ \text{ml}$.

**Answer.** $1400$ ml, which is $1\ \text{l}\ 400\ \text{ml}$.
:::

:::exercise
Mum pours $3$ cups of orange juice, $300$ ml in each cup. How many millilitres of orange juice has she poured? How many millilitres less than $1$ l is that?

::hint
**What you need:** work out $3$ hundreds times $3$ in your head; convert $1$ l to millilitres.

**Steps:**
1. Find the amount of orange juice Mum has poured.
2. Subtract it from $1000$.

::solution
The number of millilitres of orange juice Mum has poured is:
$$300\times 3=900\ \text{(ml)}.$$
The amount less than $1$ l is:
$$1000-900=100\ \text{(ml)}.$$
**Answer.** $900$ ml; it is $100$ ml less than $1$ l.
:::

:::exercise
In the morning the temperature is $22\ ^\circ\text{C}$. At noon it rises by $9\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and in the afternoon it falls by $5\ ^\circ\text{C}$. What is the temperature in the afternoon, in degrees Celsius?

::hint
**What you need:** "rises by" is an addition, "falls by" is a subtraction.

**Steps:**
1. Find the temperature at noon.
2. From that, find the temperature in the afternoon.

::solution
The temperature at noon is:
$$22+9=31\ (^\circ\text{C}).$$
The temperature in the afternoon is:
$$31-5=26\ (^\circ\text{C}).$$
**Answer.** $26\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on making a number several times larger. A small bottle holds $200$ ml of water. A large bottle holds $5$ times as much as the small bottle. How many millilitres does the large bottle hold? How many litres is that?

::hint
**What you need:** $5$ times as much means multiply by $5$.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply $200$ by $5$.
2. Convert the result to litres.

::solution
The large bottle holds:
$$200\times 5=1000\ \text{(ml)}.$$
Since $1000\ \text{ml}=1\ \text{l}$, the large bottle holds $1$ l.

**Answer.** $1000$ ml, which is $1$ l.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A jug holds $2$ l of water. You pour out $3$ cups of $300$ ml each, then pour $400$ ml more into a kettle. How many millilitres of water are left in the jug?

::hint
**What you need:** convert $2$ l to millilitres first; this problem needs three steps.

**Steps:**
1. Find the water poured into $3$ cups.
2. Add the water poured into the kettle, then subtract from $2000$ ml.

::solution
We have $2\ \text{l}=2000\ \text{ml}$. The water poured into $3$ cups is:
$$300\times 3=900\ \text{(ml)}.$$
The water poured out altogether is:
$$900+400=1300\ \text{(ml)}.$$
The water left in the jug is:
$$2000-1300=700\ \text{(ml)}.$$
**Answer.** $700$ ml.
:::
