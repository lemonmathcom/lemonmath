:::goals
- Set out and multiply a five-digit number by a one-digit number, with regrouping, for example $12\,345\times4$.
- Set out and divide a five-digit number by a one-digit number, for example $72\,915\div5$.
- Do a division with a remainder and see that the remainder is always smaller than the divisor.
- Check a multiplication and a division, and solve word problems with multiplication and division.
:::

A factory (made-up numbers) makes $12\,345$ cups every day. How many cups does the factory make in $4$ days? If all those cups are packed equally into boxes, how many cups go in each box?

These numbers have five digits, but the method is the same as for four-digit numbers: you work place by place, from right to left when multiplying and from left to right when dividing.

## Multiplying a five-digit number by a one-digit number

### Let's explore

Split $12\,345=10\,000+2\,000+300+40+5$ and multiply each part by $4$:

- $5\times4=20$.
- $40\times4=160$.
- $300\times4=1\,200$.
- $2\,000\times4=8\,000$.
- $10\,000\times4=40\,000$.

Putting it together: $20+160+1\,200+8\,000+40\,000=49\,380$.

Set it out in columns and multiply from right to left:

$$\begin{array}{r} 12\,345 \\ \times\ 4 \\ \hline 49\,380 \end{array}$$

Ones: $5\times4=20$, write $0$ and regroup $2$. Tens: $4\times4=16$, add $2$ to make $18$, write $8$ and regroup $1$. Hundreds: $3\times4=12$, add $1$ to make $13$, write $3$ and regroup $1$. Thousands: $2\times4=8$, add $1$ to make $9$. Ten-thousands: $1\times4=4$.

So the factory makes $49\,380$ cups in $4$ days.

### How to multiply

:::theorem Multiplying a five-digit number by a one-digit number
To multiply a five-digit number by a one-digit number:
1. Set out the multiplication: write the one-digit number under the ones digit.
2. Multiply from right to left, starting with the ones. Each time, add the regrouped number (if there is one), write the ones digit of the result and regroup the tens part into the column on the left.
3. Write the answer.
:::

Why regroup? Because $10$ units in one place make $1$ unit in the place on the left. When we multiply, the result in one place can be $10$ or more, so we write the ones digit and regroup the rest.

We start with a multiplication that has a $0$ digit.

:::example Set out and work out $21\,304\times3$
**Solution.** We multiply place by place, from right to left:
$$\begin{array}{r} 21\,304 \\ \times\ 3 \\ \hline 63\,912 \end{array}$$
Ones: $4\times3=12$, write $2$ and regroup $1$. Tens: $0\times3=0$, add $1$ to make $1$, write $1$. Hundreds: $3\times3=9$. Thousands: $1\times3=3$. Ten-thousands: $2\times3=6$.

So $21\,304\times3=63\,912$.
:::

Now almost every column needs regrouping.

:::example Set out and work out $28\,465\times3$
**Solution.** We multiply place by place and regroup when needed:
$$\begin{array}{r} 28\,465 \\ \times\ 3 \\ \hline 85\,395 \end{array}$$
Ones: $5\times3=15$, write $5$ and regroup $1$. Tens: $6\times3=18$, add $1$ to make $19$, write $9$ and regroup $1$. Hundreds: $4\times3=12$, add $1$ to make $13$, write $3$ and regroup $1$. Thousands: $8\times3=24$, add $1$ to make $25$, write $5$ and regroup $2$. Ten-thousands: $2\times3=6$, add $2$ to make $8$.

So $28\,465\times3=85\,395$.
:::

Let us use multiplication to solve a word problem.

:::example Shirts made
A clothing workshop (made-up numbers) makes $15\,480$ shirts every day. How many shirts does the workshop make in $6$ days?

**Solution.** Each day is the same, so we multiply. The number of shirts made in $6$ days is:
$$15\,480\times6=92\,880\ \text{(shirts)}.$$
**Answer.** $92\,880$ shirts.
:::

## Dividing a five-digit number by a one-digit number

:::theorem Dividing a five-digit number by a one-digit number
To divide a five-digit number by a one-digit number, we work from left to right, one place at a time:
1. Divide the first digit by the divisor. If that digit is smaller than the divisor, take the first two digits.
2. Write the quotient digit, multiply it by the divisor, and subtract to find the remainder.
3. Bring down the next digit next to the remainder, and divide again.
4. Repeat until the ones place. The final remainder is always smaller than the divisor.
:::

If the final remainder is $0$, the division is exact. If it is not $0$, the division has a remainder, and we write "remainder" after the quotient. To check, multiply the quotient by the divisor and add the remainder: the result must equal the dividend.

We start with a division where every digit divides exactly.

:::example Work out $86\,428\div2$
**Solution.** Every digit of the dividend is divisible by $2$:

$8\div2=4$; $6\div2=3$; $4\div2=2$; $2\div2=1$; $8\div2=4$.

So $86\,428\div2=43\,214$.
:::

Now there are remainders in the middle steps.

:::example Work out $72\,915\div5$
**Solution.** Since $7>5$, we take $7$ to divide by $5$:

$7\div5=1$ (remainder $2$). Bring down $2$ to make $22$; $22\div5=4$ (remainder $2$). Bring down $9$ to make $29$; $29\div5=5$ (remainder $4$). Bring down $1$ to make $41$; $41\div5=8$ (remainder $1$). Bring down $5$ to make $15$; $15\div5=3$ (remainder $0$).

So $72\,915\div5=14\,583$.

**Check.** $14\,583\times5=72\,915$. Correct.
:::

Sometimes a $0$ appears in the quotient, and the division has a remainder.

:::example Work out $60\,745\div6$
**Solution.** We divide step by step:

$6\div6=1$ (remainder $0$). Bring down $0$ to make $0$; $0\div6=0$, write $0$ in the quotient. Bring down $7$ to make $7$; $7\div6=1$ (remainder $1$). Bring down $4$ to make $14$; $14\div6=2$ (remainder $2$). Bring down $5$ to make $25$; $25\div6=4$ (remainder $1$).

So $60\,745\div6=10\,124$ (remainder $1$).

**Check.** $10\,124\times6=60\,744$ and $60\,744+1=60\,745$. Correct, and the remainder $1$ is smaller than the divisor $6$.
:::

Finally, a problem that needs both multiplication and division, where the remainder matters. We work in four steps: understand, plan, solve, look back.

:::example Packing light bulbs into boxes
A factory (made-up numbers) makes $14\,250$ light bulbs every day. The bulbs made in $5$ days are packed into boxes of $7$ bulbs each. What is the smallest number of boxes needed to hold all the bulbs?

**Understand.** We are told the number of bulbs made each day, the number of days and the number of bulbs in each box. We are asked for the smallest number of boxes.

**Plan.** First find the total number of bulbs by multiplying. Then divide the number of bulbs by $7$. If there is a remainder, we need one more box.

**Solve.**

Solution

The number of bulbs made in $5$ days is:
$$14\,250\times5=71\,250\ \text{(bulbs)}.$$
We have $71\,250\div7=10\,178$ (remainder $4$).

So $10\,178$ boxes are full and $4$ bulbs are left over, which need $1$ more box. The smallest number of boxes is:
$$10\,178+1=10\,179\ \text{(boxes)}.$$
Answer: $10\,179$ boxes.

**Look back.** Check: $10\,178\times7=71\,246$ and $71\,246+4=71\,250$. Correct. If we used only the quotient, we would leave out $4$ bulbs.
:::

**Connection.** Multiplication and division are inverse operations: if $a\times b=c$ then $c\div b=a$. You can use this to check both a multiplication and a division.

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting to add the regrouped number when multiplying.** Wrong: $28\,465\times3=64\,285$. Why it is wrong: in each column only the ones digit of the product was written and the regrouped number was not added. Right: $28\,465\times3=85\,395$.
- **Missing a $0$ in the quotient.** Wrong: $60\,745\div6=1\,124$ (remainder $1$). Why it is wrong: after bringing down $0$, $0\div6=0$ but the $0$ was not written in the quotient. Right: $60\,745\div6=10\,124$ (remainder $1$).
- **A remainder that is not smaller than the divisor.** Wrong: $71\,250\div7=10\,177$ (remainder $11$). Why it is wrong: the remainder $11$ can still be divided by $7$, so the quotient must be increased. Right: $71\,250\div7=10\,178$ (remainder $4$).
:::

## Summary

- To multiply a five-digit number by a one-digit number: set it out and multiply **from right to left**, adding the regrouped number each time.
- To divide a five-digit number by a one-digit number: divide **from left to right**, one place at a time. If the first digit is smaller than the divisor, take the first two digits.
- When you bring down a $0$, the quotient digit in that place may be $0$: do not leave it out. The remainder is always smaller than the divisor.
- Check: if $a\times b=c$ then $c\div b=a$; for a division with a remainder, quotient $\times$ divisor $+$ remainder $=$ dividend.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Set out in columns and work out: $32\,140\times2$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply from right to left, regrouping when a product makes $10$ or more.

**Steps:**
1. Write $2$ under the ones digit of $32\,140$.
2. Multiply place by place, starting with the ones.

::solution
$$\begin{array}{r} 32\,140 \\ \times\ 2 \\ \hline 64\,280 \end{array}$$
No place has a product of $10$ or more, so nothing is regrouped.

**Answer.** $64\,280$.
:::

:::exercise
Set out in columns and work out: $17\,285\times4$.

::hint
**What you need:** add the regrouped number each time you multiply.

**Steps:**
1. Set out the multiplication and multiply the ones: $5\times4$.
2. Multiply place by place, regrouping when needed.

::solution
$$\begin{array}{r} 17\,285 \\ \times\ 4 \\ \hline 69\,140 \end{array}$$
Ones: $5\times4=20$, write $0$ and regroup $2$. Tens: $8\times4=32$, add $2$ to make $34$, write $4$ and regroup $3$. Hundreds: $2\times4=8$, add $3$ to make $11$, write $1$ and regroup $1$. Thousands: $7\times4=28$, add $1$ to make $29$, write $9$ and regroup $2$. Ten-thousands: $1\times4=4$, add $2$ to make $6$.

**Answer.** $69\,140$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out: $96\,840\div3$.

::hint
**What you need:** divide from left to right, one place at a time.

**Steps:**
1. Divide $9$ by $3$.
2. Bring down each next digit and divide again.

::solution
$9\div3=3$ (remainder $0$). Bring down $6$ to make $6$; $6\div3=2$ (remainder $0$). Bring down $8$ to make $8$; $8\div3=2$ (remainder $2$). Bring down $4$ to make $24$; $24\div3=8$ (remainder $0$). Bring down $0$ to make $0$; $0\div3=0$.

**Answer.** $32\,280$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out: $52\,387\div4$.

::hint
**What you need:** the remainder is always smaller than the divisor.

**Steps:**
1. Divide $5$ by $4$.
2. Bring down each digit, write the final remainder, then check.

::solution
$5\div4=1$ (remainder $1$). Bring down $2$ to make $12$; $12\div4=3$ (remainder $0$). Bring down $3$ to make $3$; $3\div4=0$ (remainder $3$), write $0$. Bring down $8$ to make $38$; $38\div4=9$ (remainder $2$). Bring down $7$ to make $27$; $27\div4=6$ (remainder $3$).

**Check.** $13\,096\times4=52\,384$ and $52\,384+3=52\,387$. Correct.

**Answer.** $13\,096$ (remainder $3$).
:::

:::exercise
Find $x$: $x\div8=11\,250$.

::hint
**What you need:** the dividend equals the quotient times the divisor.

**Steps:**
1. Write $x=11\,250\times8$.
2. Set out the multiplication and work it out.

::solution
$x=11\,250\times8=90\,000$.

**Check.** $90\,000\div8=11\,250$. Correct.

**Answer.** $x=90\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on addition and subtraction within $100\,000$. A store has $80\,000$ kg of rice. It sends out $3$ loads of $15\,250$ kg each. How many kilograms of rice are left in the store?

::hint
**What you need:** find the rice sent out by multiplying, then subtract it from the starting amount.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the rice sent out in $3$ loads.
2. Subtract that from $80\,000$.

::solution
Solution

The number of kilograms sent out is:
$$15\,250\times3=45\,750\ \text{(kg)}.$$
The number of kilograms left is:
$$80\,000-45\,750=34\,250\ \text{(kg)}.$$

**Answer.** $34\,250$ kg of rice.
:::

:::exercise
Lan works out $38\,540\div5=778$. Use multiplication to check Lan's answer. If it is wrong, correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** quotient $\times$ divisor must equal the dividend.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $778\times5$.
2. If it is not $38\,540$, divide again.

::solution
We have $778\times5=3\,890$, which is not $38\,540$, so Lan's answer is wrong. Dividing again: $38\div5=7$ (remainder $3$); bring down $5$ to make $35$, $35\div5=7$ (remainder $0$); bring down $4$ to make $4$, $4\div5=0$ (remainder $4$), write $0$; bring down $0$ to make $40$, $40\div5=8$. So $38\,540\div5=7\,708$.

**Check.** $7\,708\times5=38\,540$. Correct.

**Answer.** Lan is wrong; the correct answer is $7\,708$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A number divided by $8$ gives a quotient of $12\,345$ and a remainder of $7$. Find the number.

::hint
**What you need:** the dividend equals the quotient times the divisor, plus the remainder.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $12\,345\times8$.
2. Add the remainder $7$.

::solution
We have $12\,345\times8=98\,760$. The number is:
$$98\,760+7=98\,767.$$

**Check.** $98\,767\div8=12\,345$ (remainder $7$). Correct.

**Answer.** $98\,767$.
:::
