:::goals
- Know and build the 8 times table.
- Use the 8 times table to divide by 8.
- Solve a word problem with one multiplication or one division.
:::

Each spider has $8$ legs. How many legs do $3$ spiders have altogether?

You could add $8+8+8$, but with many spiders adding is slow. This lesson helps you work it out quickly with the $8$ times table.

## Let's explore

Each spider is a group of $8$ legs. We add one spider at a time:

- $1$ spider: $8$ legs.
- $2$ spiders: $8+8=16$ legs.
- $3$ spiders: $16+8=24$ legs.

So $3$ spiders have $24$ legs, that is $8\times3=24$. Each new group adds $8$ to the product.

Here is one more trick: $8$ is double $4$, so $8\times3$ is double $4\times3=12$, and $12+12=24$.

:::theorem The 8 times table
To work out $8\times n$, take $8\times(n-1)$ and add $8$. You can also take $4\times n$ and double it.

| $8\times1$ | $8\times2$ | $8\times3$ | $8\times4$ | $8\times5$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $8$ | $16$ | $24$ | $32$ | $40$ |

| $8\times6$ | $8\times7$ | $8\times8$ | $8\times9$ | $8\times10$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $48$ | $56$ | $64$ | $72$ | $80$ |
:::

The division facts for $8$ come from the $8$ times table. Since $8\times3=24$, we have $24\div8=3$. If you split $24$ legs into groups of $8$, you get $3$ groups.

## Examples

We start with the trick of doubling the $4$ times table.

:::example Work out $8\times6$ by doubling
**Solution.** We have $4\times6=24$. Since $8$ is double $4$, $8\times6$ is double $4\times6$:
$$8\times6=24+24=48.$$
So $8\times6=48$.
:::

Now take the product before and add $8$.

:::example Work out $8\times7$
**Solution.** You know $8\times6=48$. Add one more group of $8$:
$$8\times7=8\times6+8=48+8=56.$$
So $8\times7=56$.
:::

Sometimes it is easier to take the next product and subtract $8$.

:::example Work out $8\times9$
**Solution.** You know $8\times10=80$. Take away one group of $8$:
$$8\times9=8\times10-8=80-8=72.$$
So $8\times9=72$.
:::

Next, division.

:::example Work out $64\div8$
**Solution.** Ask: which number times $8$ makes $64$? In the $8$ times table, $8\times8=64$, so
$$64\div8=8.$$
**Check.** $8\times8=64$. Correct.

So $64\div8=8$.
:::

Finally, a word problem.

:::example Cakes into boxes
There are $40$ cakes to put into boxes, $8$ cakes in each box. How many boxes can we fill?

**Solution.** We split $40$ cakes into groups of $8$, so we divide. The number of boxes is:
$$40\div8=5\ \text{(boxes)}.$$
**Check.** $8\times5=40$. Correct.

So we can fill $5$ boxes.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Using a nearby number.** Wrong: $8\times7=54$. Why it is wrong: $54$ is not in the $8$ times table. Right: $8\times7=48+8=56$.
- **Adding instead of multiplying.** Wrong: $8\times8=16$. Why it is wrong: $8+8=16$ is an addition, but $8\times8$ means $8$ groups of $8$. Right: $8\times8=64$.
- **Dividing wrongly.** Wrong: $64\div8=7$. Why it is wrong: $8\times7=56$, not $64$. Right: $8\times8=64$, so $64\div8=8$.
- **Forgetting the unit.** Wrong: "Answer: $5$". Right: "Answer: $5$ boxes".
:::

## Summary

- The $8$ times table: $8,\ 16,\ 24,\ 32,\ 40,\ 48,\ 56,\ 64,\ 72,\ 80$.
- Each new group adds $8$ to the product: $8\times n=8\times(n-1)+8$.
- $8$ is double $4$, so $8\times n$ is double $4\times n$.
- From $8\times a=b$ we get $b\div8=a$ and $b\div a=8$.
- Write the unit in the answer; check a division with a multiplication.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $8\times3$.

b) $8\times5$.

c) $8\times6$.

::hint
**What you need:** the $8$ times table: each new group adds $8$ to the product.

**Steps:**
1. Count in eights: $8,\ 16,\ 24,\ \dots$
2. Stop at the number you need.

::solution
a) $8\times3=24$.

b) $8\times5=40$.

c) $8\times6=48$.

**Answer.** a) $24$; b) $40$; c) $48$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $8\times7$.

b) $8\times9$.

c) $8\times10$.

::hint
**What you need:** $8\times n=8\times(n-1)+8$.

**Steps:**
1. Remember the product before.
2. Add $8$.

::solution
a) $8\times7=48+8=56$.

b) $8\times9=64+8=72$.

c) $8\times10=72+8=80$.

**Answer.** a) $56$; b) $72$; c) $80$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the $7$ times table. Work out and compare:

a) $7\times8$.

b) $8\times7$.

c) $7\times9$ and $8\times8$.

::hint
**What you need:** swapping the two factors does not change the product.

**Steps:**
1. Work out each product with the $7$ or $8$ times table.
2. Compare the results.

::solution
a) $7\times8=56$.

b) $8\times7=56$, the same as $7\times8$.

c) $7\times9=63$ and $8\times8=64$, so $7\times9<8\times8$.

**Answer.** a) $56$; b) $56$; c) $63$ and $64$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $16\div8$.

b) $56\div8$.

c) $80\div8$.

::hint
**What you need:** from $8\times a=b$ we get $b\div8=a$.

**Steps:**
1. Find the product equal to the dividend in the $8$ times table.
2. The other number is the quotient.

::solution
a) $8\times2=16$, so $16\div8=2$.

b) $8\times7=56$, so $56\div8=7$.

c) $8\times10=80$, so $80\div8=10$.

**Answer.** a) $2$; b) $7$; c) $10$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the box. Why?

a) $8\times6 \;\square\; 50$.

b) $72\div8 \;\square\; 8\times1$.

c) $8\times8 \;\square\; 4\times16$.

::hint
**What you need:** work out each side first, then compare the two numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Work out each side with the times table and division facts.
2. Compare the results.

::solution
a) $8\times6=48$ and $48<50$, so $8\times6<50$.

b) $72\div8=9$ and $8\times1=8$; $9>8$, so $72\div8>8\times1$.

c) $8\times8=64$ and $4\times16=64$, so the two sides are equal.

**Answer.** a) $<$; b) $>$; c) $=$.
:::

:::exercise
Tam wrote: "$8\times7=54$". Where did Tam go wrong? Correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** $8\times7=8\times6+8$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $8\times7$ again from the product before.
2. Check with a division.

::solution
$8\times7=8\times6+8=48+8=56$, not $54$.

**Check.** $56\div8=7$. Correct.

**Answer.** Tam remembered the wrong product; $8\times7=56$.
:::

:::exercise
a) Each spider has $8$ legs. How many legs do $9$ spiders have?

b) $64$ mangoes are put into baskets, $8$ mangoes in each basket. How many baskets are filled?

::hint
**What you need:** equal groups: to find the total, multiply; to find the number of groups, divide.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Choose the operation, work it out and write the answer with its unit.

::solution
a) The number of legs is $8\times9=72$ (legs).

b) The number of baskets is $64\div8=8$ (baskets).

**Answer.** a) $72$ legs; b) $8$ baskets.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
$80$ marbles are put into bags, $8$ marbles in each bag. Nam has already filled $6$ bags. Find:

a) How many bags are needed altogether?

b) How many bags are still to fill, and how many marbles are still to be bagged?

::hint
**What you need:** the number of bags is $80\div8$; the marbles already bagged are $8\times6$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the total number of bags.
2. Subtract the bags filled, then work out the marbles left.

::solution
a) The number of bags needed is $80\div8=10$ (bags).

b) The bags still to fill: $10-6=4$ (bags). The marbles already bagged: $8\times6=48$, so the marbles left are $80-48=32$. Check: $8\times4=32$.

**Answer.** a) $10$ bags; b) $4$ bags and $32$ marbles.
:::
