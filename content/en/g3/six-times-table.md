:::goals
- Know and build the 6 times table.
- Use the 6 times table to divide by 6.
- Solve a word problem with one multiplication or one division.
:::

One tray holds $6$ eggs. Mum buys $4$ such trays. How many eggs does she buy altogether?

You could add $6+6+6+6$, but adding many times is easy to get wrong. This lesson helps you work it out quickly with the $6$ times table.

## Let's explore

Each tray holds $6$ eggs. We add one tray at a time:

- $1$ tray: $6$ eggs.
- $2$ trays: $6+6=12$ eggs.
- $3$ trays: $12+6=18$ eggs.
- $4$ trays: $18+6=24$ eggs.

So $4$ trays hold $24$ eggs, that is $6\times4=24$. Each new tray adds $6$ to the product.

Here is one more trick: $6$ is double $3$, so $6\times4$ is double $3\times4=12$, and $12+12=24$.

:::theorem The 6 times table
To work out $6\times n$, take $6\times(n-1)$ and add $6$. You can also take $3\times n$ and double it.

| $6\times1$ | $6\times2$ | $6\times3$ | $6\times4$ | $6\times5$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $6$ | $12$ | $18$ | $24$ | $30$ |

| $6\times6$ | $6\times7$ | $6\times8$ | $6\times9$ | $6\times10$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $36$ | $42$ | $48$ | $54$ | $60$ |
:::

The division facts for $6$ come from the $6$ times table. Since $6\times4=24$, we have $24\div6=4$. If you put $24$ eggs into trays of $6$, you fill $4$ trays.

## Examples

We start with the trick of doubling the $3$ times table.

:::example Work out $6\times4$ by doubling
**Solution.** We have $3\times4=12$. Since $6$ is double $3$, $6\times4$ is double $3\times4$:
$$6\times4=12+12=24.$$
So $6\times4=24$.
:::

Now take the product before and add $6$.

:::example Work out $6\times7$
**Solution.** You know $6\times6=36$. Add one more group of $6$:
$$6\times7=6\times6+6=36+6=42.$$
So $6\times7=42$.
:::

Sometimes it is easier to take the next product and subtract $6$.

:::example Work out $6\times9$
**Solution.** You know $6\times10=60$. Take away one group of $6$:
$$6\times9=6\times10-6=60-6=54.$$
So $6\times9=54$.
:::

Next, division.

:::example Work out $48\div6$
**Solution.** Ask: which number times $6$ makes $48$? In the $6$ times table, $6\times8=48$, so
$$48\div6=8.$$
**Check.** $6\times8=48$. Correct.

So $48\div6=8$.
:::

Finally, a word problem.

:::example Bags of sweets
There are $30$ sweets to put into bags, $6$ sweets in each bag. How many bags can we fill?

**Solution.** We split $30$ sweets into groups of $6$, so we divide. The number of bags is:
$$30\div6=5\ \text{(bags)}.$$
**Check.** $6\times5=30$. Correct.

So we can fill $5$ bags.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Taking the wrong neighbour.** Wrong: $6\times7=48$. Why it is wrong: $48$ is $6\times8$. Right: $6\times7=36+6=42$.
- **Mixing up multiplying and adding.** Wrong: $6\times6=12$. Why it is wrong: $6+6=12$ is an addition, but $6\times6$ means $6$ groups of $6$. Right: $6\times6=36$.
- **Dividing wrongly.** Wrong: $48\div6=7$. Why it is wrong: $6\times7=42$, not $48$. Right: $6\times8=48$, so $48\div6=8$.
- **Forgetting the unit.** Wrong: "Answer: $5$". Right: "Answer: $5$ bags".
:::

## Summary

- The $6$ times table: $6,\ 12,\ 18,\ 24,\ 30,\ 36,\ 42,\ 48,\ 54,\ 60$.
- Each new group adds $6$ to the product: $6\times n=6\times(n-1)+6$.
- $6$ is double $3$, so $6\times n$ is double $3\times n$.
- From $6\times a=b$ we get $b\div6=a$ and $b\div a=6$.
- Write the unit in the answer; check a division with a multiplication.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $6\times3$.

b) $6\times5$.

c) $6\times8$.

::hint
**What you need:** the $6$ times table: each new group adds $6$ to the product.

**Steps:**
1. Count in sixes: $6,\ 12,\ 18,\ \dots$
2. Stop at the number you need.

::solution
a) $6\times3=18$.

b) $6\times5=30$.

c) $6\times8=48$.

**Answer.** a) $18$; b) $30$; c) $48$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $6\times7$.

b) $6\times9$.

c) $6\times10$.

::hint
**What you need:** $6\times n=6\times(n-1)+6$.

**Steps:**
1. Remember the product before.
2. Add $6$.

::solution
a) $6\times7=36+6=42$.

b) $6\times9=48+6=54$.

c) $6\times10=54+6=60$.

**Answer.** a) $42$; b) $54$; c) $60$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the $4$ times table. Work out and compare:

a) $4\times6$.

b) $6\times4$.

c) $4\times9$ and $6\times6$.

::hint
**What you need:** swapping the two factors does not change the product.

**Steps:**
1. Work out each product with the $4$ or $6$ times table.
2. Compare the results.

::solution
a) $4\times6=24$.

b) $6\times4=24$, the same as $4\times6$.

c) $4\times9=36$ and $6\times6=36$, so the two products are equal.

**Answer.** a) $24$; b) $24$; c) $36$ and $36$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $18\div6$.

b) $42\div6$.

c) $60\div6$.

::hint
**What you need:** from $6\times a=b$ we get $b\div6=a$.

**Steps:**
1. Find the product equal to the dividend in the $6$ times table.
2. The other number is the quotient.

::solution
a) $6\times3=18$, so $18\div6=3$.

b) $6\times7=42$, so $42\div6=7$.

c) $6\times10=60$, so $60\div6=10$.

**Answer.** a) $3$; b) $7$; c) $10$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the box. Why?

a) $6\times5 \;\square\; 28$.

b) $36\div6 \;\square\; 6\times1$.

c) $6\times8 \;\square\; 6\times7+6$.

::hint
**What you need:** work out each side first, then compare the two numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Work out each side with the times table and division facts.
2. Compare the results.

::solution
a) $6\times5=30$ and $30>28$, so $6\times5>28$.

b) $36\div6=6$ and $6\times1=6$, so the two sides are equal.

c) $6\times8=48$ and $6\times7+6=42+6=48$, so the two sides are equal.

**Answer.** a) $>$; b) $=$; c) $=$.
:::

:::exercise
Hoa wrote: "$6\times7=48$". Where did Hoa go wrong? Correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** $6\times7=6\times6+6$.

**Steps:**
1. Which product is $48$? Look in the $6$ times table.
2. Work out $6\times7$ again from the product before.

::solution
$48$ is $6\times8$. We have $6\times7=6\times6+6=36+6=42$.

**Check.** $42\div6=7$. Correct.

**Answer.** Hoa took the next product by mistake; $6\times7=42$.
:::

:::exercise
a) Each tray holds $6$ eggs. How many eggs are on $8$ such trays?

b) $54$ eggs are put into trays of $6$. How many trays are filled?

::hint
**What you need:** equal groups: to find the total, multiply; to find the number of groups, divide.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Choose the operation, work it out and write the answer with its unit.

::solution
a) The number of eggs is $6\times8=48$ (eggs).

b) The number of trays is $54\div6=9$ (trays).

**Answer.** a) $48$ eggs; b) $9$ trays.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
$60$ pupils stand in rows of $6$. The teacher has already made $7$ rows. Find:

a) How many rows are needed altogether?

b) How many rows are still to make, and how many pupils are still to stand in a row?

::hint
**What you need:** the number of rows is $60\div6$; the pupils already placed are $6\times7$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the total number of rows.
2. Subtract the rows made, then work out the pupils left.

::solution
a) The number of rows needed is $60\div6=10$ (rows).

b) The rows still to make: $10-7=3$ (rows). The pupils already placed: $6\times7=42$, so the pupils left are $60-42=18$. Check: $6\times3=18$.

**Answer.** a) $10$ rows; b) $3$ rows and $18$ pupils.
:::
