:::goals
- Know and build the 9 times table.
- Use the 9 times table to divide by 9.
- Solve a word problem with one multiplication or one division.
:::

Each box holds $9$ coloured pencils. How many pencils are in $4$ such boxes?

You could add $9+9+9+9$, and that works. But with many more boxes, adding would take a long time. The $9$ times table is a shortcut: once you know it, the answer comes straight away, and there is a trick to help you remember it.

## Let's explore

Let's count up one box at a time. Each box is a group of $9$ pencils:

- $1$ box: $9$ pencils.
- $2$ boxes: $9+9=18$ pencils.
- $3$ boxes: $18+9=27$ pencils.
- $4$ boxes: $27+9=36$ pencils.

So $4$ boxes hold $36$ pencils, that is $9\times4=36$. Did you see it? Each new box adds $9$ to the product. That pattern is the heart of the whole table.

There is a quicker way, and it rests on something simple: $9=10-1$. Nine is just one less than ten, so $9\times4=10\times4-4=40-4=36$. Multiplying by ten is easy, and then you take away one group.

:::theorem The 9 times table
To work out $9\times n$, take $10\times n$ and subtract $n$. You can also take $9\times(n-1)$ and add $9$.

| $9\times1$ | $9\times2$ | $9\times3$ | $9\times4$ | $9\times5$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $9$ | $18$ | $27$ | $36$ | $45$ |

| $9\times6$ | $9\times7$ | $9\times8$ | $9\times9$ | $9\times10$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $54$ | $63$ | $72$ | $81$ | $90$ |
:::

The $9$ times table hides a small pattern. Every two-digit product has digits that add up to $9$. For example, $63$ has $6+3=9$. You can use this to check an answer quickly.

Once you can multiply, you can divide too. The division facts for $9$ come from the $9$ times table. Since $9\times4=36$, we have $36\div9=4$. Put into pencils: if you share $36$ pencils into boxes of $9$, you fill $4$ boxes.

## Examples

Let's try the trick from just now first: take $10$ times, then subtract.

:::example Work out $9\times4$
**Solution.** $9$ is one less than $10$, so $9\times4$ is $10\times4$ minus $4$:
$$9\times4=40-4=36.$$
So $9\times4=36$.
:::

Does the trick work with a bigger number? Let's see.

:::example Work out $9\times7$
**Solution.** Take $10\times7$ and subtract $7$:
$$9\times7=70-7=63.$$
**Check.** $6+3=9$, which fits the digit-sum trick.

So $9\times7=63$.
:::

There is another route: take the product just before, then add $9$. It suits you when you already remember the product just before.

:::example Work out $9\times8$
**Solution.** You know $9\times7=63$. Add one more group of $9$:
$$9\times8=9\times7+9=63+9=72.$$
So $9\times8=72$.
:::

If you can multiply, division is not far away. Let's try some.

:::example Work out $81\div9$
**Solution.** Ask: which number times $9$ makes $81$? In the $9$ times table, $9\times9=81$, so
$$81\div9=9.$$
**Check.** $9\times9=81$. Correct.

So $81\div9=9$.
:::

Finally, back to groups and boxes from everyday life, but this time we know the total number of pupils and ask for the number of groups.

:::example Groups of pupils
$72$ pupils are split into groups of $9$. How many groups are there?

**Solution.** We split $72$ pupils into groups of $9$, so we divide. The number of groups is:
$$72\div9=8\ \text{(groups)}.$$
**Check.** $9\times8=72$. Correct.

So there are $8$ groups.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Taking one group too few.** Wrong: $9\times6=45$. Why it is wrong: $45$ is $9\times5$, so you have only $5$ groups. Right: $9\times6=45+9=54$.
- **Adding instead of multiplying.** Wrong: $9\times3=12$. Why it is wrong: $9+3$ is an addition, but $9\times3$ means $3$ groups of $9$. Right: $9\times3=27$.
- **Dividing wrongly.** Wrong: $81\div9=8$. Why it is wrong: $9\times8=72$, not $81$. Right: $9\times9=81$, so $81\div9=9$.
- **Forgetting the unit.** Wrong: "Answer: $8$". Right: "Answer: $8$ groups".
:::

## Summary

- The $9$ times table: $9,\ 18,\ 27,\ 36,\ 45,\ 54,\ 63,\ 72,\ 81,\ 90$.
- Trick: $9\times n=10\times n-n$. Or $9\times n=9\times(n-1)+9$.
- Two-digit products in the $9$ times table have digits that add up to $9$.
- From $9\times a=b$ we get $b\div9=a$ and $b\div a=9$.
- Write the unit in the answer; check a division with a multiplication.
Back to the opening question: instead of adding box by box, take ten times and subtract, or read the answer straight from the table. Say the table aloud a few times each day and it will soon stay with you.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and a hint to get started. Try it yourself first. If you get stuck, read the hint, and only then open the solution to compare.

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $9\times3$.

b) $9\times5$.

c) $9\times6$.

::hint
**What you need:** the $9$ times table: each new group adds $9$ to the product.

**Steps:**
1. Count in nines: $9,\ 18,\ 27,\ \dots$
2. Stop at the number you need.

::solution
Solution

We use the $9$ times table.

a) $9\times3=27$.

b) $9\times5=45$.

c) $9\times6=54$.

*Check.* $9\times6=9\times5+9=45+9=54$. Correct.

So $9\times3=27$, $9\times5=45$ and $9\times6=54$.

**Answer.** a) $27$; b) $45$; c) $54$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $9\times7$.

b) $9\times9$.

c) $9\times10$.

::hint
**What you need:** $9\times n=10\times n-n$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $10\times n$.
2. Subtract $n$.

::solution
Solution

We can multiply by $9$ by multiplying by $10$ and then taking away one copy of the number.

a) $9\times7=10\times7-7=70-7=63$.

b) $9\times9=10\times9-9=90-9=81$.

c) $9\times10=10\times10-10=100-10=90$.

So $9\times7=63$, $9\times9=81$ and $9\times10=90$.

**Answer.** a) $63$; b) $81$; c) $90$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the $8$ times table. Work out and compare:

a) $8\times9$.

b) $9\times8$.

c) $8\times8$ and $9\times7$.

::hint
**What you need:** swapping the two factors does not change the product.

**Steps:**
1. Work out each product with the $8$ or $9$ times table.
2. Compare the results.

::solution
Solution

a) By the $8$ times table, $8\times9=72$.

b) By the $9$ times table, $9\times8=72$. This is the same as $8\times9$, because swapping the two factors does not change the product.

c) We have $8\times8=64$ and $9\times7=63$. Since $64>63$, we get $8\times8>9\times7$.

So $8\times9=72$, $9\times8=72$, and $8\times8=64$ is greater than $9\times7=63$.

**Answer.** a) $72$; b) $72$; c) $64$ and $63$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $27\div9$.

b) $63\div9$.

c) $90\div9$.

::hint
**What you need:** from $9\times a=b$ we get $b\div9=a$.

**Steps:**
1. Find the product equal to the dividend in the $9$ times table.
2. The other number is the quotient.

::solution
Solution

To find each quotient, we recall the matching multiplication fact in the $9$ times table.

a) Since $9\times3=27$, we get $27\div9=3$.

b) Since $9\times7=63$, we get $63\div9=7$.

c) Since $9\times10=90$, we get $90\div9=10$.

So $27\div9=3$, $63\div9=7$ and $90\div9=10$.

**Answer.** a) $3$; b) $7$; c) $10$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the box. Why?

a) $9\times6 \;\square\; 50$.

b) $45\div9 \;\square\; 9\times1$.

c) $9\times8 \;\square\; 8\times9$.

::hint
**What you need:** work out each side first, then compare the two numbers.

**Steps:**
1. Work out each side with the times table and division facts.
2. Compare the results.

::solution
Solution

We work out the value of each side and then compare.

a) We have $9\times6=54$. Since $54>50$, we get $9\times6>50$.

b) We have $45\div9=5$ (because $9\times5=45$) and $9\times1=9$. Since $5<9$, we get $45\div9<9\times1$.

c) We have $9\times8=72$ and $8\times9=72$. The two sides are equal, because swapping the factors does not change the product. So $9\times8=8\times9$.

So the signs to write are $>$, $<$ and $=$ in that order.

**Answer.** a) $>$; b) $<$; c) $=$.
:::

:::exercise
Khoa wrote: "$9\times6=45$". Where did Khoa go wrong? Correct it.

::hint
**What you need:** $9\times6=9\times5+9$.

**Steps:**
1. $45$ is $9$ times which number?
2. Work out $9\times6$ again from the product before.

::solution
Solution

We check with the $9$ times table. We have $45=9\times5$, so $45$ is the result of $5$ groups of $9$, not $6$ groups of $9$. Khoa is missing one group of $9$.

The correct working is $9\times6=9\times5+9=45+9=54$.

*Check.* $54\div9=6$. Correct.

So Khoa went wrong by leaving out one group of $9$; the correct result is $9\times6=54$.

**Answer.** Khoa was one group short; $9\times6=54$.
:::

:::exercise
a) Each box holds $9$ coloured pencils. How many pencils are in $8$ boxes?

b) $81$ sweets are put into bags, $9$ sweets in each bag. How many bags are filled?

::hint
**What you need:** equal groups: to find the total, multiply; to find the number of groups, divide.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Choose the operation, work it out and write the answer with its unit.

::solution
Solution

a) Each box holds $9$ coloured pencils, so the number of pencils in $8$ boxes is:
$$9\times8=72\ \text{(pencils)}.$$

b) There are $81$ sweets and each bag holds $9$ sweets, so the number of bags filled is:
$$81\div9=9\ \text{(bags)}.$$

*Check.* $9\times9=81$. Correct.

So $8$ boxes hold $72$ pencils and $9$ bags of sweets are filled.

**Answer.** a) $72$ pencils; b) $9$ bags.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Use the digit-sum trick.

a) Which of the numbers $36,\ 45,\ 48,\ 72,\ 75$ are products in the $9$ times table?

b) Divide each number you found in part a) by $9$.

::hint
**What you need:** a two-digit product in the $9$ times table has digits that add up to $9$.

**Steps:**
1. Add the two digits of each number.
2. Pick the numbers whose digits add up to $9$, then divide them by $9$ with the division facts.

::solution
Solution

Trick: a number in the $9$ times table (less than $100$) has a digit sum equal to $9$.

a) We find the digit sum of each number:
- $36$: $3+6=9$.
- $45$: $4+5=9$.
- $48$: $4+8=12$, not $9$.
- $72$: $7+2=9$.
- $75$: $7+5=12$, not $9$.

So $36,\ 45,\ 72$ are products in the $9$ times table.

b) We divide each number we found by $9$:
$$36\div9=4,\qquad 45\div9=5,\qquad 72\div9=8.$$

*Check.* $9\times4=36$, $9\times5=45$, $9\times8=72$. Correct.

So $36,\ 45,\ 72$ are products in the $9$ times table, and dividing them by $9$ gives $4,\ 5,\ 8$ respectively.

**Answer.** a) $36,\ 45,\ 72$; b) $4,\ 5,\ 8$.
:::
