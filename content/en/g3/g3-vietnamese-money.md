- Know the Vietnamese banknotes and their values.

Nam has one $20\,000$ dong note. He buys a notebook that costs $12\,000$ dong and hands the note to the shopkeeper. How much change should the shopkeeper give Nam?

You have learned to add and subtract numbers up to $100\,000$. This lesson uses the same calculations to work with money.

## Let's explore

In Vietnam, the unit of money is the **dong**. You often see the banknotes in the table below. The right-hand column shows how many **thousand** dong each note is worth.

| Banknote (dong) | Worth in thousands of dong |
|---|---|
| $1\,000$ | $1$ thousand |
| $2\,000$ | $2$ thousand |
| $5\,000$ | $5$ thousand |
| $10\,000$ | $10$ thousand |
| $20\,000$ | $20$ thousand |
| $50\,000$ | $50$ thousand |
| $100\,000$ | $100$ thousand |

We start with a small example. Mum gives Nam two $5\,000$ dong notes and one $2\,000$ dong note. How much money does Nam have altogether?

- Each $5\,000$ dong note is $5$ thousand. Two notes make $5+5=10$ thousand.
- The $2\,000$ dong note is $2$ thousand.
- Add them up: $10+2=12$ thousand.

So Nam has $12$ thousand dong, which is $12\,000$ dong. See: we add the small numbers first and only then put the word "thousand" back.

Now go back to Nam's problem at the start of the lesson. Nam hands over $20$ thousand dong and the notebook costs $12$ thousand dong. Look at the number line: how many thousand do you need to go from $12$ up to $20$?

:::graph On a number line (unit: thousand dong): from 12 to 20 you need 8 more
alt: A number line from 0 to 24 in thousands of dong, with the points 12 and 20 marked; the part from 12 to 20 is 8 thousand long
x: 0..24
y: -1..1
segment: (12, 0) (20, 0)
point: (12, 0) "12" below
point: (20, 0) "20" below
label: (16, 0) "+8" above
:::

From $12$ to $20$ you need $8$ thousand more. You can also subtract: $20-12=8$ thousand. So the shopkeeper gives Nam $8\,000$ dong change.

:::theorem Working with money
- **The money in several notes:** change each note into thousands of dong, add them up, then write "thousand dong".
- **The total cost of several items:** add the prices of the items.
- **Change** $=$ money handed over $-$ total cost.
:::

Change is a subtraction because it is the extra money left after you have paid the full cost. You can always check: total cost $+$ change must equal the money handed over. Above, $12+8=20$, which is right.

## Examples

We start by counting a few banknotes.

:::example Counting money
Mai has one $10\,000$ dong note, one $5\,000$ dong note and two $2\,000$ dong notes. How much money does Mai have?

**Solution.** Change each note into thousands of dong and add:
$$10+5+2+2=19\ \text{(thousand dong)}.$$
So Mai has $19\,000$ dong.

**Check.** $10\,000+5\,000+2\,000+2\,000=19\,000$. It matches.
:::

The next example has more notes, but the method is the same.

:::example Counting more notes
Ha has two $20\,000$ dong notes, one $5\,000$ dong note, one $2\,000$ dong note and one $1\,000$ dong note. How much money does Ha have?

**Solution.** Two $20$ thousand notes make $20+20=40$ thousand. Add the other notes:
$$40+5+2+1=48\ \text{(thousand dong)}.$$
So Ha has $48\,000$ dong.
:::

Now work out the total cost when you buy several items.

:::example Total cost
Nam buys an exercise book for $7\,000$ dong, a pen for $4\,000$ dong and a rubber for $2\,000$ dong. How much must Nam pay?

**Solution.** Paying for all three items means putting the three prices together, so you add:
$$7\,000+4\,000+2\,000=13\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
So Nam must pay $13\,000$ dong.
:::

Once you know the total cost, you can work out the change.

:::example Change
Ms Lan buys vegetables for $35\,000$ dong and hands the seller a $50\,000$ dong note. How much change should the seller give her?

**Solution.** The change is the money handed over minus the total cost:
$$50\,000-35\,000=15\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
So the seller gives Ms Lan $15\,000$ dong change.

**Check.** $35\,000+15\,000=50\,000$. It matches the money handed over.
:::

Last, a problem where you compare the money you have with the money you need.

:::example Is there enough money?
Lan has $30\,000$ dong. She wants to buy a teddy bear for $18\,000$ dong and a pencil case for $15\,000$ dong. Does Lan have enough money? If not, how much more does she need?

**Solution.** First work out the total cost of the two items:
$$18\,000+15\,000=33\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
Since $33\,000>30\,000$, Lan does not have enough money. She is short by:
$$33\,000-30\,000=3\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
So Lan does not have enough money; she needs $3\,000$ dong more.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Missing a note when counting.** Wrong: with two $5\,000$ dong notes and one $2\,000$ dong note, working out $5\,000+2\,000=7\,000$ dong. Why it is wrong: you left out one $5\,000$ dong note. Right: $5\,000+5\,000+2\,000=12\,000$ dong.
- **Adding instead of subtracting for the change.** Wrong: $20\,000+12\,000=32\,000$ dong. Why it is wrong: change is the extra money, so it can never be more than the money handed over. Right: $20\,000-12\,000=8\,000$ dong.
- **Forgetting to turn thousands back into dong.** Wrong: "Answer: $12$ dong". Why it is wrong: $12$ thousand dong is $12\,000$ dong. Right: "Answer: $12\,000$ dong".
:::

## Summary

- The unit of Vietnamese money is the **dong**. There are $1\,000$, $2\,000$, $5\,000$, $10\,000$, $20\,000$, $50\,000$ and $100\,000$ dong notes.
- To count money, change each note into thousands of dong and add. Example: $5+5+2=12$ thousand, which is $12\,000$ dong.
- Total cost: add the prices of the items. Change $=$ money handed over $-$ total cost.
- You can check with an addition: total cost $+$ change $=$ money handed over. Remember to write the unit dong.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Count the money:

a) One $5\,000$ dong note and one $2\,000$ dong note.

b) One $10\,000$ dong note, one $2\,000$ dong note and one $1\,000$ dong note.

::hint
**What you need:** change each note into thousands of dong and add.

**Steps:**
1. Write how many thousand each note is.
2. Add them up, then write "thousand dong".

::solution
a) $5+2=7$ thousand dong, which is $7\,000$ dong.

b) $10+2+1=13$ thousand dong, which is $13\,000$ dong.

**Answer.** a) $7\,000$ dong; b) $13\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
a) You have two $20\,000$ dong notes and one $10\,000$ dong note. How much money do you have?

b) A storybook costs $15\,000$ dong and a pencil costs $3\,000$ dong. How much do you pay for both?

::hint
**What you need:** to count money, change into thousands and add; for the total cost of several items, add their prices.

**Steps:**
1. Part a): $20+20+10$ thousand.
2. Part b): $15\,000+3\,000$.

::solution
a) $20+20+10=50$ thousand dong, which is $50\,000$ dong.

b) $15\,000+3\,000=18\,000$ (dong).

**Answer.** a) $50\,000$ dong; b) $18\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson (telling the time, months and years).

a) Nam arrives at the shop at $8{:}15$ and finishes shopping $10$ minutes later. At what time does he finish?

b) Nam goes to buy books in month $5$ (May). How many days does May have?

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ hour has $60$ minutes; months $1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 10, 12$ have $31$ days.

**Steps:**
1. Part a): add $10$ minutes to $15$ minutes.
2. Part b): see which group of months May belongs to.

::solution
a) $15+10=25$, so Nam finishes at $8{:}25$.

b) May is one of the months with $31$ days.

**Answer.** a) $8{:}25$; b) $31$ days.
:::

:::exercise
a) Mum hands over $50\,000$ dong to buy things costing $28\,000$ dong. How much change should the seller give her?

b) Dad hands over $20\,000$ dong and gets $6\,000$ dong change. How much did his shopping cost?

::hint
**What you need:** change $=$ money handed over $-$ total cost. So total cost $=$ money handed over $-$ change.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Part a): find the change. Part b): find the total cost.
3. Check with an addition.

::solution
a) $50\,000-28\,000=22\,000$ (dong).

b) $20\,000-6\,000=14\,000$ (dong).

**Check.** Part a): $28\,000+22\,000=50\,000$. Part b): $14\,000+6\,000=20\,000$.

**Answer.** a) $22\,000$ dong; b) $14\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
Choose the operation (addition or subtraction) and say why.

a) Mai has $25\,000$ dong and her aunt gives her $10\,000$ dong more. How much money does Mai have altogether?

b) Mai has $35\,000$ dong and buys a book for $28\,000$ dong. How much money does Mai have left?

::hint
**What you need:** "gives more" and "altogether" mean add; "buys" and "left" mean subtract.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Choose the operation and work it out.

::solution
a) Her aunt gives her more money, so the amount goes up and we add: $25\,000+10\,000=35\,000$ (dong).

b) Mai pays out part of her money, so the amount goes down and we subtract: $35\,000-28\,000=7\,000$ (dong).

**Answer.** a) $35\,000$ dong; b) $7\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
Find the mistake in each pupil's work and correct it.

a) Ha: "I have two $10\,000$ dong notes and one $5\,000$ dong note. I have $10\,000+5\,000=15\,000$ (dong)."

b) Tu: "Tu hands over $50\,000$ dong and the shopping costs $31\,000$ dong. The change is $50\,000+31\,000=81\,000$ (dong)."

::hint
**What you need:** count every note; change is the money handed over minus the total cost.

**Steps:**
1. Part a): count how many notes there are and how many the working adds.
2. Part b): can the change be more than the money handed over?

::solution
a) Ha only added one $10\,000$ dong note and left the other out. Done correctly: $10\,000+10\,000+5\,000=25\,000$ (dong).

b) Tu added instead of subtracting. The change cannot be more than the money handed over. Done correctly: $50\,000-31\,000=19\,000$ (dong).

**Answer.** a) $25\,000$ dong; b) $19\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
A breakfast shop's price list:

| Item | Price (dong) |
|---|---|
| Bread roll | $12\,000$ |
| Milk | $8\,000$ |
| Juice | $15\,000$ |

An buys one bread roll and one carton of milk, and hands over a $50\,000$ dong note.

a) What is the total cost?

b) How much change does the seller give An?

::hint
**What you need:** the total cost is the sum of the prices; change $=$ money handed over $-$ total cost.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked? Find the prices of the bread roll and the milk in the table.
2. Part a): add the two prices. Part b): take the total cost away from $50\,000$.
3. Check with an addition.

::solution
a) Total cost: $12\,000+8\,000=20\,000$ (dong).

b) Change: $50\,000-20\,000=30\,000$ (dong).

**Check.** $20\,000+30\,000=50\,000$. It matches.

**Answer.** a) $20\,000$ dong; b) $30\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Hung has one $20\,000$ dong note, one $5\,000$ dong note and three $2\,000$ dong notes.

a) How much money does Hung have?

b) Hung wants to buy swimming goggles that cost $45\,000$ dong. How much more money does he need?

c) Mum gives Hung another $20\,000$ dong note. Does he now have enough money for the goggles? If so, how much money is left over?

::hint
**What you need:** count money by adding the thousands; compare the money you have with the price; the amount short or left over is the difference of the two numbers.

**Steps:**
1. How many thousand are three $2\,000$ dong notes?
2. Work out how much Hung has, then take it away from $45\,000$.
3. Part c): add $20\,000$, then compare with $45\,000$.

::solution
a) $20+5+2+2+2=31$ thousand dong, which is $31\,000$ dong.

b) $45\,000-31\,000=14\,000$ (dong).

c) After Mum's gift, Hung has $31\,000+20\,000=51\,000$ (dong). Since $51\,000>45\,000$, he has enough money. The money left over is $51\,000-45\,000=6\,000$ (dong).

**Answer.** a) $31\,000$ dong; b) he needs $14\,000$ dong more; c) yes, with $6\,000$ dong left over.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
One $10\,000$ dong note and two $5\,000$ dong notes make
A. $15\,000$ dong.
B. $20\,000$ dong.
C. $17\,000$ dong.
D. $25\,000$ dong.
::key B
$10\,000+5\,000+5\,000=20\,000$ dong. Option A leaves out one $5\,000$ note; option D adds one $5\,000$ note too many.

::mc
Mum hands over $50\,000$ dong for shopping that costs $32\,000$ dong. The seller gives her change of
A. $82\,000$ dong.
B. $22\,000$ dong.
C. $18\,000$ dong.
D. $28\,000$ dong.
::key C
$50\,000-32\,000=18\,000$. Option A adds instead of subtracting; option B is a subtraction slip ($50-32$ is not $22$).

::mc
An buys three items costing $9\,000$ dong, $6\,000$ dong and $4\,000$ dong. The total cost is
A. $10\,000$ dong.
B. $13\,000$ dong.
C. $15\,000$ dong.
D. $19\,000$ dong.
::key D
$9\,000+6\,000+4\,000=19\,000$. Options B, C and A add only two of the items ($9+4$, $9+6$, $6+4$).

::mc
Lan has $40\,000$ dong and wants to buy two items costing $25\,000$ dong and $18\,000$ dong. Which statement is true?
A. Lan does not have enough money; she is $3\,000$ dong short.
B. Lan has enough money with $15\,000$ dong left over.
C. Lan has enough money with $3\,000$ dong left over.
D. Lan does not have enough money; she is $43\,000$ dong short.
::key A
The total cost is $25\,000+18\,000=43\,000$. Since $43\,000>40\,000$, Lan is $43\,000-40\,000=3\,000$ dong short. Option B only counts one item; option D takes the total cost as the amount short.

::tf
Nam has one $20\,000$ dong note, one $10\,000$ dong note and one $5\,000$ dong note.
a) Nam has $35\,000$ dong.
b) Nam can buy something that costs $40\,000$ dong.
c) If Nam buys something for $28\,000$ dong, he gets $7\,000$ dong change.
d) If Nam hands over all three notes for something costing $30\,000$ dong, he gets $15\,000$ dong change.
::key T F T F
a) True: $20+10+5=35$ thousand. b) False: $35\,000<40\,000$, so he does not have enough. c) True: $35\,000-28\,000=7\,000$. d) False: $35\,000-30\,000=5\,000$, not $15\,000$.
