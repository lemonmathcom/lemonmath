:::goals
- Understand that speed tells how far an object travels each hour (or second), and find speed $v=s\div t$.
- Find distance $s=v\times t$ and time $t=s\div v$ in uniform motion.
- Use the units km/h and m/s correctly, and change a time to the same unit as the speed.
- Solve word problems about uniform motion.
:::

A car travels $150$ km in $3$ hours. On average, how many kilometres does it travel each hour? If it keeps going at that speed, how far does it travel in $5$ hours?

On a real road a vehicle speeds up and slows down. In this lesson we pretend it moves at the same speed all the way. We call this **uniform motion**.

## Speed in uniform motion

The car travels $150$ km in $3$ hours. If it moves uniformly, it covers the same number of kilometres each hour:
$$150\div3=50\ (\text{km}).$$
We say the speed of the car is $50$ km/h, read "fifty kilometres per hour". Let's draw the distance against the time: the points lie on a straight line, because the car covers another $50$ km each hour.

:::theorem Speed
The speed of an object in uniform motion is the distance it travels in one unit of time. To find the speed, divide the distance by the time:
$$v=s\div t.$$
If $s$ is in km and $t$ is in hours, then $v$ is in km/h; if $s$ is in m and $t$ is in seconds, then $v$ is in m/s.
:::

:::example Finding a speed
A motorbike travels $90$ km in $2$ hours. Find its speed.

**Solution.** Using $v=s\div t$, we have:
$$v=90\div2=45\ (\text{km/h}).$$
So the speed of the motorbike is $45$ km/h.
:::

:::example Running in the school yard
A pupil runs $60$ m in $12$ seconds. Find the pupil's speed in m/s.

**Solution.** The distance is in metres and the time is in seconds, so the speed is in m/s:
$$v=60\div12=5\ (\text{m/s}).$$
So the pupil's speed is $5$ m/s.
:::

## Distance and time in uniform motion

From $v=s\div t$ we get the other two formulas. Each hour the vehicle travels $v$ km, so in $t$ hours it travels $v\times t$ km. To find how long a journey takes, we see how many times the speed fits into the distance.

:::theorem Distance and time
$$s=v\times t,\qquad t=s\div v.$$
Remember that speed, distance and time must use matching units. If the speed is in km/h, the time must be in hours.
:::

Now we answer the question at the start.

:::example The car's distance
A car travels $150$ km in $3$ hours. If it keeps going at that speed, how many kilometres does it travel in $5$ hours?

**Solution.** The speed of the car is:
$$150\div3=50\ (\text{km/h}).$$
The distance the car travels in $5$ hours is:
$$50\times5=250\ (\text{km}).$$
**Answer.** $50$ km/h; $250$ km.
:::

:::example Time on a bicycle
A cyclist rides at $12$ km/h. How long does the cyclist take to ride $30$ km?

**Solution.** The time taken is:
$$30\div12=2.5\ (\text{hours}).$$
Since $0.5$ hour $=30$ minutes, $2.5$ hours $=2$ hours $30$ minutes.

**Answer.** $2$ hours $30$ minutes.
:::

When the time is given in hours and minutes, change it to hours first.

:::example A bus
A bus travels at $40$ km/h for $1$ hour $30$ minutes. Find the distance it travels.

**Solution.** We change $1$ h $30$ min $=1.5$ h. The distance is:
$$s=40\times1.5=60\ (\text{km}).$$
**Check.** In $1$ hour the bus goes $40$ km, in half an hour it goes $20$ km, and $40+20=60$. Correct.

So the bus travels $60$ km.
:::

The next problem has more information, so we use *Understand, Plan, Solve, Look back*.

:::example A journey with a rest
A motorcyclist leaves at $6{:}15$, travels $120$ km at $40$ km/h and rests for $30$ minutes on the way. At what time does the motorcyclist arrive?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* The distance is $120$ km, the speed is $40$ km/h, the rest lasts $30$ min and the start is $6{:}15$. We need the arrival time.

*Plan.* Find the travelling time, add the rest, then add the total to the start time.

*Solve.* The travelling time is $120\div40=3$ (hours). The whole journey takes $3$ h $+$ $30$ min $=3$ h $30$ min. The arrival time is:
$$6\text{ h }15\text{ min}+3\text{ h }30\text{ min}=9\text{ h }45\text{ min}.$$
*Look back.* Without the rest, the arrival time would be $9{:}15$. At $60$ km/h the travelling time would be $120\div60=2$ hours, which is $1$ hour earlier. The greater the speed, the less time it takes.

**Answer.** $9{:}45$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Dividing the wrong way round.** Wrong: $v=3\div150=0.02$ km/h. Why it is wrong: the distance is the number being divided. Right: $150\div3=50$ km/h.
- **Not changing the time to hours.** Wrong: $40$ km/h for $30$ minutes gives $s=40\times30=1200$ km. Why it is wrong: $30$ minutes is $0.5$ hour. Right: $40\times0.5=20$ km.
- **Writing the wrong unit.** Wrong: "The speed of the car is $50$ km". Why it is wrong: km is a unit of distance. Right: $50$ km/h.
:::

## Summary

- Speed is the distance travelled in one unit of time: $v=s\div t$.
- Distance $s=v\times t$; time $t=s\div v$.
- Units must match: km and hours give km/h; m and seconds give m/s.
- If the time is in hours and minutes, change it to hours before calculating ($30$ minutes $=0.5$ hour).

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
A motorbike travels $135$ km in $3$ hours. Find its speed.

::hint
**What you need:** $v=s\div t$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. Divide the distance by the time.

::solution
The speed of the motorbike is:
$$135\div3=45\ (\text{km/h}).$$
**Answer.** $45$ km/h.
:::

:::exercise
A snail crawls $3$ m in $5$ minutes. Find the snail's speed in m/min.

::hint
**What you need:** $v=s\div t$; the distance is in m and the time is in minutes.

**Steps:**
1. Divide $3$ by $5$.
2. Write the unit m/min.

::solution
$$v=3\div5=0.6\ (\text{m/min}).$$
**Answer.** $0.6$ m/min.
:::

:::exercise
A car travels at $54$ km/h for $2.5$ hours. Find the distance it travels.

::hint
**What you need:** $s=v\times t$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. Multiply the speed by the time.
3. Check: $54\times2=108$, and half an hour more is $27$ km.

::solution
The distance the car travels is:
$$54\times2.5=135\ (\text{km}).$$
**Answer.** $135$ km.
:::

:::exercise
A walker goes at $5$ km/h. How long does the walker take to cover $12$ km? (Give the answer in hours and minutes.)

::hint
**What you need:** $t=s\div v$; $0.1$ hour $=6$ minutes.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $12\div5$.
2. Change the decimal part of the hour to minutes.

::solution
The time is $12\div5=2.4$ (hours). Since $0.4\times60=24$, $2.4$ hours $=2$ hours $24$ minutes.

**Answer.** $2$ hours $24$ minutes.
:::

:::exercise
A fishing boat travels at $18$ km/h from $6{:}00$ to $8{:}30$. How many kilometres does the boat travel?

::hint
**What you need:** $s=v\times t$; travelling time $=$ arrival time $-$ departure time.

**Steps:**
1. Find the travelling time: $2$ h $30$ min $=2.5$ h.
2. Multiply by the speed.

::solution
The travelling time is $8$ h $30$ min $-$ $6$ h $=2$ h $30$ min $=2.5$ h. The distance is:
$$18\times2.5=45\ (\text{km}).$$
**Answer.** $45$ km.
:::

:::exercise
A motorcyclist covers $100$ km in $2$ hours $30$ minutes. Find the speed of the motorcyclist.

::hint
**What you need:** change $2$ h $30$ min to hours, then divide.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. $2$ h $30$ min $=2.5$ h.
3. Check: does $40\times2.5$ equal $100$?

::solution
We have $2$ h $30$ min $=2.5$ h. The speed is:
$$100\div2.5=40\ (\text{km/h}).$$
**Answer.** $40$ km/h.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on adding and subtracting times. A vehicle leaves at $7{:}45$ and travels for $2$ h $30$ min at $48$ km/h to reach B. Find the distance AB and the time of arrival at B.

::hint
**What you need:** $s=v\times t$; arrival time $=$ departure time $+$ travelling time.

**Steps:**
1. Change $2$ h $30$ min $=2.5$ h.
2. Find the distance, then the arrival time.

::solution
The distance AB is $48\times2.5=120$ (km). The vehicle arrives at:
$$7\text{ h }45\text{ min}+2\text{ h }30\text{ min}=9\text{ h }75\text{ min}=10\text{ h }15\text{ min}.$$
**Answer.** $120$ km; $10{:}15$.
:::

:::exercise
Car A travels $120$ km in $3$ hours. Car B travels $150$ km in $4$ hours. Which car is faster, and by how many kilometres per hour?

::hint
**What you need:** compare the speeds of the two cars.

**Steps:**
1. Find the speed of each car.
2. Compare, then find the difference.

::solution
The speed of car A is $120\div3=40$ (km/h). The speed of car B is $150\div4=37.5$ (km/h). Since $40>37.5$, car A is faster, by $40-37.5=2.5$ (km/h).

**Answer.** Car A is faster, by $2.5$ km/h.
:::

:::exercise
An cycles from home to school at $12$ km/h and takes $20$ minutes. Binh walks from home to school (the same distance) at $4$ km/h. How long does Binh take?

::hint
**What you need:** find the distance from home to school first; $20$ min $=\dfrac13$ hour.

**Steps:**
1. Find the distance using $s=v\times t$ with An's data.
2. Find Binh's time using $t=s\div v$.

::solution
Since $20$ min $=\dfrac13$ hour, the distance from home to school is:
$$12\times\frac13=4\ (\text{km}).$$
Binh's time is $4\div4=1$ (hour).

**Answer.** $1$ hour.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A girl runs at $5$ m/s. If she keeps this speed, how many kilometres does she run in one hour? So how many km/h is $5$ m/s?

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ hour $=3600$ seconds; $1$ km $=1000$ m.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the number of metres run in $3600$ seconds.
2. Change metres to kilometres.

::solution
In $1$ hour $=3600$ seconds she runs $5\times3600=18\,000$ (m) $=18$ (km). So $5$ m/s $=18$ km/h.

**Answer.** $18$ km; $5$ m/s $=18$ km/h.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
A car travels $160$ km in $4$ hours. Its speed is
A. $40$ km/h.
B. $0.025$ km/h.
C. $640$ km/h.
D. $164$ km/h.
::key A
$v=160\div4=40$ km/h. Option C multiplies instead of dividing, B divides the wrong way round ($4\div160$) and D adds the two numbers.

::mc
A vehicle travels at $45$ km/h for $2$ hours $30$ minutes. The distance it travels is
A. $103.5$ km.
B. $112.5$ km.
C. $90$ km.
D. $18$ km.
::key B
$2$ h $30$ min $=2.5$ h, so $s=45\times2.5=112.5$ km. Option A treats $30$ minutes as $0.3$ hour, C ignores the $30$ minutes and D is $45\div2.5$.

::mc
A distance is $36$ km and a cyclist's speed is $12$ km/h. The time taken is
A. $432$ hours.
B. $48$ hours.
C. $3$ hours.
D. $24$ hours.
::key C
$t=36\div12=3$ hours. Option A multiplies, B adds and D subtracts the two given numbers.

::mc
The distance AB is $90$ km. A vehicle leaves A at $7{:}15$ at $45$ km/h. It arrives at B at
A. $7{:}45$.
B. $7{:}17$.
C. $8{:}45$.
D. $9{:}15$.
::key D
$t=90\div45=2$ hours, so the vehicle arrives at $9{:}15$. Option A divides the wrong way round ($45\div90=0.5$ hour), C treats $90$ km as $90$ minutes and B adds $2$ minutes instead of $2$ hours.

::tf
A motorbike travels $90$ km in $2$ hours $30$ minutes.
a) The speed of the motorbike is $45$ km/h.
b) The speed of the motorbike is $36$ km/h.
c) At that speed, the motorbike travels $54$ km in $1$ hour $30$ minutes.
d) At that speed, the motorbike takes $4$ hours to travel $144$ km.
::key F T T T
a) False: $2$ h $30$ min is not $2$ hours, so $90\div2=45$ is wrong. b) True: $90\div2.5=36$. c) True: $36\times1.5=54$. d) True: $144\div36=4$.

::sa
A car travels for $2.5$ hours at $48$ km/h. How many kilometres does it travel?
::key 120
$s=48\times2.5=120$ (km).

::sa
A distance is $18$ km and a walker's speed is $4$ km/h. How many hours does the walker take?
::key 4.5
$t=18\div4=4.5$ (hours).
