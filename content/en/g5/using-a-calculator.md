:::goals
- Know the basic keys of a calculator and enter decimals.
- Use a calculator to add, subtract, multiply and divide decimals and to find percentages.
- Round the result shown on the display as required.
- Use estimates to check a result and spot a wrong key press.
:::

Mum buys $4.75$ kg of meat at $135\,000$ dong per kilogram (made-up figures). How much does she have to pay?

The multiplication $4.75\times135\,000$ has many digits, so it takes a while by hand. Use a calculator, and remember to check whether the result is reasonable.

## Let's explore

A calculator has these main keys:

- the digit keys from **[0]** to **[9]**;
- the decimal point key **[.]**;
- the operation keys **[+]**, **[−]**, **[×]**, **[÷]**;
- the **[=]** key to show the result and the **[AC]** (or **[C]**) key to clear.

To work out $4.75\times135\,000$, press in order: **[4] [.] [7] [5] [×] [1] [3] [5] [0] [0] [0] [=]**. The display shows $641250$.

Now estimate to check: $4.75$ is close to $5$ and $135\,000$ is close to $130\,000$, and $5\times130\,000=650\,000$, which is close to $641\,250$. The result is reasonable.

:::theorem Steps for using a calculator
1. Switch the calculator on and press **[AC]** to clear any old number.
2. Enter the numbers and operations in the order given in the problem; use the decimal point key for decimals.
3. Press **[=]** and read the result on the display.
4. Check the result with an estimate; round it if the problem asks you to.
:::

Why estimate? A calculator only does what you press. If you leave out a decimal point or press a wrong digit, it still shows a result, but the result is wrong. An estimate helps you spot mistakes straight away.

**Connection.** A calculator speeds up the calculations you have learnt: multiplying and dividing decimals, percentages, and the circumference and area of a circle. You still need to understand the method so that you know what to press and how to check the result.

## Examples

We start with multiplying two decimals.

:::example Work out $12.5\times3.6$
**Solution.** Press: **[1] [2] [.] [5] [×] [3] [.] [6] [=]**. The display shows $45$.

Estimate: $12\times4=48$, which is close to $45$. The result is reasonable.

So $12.5\times3.6=45$.
:::

Next, adding and subtracting numbers with many digits.

:::example Work out
a) $1234.5+678.9$; b) $1000-356.75$.

**Solution.** a) Press: **[1] [2] [3] [4] [.] [5] [+] [6] [7] [8] [.] [9] [=]**, and the display shows $1913.4$.

b) Press: **[1] [0] [0] [0] [−] [3] [5] [6] [.] [7] [5] [=]**, and the display shows $643.25$.

Estimates: a) $1200+700=1900$; b) $1000-360=640$. Both results are reasonable.

So a) $1913.4$; b) $643.25$.
:::

Some divisions give a long result or never end, so you round as required.

:::example Work out
a) $17\div6$, rounded to two decimal places; b) $245.7\div3.5$.

**Solution.** a) Press: **[1] [7] [÷] [6] [=]**, and the display shows $2.8333333$. The thousandths digit is $3<5$, so rounding to two decimal places gives $2.83$.

b) Press: **[2] [4] [5] [.] [7] [÷] [3] [.] [5] [=]**, and the display shows $70.2$.

So a) $17\div6\approx2.83$; b) $245.7\div3.5=70.2$.
:::

Now a real-life problem, solved in four steps.

:::example Buying meat
Suppose Mum buys $4.75$ kg of meat at $135\,000$ dong per kilogram. How much does she have to pay?

**Understand.** We know the mass of meat and the price of one kilogram. We need the amount to pay.

**Plan.** The amount is the price of one kilogram times the number of kilograms; use the calculator to multiply.

**Solution.** The amount Mum has to pay is:
$$4.75\times135\,000=641\,250\ \text{(dong)}.$$
So she has to pay $641\,250$ dong.

**Look back.** Is the result reasonable? Yes: $5\times130\,000=650\,000$, close to $641\,250$. Is there another way to check? Yes: work out $641\,250\div135\,000$ on the calculator and get $4.75$.
:::

A calculator also helps you find percentages.

:::example A percentage
A school has $450$ students, of whom $189$ are girls. What percentage of the students in the school are girls?

**Solution.** The percentage of girls out of all the students is $189\div450$. Press: **[1] [8] [9] [÷] [4] [5] [0] [=]**, and the display shows $0.42$. Multiply by $100$ to get $42$.

So girls make up $42\%$ of the students in the school.
:::

Finally, using the number $3.14$ for a circle.

:::example The circumference and area of a circle
A circle has radius $12.5$ cm. Find its circumference and its area (take $\pi\approx3.14$ and round the area to two decimal places).

**Solution.** Circumference: $2\times3.14\times12.5=78.5$ (cm). Area: $3.14\times12.5\times12.5=490.625\ \text{(cm}^2\text{)}$. The thousandths digit is $5$, so rounding to two decimal places gives $490.63$.

So the circumference is $78.5$ cm and the area is about $490.63\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Entering a wrong number and trusting the result.** Wrong: entering $4.75$ as $475$ and getting $64\,125\,000$ dong. Why it is wrong: the estimate $5\times130\,000=650\,000$ shows that the result is far too large. Right: $641\,250$ dong.
- **Copying the result without rounding.** Wrong: $17\div6=2.8333333$ when the problem asks for two decimal places. Why it is wrong: the problem asks for a number with two decimal places. Right: $2.83$.
- **Dividing in the wrong order.** Wrong: the percentage of $189$ out of $450$ is $450\div189\times100\approx238\%$. Why it is wrong: the girls are only part of the whole school, so the percentage must be less than $100\%$. Right: $189\div450\times100=42\%$.
:::

## Summary

- A calculator has digit keys, a decimal point key, the operation keys, the **[=]** key and a clear key.
- Enter the numbers and operations in the order given in the problem, then press **[=]**.
- A calculator only does what you press, so always estimate to check the result.
- Round the result on the display as the problem requires.
- The percentage of $a$ out of $b$: work out $a\div b$, then multiply by $100$.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Use a calculator to work out: a) $25.6\times4.5$; b) $3456.78+1234.56$; c) $9000-2718.45$; d) $78.4\div1.6$.

::hint
**What you need:** enter decimals with the decimal point key, then press **[=]**.

**Steps:**
1. Press **[AC]**, then enter the numbers and operations in order.
2. Estimate to check the result.

::solution
a) $25.6\times4.5=115.2$ (estimate: $26\times4.5\approx117$).

b) $3456.78+1234.56=4691.34$ (estimate: $3500+1200=4700$).

c) $9000-2718.45=6281.55$ (estimate: $9000-2700=6300$).

d) $78.4\div1.6=49$ (estimate: $80\div2=40$, and $1.6<2$, so the result is a little more than $40$).

**Answer.** a) $115.2$; b) $4691.34$; c) $6281.55$; d) $49$.
:::

:::exercise
Estimate, then use a calculator to work out $48.7\times9.8$. Is the calculator's result reasonable?

::hint
**What you need:** round each factor to a round number to estimate.

**Steps:**
1. Round $48.7$ to $50$ and $9.8$ to $10$.
2. Multiply the two round numbers, then compare with the calculator's result.

::solution
Estimate: $50\times10=500$. Using a calculator: $48.7\times9.8=477.26$.

The result $477.26$ is close to $500$, so it is reasonable.

**Answer.** $477.26$.
:::

:::exercise
Use a calculator, then round: a) $23\div7$, rounded to two decimal places; b) $100\div3$, rounded to one decimal place.

::hint
**What you need:** to round to a place, look at the digit immediately to the right of that place.

**Steps:**
1. Press the division and read the result on the display.
2. Round from the full result shown on the display.

::solution
a) $23\div7=3.2857142\ldots$ The thousandths digit is $5$, so rounding gives $3.29$.

b) $100\div3=33.333\ldots$ The hundredths digit is $3<5$, so rounding gives $33.3$.

**Answer.** a) $3.29$; b) $33.3$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose $1$ m of wire costs $12\,750$ dong. How much does $6.4$ m of this wire cost?

::hint
**What you need:** the cost is the price per metre times the number of metres.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Use a calculator to multiply, then estimate to check. Remember the unit.

::solution
The cost of the wire is:
$$12\,750\times6.4=81\,600\ \text{(dong)}.$$

**Check.** Estimate: $13\,000\times6=78\,000$, close to $81\,600$. Reasonable.

**Answer.** $81\,600$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a library has $1250$ books, of which $325$ are comics. What percentage of the library's books are comics?

::hint
**What you need:** the percentage of $a$ out of $b$ is $a\div b\times100$.

**Steps:**
1. Divide $325$ by $1250$ on the calculator.
2. Multiply the result by $100$.

::solution
We have $325\div1250=0.26$ and $0.26\times100=26$.

So comics make up $26\%$ of the library's books.

**Answer.** $26\%$.
:::

:::exercise
A bicycle wheel has diameter $0.65$ m. How many metres does the wheel travel in one turn? How many metres does it travel in $100$ turns? (Take $\pi\approx3.14$.)

::hint
**What you need:** the circumference of a circle is the diameter times $3.14$. Multiplying by $100$ moves the decimal point two places to the right.

**Steps:**
1. Use a calculator to find the circumference of the wheel.
2. Multiply the circumference by $100$.

::solution
The circumference of the wheel is $0.65\times3.14=2.041$ (m). In one turn the wheel travels $2.041$ m.

In $100$ turns the wheel travels $2.041\times100=204.1$ (m).

**Answer.** $2.041$ m in one turn; $204.1$ m in $100$ turns.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on multiplying and dividing decimals by $10$; $100$; $1000$. Use a calculator to work out $3.48\times100$ and $56.7\div1000$, then describe how the decimal point moved.

::hint
**What you need:** multiplying by $100$ moves the decimal point two places to the right; dividing by $1000$ moves it three places to the left.

**Steps:**
1. Press the two calculations.
2. Compare the position of the decimal point before and after.

::solution
Using a calculator: $3.48\times100=348$ and $56.7\div1000=0.0567$.

Multiplying by $100$: the decimal point moves two places to the right. Dividing by $1000$: the decimal point moves three places to the left.

**Answer.** $348$ and $0.0567$.
:::

:::exercise
You use a calculator to work out $36\div0.4$ and the display shows $90$. An thinks: "If a division gives a result greater than the dividend, I must have pressed a wrong key." Do you agree with An? Why or why not?

::hint
**What you need:** dividing by a number less than $1$ gives a quotient greater than the dividend.

**Steps:**
1. Estimate $36\div0.5$.
2. Compare $0.4$ with $0.5$ to decide whether the quotient is greater or less than the estimate.

::solution
No, I do not agree. Estimate: $36\div0.5=72$. Since $0.4<0.5$, the value of $36\div0.4$ must be greater than $72$, and $90$ is reasonable. Dividing by a number less than $1$ gives a quotient greater than the dividend.

**Answer.** The calculator is right and An is mistaken.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Use a calculator to work out $1\div7$. How do the digits after the decimal point repeat? Which digit is the $20$th digit after the decimal point?

::hint
**What you need:** if a group of digits repeats, you only need to know how many digits are in the group.

**Steps:**
1. Read the digits on the display and find the repeating group.
2. Divide $20$ by the number of digits in the group to see where the $20$th digit falls in the group.

::solution
The display shows $0.142857142857\ldots$ The group $142857$ ($6$ digits) repeats for ever.

Since $20=3\times6+2$, the $20$th digit is the second digit of the group, which is $4$.

**Answer.** The $20$th digit is $4$.
:::
