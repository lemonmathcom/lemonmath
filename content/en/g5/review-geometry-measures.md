:::goals
- Change between units of length, mass and area.
- Find the perimeter and area of rectangles and squares.
- Solve word problems about measures and rectangles.
- Understand why units of area are $100$ times apart.
:::

Mrs Hoa's family is tiling the floor of a rectangular room that is $6$ m long and $4$ m wide. Each tile is a square with side $40$ cm. How many tiles do they need to cover the floor completely?

The room is measured in metres and the tiles in centimetres. You have to write both in the same unit before you can work it out.

## Let's explore

:::diagram A rectangular room, 6 m long and 4 m wide
alt: A rectangle ABCD with length 6 m and width 4 m
point A 0,0 below-left
point B 6,0 below-right
point C 6,4 above-right
point D 0,4 above-left
polygon A B C D
label 3,0.5 "length 6 m"
label 5,2 "width 4 m"
:::

Change to centimetres: the length is $6\ \text{m}=600\ \text{cm}$ and the width is $4\ \text{m}=400\ \text{cm}$.

- Along the length, $600\div40=15$ tiles fit.
- Along the width, $400\div40=10$ tiles fit.
- The whole floor has $15\times10=150$ tiles.

So Mrs Hoa needs $150$ tiles.

:::theorem Units and formulas to remember
- **Length:** $1\ \text{km}=1000\ \text{m}$; $1\ \text{m}=10\ \text{dm}=100\ \text{cm}$; $1\ \text{cm}=10\ \text{mm}$.
- **Mass:** $1$ tonne $=1000\ \text{kg}$; $1$ tạ $=100\ \text{kg}$; $1$ yến $=10\ \text{kg}$ (tạ and yến are Vietnamese units); $1\ \text{kg}=1000\ \text{g}$.
- **Area:** $1\ \text{m}^2=100\ \text{dm}^2$; $1\ \text{dm}^2=100\ \text{cm}^2$; so $1\ \text{m}^2=10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$.
- **Rectangle** with length $a$ and width $b$ (in the same unit): $P=(a+b)\times2$ and $S=a\times b$.
- **Square** with side $a$: $P=a\times4$ and $S=a\times a$.
:::

Why is $1\ \text{m}^2=100\ \text{dm}^2$? A square of side $1$ m is also a square of side $10$ dm, so its area is $10\times10=100\ \text{dm}^2$. Units of length are $10$ times apart, but units of area are $10\times10=100$ times apart.

**Connection.** This lesson is the base for the lessons that follow: writing measurements as decimals, square kilometres, hectares, the area of a triangle and volume.

## Examples

We start by changing units.

:::example Find the missing number
a) $3\ \text{m}\ 25\ \text{cm}=?\ \text{cm}$; b) $2\ \text{kg}\ 40\ \text{g}=?\ \text{g}$.

**Solution.** a) We have $3\ \text{m}=300\ \text{cm}$, so $3\ \text{m}\ 25\ \text{cm}=300+25=325\ \text{cm}$.

b) We have $2\ \text{kg}=2000\ \text{g}$, so $2\ \text{kg}\ 40\ \text{g}=2000+40=2040\ \text{g}$.

So a) $325\ \text{cm}$; b) $2040\ \text{g}$.
:::

For units of area, each step is $100$ times apart.

:::example Find the missing number
a) $7\ \text{dm}^2=?\ \text{cm}^2$; b) $4\ \text{m}^2\ 5\ \text{dm}^2=?\ \text{dm}^2$; c) $2\ \text{m}^2=?\ \text{cm}^2$.

**Solution.** a) $7\ \text{dm}^2=7\times100=700\ \text{cm}^2$.

b) $4\ \text{m}^2=400\ \text{dm}^2$, so $4\ \text{m}^2\ 5\ \text{dm}^2=400+5=405\ \text{dm}^2$.

c) $2\ \text{m}^2=2\times10\,000=20\,000\ \text{cm}^2$.

So a) $700\ \text{cm}^2$; b) $405\ \text{dm}^2$; c) $20\,000\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

Now the perimeter and area of a rectangle.

:::example A rectangle with length $12$ cm and width $5$ cm
Find the perimeter and the area of the rectangle.

**Solution.** The perimeter of the rectangle is:
$$(12+5)\times2=34\ \text{(cm)}.$$
The area of the rectangle is:
$$12\times5=60\ \text{(cm}^2\text{)}.$$
So the perimeter is $34$ cm and the area is $60\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

When the perimeter of a square is given, find the side first.

:::example A square with perimeter $28$ cm
Find the area of the square.

**Solution.** A square has four equal sides, so the side of the square is:
$$28\div4=7\ \text{(cm)}.$$
The area of the square is:
$$7\times7=49\ \text{(cm}^2\text{)}.$$
So the area of the square is $49\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

Finally, back to the tiles, this time using area.

:::example Tiling the floor of a room
A rectangular room is $6$ m long and $4$ m wide. Each tile is a square with side $40$ cm. How many tiles are needed to cover the floor completely?

**Understand.** We know the size of the room and of a tile, in different units. We need the number of tiles.

**Plan.** Find the area of the floor and the area of one tile (both in cm²), then divide.

**Solve.** The area of the floor is $6\times4=24\ \text{m}^2=240\,000\ \text{cm}^2$. The area of one tile is $40\times40=1600\ \text{cm}^2$. The number of tiles needed is:
$$240\,000\div1600=150\ \text{(tiles)}.$$
So $150$ tiles are needed.

**Look back.** Is there another way? Yes, the rows method from the exploration: $15\times10=150$, the same answer. What if the tiles had side $50$ cm? Along the length $600\div50=12$ tiles fit and along the width $400\div50=8$ tiles fit, so $12\times8=96$ tiles are needed.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Using $1\ \text{m}^2=10\ \text{dm}^2$.** Wrong: $3\ \text{m}^2=30\ \text{dm}^2$. Why it is wrong: a square of side $1$ m is a square of side $10$ dm, with area $10\times10$. Right: $3\ \text{m}^2=300\ \text{dm}^2$.
- **Multiplying measurements in different units.** Wrong: a rectangle $2$ m long and $50$ cm wide has area $2\times50=100$. Why it is wrong: $2$ m and $50$ cm are not in the same unit. Right: $200\times50=10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$.
- **Mixing up perimeter and area.** Wrong: a rectangle $8$ cm long and $3$ cm wide has perimeter $8\times3=24$ cm. Why it is wrong: $8\times3$ is the area, while the perimeter is the length of the outline. Right: $(8+3)\times2=22$ cm.
:::

## Summary

- To work out or compare measurements, write them in the **same unit**.
- Units of length are $10$ times apart; neighbouring units of area are $100$ times apart, so $1\ \text{m}^2=10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$.
- Rectangle: $P=(a+b)\times2$, $S=a\times b$.
- Square: $P=a\times4$, $S=a\times a$.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Find the missing numbers:
a) $4\ \text{m}\ 6\ \text{dm}=?\ \text{cm}$; b) $6\ \text{kg}\ 80\ \text{g}=?\ \text{g}$; c) $3$ tạ $20\ \text{kg}=?\ \text{kg}$.

::hint
**What you need:** $1\ \text{m}=100\ \text{cm}$, $1\ \text{m}=10\ \text{dm}$; $1\ \text{kg}=1000\ \text{g}$; $1$ tạ $=100\ \text{kg}$.

**Steps:**
1. Change the larger unit into the smaller unit you need.
2. Add the remaining part in the smaller unit.

::solution
a) $4\ \text{m}=400\ \text{cm}$ and $6\ \text{dm}=60\ \text{cm}$, so $400+60=460\ \text{cm}$.

b) $6\ \text{kg}=6000\ \text{g}$, so $6000+80=6080\ \text{g}$.

c) $3$ tạ $=300\ \text{kg}$, so $300+20=320\ \text{kg}$.

**Answer.** a) $460$; b) $6080$; c) $320$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the missing numbers:
a) $3\ \text{m}^2=?\ \text{dm}^2$; b) $8\ \text{dm}^2\ 15\ \text{cm}^2=?\ \text{cm}^2$; c) $20\,000\ \text{cm}^2=?\ \text{m}^2$.

::hint
**What you need:** $1\ \text{m}^2=100\ \text{dm}^2$, $1\ \text{dm}^2=100\ \text{cm}^2$, $1\ \text{m}^2=10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$.

**Steps:**
1. How many times apart are neighbouring units of area?
2. Larger unit to smaller unit: multiply. Smaller unit to larger unit: divide.

::solution
a) $3\ \text{m}^2=3\times100=300\ \text{dm}^2$.

b) $8\ \text{dm}^2=800\ \text{cm}^2$, so $800+15=815\ \text{cm}^2$.

c) $20\,000\div10\,000=2$, so $20\,000\ \text{cm}^2=2\ \text{m}^2$.

**Answer.** a) $300$; b) $815$; c) $2$.
:::

:::exercise
A rectangle has length $15$ m and width $8$ m. Find the perimeter and the area of the rectangle.

::hint
**What you need:** $P=(a+b)\times2$ and $S=a\times b$.

**Steps:**
1. Are the two measurements in the same unit?
2. Work out the perimeter (in metres), then the area (in square metres).

::solution
The perimeter of the rectangle is $(15+8)\times2=46$ (m).

The area of the rectangle is $15\times8=120$ ($\text{m}^2$).

**Answer.** Perimeter $46$ m; area $120\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
A square has perimeter $36$ dm. Find the side and the area of the square.

::hint
**What you need:** a square has four equal sides: $P=a\times4$.

**Steps:**
1. Find the side: divide the perimeter by $4$.
2. Find the area: side times side.

::solution
The side of the square is $36\div4=9$ (dm).

The area of the square is $9\times9=81$ ($\text{dm}^2$).

**Answer.** Side $9$ dm; area $81\ \text{dm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
Mr Nam fences all round a rectangular garden that is $30$ m long and $20$ m wide, leaving a gate $2$ m wide. How many metres of fence does he need?

::hint
**What you need:** the fence runs along the outline, which is the perimeter.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked? Find the perimeter of the garden.
2. Take away the gate, which needs no fence. Check: the answer must be less than the perimeter.

::solution
The perimeter of the garden is $(30+20)\times2=100$ (m).

The length of fence needed is $100-2=98$ (m).

**Answer.** $98$ m.
:::

:::exercise
A shop receives $2$ tạ of rice and has sold $75$ kg. How many kilograms of rice are left in the shop?

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ tạ $=100\ \text{kg}$; "left" means taking away: use subtraction.

**Steps:**
1. Change $2$ tạ into kilograms.
2. Subtract the rice sold from the rice received.

::solution
$2$ tạ $=200\ \text{kg}$. The rice left is:
$$200-75=125\ \text{(kg)}.$$

**Answer.** $125$ kg.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on adding and subtracting fractions with different denominators. A rectangle has length $\dfrac{3}{4}$ m and width $\dfrac{1}{2}$ m. Find the perimeter of the rectangle.

::hint
**What you need:** $P=(a+b)\times2$; to add fractions with different denominators, write them over a common denominator first.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the sum of the length and the width.
2. Multiply that sum by $2$, then simplify.

::solution
Half the perimeter is $\dfrac{3}{4}+\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{3}{4}+\dfrac{2}{4}=\dfrac{5}{4}$ (m).

The perimeter of the rectangle is $\dfrac{5}{4}\times2=\dfrac{5}{2}=2\dfrac{1}{2}$ (m).

**Answer.** $2\dfrac{1}{2}$ m.
:::

:::exercise
A rectangular sheet of card $90$ cm long and $60$ cm wide is cut into squares of side $30$ cm, with no card left over. How many squares can be cut?

::hint
**What you need:** arrange the squares in rows and columns, like tiles on a floor.

**Steps:**
1. How many squares fit along the length? How many along the width?
2. Multiply the two numbers. Check using area.

::solution
Along the length, $90\div30=3$ squares fit. Along the width, $60\div30=2$ squares fit. The number of squares is:
$$3\times2=6\ \text{(squares)}.$$

**Check.** $90\times60=5400\ \text{cm}^2$ and $6\times30\times30=5400\ \text{cm}^2$. Correct.

**Answer.** $6$ squares.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A rectangle has perimeter $24$ cm, and both sides are whole numbers of centimetres. Find the greatest possible area of the rectangle.

::hint
**What you need:** half the perimeter equals the length plus the width.

**Steps:**
1. Find half the perimeter, then list the pairs of whole numbers with that sum.
2. Work out the area for each pair and compare.

::solution
Half the perimeter is $24\div2=12$ cm, so the length plus the width is $12$ cm. The pairs give: $1\times11=11$; $2\times10=20$; $3\times9=27$; $4\times8=32$; $5\times7=35$; $6\times6=36$ ($\text{cm}^2$).

The greatest area is $36\ \text{cm}^2$, when the rectangle is a square of side $6$ cm.

**Answer.** $36\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
$3\ \text{m}^2\ 5\ \text{dm}^2$ changed into square decimetres is
A. $305\ \text{dm}^2$.
B. $3005\ \text{dm}^2$.
C. $3050\ \text{dm}^2$.
D. $35\ \text{dm}^2$.
::key A
$3\ \text{m}^2=300\ \text{dm}^2$, so the total is $305\ \text{dm}^2$. Option D uses $1\ \text{m}^2=10\ \text{dm}^2$; B and C get the number of square decimetres in $1\ \text{m}^2$ wrong.

::mc
A rectangle is $9$ cm long and $4$ cm wide. Its perimeter is
A. $36$ cm.
B. $26$ cm.
C. $13$ cm.
D. $52$ cm.
::key B
$(9+4)\times2=26$ cm. Option A is the area; C forgets to multiply by $2$; D multiplies by $4$ as for the perimeter of a square.

::mc
A square has perimeter $20$ cm. Its area is
A. $20\ \text{cm}^2$.
B. $100\ \text{cm}^2$.
C. $25\ \text{cm}^2$.
D. $400\ \text{cm}^2$.
::key C
The side is $20\div4=5$ cm, so the area is $5\times5=25\ \text{cm}^2$. Option B multiplies the perimeter by the side; C multiplies the side by $4$; D multiplies the perimeter by itself.

::mc
The floor of a rectangular room $5$ m long and $4$ m wide is covered with square tiles of side $50$ cm. The number of tiles needed is
A. $20$ tiles.
B. $40$ tiles.
C. $8$ tiles.
D. $80$ tiles.
::key D
Along the length $500\div50=10$ tiles fit, along the width $400\div50=8$ tiles fit, so $10\times8=80$ tiles are needed. Option A forgets to change units; B takes $50$ as the area of one tile; D uses $1\ \text{m}^2=10\ \text{dm}^2$.

::tf
A rectangle has length $8$ m and width $5$ m.
a) The area of the rectangle is $40\ \text{m}^2$.
b) The perimeter of the rectangle is $8\times5=40$ m.
c) The area of the rectangle is $400\,000\ \text{cm}^2$.
d) If the length is increased by $2$ m, the perimeter increases by $2$ m.
::key T F T F
a) True: $8\times5=40$. b) False: $8\times5$ is the area; the perimeter is $(8+5)\times2=26$ m. c) True: $40\times10\,000=400\,000$. d) False: if the length increases by $2$ m then $(a+b)$ increases by $2$ m, so the perimeter increases by $4$ m.

::sa
Find the missing number: $6\ \text{m}^2\ 40\ \text{dm}^2=?\ \text{dm}^2$.
::key 640
$6\ \text{m}^2=600\ \text{dm}^2$, so $600+40=640\ \text{dm}^2$.

::sa
A rectangular playground is $15$ m long and $8$ m wide. You walk exactly twice round it. How many metres do you walk?
::key 92
One lap is $(15+8)\times2=46$ m, and two laps are $46\times2=92$ m.
:::
