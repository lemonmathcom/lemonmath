:::goals
- Understand that a decimal has a whole-number part and a decimal part, and read and write decimals.
- Write decimal fractions and mixed numbers as decimals, and the other way round.
- Know that adding or removing the digit $0$ at the end of the decimal part does not change the value.
- Compare two decimals and put decimals in order.
:::

In a $100$ m practice run, Nam took $18.4$ seconds and Mai took $18.25$ seconds. The teacher wrote the results as numbers with a dot in them.

How do you read these numbers? And who ran faster? This lesson helps you answer both questions.

## The idea of a decimal

### Let's explore

Mai is $1$ m $35$ cm tall. Let us write her height as a single number.

- $1$ dm is $\dfrac{1}{10}$ of a metre, which we write as $0.1$ m.
- $1$ cm is $\dfrac{1}{100}$ of a metre, which we write as $0.01$ m.
- $1$ m $35$ cm is $1$ m, $3$ dm and $5$ cm, so $1+\dfrac{3}{10}+\dfrac{5}{100}=1.35$ (m).

So Mai is $1.35$ m tall.

:::definition Decimal
A **decimal** has two parts, separated by the **decimal point**: the whole-number part on the left and the decimal part on the right. For example, $18.25$ has whole-number part $18$ and decimal part $25$.

The places in the decimal part are tenths, hundredths, thousandths, ... with $0.1=\dfrac{1}{10}$, $0.01=\dfrac{1}{100}$, $0.001=\dfrac{1}{1000}$.
:::

For the number $18.25$, the places are:

| Tens | Ones | Tenths | Hundredths |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1$ | $8$ | $2$ | $5$ |

So $18.25=10+8+\dfrac{2}{10}+\dfrac{5}{100}$. In English we read it digit by digit after the point: "eighteen point two five".

Why are the places like this? Each place is $10$ times smaller than the place before it. So $0.5=\dfrac{5}{10}=\dfrac{50}{100}=0.50$, which means that **adding or removing the digit $0$ at the end of the decimal part does not change the value**.

**Connection.** A decimal is another way of writing the decimal fractions and mixed numbers you already know. It lets you record measurements such as heights, running times and masses more precisely.

### Examples

We start by writing decimal fractions as decimals.

:::example Write as decimals
a) $\dfrac{7}{10}$; b) $\dfrac{35}{100}$; c) $12\dfrac{8}{100}$.

**Solution.** a) $\dfrac{7}{10}$ is $7$ tenths, so $\dfrac{7}{10}=0.7$.

b) $\dfrac{35}{100}$ is $3$ tenths and $5$ hundredths, so $\dfrac{35}{100}=0.35$.

c) $12\dfrac{8}{100}=12+\dfrac{8}{100}$. There are no tenths, so we write the digit $0$ in that place: $12\dfrac{8}{100}=12.08$.

So a) $0.7$; b) $0.35$; c) $12.08$.
:::

Next, reading a number and giving the value of each digit.

:::example The number $306.72$
Give the whole-number part, the decimal part and the value of the digit $7$ in $306.72$.

**Solution.** The whole-number part is $306$ and the decimal part is $72$. The digit $7$ is in the tenths place, so its value is $\dfrac{7}{10}=0.7$. The number is read "three hundred and six point seven two".

So the digit $7$ has value $0.7$.
:::

To finish this part, adding and removing the digit $0$.

:::example Simplifying and adding the digit $0$
a) Write $7.50$, $12.300$ and $0.60$ as simply as possible. b) Write $3.2$ with three digits in the decimal part.

**Solution.** a) Remove the digits $0$ at the end of the decimal part: $7.50=7.5$; $12.300=12.3$; $0.60=0.6$.

b) Add two digits $0$ at the end of the decimal part: $3.2=3.200$.

So a) $7.5$; $12.3$; $0.6$; b) $3.200$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Removing the digit $0$ in the middle.** Wrong: $12\dfrac{8}{100}=12.8$. Why it is wrong: $\dfrac{8}{100}$ is $8$ hundredths, so the digit $8$ must be in the hundredths place. Right: $12.08$.
- **Adding the digit $0$ straight after the decimal point.** Wrong: $3.2=3.02$. Why it is wrong: the digit $2$ is pushed into the hundredths place. Right: $3.2=3.20$.
:::

## Comparing decimals

### Let's explore

Back to the $100$ m run: who was faster, Nam with $18.4$ seconds or Mai with $18.25$ seconds? Both numbers have the same whole-number part, so we compare the decimal parts. Write $18.4=18.40$ so that both numbers have two digits in the decimal part. Then $40$ hundredths is greater than $25$ hundredths.

:::theorem Comparing two decimals
1. Compare the whole-number parts: the number with the greater whole-number part is greater.
2. If the whole-number parts are equal, compare the decimal parts place by place, from left to right: tenths, hundredths, thousandths, ... At the first place where the digits differ, the number with the greater digit is greater.
3. If every place is equal, the two numbers are equal.
:::

You can add digits $0$ so that both numbers have the same number of digits in the decimal part, then compare as you would compare whole numbers.

### Examples

:::example Who ran faster?
Nam ran $100$ m in $18.4$ seconds and Mai ran it in $18.25$ seconds. Who ran faster?

**Understand.** We know the running times of the two friends. We need to compare them: the one who took less time ran faster.

**Plan.** Compare the decimals $18.4$ and $18.25$.

**Solve.** The whole-number parts are equal, both $18$. Write $18.4=18.40$. Since $40>25$, we have $18.40>18.25$, that is, $18.4>18.25$.

Mai took less time, so Mai ran faster.

So Mai ran faster.

**Look back.** Is the answer reasonable? Yes: in hundredths of a second, Nam took $1840$ and Mai took $1825$, and $1825<1840$. What if Mai had taken $18.5$ seconds? Then $18.5>18.4$ and Nam would be faster.
:::

Next, three pairs of numbers, each with a point to watch.

:::example Compare
a) $9.8$ and $10.2$; b) $0.59$ and $0.6$; c) $5.40$ and $5.4$.

**Solution.** a) The whole-number parts satisfy $9<10$, so $9.8<10.2$.

b) Write $0.6=0.60$. Tenths: $5<6$, so $0.59<0.60$, that is, $0.59<0.6$.

c) Remove the digit $0$ at the end: $5.40=5.4$.

So a) $9.8<10.2$; b) $0.59<0.6$; c) $5.40=5.4$.
:::

Finally, putting four numbers in order.

:::example Put in order from least to greatest: $6.09$; $6.9$; $6.1$; $5.99$
**Solution.** The number $5.99$ has the smallest whole-number part, so it is the least. The other three have whole-number part $6$, so we compare the tenths: $6.09$ has $0$, $6.1$ has $1$ and $6.9$ has $9$. Since $0<1<9$, we have $6.09<6.1<6.9$.

So the order from least to greatest is $5.99$; $6.09$; $6.1$; $6.9$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Thinking that the number with more digits is greater.** Wrong: $0.59>0.6$ because $59>6$. Why it is wrong: $0.6=0.60$ and $60>59$. Right: $0.59<0.6$.
- **Looking only at the decimal parts.** Wrong: $9.8>10.2$ because $8>2$. Why it is wrong: the whole-number parts decide first, and $9<10$. Right: $9.8<10.2$.
:::

## Summary

- A decimal has a whole-number part and a decimal part, separated by the decimal point.
- The decimal places are tenths ($0.1$), hundredths ($0.01$) and thousandths ($0.001$); each place is $10$ times smaller than the one before.
- Adding or removing the digit $0$ at the **end** of the decimal part does not change the value; a digit $0$ in the middle must not be removed.
- To compare, look at the whole-number parts first, then the decimal places from left to right.
- To make comparing easier, write both numbers with the same number of digits in the decimal part.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Write as decimals: a) $\dfrac{9}{10}$; b) $\dfrac{47}{100}$; c) $5\dfrac{7}{100}$; d) $\dfrac{123}{1000}$.

::hint
**What you need:** $\dfrac{1}{10}=0.1$, $\dfrac{1}{100}=0.01$, $\dfrac{1}{1000}=0.001$.

**Steps:**
1. The denominator tells you how many digits there are in the decimal part.
2. Remember to write a $0$ in any place that is empty.

::solution
a) $\dfrac{9}{10}=0.9$. b) $\dfrac{47}{100}=0.47$. c) $5\dfrac{7}{100}=5.07$. d) $\dfrac{123}{1000}=0.123$.

**Answer.** a) $0.9$; b) $0.47$; c) $5.07$; d) $0.123$.
:::

:::exercise
Write as decimal fractions or mixed numbers: a) $0.6$; b) $0.08$; c) $4.25$.

::hint
**What you need:** one digit in the decimal part means denominator $10$; two digits mean denominator $100$.

**Steps:**
1. Count the digits in the decimal part.
2. Write the decimal part as a fraction and keep the whole-number part.

::solution
a) $0.6=\dfrac{6}{10}$. b) $0.08=\dfrac{8}{100}$. c) $4.25=4\dfrac{25}{100}$.

**Answer.** a) $\dfrac{6}{10}$; b) $\dfrac{8}{100}$; c) $4\dfrac{25}{100}$.
:::

:::exercise
In each number, which place is the digit $7$ in, and what is its value? a) $24.7$; b) $5.07$; c) $70.3$.

::hint
**What you need:** the places from left to right: tens, ones, decimal point, tenths, hundredths.

**Steps:**
1. Find the position of the digit $7$ compared with the decimal point.
2. Write the value of that digit.

::solution
a) The digit $7$ is in the tenths place, with value $0.7$.

b) The digit $7$ is in the hundredths place, with value $0.07$.

c) The digit $7$ is in the tens place, with value $70$.

**Answer.** a) tenths, $0.7$; b) hundredths, $0.07$; c) tens, $70$.
:::

:::exercise
Write as simply as possible: $8.500$; $0.70$; $12.0$.

::hint
**What you need:** removing the digits $0$ at the end of the decimal part does not change the value.

**Steps:**
1. Cross out the digits $0$ at the end of the decimal part.
2. If nothing is left in the decimal part, remove the decimal point too.

::solution
$8.500=8.5$; $0.70=0.7$; $12.0=12$.

**Answer.** $8.5$; $0.7$; $12$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the gap: a) $7.35\ \ldots\ 7.53$; b) $0.8\ \ldots\ 0.80$; c) $15.09\ \ldots\ 15.1$; d) $2.45\ \ldots\ 2.4$.

::hint
**What you need:** compare the whole-number parts first, then the decimal places from left to right.

**Steps:**
1. Write both numbers with the same number of digits in the decimal part.
2. Compare the tenths, then the hundredths.

::solution
a) Tenths: $3<5$, so $7.35<7.53$.

b) $0.80=0.8$, so $0.8=0.80$.

c) $15.1=15.10$. Tenths: $0<1$, so $15.09<15.1$.

d) $2.4=2.40$. The tenths are equal; hundredths: $5>0$, so $2.45>2.4$.

**Answer.** a) $<$; b) $=$; c) $<$; d) $>$.
:::

:::exercise
Put in order from greatest to least: $3.4$; $3.04$; $3.44$; $2.99$.

::hint
**What you need:** the greater whole-number part means the greater number; if they are equal, compare the tenths, then the hundredths.

**Steps:**
1. Find the number with the smallest whole-number part.
2. Compare the other three, writing $3.4=3.40$.

::solution
The number $2.99$ has the smallest whole-number part, so it is the least. The other three have whole-number part $3$. Write $3.4=3.40$. Tenths: $3.04$ has $0$, while $3.40$ and $3.44$ have $4$. Hundredths: $4>0$, so $3.44>3.40$.

**Answer.** $3.44>3.4>3.04>2.99$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose three friends run $60$ m in these times: An $10.5$ seconds, Binh $10.25$ seconds, Chi $10.3$ seconds. Who ran fastest? Put the friends in order from fastest to slowest.

::hint
**What you need:** the shorter time means the faster runner.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Write the three numbers with two digits in the decimal part, then compare.

::solution
Write $10.5=10.50$ and $10.3=10.30$. We have $10.25<10.30<10.50$.

Binh took the least time, then Chi, then An.

**Answer.** Binh ran fastest; from fastest to slowest: Binh, Chi, An.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on mixed numbers. Compare $2\dfrac{3}{10}$ and $2\dfrac{3}{4}$ by writing them as decimals.

::hint
**What you need:** $\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{3\times25}{4\times25}=\dfrac{75}{100}$.

**Steps:**
1. Change $\dfrac{3}{4}$ into a fraction with denominator $100$.
2. Write both mixed numbers as decimals, then compare.

::solution
We have $2\dfrac{3}{10}=2.3$. Since $\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{75}{100}$, we have $2\dfrac{3}{4}=2.75$.

Write $2.3=2.30$. Since $30<75$, we have $2.3<2.75$.

**Answer.** $2\dfrac{3}{10}<2\dfrac{3}{4}$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose four packets of rice have masses $1.25$ kg; $1.5$ kg; $1.05$ kg; $1.2$ kg. Which packet is the heaviest and which is the lightest?

::hint
**What you need:** write the numbers with the same number of digits in the decimal part to compare them.

**Steps:**
1. Write each number with two digits in the decimal part.
2. Find the greatest and the least number.

::solution
Write the numbers as $1.25$; $1.50$; $1.05$; $1.20$. The greatest tenths digit is $5$ (in $1.50$) and the least is $0$ (in $1.05$).

So $1.05<1.20<1.25<1.50$.

**Answer.** The heaviest packet is $1.5$ kg; the lightest is $1.05$ kg.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
How many decimals with two digits in the decimal part are greater than $0.3$ and less than $0.4$?

::hint
**What you need:** $0.3=0.30$ and $0.4=0.40$.

**Steps:**
1. What is the tenths digit of these numbers?
2. Which digits can be in the hundredths place? Must any number be left out?

::solution
The numbers we want have the form $0.3a$ with tenths digit $3$. Since the number is greater than $0.30$, the digit $a$ is not $0$, so $a\in\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9\}$. The numbers are $0.31$; $0.32$; ...; $0.39$.

**Answer.** $9$ numbers.
:::

::mc
The decimal with $7$ ones, $0$ tenths and $6$ hundredths is written
A. $7.6$.
B. $70.6$.
C. $7.06$.
D. $7.006$.
::key C
The tenths digit is $0$ and the hundredths digit is $6$, so the number is $7.06$. Option A drops the digit $0$; C puts the $7$ in the tens place; D puts the $6$ in the thousandths place.

::mc
The value of the digit $8$ in $25.184$ is
A. $8$.
B. $0.8$.
C. $0.008$.
D. $0.08$.
::key D
In $25.184$ the digit $1$ is in the tenths place and the digit $8$ is in the hundredths place, so its value is $0.08$. Option B puts the $8$ in the tenths place; C puts the $8$ in the thousandths place.

::mc
The greatest of $4.9$; $4.19$; $4.09$; $3.99$ is
A. $4.9$.
B. $4.19$.
C. $4.09$.
D. $3.99$.
::key A
The number $3.99$ has the smallest whole-number part. For the other three, the tenths digits are $9$, $1$, $0$, so $4.9$ is the greatest. Option B assumes $19>9$; C looks only at the digit $9$ at the end; D looks only at the decimal part.

Four friends run $100$ m in these times: Ha $12.8$ seconds, Lan $12.75$ seconds, Mai $12.08$ seconds, Ngoc $12.5$ seconds. The fastest is

C. Lan.

::key B
