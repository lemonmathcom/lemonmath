:::goals
- Remember how the units of time are related: century, year, month, week, day, hour, minute and second.
- Change between units of time, recognise leap years and know the number of days in each month.
- Add and subtract times given in two units, regrouping $60$ minutes as $1$ hour and the other way round.
- Solve word problems about time.
:::

You watch a film that lasts $1$ hour $45$ minutes. It starts at $19{:}30$. At what time does it finish?

You cannot add as you do with decimals, because it takes $60$ minutes, not $100$, to make $1$ hour. This lesson helps you add and subtract times without mistakes.

## Units of time

Let's revise the units of time:

| Larger unit | Equals |
|---|---|
| $1$ century | $100$ years |
| $1$ year | $12$ months |
| $1$ week | $7$ days |
| $1$ day | $24$ hours |
| $1$ hour | $60$ minutes |
| $1$ minute | $60$ seconds |

A year has $365$ days; we call it a **common year**. Every $4$ years there is a **leap year** with $366$ days, because February has $29$ days. The year $2024$ was a leap year. Count on another $4$ years: $2028$ is a leap year too.

The months have different numbers of days:
- Months $1$, $3$, $5$, $7$, $8$, $10$, $12$ have $31$ days.
- Months $4$, $6$, $9$, $11$ have $30$ days.
- Month $2$ has $28$ days (common year) or $29$ days (leap year).

The 21st century began in the year $2001$ and ends in $2100$, so the year $2026$ is in the 21st century.

When changing units, multiply or divide by $60$ (not $100$). For example $\dfrac14$ hour is $60\div4=15$ minutes, and $0.5$ hour is $30$ minutes.

:::example Changing units of time
Fill in the missing numbers.
a) $3$ h $12$ min $=\ ?$ min.
b) $150$ s $=\ ?$ min $?$ s.

**Solution.** a) We change $3$ hours to minutes and add the extra minutes:
$$3\text{ h }12\text{ min}=3\times60+12=192\text{ min}.$$
b) We have $150\div60=2$ remainder $30$, so
$$150\text{ s}=2\text{ min }30\text{ s}.$$
:::

:::example Leap years
Is $2028$ a leap year? How many days does the year $2028$ have? How many days does February $2026$ have?

**Solution.** The year $2024$ was a leap year and $2028-2024=4$, so $2028$ is a leap year. It has $366$ days.

Only $2$ years pass from $2024$ to $2026$, so $2026$ is a common year. February $2026$ has $28$ days.

So $2028$ is a leap year with $366$ days, and February $2026$ has $28$ days.
:::

## Adding times

We answer the opening question on a number line: from $19.5$ (that is $19{:}30$) jump $1$ hour, then jump $45$ minutes more.

:::graph From 19:30, add 1 hour and then 45 minutes
alt: A number line from 19 to 22 hours with 19:30, 20:30 and 21:15 marked and the stretch from 19:30 to 21:15 highlighted
x: 19..22
y: -1..1
segment: (19.5, 0) (21.25, 0)
point: (19.5, 0) "19:30" below
point: (20.5, 0) "20:30" above
point: (21.25, 0) "21:15" below
:::

From $20{:}30$, another $30$ minutes gives $21{:}00$, and $15$ minutes more gives $21{:}15$. We can do this with an addition.

:::theorem Adding times
To add two times:
1. Add the same units together (minutes with minutes, hours with hours).
2. If the minutes make $60$ or more, change $60$ minutes to $1$ hour and add it to the hours.
:::

Adding the same units makes sense, just like adding tenths to tenths. Changing $60$ minutes to $1$ hour is like regrouping when adding whole numbers, but we regroup at $60$, not at $10$.

:::example When does the film finish?
A film starts at $19{:}30$ and lasts $1$ hour $45$ minutes. At what time does it finish?

**Solution.** The finishing time is:
$$\begin{aligned} 19\text{ h }30\text{ min}+1\text{ h }45\text{ min}&=20\text{ h }75\text{ min}\\ &=21\text{ h }15\text{ min}. \end{aligned}$$
We changed $75$ min $=1$ h $15$ min.

**Answer.** $21{:}15$.
:::

## Subtracting times

When subtracting, if the minutes in the first time are fewer than the minutes in the second, change $1$ hour to $60$ minutes first.

:::theorem Subtracting times
To subtract two times:
1. Subtract the same units.
2. If the minutes in the first time are fewer, change $1$ hour to $60$ minutes and add them to the minutes, then subtract.
:::

:::example Subtracting with regrouping
Work out $5$ h $20$ min $-$ $2$ h $45$ min.

**Solution.** Since $20$ min is less than $45$ min, we change $1$ hour to $60$ minutes:
$$5\text{ h }20\text{ min}=4\text{ h }80\text{ min}.$$
Then we subtract:
$$4\text{ h }80\text{ min}-2\text{ h }45\text{ min}=2\text{ h }35\text{ min}.$$
**Check.** $2$ h $35$ min $+$ $2$ h $45$ min $=4$ h $80$ min $=5$ h $20$ min. Correct.

So $5$ h $20$ min $-$ $2$ h $45$ min $=2$ h $35$ min.
:::

The next problem has several stages, so we use *Understand, Plan, Solve, Look back*.

:::example The coach journey
A coach leaves at $7{:}40$. It travels for $2$ hours $50$ minutes, stops for a break of $20$ minutes, then travels for another $1$ hour $35$ minutes and arrives. At what time does it arrive?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* The coach leaves at $7{:}40$ and has three stages: travelling, resting, travelling again. We need the arrival time.

*Plan.* Add the times of the three stages to find the length of the journey, then add it to the departure time.

*Solve.* The whole journey takes:
$$\begin{aligned} 2\text{ h }50\text{ min}+20\text{ min}+1\text{ h }35\text{ min}&=3\text{ h }105\text{ min}\\ &=4\text{ h }45\text{ min}. \end{aligned}$$
The arrival time is:
$$7\text{ h }40\text{ min}+4\text{ h }45\text{ min}=11\text{ h }85\text{ min}=12\text{ h }25\text{ min}.$$
*Look back.* Let's add stage by stage: $7{:}40$ plus $2$ h $50$ min is $10{:}30$; plus $20$ min is $10{:}50$; plus $1$ h $35$ min is $12{:}25$. Both ways give the same answer. Without the break, the coach would arrive $20$ minutes earlier, at $12{:}05$.

**Answer.** $12{:}25$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Treating $1$ hour as $100$ minutes.** Wrong: $2$ h $15$ min $=215$ min. Why it is wrong: $1$ hour is $60$ minutes, not $100$. Right: $2\times60+15=135$ min.
- **Not regrouping $60$ minutes as $1$ hour.** Wrong: $3$ h $40$ min $+$ $2$ h $35$ min $=5$ h $75$ min. Why it is wrong: $75$ minutes is more than $1$ hour. Right: $5$ h $75$ min $=6$ h $15$ min.
- **Subtracting the minutes the wrong way round.** Wrong: $5$ h $20$ min $-$ $2$ h $45$ min $=3$ h $25$ min (working out $45-20$). Why it is wrong: the first time has to be the larger one, so we must change $1$ hour to $60$ minutes. Right: $2$ h $35$ min.
:::

## Summary

- $1$ century $=100$ years; $1$ year $=12$ months; $1$ week $=7$ days; $1$ day $=24$ hours; $1$ hour $=60$ minutes; $1$ minute $=60$ seconds.
- A common year has $365$ days and a leap year has $366$ days (February has $29$ days). Every $4$ years there is a leap year.
- To add, add the same units; every $60$ minutes becomes $1$ hour.
- To subtract, if there are not enough minutes, change $1$ hour to $60$ minutes.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Fill in the missing numbers.

a) $3$ h $=\ ?$ min.

b) $5$ min $20$ s $=\ ?$ s.

c) $2$ years $6$ months $=\ ?$ months.

d) $200$ min $=\ ?$ h $?$ min.

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ h $=60$ min; $1$ min $=60$ s; $1$ year $=12$ months.

**Steps:**
1. Parts a, b, c: multiply, then add the smaller part.
2. Part d: divide $200$ by $60$.

::solution
a) $3\times60=180$ (min).

b) $5\times60+20=320$ (s).

c) $2\times12+6=30$ (months).

d) $200\div60=3$ remainder $20$, so $200$ min $=3$ h $20$ min.

**Answer.** a) $180$ min; b) $320$ s; c) $30$ months; d) $3$ h $20$ min.
:::

:::exercise
How many days does the year $2028$ have? How many days does February $2028$ have? How many days does February $2026$ have?

::hint
**What you need:** every $4$ years there is a leap year; $2024$ was a leap year.

**Steps:**
1. How many years after $2024$ is $2028$?
2. A leap year has $366$ days and February has $29$ days.

::solution
Since $2028-2024=4$, the year $2028$ is a leap year: it has $366$ days and February has $29$ days. The year $2026$ is a common year, so February has $28$ days.

**Answer.** $366$ days; $29$ days; $28$ days.
:::

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $2$ h $25$ min $+$ $3$ h $40$ min.

b) $3$ min $45$ s $+$ $2$ min $30$ s.

::hint
**What you need:** add the same units; when you reach $60$, change to the next larger unit.

**Steps:**
1. Add hours to hours and minutes to minutes.
2. Regroup if the minutes (or seconds) make $60$ or more.

::solution
a) $2$ h $25$ min $+$ $3$ h $40$ min $=5$ h $65$ min $=6$ h $5$ min.

b) $3$ min $45$ s $+$ $2$ min $30$ s $=5$ min $75$ s $=6$ min $15$ s.

**Answer.** a) $6$ h $5$ min; b) $6$ min $15$ s.
:::

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $7$ h $10$ min $-$ $3$ h $35$ min.

b) $9$ min $15$ s $-$ $4$ min $40$ s.

::hint
**What you need:** if the first time has too few minutes (or seconds), change $1$ hour (or $1$ minute) to $60$.

**Steps:**
1. Compare $10$ with $35$; $15$ with $40$.
2. Regroup, then subtract.

::solution
a) $7$ h $10$ min $=6$ h $70$ min, so $6$ h $70$ min $-$ $3$ h $35$ min $=3$ h $35$ min.

b) $9$ min $15$ s $=8$ min $75$ s, so $8$ min $75$ s $-$ $4$ min $40$ s $=4$ min $35$ s.

**Answer.** a) $3$ h $35$ min; b) $4$ min $35$ s.
:::

:::exercise
Lan starts a test at $8{:}15$ and hands it in at $9{:}05$. How long does Lan spend on the test?

::hint
**What you need:** time spent $=$ finishing time $-$ starting time.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. $5$ min is less than $15$ min, so change $1$ hour to $60$ minutes.
3. Check: does $8{:}15$ plus your answer make $9{:}05$?

::solution
The time Lan spends on the test is:
$$9\text{ h }5\text{ min}-8\text{ h }15\text{ min}=8\text{ h }65\text{ min}-8\text{ h }15\text{ min}=50\text{ min}.$$
**Answer.** $50$ minutes.
:::

:::exercise
The school concert starts at $18{:}40$ and lasts $1$ hour $55$ minutes. At what time does it finish?

::hint
**What you need:** finishing time $=$ starting time $+$ length of the event.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. Add, then change $60$ minutes to $1$ hour.

::solution
The concert finishes at:
$$18\text{ h }40\text{ min}+1\text{ h }55\text{ min}=19\text{ h }95\text{ min}=20\text{ h }35\text{ min}.$$
**Answer.** $20{:}35$.
:::

:::exercise
Nam goes to bed at $21{:}30$ and wakes up at $6{:}15$ the next morning. How long does Nam sleep?

::hint
**What you need:** from bedtime to midnight ($24$ hours), then from midnight to the time he wakes up.

**Steps:**
1. How long is it from $21{:}30$ to $24{:}00$?
2. Add the time from $0{:}00$ to $6{:}15$.

::solution
From $21{:}30$ to $24{:}00$ is $24$ h $-$ $21$ h $30$ min $=2$ h $30$ min. The time asleep is:
$$2\text{ h }30\text{ min}+6\text{ h }15\text{ min}=8\text{ h }45\text{ min}.$$
**Answer.** $8$ hours $45$ minutes.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on adding decimals. Change:

a) $2.5$ h $=\ ?$ h $?$ min.

b) $1.75$ h $=\ ?$ h $?$ min.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply the decimal part of the hour by $60$ to get minutes.

**Steps:**
1. Separate the whole part and the decimal part.
2. Multiply the decimal part by $60$.

::solution
a) $0.5\times60=30$, so $2.5$ h $=2$ h $30$ min.

b) $0.75\times60=45$, so $1.75$ h $=1$ h $45$ min.

**Answer.** a) $2$ h $30$ min; b) $1$ h $45$ min.
:::

:::exercise
A runner trains like this: runs for $25$ min $40$ s, rests for $3$ min $30$ s, then runs for another $18$ min $50$ s. What is the total time from start to finish?

::hint
**What you need:** add times given in two units.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. Add the first two stages, then add the last stage.
3. Change $60$ seconds to $1$ minute.

::solution
The total time is:
$$\begin{aligned} 25\text{ min }40\text{ s}+3\text{ min }30\text{ s}&=28\text{ min }70\text{ s}=29\text{ min }10\text{ s},\\ 29\text{ min }10\text{ s}+18\text{ min }50\text{ s}&=47\text{ min }60\text{ s}=48\text{ min}. \end{aligned}$$
**Answer.** $48$ minutes.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
How many seconds are there in one day?

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ day $=24$ h; $1$ h $=60$ min; $1$ min $=60$ s.

**Steps:**
1. How many seconds are there in one hour?
2. Multiply by $24$.

::solution
One hour has $60\times60=3600$ (s). One day has:
$$3600\times24=86\,400\text{ (s)}.$$
**Answer.** $86\,400$ seconds.
:::

$2\times60+15=135$. Option A treats $1$ hour as $100$ minutes, B forgets the extra $15$ minutes and D is $60+15$ (not multiplied by $2$).

$4$ h $50$ min $+$ $2$ h $25$ min $=6$ h $75$ min $=7$ h $15$ min. Option A does not regroup $75$ minutes, B forgets to add $1$ hour and C is the subtraction $4$ h $50$ min $-$ $2$ h $25$ min.

::mc
$6$ h $10$ min $-$ $2$ h $40$ min equals
A. $3$ h $30$ min.
B. $3$ h $70$ min.

D. $8$ h $50$ min.
::key A
$6$ h $10$ min $=5$ h $70$ min, so $5$ h $70$ min $-$ $2$ h $40$ min $=3$ h $30$ min. Option C works out $40-10$, B changes $1$ hour to $100$ minutes and D is the addition.

::mc
Which of these years is a leap year (February has $29$ days)?
A. $2026$.
B. $2028$.
C. $2027$.
D. $2029$.
::key B
$2024$ was a leap year and $2028-2024=4$. The years $2026$ and $2027$ are only $2$ and $3$ years after $2024$, and $2029$ is $5$ years after, so none of these gaps is a multiple of $4$ years.
