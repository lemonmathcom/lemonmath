- Find the volume of a cuboid and a cube.

- Solve word problems that combine shapes and measures.

Mr Hung wants to tile a rectangular floor that is $6$ m long and $4.5$ m wide. Each tile is a square with sides of $30$ cm. How many tiles does he need to buy?

To answer, you need area and a change of units. This lesson revises the shapes and measures from primary school.

## Perimeter and area of flat shapes

Let's revise the formulas. Here $a$ and $b$ are sides (or parallel sides), $h$ is the height, $r$ is the radius, $d$ is the diameter, and we use $3.14$ for $\pi$.

| Shape | Perimeter | Area |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangle | $(a+b)\times2$ | $a\times b$ |
| Square with side $a$ | $a\times4$ | $a\times a$ |
| Triangle | sum of three sides | $a\times h\div2$ |
| Trapezium | sum of four sides | $(a+b)\times h\div2$ |
| Circle | $d\times3.14$ or $r\times2\times3.14$ | $r\times r\times3.14$ |

The area of a triangle is half the area of the rectangle with the same base and height, which is why there is a "$\div2$". The area of a trapezium is the mean of the two parallel sides times the height.

:::example Triangle and trapezium
a) Find the area of a triangle with base $12$ cm and height $7$ cm.
b) Find the area of a trapezium with parallel sides $10$ cm and $6$ cm and height $5$ cm.

**Solution.** a) We have $S=12\times7\div2=42\ (\text{cm}^2)$.

b) We have $S=(10+6)\times5\div2=16\times5\div2=40\ (\text{cm}^2)$.

So the area of the triangle is $42\ \text{cm}^2$ and the area of the trapezium is $40\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::example Circle
A circle has a radius of $5$ cm. Find its circumference and area.

**Solution.** The circumference is:
$$C=5\times2\times3.14=31.4\ (\text{cm}).$$
The area is:
$$S=5\times5\times3.14=78.5\ (\text{cm}^2).$$
So the circumference is $31.4$ cm and the area is $78.5\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

## Volume and changing units

The volume of a cuboid is length times width times height: $V=a\times b\times c$. The volume of a cube with edge $a$ is $V=a\times a\times a$.

Remember how the units are related:
- $1\ \text{m}^2=10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$ and $1\ \text{dm}^2=100\ \text{cm}^2$; $1$ ha $=10\,000\ \text{m}^2$.
- $1\ \text{m}^3=1000\ \text{dm}^3$ and $1\ \text{dm}^3=1000\ \text{cm}^3$; $1\ \text{dm}^3=1$ litre.

Units of area differ by a factor of $100$, because each unit has two dimensions ($10\times10$). Units of volume differ by a factor of $1000$, because they have three dimensions ($10\times10\times10$).

:::example A water tank
A cuboid water tank is $8$ dm long, $5$ dm wide and $6$ dm high. How many litres of water does it hold when full?

**Solution.** The volume of the tank is:
$$8\times5\times6=240\ (\text{dm}^3).$$
Since $1\ \text{dm}^3=1$ litre, the tank holds $240$ litres when full.

**Answer.** $240$ litres.
:::

:::example Changing units
Fill in the missing numbers.
a) $3.5$ ha $=\ ?\ \text{m}^2$.
b) $2400\ \text{cm}^2=\ ?\ \text{dm}^2$.
c) $5\ \text{m}^3=\ ?\ \text{dm}^3$.

**Solution.** a) Since $1$ ha $=10\,000\ \text{m}^2$, $3.5$ ha $=3.5\times10\,000=35\,000\ \text{m}^2$.

b) Since $100\ \text{cm}^2=1\ \text{dm}^2$, $2400\ \text{cm}^2=2400\div100=24\ \text{dm}^2$.

c) Since $1\ \text{m}^3=1000\ \text{dm}^3$, $5\ \text{m}^3=5000\ \text{dm}^3$.
:::

Now we answer the tiling problem, using *Understand, Plan, Solve, Look back*.

:::example Tiling a floor
A rectangular floor is $6$ m long and $4.5$ m wide. Each tile is a square with sides of $30$ cm. How many tiles are needed to cover the floor completely?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* The floor is $6\ \text{m}\times4.5\ \text{m}$ and each tile has side $30$ cm. We need the number of tiles.

*Plan.* Change everything to metres, find the area of the floor and of one tile, then divide the area of the floor by the area of one tile.

*Solve.* The area of the floor is $6\times4.5=27\ (\text{m}^2)$. We change $30\ \text{cm}=0.3\ \text{m}$, so the area of one tile is $0.3\times0.3=0.09\ (\text{m}^2)$. The number of tiles needed is:
$$27\div0.09=300\ (\text{tiles}).$$
*Look back.* Another way: along the length we fit $6\div0.3=20$ tiles, along the width $4.5\div0.3=15$ tiles, and $20\times15=300$. If the tiles had sides of $40$ cm, then $4.5\div0.4=11.25$ is not a whole number, so tiles would have to be cut at the edge.

**Answer.** $300$ tiles.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting to divide by $2$.** Wrong: a triangle with base $12$ cm and height $7$ cm has $S=12\times7=84\ \text{cm}^2$. Why it is wrong: that is the area of a rectangle. Right: $12\times7\div2=42\ \text{cm}^2$.
- **Mixing up radius and diameter.** Wrong: a circle with diameter $10$ cm has $S=10\times10\times3.14=314\ \text{cm}^2$. Why it is wrong: the formula uses the radius. Right: $r=5$ cm, $S=5\times5\times3.14=78.5\ \text{cm}^2$.
- **Changing units of area like units of length.** Wrong: $1\ \text{m}^2=100\ \text{cm}^2$. Why it is wrong: $1\ \text{m}=100$ cm, so $1\ \text{m}^2=100\times100\ \text{cm}^2$. Right: $1\ \text{m}^2=10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

## Summary

- Area: rectangle $a\times b$; triangle $a\times h\div2$; trapezium $(a+b)\times h\div2$; circle $r\times r\times3.14$.
- Circumference of a circle: $d\times3.14=r\times2\times3.14$.
- Volume: cuboid $a\times b\times c$; cube $a\times a\times a$.
- Units of area differ by $100$ times, units of volume by $1000$ times; $1\ \text{dm}^3=1$ litre, $1$ ha $=10\,000\ \text{m}^2$.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
A rectangle is $15$ m long and $8$ m wide. Find its perimeter and area.

::hint
**What you need:** $P=(a+b)\times2$; $S=a\times b$.

**Steps:**
1. Find the perimeter.
2. Find the area.

::solution
The perimeter is $(15+8)\times2=46\ (\text{m})$. The area is $15\times8=120\ (\text{m}^2)$.

**Answer.** $46$ m; $120\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
A right-angled triangle has two shorter sides of $9$ cm and $12$ cm. Find the area of the triangle.

::hint
**What you need:** in a right-angled triangle, if one shorter side is the base, the other shorter side is the height.

**Steps:**
1. Choose the base and the height.
2. Use $S=a\times h\div2$.

::solution
$S=9\times12\div2=54\ (\text{cm}^2)$.

**Answer.** $54\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
A trapezium has parallel sides of $14$ m and $8$ m and a height of $6$ m. Find its area.

::hint
**What you need:** $S=(a+b)\times h\div2$.

**Steps:**
1. Add the two parallel sides.
2. Multiply by the height and divide by $2$.

::solution
$S=(14+8)\times6\div2=22\times6\div2=66\ (\text{m}^2)$.

**Answer.** $66\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
A circle has a diameter of $20$ cm. Find its circumference and area.

::hint
**What you need:** $C=d\times3.14$; the radius is half the diameter.

**Steps:**
1. Find the circumference from the diameter.
2. Find the radius, then the area.

::solution
The circumference is $20\times3.14=62.8\ (\text{cm})$. The radius is $20\div2=10\ (\text{cm})$, so the area is $10\times10\times3.14=314\ (\text{cm}^2)$.

**Answer.** $62.8$ cm; $314\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
A cuboid water tank is $1.5$ m long, $0.8$ m wide and $1$ m high. How many litres of water does it hold when full?

::hint
**What you need:** $V=a\times b\times c$; $1\ \text{m}^3=1000$ litres.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. Find the volume in $\text{m}^3$, then change it to litres.
3. Check: $0.8\ \text{m}=8$ dm, $1.5\ \text{m}=15$ dm, $1\ \text{m}=10$ dm; does $15\times8\times10$ give the same result?

::solution
The volume of the tank is $1.5\times0.8\times1=1.2\ (\text{m}^3)$. Since $1\ \text{m}^3=1000\ \text{dm}^3=1000$ litres, the tank holds $1.2\times1000=1200$ (litres).

**Answer.** $1200$ litres.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a field is a trapezium with parallel sides of $80$ m and $60$ m and a height of $50$ m. Each square metre yields $0.5$ kg of rice. How many kilograms of rice does the whole field yield?

::hint
**What you need:** find the area of the field, then multiply by the kilograms per square metre.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. Find the area of the trapezium.
3. Multiply by $0.5$.

::solution
The area of the field is $(80+60)\times50\div2=3500\ (\text{m}^2)$. The mass of rice is $3500\times0.5=1750$ (kg).

**Answer.** $1750$ kg.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on the volume of a cube. A cube-shaped tank with an edge of $6$ dm holds an amount of water equal to $\dfrac23$ of its volume. How many litres of water are in the tank?

::hint
**What you need:** $V=a\times a\times a$; a fraction of an amount.

**Steps:**
1. Find the volume of the tank.
2. Take $\dfrac23$ of the volume.

::solution
The volume of the tank is $6\times6\times6=216\ (\text{dm}^3)$. The amount of water is $216\times\dfrac23=144\ (\text{dm}^3)=144$ litres.

**Answer.** $144$ litres.
:::

:::exercise
Fill in the missing numbers.

a) $3.5$ ha $=\ ?\ \text{m}^2$.

b) $7200\ \text{cm}^2=\ ?\ \text{m}^2$.

c) $4.2\ \text{m}^3=\ ?\ \text{dm}^3$.

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ ha $=10\,000\ \text{m}^2$; $1\ \text{m}^2=10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$; $1\ \text{m}^3=1000\ \text{dm}^3$.

**Steps:**
1. Changing from a larger unit to a smaller one: multiply.
2. Changing from a smaller unit to a larger one: divide.

::solution
a) $3.5\times10\,000=35\,000$.

b) $7200\div10\,000=0.72$.

c) $4.2\times1000=4200$.

**Answer.** a) $35\,000\ \text{m}^2$; b) $0.72\ \text{m}^2$; c) $4200\ \text{dm}^3$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A plot of land is made of a rectangle $20$ m long and $12$ m wide, with a semicircle of diameter $12$ m attached to one of its $12$ m sides. Find the area of the plot.

::hint
**What you need:** the area of the plot is the area of the rectangle plus the area of the semicircle.

**Steps:**
1. Find the radius of the semicircle.
2. Find the area of each part, then add.

::solution
The radius of the semicircle is $12\div2=6$ (m). The area of the rectangle is $20\times12=240\ (\text{m}^2)$. The area of the semicircle is $6\times6\times3.14\div2=56.52\ (\text{m}^2)$. The area of the plot is $240+56.52=296.52\ (\text{m}^2)$.

**Answer.** $296.52\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
A triangle with base $10$ cm and height $6$ cm has an area of
A. $30\ \text{cm}^2$.
B. $16\ \text{cm}^2$.
C. $120\ \text{cm}^2$.
D. $60\ \text{cm}^2$.
::key A
$S=10\times6\div2=30\ (\text{cm}^2)$. Option D forgets to divide by $2$, B is the sum of the two numbers and C multiplies by $2$ again.

::mc
A circle with radius $3$ cm has a circumference of
A. $28.26$ cm.
B. $18.84$ cm.
C. $9.42$ cm.
D. $37.68$ cm.
::key B
$C=3\times2\times3.14=18.84$ cm. Option A is the area ($3\times3\times3.14$), C treats $3$ cm as the diameter and D uses a radius of $6$ cm.

::mc
A cuboid tank that is $8$ dm long, $5$ dm wide and $6$ dm high holds, when full,
A. $2400$ litres.
B. $19$ litres.
C. $240$ litres.
D. $120$ litres.
::key C
$8\times5\times6=240\ (\text{dm}^3)=240$ litres. Option B adds the three numbers, A multiplies by $10$ again and D divides by $2$ as for the area of a triangle.

::mc
$3.5$ ha equals
A. $350\ \text{m}^2$.
B. $350\,000\ \text{m}^2$.
C. $3500\ \text{m}^2$.
D. $35\,000\ \text{m}^2$.
::key D
$1$ ha $=10\,000\ \text{m}^2$, so $3.5\times10\,000=35\,000$. The other options multiply by $100$, $1000$ or $100\,000$.

::tf
A trapezium has parallel sides of $14$ m and $8$ m and a height of $6$ m.
a) The area of the trapezium is $132\ \text{m}^2$.
b) The area of the trapezium is $66\ \text{m}^2$.
c) This trapezium has the same area as a triangle with base $14$ m and height $6$ m.
d) If the height is doubled (the parallel sides stay the same), the area is $132\ \text{m}^2$.
::key F T F T
a) False: $(14+8)\times6=132$ has not been divided by $2$. b) True: $132\div2=66$. c) False: that triangle has area $14\times6\div2=42\ \text{m}^2$. d) True: $(14+8)\times12\div2=132$.

::sa
Find the area of a circle with radius $10$ cm, using $3.14$ (in $\text{cm}^2$).
::key 314
$S=10\times10\times3.14=314$.

::sa
Change $0.5\ \text{m}^3$ to $\text{dm}^3$.
::key 500
$0.5\times1000=500$.
:::
