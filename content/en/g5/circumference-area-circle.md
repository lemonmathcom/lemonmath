Suppose the bicycle wheel of your friend Nam has diameter $0.6$ m. When the wheel rolls once along the road, the bicycle travels exactly the length of the outline of the wheel.

How far does the bicycle travel when the wheel rolls once? And how far does it travel when the wheel rolls $100$ times?

## Let's explore

Take a piece of thread and wrap it exactly once round three round objects, then measure the length of the thread. Suppose the results are as follows (rounded):

| Diameter | Circumference (length of thread) | Circumference ÷ diameter |
|---|---|---|
| $5$ cm | $15.7$ cm | $3.14$ |
| $10$ cm | $31.4$ cm | $3.14$ |
| $20$ cm | $62.8$ cm | $3.14$ |

Whether the object is big or small, the circumference divided by the diameter always gives about the same number. That number is called **pi** (written $\pi$), and in this lesson we use $\pi\approx3.14$.

:::definition Circle and disc
A **circle** with centre $O$ and radius $r$ is made of all the points at distance $r$ from $O$. A **disc** is the part of the plane inside the circle.

A segment joining the centre to a point on the circle is a **radius** $r$. A segment through the centre joining two points on the circle is a **diameter** $d$, and $d=r\times2$.
:::

:::theorem Circumference and area of a circle
For a circle with diameter $d$ and radius $r$:
- **Circumference:** $C=d\times3.14$ or $C=r\times2\times3.14$.
- **Area:** $S=r\times r\times3.14$.
:::

Why does this work? The circumference formula is just how we define $\pi$: the circumference divided by the diameter is always $\pi\approx3.14$. For the area, cut the disc into many small sectors and place them side by side, alternately upside down, and you get a shape close to a rectangle whose length is half the circumference, that is, $r\times3.14$, and whose width is $r$. The area is $r\times3.14\times r$.

**Connection.** The number $\pi\approx3.14$ is a decimal, so you use the multiplication and division of decimals and the rounding you have learnt.

## Examples

We start with radius and diameter.

:::example Radius and diameter
a) A circle has radius $4$ cm. Find its diameter. b) A circle has diameter $9$ dm. Find its radius.

**Solution.** a) The diameter is $4\times2=8$ cm.

b) The radius is $9\div2=4.5$ dm.

So a) $8$ cm; b) $4.5$ dm.
:::

Next, the circumference.

:::example Finding the circumference
a) A circle with diameter $12$ cm. b) A circle with radius $5$ m.

**Solution.** a) The circumference of the circle is:
$$12\times3.14=37.68\ \text{(cm)}.$$

b) The circumference of the circle is:
$$5\times2\times3.14=31.4\ \text{(m)}.$$

So a) $37.68$ cm; b) $31.4$ m.
:::

Now the area; remember to change the diameter into the radius.

:::example Finding the area
a) A circle with radius $5$ cm. b) A circle with diameter $8$ dm.

**Solution.** a) The area of the circle is:
$$5\times5\times3.14=78.5\ \text{(cm}^2\text{)}.$$

b) The radius is $8\div2=4$ dm. The area of the circle is:
$$4\times4\times3.14=50.24\ \text{(dm}^2\text{)}.$$

So a) $78.5\ \text{cm}^2$; b) $50.24\ \text{dm}^2$.
:::

If you know the circumference, you can find the diameter.

:::example Finding the diameter
A circle has circumference $62.8$ cm. Find the diameter and the radius of the circle.

**Solution.** From $C=d\times3.14$ we get $d=C\div3.14$. The diameter is:
$$62.8\div3.14=20\ \text{(cm)}.$$
The radius is $20\div2=10$ cm.

So the diameter is $20$ cm and the radius is $10$ cm.
:::

Now back to Nam's bicycle wheel, solved in four steps.

:::example A bicycle wheel
Nam's bicycle wheel has diameter $0.6$ m. How far does the bicycle travel when the wheel rolls $100$ times?

**Understand.** We know the diameter of the wheel. Each time the wheel rolls once, the bicycle travels a distance equal to the circumference of the wheel. We need the distance after $100$ rolls.

**Plan.** Find the circumference of the wheel, then multiply by $100$.

**Solve.** The circumference of the wheel is:
$$0.6\times3.14=1.884\ \text{(m)}.$$
The distance travelled after $100$ rolls is:
$$1.884\times100=188.4\ \text{(m)}.$$
So the bicycle travels $188.4$ m.

**Look back.** Is the answer reasonable? Yes: the circumference is about $3$ times the diameter, $3\times0.6=1.8$ m, close to $1.884$ m. If you round the circumference to two decimal places you get $1.88$ m, so each roll is out by a very small amount.
:::

Finally, a problem about area.

:::example A tablecloth
Suppose a round tablecloth has diameter $1.2$ m. Find the area of the tablecloth, rounded to two decimal places.

**Solution.** The radius of the tablecloth is $1.2\div2=0.6$ m. The area of the tablecloth is:
$$0.6\times0.6\times3.14=1.1304\ \text{(m}^2\text{)}.$$
The thousandths digit is $0<5$, so rounding to two decimal places gives $1.13\ \text{m}^2$.

So the area is about $1.13\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting to multiply by $2$ when finding the circumference from the radius.** Wrong: a radius of $5$ m gives circumference $5\times3.14=15.7$ m. Why it is wrong: $5$ m is the radius, and the diameter is $5\times2=10$ m. Right: $5\times2\times3.14=31.4$ m.
- **Using the diameter to find the area.** Wrong: a diameter of $8$ dm gives $S=8\times8\times3.14=200.96\ \text{dm}^2$. Why it is wrong: the area formula uses the radius. Right: $4\times4\times3.14=50.24\ \text{dm}^2$.
- **Writing the unit of area without the square.** Wrong: $S=78.5$ cm. Why it is wrong: area is measured in square units. Right: $78.5\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

## Summary

- The diameter is $d=r\times2$; the diameter is twice the radius.
- Circumference of a circle: $C=d\times3.14=r\times2\times3.14$.
- Area of a circle: $S=r\times r\times3.14$ (use the **radius**).
- If you know the circumference, the diameter is the circumference divided by $3.14$.
- The number $\pi\approx3.14$ is an approximation; round your answer if the question asks you to.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Find the missing numbers:
a) A radius of $7$ cm means a diameter of $?$ cm; b) a diameter of $1.6$ m means a radius of $?$ m; c) a radius of $2.5$ dm means a diameter of $?$ dm.

::hint
**What you need:** the diameter is twice the radius.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply the radius by $2$ to get the diameter.
2. Divide the diameter by $2$ to get the radius.

::solution
a) $7\times2=14$ cm.

b) $1.6\div2=0.8$ m.

c) $2.5\times2=5$ dm.

**Answer.** a) $14$; b) $0.8$; c) $5$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the circumference of the circle with: a) diameter $5$ cm; b) radius $3$ cm; c) diameter $0.4$ m.

::hint
**What you need:** $C=d\times3.14$ or $C=r\times2\times3.14$.

**Steps:**
1. Decide whether you are given the diameter or the radius.
2. Multiply by $3.14$ (and by $2$ first if it is the radius).

::solution
a) $5\times3.14=15.7$ cm.

b) $3\times2\times3.14=18.84$ cm.

c) $0.4\times3.14=1.256$ m.

**Answer.** a) $15.7$ cm; b) $18.84$ cm; c) $1.256$ m.
:::

:::exercise
Find the area of the circle with: a) radius $3$ cm; b) diameter $10$ dm; c) radius $1.5$ m.

::hint
**What you need:** $S=r\times r\times3.14$.

**Steps:**
1. Find the radius (part b: divide the diameter by $2$).
2. Multiply the radius by itself, then by $3.14$.

::solution
a) $3\times3\times3.14=28.26\ \text{cm}^2$.

b) The radius is $10\div2=5$ dm, so $S=5\times5\times3.14=78.5\ \text{dm}^2$.

c) $1.5\times1.5\times3.14=2.25\times3.14=7.065\ \text{m}^2$.

**Answer.** a) $28.26\ \text{cm}^2$; b) $78.5\ \text{dm}^2$; c) $7.065\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the diameter and the radius of the circle with circumference: a) $31.4$ cm; b) $25.12$ m.

::hint
**What you need:** $d=C\div3.14$ and $r=d\div2$.

**Steps:**
1. Divide the circumference by $3.14$ by moving the decimal point.
2. Divide the diameter by $2$.

::solution
a) $d=31.4\div3.14=10$ cm and $r=10\div2=5$ cm.

b) $d=25.12\div3.14=8$ m and $r=8\div2=4$ m.

**Answer.** a) $d=10$ cm, $r=5$ cm; b) $d=8$ m, $r=4$ m.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose Lan's bicycle wheel has diameter $0.65$ m. How far does the bicycle travel when the wheel rolls $10$ times?

::hint
**What you need:** each time the wheel rolls once, the bicycle travels a distance equal to the circumference of the wheel.

**Steps:**
1. Find the circumference of the wheel.
2. Multiply it by $10$ by moving the decimal point.

::solution
The circumference of the wheel is $0.65\times3.14=2.041$ m.

The distance travelled after $10$ rolls is $2.041\times10=20.41$ (m).

**Answer.** $20.41$ m.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a round pond has radius $20$ m. Find the circumference and the area of the pond.

::hint
**What you need:** $C=r\times2\times3.14$ and $S=r\times r\times3.14$.

**Steps:**
1. You are given the radius, so find the circumference with $r\times2\times3.14$.
2. Find the area with $r\times r\times3.14$.

::solution
The circumference of the pond is $20\times2\times3.14=125.6$ m.

The area of the pond is $20\times20\times3.14=1256\ \text{m}^2$.

**Answer.** Circumference $125.6$ m; area $1256\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
A piece of string $94.2$ cm long goes exactly once round a round box. Find the diameter of the box.

::hint
**What you need:** a string that goes exactly once round has length equal to the circumference of the box.

**Steps:**
1. The length of the string is the circumference of the circle.
2. Divide the circumference by $3.14$. Check by multiplying.

::solution
The diameter of the box is:
$$94.2\div3.14=30\ \text{(cm)}.$$

**Check.** $30\times3.14=94.2$. Correct.

**Answer.** $30$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the review lesson on the area of a square. A square has side $10$ cm, and inside it is drawn a circle with diameter $10$ cm (the circle touches all four sides of the square). Find the area of the part of the square outside the circle.

::hint
**What you need:** the area of a square is side times side; a circle with diameter $10$ cm has radius $5$ cm.

**Steps:**
1. Find the area of the square and the area of the circle.
2. Subtract the area of the circle from the area of the square.

::solution
The area of the square is $10\times10=100\ \text{cm}^2$. The area of the circle is $5\times5\times3.14=78.5\ \text{cm}^2$.

The area of the part outside the circle is $100-78.5=21.5\ \text{cm}^2$.

**Answer.** $21.5\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Circle $A$ has radius $3$ cm and circle $B$ has radius $6$ cm (twice as big). How many times as great as the circumference of $A$ is the circumference of $B$? How many times as great as the area of $A$ is the area of $B$?

::hint
**What you need:** work out the circumference and area of each circle, then divide.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $C$ and $S$ for $A$ and for $B$.
2. Divide the results for $B$ by the results for $A$.

::solution
Circle $A$: $C=3\times2\times3.14=18.84$ cm and $S=3\times3\times3.14=28.26\ \text{cm}^2$.

Circle $B$: $C=6\times2\times3.14=37.68$ cm and $S=6\times6\times3.14=113.04\ \text{cm}^2$.

Since $37.68\div18.84=2$ and $113.04\div28.26=4$, the circumference is $2$ times as great and the area is $4$ times as great.

**Answer.** The circumference is $2$ times as great; the area is $4$ times as great.
:::
