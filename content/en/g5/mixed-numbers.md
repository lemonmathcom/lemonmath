:::goals
- Know that a mixed number has a whole-number part and a fractional part, and read and write mixed numbers.
- Change a mixed number into a fraction, and a fraction greater than $1$ into a mixed number.
- Compare two mixed numbers.
- Add and subtract two mixed numbers and solve word problems.
:::

Mum has $2$ whole cakes and another $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a cake. She cuts every cake into $4$ equal pieces to share with the family. How many pieces of cake does she have altogether?

Mum's cake has a whole part and a part left over. This lesson teaches you how to write and work with numbers like this.

## Let's explore

Each cake is cut into $4$ pieces, and each piece is $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of a cake.

- $2$ whole cakes make $2\times4=8$ pieces.
- Add $3$ more pieces and we have $8+3=11$ pieces.
- Each piece is $\dfrac{1}{4}$, so Mum has $\dfrac{11}{4}$ of a cake.

We write this amount as $2\dfrac{3}{4}$ (read "two and three quarters"), and $2\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{11}{4}$.

:::definition Mixed number
A **mixed number** is a whole number (the whole-number part) written next to a fraction smaller than $1$ (the fractional part). For example, $2\dfrac{3}{4}$ has whole-number part $2$ and fractional part $\dfrac{3}{4}$, which means $2\dfrac{3}{4}=2+\dfrac{3}{4}$.
:::

:::theorem Changing between mixed numbers and fractions
- **Mixed number to fraction:** multiply the whole-number part by the denominator, add the numerator, and keep the denominator: $a\dfrac{b}{c}=\dfrac{a\times c+b}{c}$.
- **Fraction greater than $1$ to mixed number:** divide the numerator by the denominator. The quotient is the whole-number part, the remainder is the new numerator, and the denominator stays the same.
:::

Why does this work? Each whole is made of $c$ equal parts, so $a$ wholes have $a\times c$ parts. Adding the $b$ extra parts gives $a\times c+b$ parts. Going back is a division with a remainder: $23\div6=3$ remainder $5$ means that $23$ parts make $3$ complete wholes of $6$ parts, with $5$ parts left over.

**Connection.** A mixed number always lies between two consecutive whole numbers, as $2\dfrac{3}{4}$ lies between $2$ and $3$. This view will help you understand the decimal $2.75$ in later lessons.

## Examples

We start with changing a mixed number into a fraction.

:::example Change $3\dfrac{2}{5}$ into a fraction
**Solution.** We have $3\times5=15$ and $15+2=17$. Keep the denominator $5$:
$$3\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{3\times5+2}{5}=\dfrac{17}{5}.$$
So $3\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{17}{5}$.
:::

Now we go back, using division with a remainder.

:::example Change $\dfrac{23}{6}$ into a mixed number
**Solution.** We have $23\div6=3$ remainder $5$. The quotient $3$ is the whole-number part and the remainder $5$ is the numerator of the fractional part:
$$\dfrac{23}{6}=3\dfrac{5}{6}.$$
So $\dfrac{23}{6}=3\dfrac{5}{6}$.
:::

To compare mixed numbers, compare the whole-number parts first.

:::example Compare $2\dfrac{3}{4}$ with $2\dfrac{4}{5}$, and $3\dfrac{1}{2}$ with $2\dfrac{9}{10}$
**Solution.** The mixed numbers $2\dfrac{3}{4}$ and $2\dfrac{4}{5}$ have the same whole-number part, so we compare the fractional parts. Over a common denominator: $\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{15}{20}$ and $\dfrac{4}{5}=\dfrac{16}{20}$. Since $15<16$, we have $2\dfrac{3}{4}<2\dfrac{4}{5}$.

The mixed numbers $3\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $2\dfrac{9}{10}$ have different whole-number parts. Since $3>2$, we have $3\dfrac{1}{2}>2\dfrac{9}{10}$.
:::

There are two ways to add mixed numbers. Try them in a real-life problem.

:::example Pieces of cloth
Suppose a red piece of cloth $1\dfrac{1}{2}$ m long is joined to a blue piece $2\dfrac{3}{4}$ m long. How long is the joined cloth?

**Understand.** We know the lengths of the two pieces. We need the total length.

**Plan.** Add the whole-number parts, add the fractional parts, then put the two results together.

**Solve.** The whole-number parts add up to $1+2=3$. The fractional parts add up to:
$$\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{2}{4}+\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{5}{4}=1\dfrac{1}{4}.$$
The length of the joined cloth is:
$$3+1\dfrac{1}{4}=4\dfrac{1}{4}\ \text{(m)}.$$
So the joined cloth is $4\dfrac{1}{4}$ m long.

**Look back.** Is there another way? Yes: change to fractions, $\dfrac{3}{2}+\dfrac{11}{4}=\dfrac{6}{4}+\dfrac{11}{4}=\dfrac{17}{4}=4\dfrac{1}{4}$, the same answer. Is it reasonable? Yes, because $1\dfrac{1}{2}$ is close to $2$ and $2\dfrac{3}{4}$ is close to $3$, so the total is close to $5$.
:::

Finally, subtraction, where it is best to change to fractions.

:::example Work out $5\dfrac{1}{3}-2\dfrac{3}{4}$
**Solution.** Change both mixed numbers into fractions, then write over a common denominator:
$$5\dfrac{1}{3}-2\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{16}{3}-\dfrac{11}{4}=\dfrac{64}{12}-\dfrac{33}{12}=\dfrac{31}{12}=2\dfrac{7}{12}.$$
So $5\dfrac{1}{3}-2\dfrac{3}{4}=2\dfrac{7}{12}$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Writing the digits side by side.** Wrong: $2\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{23}{4}$. Why it is wrong: $\dfrac{23}{4}=5\dfrac{3}{4}$, which is much bigger than $2\dfrac{3}{4}$. Right: $\dfrac{2\times4+3}{4}=\dfrac{11}{4}$.
- **Mixing up the quotient and the remainder.** Wrong: $\dfrac{23}{6}=5\dfrac{3}{6}$. Why it is wrong: $23\div6=3$ remainder $5$, so the whole-number part is the quotient $3$ and the numerator is the remainder $5$. Right: $\dfrac{23}{6}=3\dfrac{5}{6}$.
- **Leaving a fractional part greater than $1$.** Wrong: $1\dfrac{1}{2}+2\dfrac{3}{4}=3\dfrac{5}{4}$. Why it is wrong: the fractional part of a mixed number must be smaller than $1$, so $\dfrac{5}{4}$ must become $1\dfrac{1}{4}$. Right: $3+1\dfrac{1}{4}=4\dfrac{1}{4}$.
:::

## Summary

- A mixed number $a\dfrac{b}{c}$ has whole-number part $a$ and fractional part $\dfrac{b}{c}$ smaller than $1$.
- Mixed number to fraction: $a\dfrac{b}{c}=\dfrac{a\times c+b}{c}$.
- Fraction greater than $1$ to mixed number: divide the numerator by the denominator; the quotient is the whole-number part and the remainder is the new numerator.
- To compare mixed numbers, compare the whole-number parts first; if they are equal, compare the fractional parts.
- To add or subtract mixed numbers, change to fractions, or work out the whole-number parts and fractional parts separately.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Change these mixed numbers into fractions: a) $2\dfrac{1}{5}$; b) $6\dfrac{3}{4}$.

::hint
**What you need:** $a\dfrac{b}{c}=\dfrac{a\times c+b}{c}$.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply the whole-number part by the denominator.
2. Add the numerator and keep the denominator.

::solution
a) $2\dfrac{1}{5}=\dfrac{2\times5+1}{5}=\dfrac{11}{5}$.

b) $6\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{6\times4+3}{4}=\dfrac{27}{4}$.

**Answer.** a) $\dfrac{11}{5}$; b) $\dfrac{27}{4}$.
:::

:::exercise
Change these fractions into mixed numbers: a) $\dfrac{7}{2}$; b) $\dfrac{29}{8}$.

::hint
**What you need:** divide the numerator by the denominator; the quotient is the whole-number part and the remainder is the new numerator.

**Steps:**
1. Do the division with a remainder.
2. Write the mixed number and keep the denominator.

::solution
a) $7\div2=3$ remainder $1$, so $\dfrac{7}{2}=3\dfrac{1}{2}$.

b) $29\div8=3$ remainder $5$, so $\dfrac{29}{8}=3\dfrac{5}{8}$.

**Answer.** a) $3\dfrac{1}{2}$; b) $3\dfrac{5}{8}$.
:::

:::exercise
Compare $3\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $3\dfrac{5}{8}$.

::hint
**What you need:** if two mixed numbers have the same whole-number part, compare the fractional parts.

**Steps:**
1. Are the whole-number parts equal?
2. Write the fractional parts over a common denominator and compare.

::solution
The whole-number parts are both $3$. Over a common denominator: $\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{16}{24}$ and $\dfrac{5}{8}=\dfrac{15}{24}$. Since $16>15$, we have $\dfrac{2}{3}>\dfrac{5}{8}$.

**Answer.** $3\dfrac{2}{3}>3\dfrac{5}{8}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out: $2\dfrac{1}{3}+1\dfrac{1}{6}$.

::hint
**What you need:** add the whole-number parts to each other and the fractional parts to each other.

**Steps:**
1. Add the two whole-number parts.
2. Write over a common denominator and add the fractional parts, then simplify.

::solution
$$2\dfrac{1}{3}+1\dfrac{1}{6}=3+\left(\dfrac{2}{6}+\dfrac{1}{6}\right)=3+\dfrac{3}{6}=3\dfrac{1}{2}.$$

**Answer.** $3\dfrac{1}{2}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out: $4\dfrac{1}{2}-1\dfrac{3}{4}$.

::hint
**What you need:** change both mixed numbers into fractions, then subtract.

**Steps:**
1. Change $4\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $1\dfrac{3}{4}$ into fractions.
2. Write over a common denominator, subtract, then change the result into a mixed number.

::solution
$$4\dfrac{1}{2}-1\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{9}{2}-\dfrac{7}{4}=\dfrac{18}{4}-\dfrac{7}{4}=\dfrac{11}{4}=2\dfrac{3}{4}.$$

**Answer.** $2\dfrac{3}{4}$.
:::

:::exercise
This morning Nam jogged $1\dfrac{1}{2}$ km, and this afternoon he jogged another $2\dfrac{1}{4}$ km. How many kilometres did he jog altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** "altogether" means putting together: use addition.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Add the two mixed numbers. Check: the sum must be greater than each addend.

::solution
Nam jogged altogether:
$$1\dfrac{1}{2}+2\dfrac{1}{4}=3+\left(\dfrac{2}{4}+\dfrac{1}{4}\right)=3\dfrac{3}{4}\ \text{(km)}.$$

**Answer.** $3\dfrac{3}{4}$ km.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose Aunt Lan has $3\dfrac{1}{2}$ kg of rice. She uses $1\dfrac{3}{4}$ kg to cook. How many kilograms of rice does she have left?

::hint
**What you need:** "left" means taking away: use subtraction.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Change to fractions, subtract, then write the result as a mixed number. Remember the unit.

::solution
The mass of rice left is:
$$3\dfrac{1}{2}-1\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{7}{2}-\dfrac{7}{4}=\dfrac{14}{4}-\dfrac{7}{4}=\dfrac{7}{4}=1\dfrac{3}{4}\ \text{(kg)}.$$

**Answer.** $1\dfrac{3}{4}$ kg.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on the perimeter of a rectangle. A rectangular garden is $2\dfrac{1}{2}$ m long and $1\dfrac{1}{4}$ m wide. Work out the perimeter of the garden.

::hint
**What you need:** the perimeter of a rectangle is the sum of the length and the width, multiplied by $2$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the sum of the length and the width.
2. Multiply it by $2$, then write the result as a mixed number.

::solution
Half the perimeter is $2\dfrac{1}{2}+1\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{10}{4}+\dfrac{5}{4}=\dfrac{15}{4}$ (m).

The perimeter of the garden is $\dfrac{15}{4}\times2=\dfrac{15}{2}=7\dfrac{1}{2}$ (m).

**Answer.** $7\dfrac{1}{2}$ m.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Work out quickly: $1\dfrac{1}{2}+2\dfrac{1}{2}+3\dfrac{1}{2}+4\dfrac{1}{2}$.

::hint
**What you need:** add the whole-number parts and the fractional parts separately.

**Steps:**
1. Add the whole-number parts $1+2+3+4$.
2. How many copies of $\dfrac{1}{2}$ are there? Which number do two copies of $\dfrac{1}{2}$ make?

::solution
The whole-number parts add up to $1+2+3+4=10$. Four copies of $\dfrac{1}{2}$ add up to $4\times\dfrac{1}{2}=2$. Hence:
$$1\dfrac{1}{2}+2\dfrac{1}{2}+3\dfrac{1}{2}+4\dfrac{1}{2}=10+2=12.$$

**Answer.** $12$.
:::

## Mini test

::mc
The mixed number $3\dfrac{4}{7}$ changed into a fraction is
A. $\dfrac{25}{7}$.
B. $\dfrac{34}{7}$.
C. $\dfrac{12}{7}$.
D. $\dfrac{25}{4}$.
::key A
$3\times7+4=25$, so $3\dfrac{4}{7}=\dfrac{25}{7}$. Option C multiplies the whole-number part by the numerator; B writes the digits side by side; D uses the wrong denominator.

::mc
The fraction $\dfrac{31}{9}$ changed into a mixed number is
A. $4\dfrac{3}{9}$.
B. $3\dfrac{4}{9}$.
C. $3\dfrac{5}{9}$.
D. $4\dfrac{4}{9}$.
::key B
$31\div9=3$ remainder $4$, so $\dfrac{31}{9}=3\dfrac{4}{9}$. Option A swaps the quotient and the remainder; C gets the remainder wrong; D rounds the quotient up.

::mc
The largest of these numbers is
A. $2\dfrac{9}{10}$.
B. $3\dfrac{2}{5}$.
C. $3\dfrac{1}{2}$.
D. $2\dfrac{3}{4}$.
::key C
The two numbers with whole-number part $3$ are greater than the two with whole-number part $2$. Between $3\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $3\dfrac{2}{5}$: $\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{5}{10}>\dfrac{4}{10}=\dfrac{2}{5}$. Option A looks only at the largest fractional part; B assumes that a bigger denominator means a bigger fraction.

::mc
$2\dfrac{3}{4}+1\dfrac{2}{3}$ equals
A. $3\dfrac{5}{7}$.
B. $3\dfrac{5}{12}$.
C. $3\dfrac{17}{12}$.
D. $4\dfrac{5}{12}$.
::key D
$\dfrac{3}{4}+\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{17}{12}=1\dfrac{5}{12}$ and $2+1=3$, so the sum is $4\dfrac{5}{12}$. Option A adds numerators and adds denominators; C leaves a fractional part greater than $1$; D forgets to carry $1$.

::tf
Look at the mixed number $3\dfrac{2}{5}$.
a) $3\dfrac{2}{5}=3\times\dfrac{2}{5}$.
b) $3\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{17}{5}$.
c) $3\dfrac{2}{5}>3\dfrac{1}{2}$.
d) $3\dfrac{2}{5}$ is greater than $3$ but less than $4$.
::key F T F T
a) False: $3\dfrac{2}{5}$ means $3+\dfrac{2}{5}$, not $3\times\dfrac{2}{5}$. b) True: $3\times5+2=17$. c) False: $\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{4}{10}<\dfrac{5}{10}=\dfrac{1}{2}$. d) True: the fractional part $\dfrac{2}{5}$ is less than $1$.

::sa
Find the missing number: $4\dfrac{3}{8}=\dfrac{?}{8}$.
::key 35
$4\times8+3=35$.

::sa
Two bags of sugar have masses $2\dfrac{3}{4}$ kg and $1\dfrac{1}{2}$ kg. Together they weigh $\dfrac{a}{4}$ kg. Find $a$.
::key 17
$\dfrac{11}{4}+\dfrac{6}{4}=\dfrac{17}{4}$, so $a=17$.
