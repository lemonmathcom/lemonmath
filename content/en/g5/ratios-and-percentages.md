:::goals
- Write the ratio of two numbers or two quantities of the same kind and simplify a ratio.
- Find one number as a percentage of another, a percentage of a number, and a number when a percentage of it is known.
- Understand map scales and find real distances and distances on a map.
- Solve word problems about ratios, percentages and maps.
:::

Class $5$A has $40$ pupils, of whom $24$ are girls. The class teacher says, "The girls make up $60\%$ of the class." The class is about to go on a trip, and on a map with scale $1:1\,000\,000$ the road from the school to the place they will visit is $7$ cm long.

Why does the teacher say the girls make up $60\%$? And how long is the real road in kilometres? This lesson helps you answer both questions.

## Ratios

### Let's explore

Class $5$A has $16$ boys and $24$ girls. The ratio of the number of boys to the number of girls is $16:24$. Dividing both numbers by $8$ gives $2:3$. This means that for every $2$ boys there are $3$ girls.

:::definition Ratio
The **ratio** of two numbers $a$ and $b$ ($b\ne0$) is $a:b$, also written $\dfrac{a}{b}$. Two quantities of the same kind must be measured in the same unit. We simplify a ratio by dividing both numbers by a common factor.
:::

A ratio tells you how many times as big one number is, or what fraction of the other it is. For example the ratio $2:3$ means that the number of boys is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of the number of girls.

### Examples

:::example Writing ratios
a) The ratio of $16$ to $24$; b) the ratio of $50$ cm to $2$ m.

**Solution.** a) $16:24=2:3$ (dividing both numbers by $8$).

b) The two measurements are in different units, so we change: $2$ m $=200$ cm. The ratio of $50$ cm to $200$ cm is $50:200=1:4$.

So a) $2:3$; b) $1:4$.
:::

A ratio can also compare a part with the whole.

:::example Ratio of red pens to all pens
Suppose a box has $12$ red pens and $18$ blue pens. Find the ratio of the number of red pens to the total number of pens in the box.

**Solution.** The total number of pens is $12+18=30$. The ratio of the number of red pens to the total is $12:30=2:5$.

So the ratio is $2:5$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Not changing to the same unit.** Wrong: the ratio of $50$ cm to $2$ m is $50:2=25:1$. Why it is wrong: the two measurements are in different units, so you cannot compare them directly. Right: $50:200=1:4$.
:::

## Percentages

### Let's explore

Class $5$A has $24$ girls among $40$ pupils. The ratio of the number of girls to the number of pupils in the class is $24:40=\dfrac{24}{40}=\dfrac{3}{5}$. Writing it as a fraction with denominator $100$: $\dfrac{3}{5}=\dfrac{60}{100}$. We say the girls make up $60\%$ of the class (read "sixty per cent").

:::definition Percentage
A ratio of $a$ to $b$ written in the form $\dfrac{x}{100}$ is called a **percentage** and is written $x\%$. To find it: divide $a$ by $b$, multiply the result by $100$ (move the decimal point two places to the right), then write the symbol $\%$.
:::

There are three common types of problem.

:::theorem Three types of percentage problem
1. **Find $A$ as a percentage of $B$:** $A\div B\times100\%$.
2. **Find $x\%$ of a number $B$:** $B\times x\div100$.
3. **Find a number when $x\%$ of it is $A$:** $A\div x\times100$.
:::

Why does this work? Because $x\%=\dfrac{x}{100}$. Finding $x\%$ of $B$ means taking $\dfrac{x}{100}$ of $B$, that is, $B\times\dfrac{x}{100}$. Type $3$ is the inverse: if $x$ per cent of the number is $A$, then $1$ per cent is $A\div x$, and the whole number (that is, $100$ per cent) is $A\div x\times100$.

**Connection.** You use the division of decimals again, and multiplying and dividing by $100$ by moving the decimal point.

### Examples

:::example Finding a percentage
Find the percentage: a) $18$ out of $40$; b) $3$ out of $8$.

**Solution.** a) $18\div40=0.45=45\%$.

b) $3\div8=0.375=37.5\%$.

So a) $45\%$; b) $37.5\%$.
:::

:::example Finding a percentage of a number
Find a) $20\%$ of $80$; b) $15\%$ of $240$.

**Solution.** a) $80\times20\div100=16$.

b) $240\times15\div100=36$.

So a) $16$; b) $36$.
:::

:::example Finding a number when a percentage of it is known
A number has $30\%$ equal to $21$. Find the number.

**Solution.** $1\%$ of the number is $21\div30=0.7$. The whole number is $0.7\times100=70$, or $21\div30\times100=70$.

**Check.** $30\%$ of $70$ is $70\times30\div100=21$. Correct.

So the number is $70$.
:::

Now a real-life problem, solved in four steps.

:::example A discount on a school bag
Suppose a school bag has a marked price of $250\,000$ dong and the shop gives a discount of $20\%$. What is the price of the bag after the discount, in dong?

**Understand.** We know the original price and the percentage discount. We need the price after the discount.

**Plan.** Find the amount of the discount, which is $20\%$ of $250\,000$ dong, then subtract it from the original price.

**Solve.** The amount of the discount is:
$$250\,000\times20\div100=50\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
The price of the bag after the discount is:
$$250\,000-50\,000=200\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
So the price is $200\,000$ dong.

**Look back.** Is there another way? Yes: after a $20\%$ discount the price is $100\%-20\%=80\%$ of the original price, which is $250\,000\times80\div100=200\,000$ dong. Is it reasonable? Yes, because a $20\%$ discount is one fifth off, and $250\,000\div5=50\,000$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting to divide by $100$.** Wrong: $20\%$ of $80$ is $80\times20=1600$. Why it is wrong: $20\%=\dfrac{20}{100}$, so you must divide by $100$. Right: $80\times20\div100=16$.
- **Forgetting to multiply by $100$ when finding a percentage.** Wrong: $24$ as a percentage of $40$ is $24\div40=0.6\%$. Why it is wrong: $0.6$ must be multiplied by $100$ before you write $\%$. Right: $60\%$.
:::

## Map scales and their uses

### Let's explore

A map shows real distances drawn smaller. A **map scale** $1:n$ tells you that a length of $1$ unit on the map stands for $n$ units of the same kind in real life.

On a map with scale $1:1\,000\,000$, $1$ cm on the map stands for $1\,000\,000$ cm in real life. And $1\,000\,000\ \text{cm}=10\,000\ \text{m}=10$ km. So $1$ cm on the map stands for $10$ km in real life.

:::theorem Calculating with a map scale
For a map with scale $1:n$:
- Real distance $=$ distance on the map $\times\ n$.
- Distance on the map $=$ real distance $\div\ n$ (in the same unit).

To change centimetres into kilometres, divide by $100\,000$, because $1$ km $=100\,000$ cm.
:::

Why does this work? The scale $1:n$ is the ratio of the distance on the map to the real distance in the same unit, so the real distance is $n$ times the distance on the map.

### Examples

:::example A real distance
On a map with scale $1:1\,000\,000$, the road from the school to the place to be visited is $7$ cm long. How many kilometres long is the real road?

**Solution.** The real road is:
$$7\times1\,000\,000=7\,000\,000\ \text{(cm)}.$$
Change to kilometres: $7\,000\,000\div100\,000=70$ km.

So the real road is $70$ km long.
:::

:::example Distance on a map and the scale of a map
a) On a map with scale $1:500\,000$, two cities are $45$ km apart in real life. How many centimetres apart are they on the map? b) On a map, a real road of $20$ km is drawn $4$ cm long. What is the scale of the map?

**Solution.** a) $45$ km $=4\,500\,000$ cm. The distance on the map is $4\,500\,000\div500\,000=9$ cm.

b) $20$ km $=2\,000\,000$ cm. The ratio of $4$ cm to $2\,000\,000$ cm is $4:2\,000\,000=1:500\,000$.

So a) $9$ cm; b) scale $1:500\,000$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting to change units.** Wrong: with scale $1:1\,000\,000$, a length of $7$ cm on the map stands for $7\,000\,000$ km in real life. Why it is wrong: $7\,000\,000$ is a number of centimetres, which must be changed to kilometres. Right: $70$ km.
:::

## Summary

- The ratio of $a$ to $b$ is $a:b$; the measurements must be in the same unit, and you can simplify a ratio.
- To find a percentage: divide $a$ by $b$, multiply by $100$, write $\%$.
- $x\%$ of $B$ is $B\times x\div100$; the number with $x\%$ equal to $A$ is $A\div x\times100$.
- On a map with scale $1:n$, the real distance is the distance on the map times $n$.
- Remember to change units: $1$ km $=100\,000$ cm.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Write the ratio of: a) $15$ to $20$; b) $18$ to $12$; c) $30$ cm to $1.5$ m.

::hint
**What you need:** the ratio of $a$ to $b$ is $a:b$; the measurements must be in the same unit.

**Steps:**
1. Change to the same unit if needed.
2. Write the ratio and simplify it.

::solution
a) $15:20=3:4$.

b) $18:12=3:2$.

c) $1.5$ m $=150$ cm, so $30:150=1:5$.

**Answer.** a) $3:4$; b) $3:2$; c) $1:5$.
:::

:::exercise
A yard has $12$ chickens and $28$ ducks. Find the ratio of the number of chickens to the number of ducks, and the ratio of the number of chickens to the total number of chickens and ducks.

::hint
**What you need:** a ratio can compare one part with another part, or with the whole.

**Steps:**
1. Write the ratio of chickens to ducks.
2. Work out the total number of animals, then write the ratio of chickens to the total.

::solution
The ratio of the number of chickens to the number of ducks is $12:28=3:7$.

The total number of chickens and ducks is $12+28=40$. The ratio of the number of chickens to the total is $12:40=3:10$.

**Answer.** $3:7$ and $3:10$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the percentage: a) $7$ out of $20$; b) $9$ out of $12$; c) $3$ out of $8$.

::hint
**What you need:** divide $a$ by $b$, then multiply by $100$.

**Steps:**
1. Divide the first number by the second.
2. Multiply the result by $100$ and write $\%$.

::solution
a) $7\div20=0.35=35\%$.

b) $9\div12=0.75=75\%$.

c) $3\div8=0.375=37.5\%$.

**Answer.** a) $35\%$; b) $75\%$; c) $37.5\%$.
:::

:::exercise
Find: a) $25\%$ of $120$; b) $8\%$ of $350$; c) $150\%$ of $60$.

::hint
**What you need:** $x\%$ of $B$ is $B\times x\div100$.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply the number by the percentage.
2. Divide by $100$.

::solution
a) $120\times25\div100=30$.

b) $350\times8\div100=28$.

c) $60\times150\div100=90$.

**Answer.** a) $30$; b) $28$; c) $90$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the number, given that: a) $40\%$ of it is $18$; b) $15\%$ of it is $12$.

::hint
**What you need:** the number you want is $A\div x\times100$.

**Steps:**
1. Find $1\%$ of the number.
2. Multiply by $100$. Check by finding $x\%$ of the number you found.

::solution
a) $18\div40\times100=45$. Check: $45\times40\div100=18$.

b) $12\div15\times100=80$. Check: $80\times15\div100=12$.

**Answer.** a) $45$; b) $80$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a school has $600$ pupils, of whom $45\%$ are girls. Find the number of girls and the number of boys in the school.

::hint
**What you need:** the number of girls is $45\%$ of $600$; the number of boys is the rest.

**Steps:**
1. Find $45\%$ of $600$.
2. Subtract the number of girls from the total number of pupils.

::solution
The number of girls is $600\times45\div100=270$ (pupils).

The number of boys is $600-270=330$ (pupils).

**Answer.** $270$ girls; $330$ boys.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a shirt has a marked price of $180\,000$ dong and a discount of $15\%$. What is the price of the shirt after the discount, in dong?

::hint
**What you need:** the amount of the discount is $15\%$ of the marked price.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the amount of the discount.
2. Subtract it from the original price.

::solution
The amount of the discount is $180\,000\times15\div100=27\,000$ dong.

The price of the shirt after the discount is $180\,000-27\,000=153\,000$ (dong).

**Answer.** $153\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on multiplying and dividing decimals by $10$; $100$; $1000$. On a map with scale $1:100\,000$, the distance between two villages is $6.5$ cm. How many kilometres apart are the villages in real life?

::hint
**What you need:** the real distance is the distance on the map times $100\,000$; $1$ km $=100\,000$ cm.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply $6.5$ by $100\,000$ by moving the decimal point.
2. Change the result (in centimetres) into kilometres.

::solution
The real distance is $6.5\times100\,000=650\,000$ cm.

Change to kilometres: $650\,000\div100\,000=6.5$ km.

**Answer.** $6.5$ km.
:::

:::exercise
On a map with scale $1:2\,000\,000$, two cities are $60$ km apart in real life. How many centimetres apart are they on the map?

::hint
**What you need:** the distance on the map is the real distance divided by $n$ (in the same unit).

**Steps:**
1. Change $60$ km into centimetres.
2. Divide by $2\,000\,000$.

::solution
$60$ km $=6\,000\,000$ cm. The distance on the map is $6\,000\,000\div2\,000\,000=3$ cm.

**Answer.** $3$ cm.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
The price of an item is increased by $20\%$, and then decreased by $20\%$ (of the increased price). What percentage of the original price is the final price?

::hint
**What you need:** choose the original price to be an easy number, for example $100\,000$ dong.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the price after the $20\%$ increase.
2. Work out the price after a $20\%$ decrease of that price, then compare with the original price.

::solution
Take the original price as $100\,000$ dong. After the $20\%$ increase, the price is $100\,000+100\,000\times20\div100=120\,000$ dong.

A $20\%$ decrease of $120\,000$ dong is $120\,000\times20\div100=24\,000$ dong, so the final price is $120\,000-24\,000=96\,000$ dong.

$96\,000$ out of $100\,000$ as a percentage is $96\,000\div100\,000=0.96=96\%$.

**Answer.** $96\%$ of the original price (a decrease of $4\%$).
:::
