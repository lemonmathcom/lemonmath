:::goals
- Read and write natural numbers with many digits and state the value of each digit.
- Compare natural numbers and round a number to a given place.
- Carry out the operations in an expression in the correct order.
- Use the divisibility tests for $2$, $3$, $5$ and $9$ and solve multi-step word problems.
:::

Suppose a city has $1\,250\,400$ residents, and another city has $1\,205\,900$ residents. In a news report, someone wants to write the population of the first city in a shorter way.

How do you read the number $1\,250\,400$? Which city has more residents? When the population of the first city is rounded to the nearest hundred thousand, what number do you get? This lesson helps you review the key ideas about natural numbers so you can answer questions like these.

## Let's explore

The value of each digit of a number depends on the place it is in. Three consecutive places make a **period**. Put the number $1\,250\,400$ into a place-value table:

| Millions period | | | Thousands period | | | Ones period | | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hundred millions | ten millions | millions | hundred thousands | ten thousands | thousands | hundreds | tens | ones |
| | | 1 | 2 | 5 | 0 | 4 | 0 | 0 |

Read from left to right, one period at a time: **one million two hundred and fifty thousand four hundred**. The digit $5$ is in the ten thousands place, so its value is $50\,000$.

:::theorem Rules to remember
- **Comparing:** the number with more digits is greater. If two numbers have the same number of digits, compare the digits in the same place, from left to right, until you reach a pair of digits that differ.
- **Rounding to a place:** look at the digit in the place immediately to its right. If that digit is less than $5$, keep the digit in the place being rounded; if it is $5$ or more, increase that digit by $1$. The digits to the right become $0$.
- **Order of operations:** do brackets first; multiplication and division before addition and subtraction; operations of the same level from left to right.
- **Divisibility tests:** by $2$ if the last digit is $0$, $2$, $4$, $6$ or $8$; by $5$ if the last digit is $0$ or $5$; by $9$ if the sum of the digits is divisible by $9$; by $3$ if the sum of the digits is divisible by $3$.
:::

Why do we compare from left to right? A digit in a higher place is worth much more, so we only need to compare the digits in the highest place first. Why does the digit sum tell us about divisibility by $9$? Because each of $10$, $100$, $1\,000$, ... leaves remainder $1$ when divided by $9$, so the whole number leaves the same remainder as its digit sum when divided by $9$.

**Connection.** This lesson gathers what you learned in earlier years: reading and writing numbers, comparing, the four operations and divisibility tests. It prepares you for fractions and decimals in Year $5$.

## Examples

We start with reading numbers and the value of digits.

:::example Reading a number and the value of a digit
Read the number $1\,250\,400$ and state the value of the digit $2$ in it.

**Solution.** The number $1\,250\,400$ is made of $1$ million, $250$ thousand and $400$ ones. We read it as: one million two hundred and fifty thousand four hundred.

The digit $2$ is in the hundred thousands place, so its value is $200\,000$.

So the digit $2$ in $1\,250\,400$ has value $200\,000$.
:::

Now comparing two numbers.

:::example Comparing two numbers
Compare $1\,250\,400$ and $1\,205\,900$.

**Solution.** Both numbers have $7$ digits. We compare from left to right:
- Millions: $1=1$.
- Hundred thousands: $2=2$.
- Ten thousands: $5>0$.

So $1\,250\,400>1\,205\,900$, which means the first city has more residents.
:::

Next, rounding.

:::example Rounding a number
Round $1\,250\,400$ to the nearest thousand and to the nearest hundred thousand.

**Solution.** To the nearest thousand: the thousands digit is $0$, the hundreds digit just to its right is $4<5$, so we keep it: $1\,250\,000$.

To the nearest hundred thousand: the hundred thousands digit is $2$, the ten thousands digit just to its right is $5$, so we increase $2$ by $1$ to make $3$: $1\,300\,000$.

So to the nearest thousand we get $1\,250\,000$ and to the nearest hundred thousand we get $1\,300\,000$.
:::

The order of operations matters a lot.

:::example Order of operations
Work out the values of these expressions:
a) $48+36\div4\times3-5$
b) $(48+36)\div4\times3-5$

**Solution.** a) Multiplication and division first (from left to right), then addition and subtraction:
$$\begin{aligned} 48+36\div4\times3-5 &= 48+9\times3-5 \\ &= 48+27-5 \\ &= 70. \end{aligned}$$
b) Brackets first:
$$\begin{aligned} (48+36)\div4\times3-5 &= 84\div4\times3-5 \\ &= 21\times3-5 \\ &= 58. \end{aligned}$$
So a) $70$; b) $58$.
:::

Divisibility tests give you a quick check.

:::example Divisibility tests
Which of the numbers $2$, $3$, $5$, $9$ does $4\,320$ divide by?

**Solution.** The last digit is $0$, so $4\,320$ is divisible by $2$ and by $5$.

The digit sum is $4+3+2+0=9$. Since $9$ is divisible by $9$ and by $3$, $4\,320$ is divisible by $9$ and by $3$.

So $4\,320$ is divisible by all of $2$, $3$, $5$ and $9$.
:::

Finally, a multi-step problem that uses all four operations.

:::example A stationery shop
A shop receives $36$ cartons of exercise books, each carton holding $120$ books. During the week, the shop sells $2\,500$ books. The remaining books are packed into boxes, each box holding $20$ books. How many boxes can be filled?

**Understand.** There are $36$ cartons of $120$ books each; $2\,500$ books have been sold; the rest are packed into boxes of $20$ books. We need the number of boxes.

**Plan.** Find the total number of books (multiply), the number left (subtract), then the number of boxes (divide).

**Solve.** The number of books received is $36\times120=4\,320$ (books).

The number of books left is $4\,320-2\,500=1\,820$ (books).

The number of boxes filled is $1\,820\div20=91$ (boxes).

**Answer:** $91$ boxes.

**Look back.** Check: $91\times20=1\,820$ and $1\,820+2\,500=4\,320$. Correct. Since $1\,820$ is divisible by $20$, no books are left over.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Comparing from right to left.** Wrong: $1\,205\,900>1\,250\,400$ because $9>4$. Why it is wrong: digits in higher places are worth more, so we must compare from left to right. Right: $1\,250\,400>1\,205\,900$ because $5>0$ in the ten thousands place.
- **Rounding by looking only at the digit being rounded.** Wrong: $1\,250\,400$ rounded to the nearest hundred thousand is $1\,200\,000$. Why it is wrong: we must look at the digit to the right, which is $5$, so we round up. Right: $1\,300\,000$.
- **Adding before dividing.** Wrong: $48+36\div4=84\div4=21$. Why it is wrong: division must be done before addition. Right: $48+36\div4=48+9=57$.
:::

## Summary

- Natural numbers are read one period at a time; the value of a digit depends on its place.
- Comparing natural numbers: more digits means greater; with the same number of digits, compare place by place from left to right.
- Rounding to a place: look at the digit to the right; less than $5$ means keep, $5$ or more means increase by $1$.
- Order of operations: brackets, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction.
- Divisibility by $2$ and $5$ depends on the last digit; divisibility by $3$ and $9$ depends on the digit sum.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Write the number: eight million four hundred and five thousand two hundred and thirty.

::hint
**What you need:** write the periods in turn: millions, thousands, ones.

**Steps:**
1. Write the millions, the thousands and the ones.
2. Use the digit $0$ for any empty place.

::solution
Eight million is $8$; four hundred and five thousand is $405$ thousand; two hundred and thirty is $230$.

**Answer.** $8\,405\,230$.
:::

:::exercise
State the value of the digit $7$ in the number $3\,571\,092$.

::hint
**What you need:** the value of a digit depends on its place.

**Steps:**
1. Which place is the digit $7$ in?
2. Write its value.

::solution
In the number $3\,571\,092$ the digit $7$ is in the ten thousands place.

**Answer.** The value of the digit $7$ is $70\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
Put $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the gap: $3\,571\,092\ \ldots\ 3\,517\,920$.

::hint
**What you need:** for numbers with the same number of digits, compare from left to right.

**Steps:**
1. Compare the millions and the hundred thousands.
2. Stop at the first pair of digits that differ.

::solution
Both numbers have $7$ digits. Millions: $3=3$. Hundred thousands: $5=5$. Ten thousands: $7>1$.

**Answer.** $3\,571\,092>3\,517\,920$.
:::

:::exercise
Put the numbers $45\,720$; $45\,270$; $54\,027$; $5\,472$ in order from least to greatest.

::hint
**What you need:** the number with fewer digits is less; with the same number of digits, compare place by place.

**Steps:**
1. Find the number with the fewest digits.
2. Compare the others from left to right.

::solution
The number $5\,472$ has $4$ digits, so it is the least. Among the numbers with $5$ digits: $45\,270<45\,720<54\,027$.

**Answer.** $5\,472<45\,270<45\,720<54\,027$.
:::

:::exercise
Round $6\,481\,300$ to the nearest thousand and to the nearest hundred thousand.

::hint
**What you need:** look at the digit immediately to the right of the place you are rounding to.

**Steps:**
1. Thousands: look at the hundreds digit.
2. Hundred thousands: look at the ten thousands digit.

::solution
To the nearest thousand: the hundreds digit is $3<5$, so we get $6\,481\,000$.

To the nearest hundred thousand: the ten thousands digit is $8\geq5$, so increase $4$ by $1$ to make $5$, giving $6\,500\,000$.

**Answer.** $6\,481\,000$ and $6\,500\,000$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out the value of the expression: $125\times8-360\div6+14$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiplication and division first; then addition and subtraction from left to right.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $125\times8$ and $360\div6$.
2. Then subtract and add from left to right.

::solution
$$\begin{aligned} 125\times8-360\div6+14 &= 1\,000-60+14 \\ &= 954. \end{aligned}$$

**Answer.** $954$.
:::

:::exercise
A publisher prints $24\,500$ books in January and $31\,750$ books in February. How many books does the publisher print in the two months altogether? Round the result to the nearest thousand.

::hint
**What you need:** "in the two months altogether" means addition; to round, look at the hundreds digit.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Add the two numbers, then round to the nearest thousand.

::solution
The number of books printed in the two months is $24\,500+31\,750=56\,250$ (books).

To the nearest thousand: the hundreds digit is $2<5$, so we get $56\,000$.

**Answer.** $56\,250$ books, about $56\,000$ books.
:::

:::exercise
Mum has $100\,000$ dong and buys $3$ cartons of milk at $24\,500$ dong each. How much money does Mum have left?

::hint
**What you need:** multiply the number of cartons by the price of one, then subtract.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Work out the cost of the milk, then subtract it from $100\,000$.

::solution
The cost of $3$ cartons of milk is $24\,500\times3=73\,500$ (dong).

The money Mum has left is $100\,000-73\,500=26\,500$ (dong).

**Answer.** $26\,500$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on properties of multiplication. Calculate in the easy way: $25\times37\times4$.

::hint
**What you need:** $25\times4=100$; the commutative and associative properties.

**Steps:**
1. Group $25$ with $4$.
2. Multiply the result by $37$.

::solution
$$25\times37\times4=(25\times4)\times37=100\times37=3\,700.$$

**Answer.** $3\,700$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find the digit $a$ for which the number $\overline{4a5}$ is divisible by both $5$ and $9$.

::hint
**What you need:** a number ending in $5$ is divisible by $5$; it is divisible by $9$ when its digit sum is divisible by $9$.

**Steps:**
1. Write the digit sum: $4+a+5$.
2. Find $a$ so that this sum is divisible by $9$.

::solution
The number $\overline{4a5}$ ends in $5$, so it is always divisible by $5$.

The digit sum is $4+a+5=9+a$. For this to be divisible by $9$, $a$ must be $0$ or $9$.

**Check.** $405\div9=45$ and $495\div9=55$. Correct.

**Answer.** $a=0$ or $a=9$.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
The number "two million three hundred and five thousand four hundred" is written
A. $2\,350\,400$.
B. $2\,035\,400$.
C. $2\,305\,400$.
D. $2\,305\,040$.
::key C
Two million is $2$, three hundred and five thousand is $305$ thousand, four hundred is $400$: $2\,305\,400$.

::mc
$3\,467\,000$ rounded to the nearest hundred thousand is
A. $3\,400\,000$.
B. $3\,460\,000$.
C. $3\,000\,000$.
D. $3\,500\,000$.
::key D
The ten thousands digit is $6\geq5$, so increase the hundred thousands digit $4$ by $1$ to make $5$: $3\,500\,000$.

::mc
The value of the expression $20-18\div3+4\times2$ is
A. $22$.
B. $30$.
C. $6$.
D. $14$.
::key A
$18\div3=6$ and $4\times2=8$, so $20-6+8=22$. Do multiplication and division first, then addition and subtraction from left to right.

::mc
Which of these numbers is divisible by both $2$ and $9$?
A. $4\,325$.
B. $4\,320$.
C. $4\,323$.
D. $4\,322$.
::key B
$4\,320$ ends in $0$ and its digit sum is $9$. Option A is odd, option C is odd and has digit sum $12$, and option D has digit sum $11$.

::tf
Look at the number $4\,320$.
a) The digit sum of $4\,320$ is $10$.
b) $4\,320$ is divisible by $9$.
c) $4\,320$ is divisible by $2$.
d) $4\,320$ rounded to the nearest thousand is $5\,000$.
::key F T T F
a) False: $4+3+2+0=9$. b) True: the digit sum is $9$. c) True: it ends in $0$. d) False: the hundreds digit is $3<5$, so we get $4\,000$.

::sa
Work out $125\times8-360\div6$.
::key 940
$125\times8=1\,000$ and $360\div6=60$, so $1\,000-60=940$.

::sa
A store room has $5\,000$ kg of rice. A shop sells rice $3$ times, $1\,250$ kg each time. How many kilograms of rice are left in the store room?
::key 1250
The rice sold is $1\,250\times3=3\,750$ (kg). The rice left is $5\,000-3\,750=1\,250$ (kg).
:::
