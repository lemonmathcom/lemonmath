:::goals
- Know how to find the volume of a cuboid: $V=a\times b\times c$.
- Change the lengths to the same unit before you calculate.
- Solve word problems about volume, and find a missing length when you know the volume.
:::

Mai has a box in the shape of a cuboid. It is $4$ cm long, $3$ cm wide and $2$ cm high. Mai wants to fill it with small cubes, each with edges of $1$ cm. How many cubes does she need?

You could pack the cubes and count them one by one. But with a bigger box that takes a very long time. This lesson shows you a quick way.

## Let's explore

Each small cube with edges of $1$ cm has a volume of $1$ cm³. So the number of cubes that fill the box is the volume of the box, in cm³.

Mai packs the box layer by layer:

- The bottom layer has $4$ cubes along the length and $3$ rows along the width. This layer has $4\times3=12$ cubes.
- The box is $2$ cm high, so there are $2$ layers like this.
- The number of cubes is $12\times2=24$.

So the box has a volume of $24$ cm³. You see: $24=4\times3\times2$, which is the three measurements of the box multiplied together.

:::theorem Volume of a cuboid
A cuboid with length $a$, width $b$ and height $c$ (all in the same unit) has volume
$$V=a\times b\times c.$$
If $a$, $b$ and $c$ are in cm, then $V$ is in cm³. It is the same with dm and m.
:::

The rule works because the bottom layer has $a\times b$ cubes, which is the base area. The box has $c$ layers, so the number of cubes is $a\times b\times c$. So you can also remember: volume is base area times height.

## Examples

We start with a cuboid whose three measurements are in the same unit.

:::example Find the volume of a cuboid $6$ cm long, $4$ cm wide and $5$ cm high
**Solution.** All three measurements are in cm, so multiply the three numbers:
$$V=6\times4\times5=120\ (\text{cm}^3).$$
So the volume of the cuboid is $120$ cm³.
:::

Now use the rule on an object from everyday life.

:::example Volume of a classroom
A classroom is a cuboid $8$ m long, $6$ m wide and $3$ m high. What is the volume of the air in the room, in cubic metres?

**Solution.** The room is a cuboid and all measurements are in m, so use $V=a\times b\times c$:
$$V=8\times6\times3=144\ (\text{m}^3).$$
So the volume of air in the room is $144$ m³.
:::

Sometimes the measurements are in different units. Then you change them to the same unit first.

:::example A box with two units
A cuboid-shaped box is $2$ dm long, $15$ cm wide and $10$ cm high. Find its volume in cm³.

**Solution.** The measurements are in dm and cm, so change the length to cm: $2$ dm $=20$ cm. Then calculate:
$$V=20\times15\times10=3000\ (\text{cm}^3).$$
So the volume of the box is $3000$ cm³.
:::

Finally, we go the other way: we know the volume and look for a missing measurement.

:::example Find the height
A cuboid has volume $90$ cm³, length $6$ cm and width $5$ cm. Find its height.

**Solution.** Volume is base area times height, so the height is the volume divided by the base area. The base area is:
$$6\times5=30\ (\text{cm}^2).$$
The height is:
$$90\div30=3\ (\text{cm}).$$
**Check.** $6\times5\times3=90$. Correct.

So the height of the cuboid is $3$ cm.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Adding instead of multiplying.** Wrong: $4+3+2=9$. Why it is wrong: volume counts the cubes in the layers, so you must multiply. Right: $4\times3\times2=24$.
- **Not changing to the same unit.** Wrong: length $2$ dm, width $15$ cm, height $10$ cm, then $2\times15\times10=300$. Why it is wrong: $2$ dm and $15$ cm are not in the same unit. Right: $20\times15\times10=3000$ (cm³).
- **Mixing up cm² and cm³.** Wrong: "The volume is $24$ cm²". Why it is wrong: volume counts cubes, so the unit is a cubic unit. Right: "$24$ cm³".
:::

## Summary

- Volume of a cuboid: $V=a\times b\times c$ (length × width × height).
- The three measurements must be in the same unit. If they are not, change them before you calculate.
- Units of volume are cm³, dm³, m³.
- Volume is base area times height, so height is volume divided by base area.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Find the volume of a cuboid $7$ cm long, $3$ cm wide and $4$ cm high.

::hint
**What you need:** $V=a\times b\times c$.

**Steps:**
1. Write the product of the three measurements.
2. Multiply step by step, then write the unit cm³.

::solution
$V=7\times3\times4=21\times4=84\ (\text{cm}^3)$.

**Answer.** $84$ cm³.
:::

:::exercise
Find the volume of a cuboid $10$ cm long, $5$ cm wide and $2$ cm high.

::hint
**What you need:** $V=a\times b\times c$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $10\times5$ first.
2. Multiply the result by $2$.

::solution
$V=10\times5\times2=50\times2=100\ (\text{cm}^3)$.

**Answer.** $100$ cm³.
:::

:::exercise
Find the volume of a cuboid $12$ dm long, $5$ dm wide and $3$ dm high.

::hint
**What you need:** the measurements are all in dm, so the volume is in dm³.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $12\times5$.
2. Multiply the result by $3$ and write the unit dm³.

::solution
$V=12\times5\times3=60\times3=180\ (\text{dm}^3)$.

**Answer.** $180$ dm³.
:::

:::exercise
Ha's pencil case is a cuboid $18$ cm long, $6$ cm wide and $4$ cm high. What is the volume of the pencil case in cubic centimetres?

::hint
**What you need:** volume of a cuboid is length × width × height.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? The three measurements of the case. What is asked? The volume.
2. Work out $18\times6\times4$ and write the answer with its unit.

::solution
The volume of the pencil case is:
$$18\times6\times4=108\times4=432\ (\text{cm}^3).$$

**Answer.** $432$ cm³.
:::

:::exercise
A fish tank is a cuboid $1$ m long, $6$ dm wide and $5$ dm high. Find the volume of the tank in dm³.

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ m $=10$ dm. The three measurements must be in the same unit.

**Steps:**
1. Change the length to dm.
2. Multiply the three measurements and write the unit dm³.

::solution
Change $1$ m $=10$ dm. The volume of the tank is:
$$10\times6\times5=300\ (\text{dm}^3).$$

**Answer.** $300$ dm³.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson: a cube has edges of $4$ cm. Find its lateral surface area and its total surface area.

::hint
**What you need:** the area of one face is edge × edge. The lateral surface has $4$ faces and the total surface has $6$ faces.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the area of one face.
2. Multiply by $4$ for the lateral surface area, and by $6$ for the total surface area.

::solution
The area of one face is $4\times4=16$ (cm²).

Lateral surface area: $16\times4=64$ (cm²). Total surface area: $16\times6=96$ (cm²).

**Answer.** Lateral surface area $64$ cm², total surface area $96$ cm².
:::

:::exercise
A cuboid has volume $120$ cm³, length $8$ cm and width $5$ cm. Find its height.

::hint
**What you need:** height is the volume divided by the base area.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the base area $8\times5$.
2. Divide $120$ by the base area.

::solution
The base area is $8\times5=40$ (cm²). The height is $120\div40=3$ (cm).

**Check.** $8\times5\times3=120$. Correct.

**Answer.** $3$ cm.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A crate is a cuboid $40$ cm long, $30$ cm wide and $20$ cm high. You pack small cuboid boxes, each $10$ cm long, $10$ cm wide and $5$ cm high, into the crate with no gaps until it is full. What is the greatest number of small boxes that fit?

::hint
**What you need:** the small boxes fill the crate, so the volume of the crate equals the total volume of the small boxes.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the volume of the crate and the volume of one small box.
2. Divide the volume of the crate by the volume of one small box.

::solution
The volume of the crate is $40\times30\times20=24\,000$ (cm³). The volume of one small box is $10\times10\times5=500$ (cm³). The number of small boxes is $24\,000\div500=48$ (boxes).

**Check.** Along the length there are $40\div10=4$ boxes, along the width $30\div10=3$ boxes, and in height $20\div5=4$ layers. That is $4\times3\times4=48$ boxes. Correct.

**Answer.** $48$ boxes.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
The volume of a cuboid $5$ cm long, $4$ cm wide and $3$ cm high is
A. $60$ cm³.
B. $12$ cm³.
C. $20$ cm³.
D. $60$ cm².
::key A
$5\times4\times3=60$ (cm³). Option D has the wrong unit, B adds the three numbers, and C forgets the height.

::mc
A cuboid-shaped box is $3$ dm long, $20$ cm wide and $10$ cm high. Its volume is
A. $33$ cm³.
B. $6000$ cm³.
C. $600$ cm³.
D. $6$ cm³.
::key B
Change $3$ dm $=30$ cm, so $30\times20\times10=6000$ (cm³). Option C is the mistake of not changing the unit.

::mc
A cuboid has volume $96$ cm³, length $8$ cm and width $4$ cm. Its height is
A. $24$ cm.
B. $12$ cm.
C. $3$ cm.
D. $84$ cm.
::key C
The base area is $8\times4=32$ (cm²), so the height is $96\div32=3$ (cm). Options B and A divide by only one measurement, and D subtracts.

::mc
Which cuboid has the greatest volume?
A. Length $6$ cm, width $3$ cm, height $2$ cm.
B. Length $12$ cm, width $1$ cm, height $1$ cm.
C. Length $8$ cm, width $2$ cm, height $2$ cm.
D. Length $4$ cm, width $4$ cm, height $3$ cm.
::key D
The volumes are $36$, $48$, $32$ and $12$ (cm³). Option B has the greatest sum of measurements, but the smallest volume.

::tf
A box is a cuboid $6$ cm long, $5$ cm wide and $4$ cm high.
a) The volume of the box is $120$ cm³.
b) The volume of the box is $6+5+4=15$ cm³.
c) If the length and width stay the same and the height becomes $8$ cm, the volume is $240$ cm³.
d) You can say the volume of the box is $120$ cm².
::key T F T F
a) True: $6\times5\times4=120$. b) False: you must multiply, not add. c) True: $6\times5\times8=240$. d) False: the unit of volume is cm³, not cm².

::sa
Find the volume of a cuboid $9$ cm long, $5$ cm wide and $4$ cm high (in cm³).
::key 180
$9\times5\times4=45\times4=180$ (cm³).

::sa
Small cubes with edges of $1$ cm fill a box $5$ cm long, $3$ cm wide and $2$ cm high. How many cubes are needed?
::key 30
The number of cubes equals the volume of the box: $5\times3\times2=30$ (cubes).
:::
