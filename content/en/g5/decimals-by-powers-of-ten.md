:::goals
- Multiply and divide decimals by $10$; $100$; $1000$; ... by moving the decimal point.
- Multiply and divide decimals by $0.1$; $0.01$; $0.001$; ... by moving the decimal point.
- Use this to change units of measurement quickly.
- Solve word problems using these operations.
:::

A roll of electric cable is $2.35$ m long, and another piece of cable is $45$ cm long. You want to change $2.35$ m into centimetres and $45$ cm into metres.

How many centimetres is $2.35$ m, and how many metres is $45$ cm? Is there a very quick way to change them?

## Let's explore

We have $1$ m $=100$ cm, so changing metres to centimetres means multiplying by $100$:
$$2.35\times100=\dfrac{235}{100}\times100=235.$$
So $2.35$ m $=235$ cm. The decimal point of $2.35$ has moved two places to the right.

Changing back, $1$ cm $=\dfrac{1}{100}$ m $=0.01$ m, so $45$ cm $=45\times0.01=\dfrac{45}{100}=0.45$ m. This calculation is also dividing $45$ by $100$, and the decimal point moves two places to the left.

See how the digits of $2.35$ change place when you multiply by $10$ and by $100$:

| Number | Hundreds | Tens | Ones | Tenths | Hundredths |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $2.35$ | | | $2$ | $3$ | $5$ |
| $2.35\times10=23.5$ | | $2$ | $3$ | $5$ | |
| $2.35\times100=235$ | $2$ | $3$ | $5$ | | |

:::theorem Multiplying and dividing by $10$; $100$; $1000$; ... and $0.1$; $0.01$; $0.001$; ...
- **Multiplying by $10$; $100$; $1000$; ...:** move the decimal point **right** by one, two, three, ... places (write the digit $0$ on the right if there are not enough digits).
- **Dividing by $10$; $100$; $1000$; ...:** move the decimal point **left** by one, two, three, ... places (write the digit $0$ on the left if there are not enough digits).
- **Multiplying by $0.1$; $0.01$; $0.001$; ...** is the same as dividing by $10$; $100$; $1000$; ...
- **Dividing by $0.1$; $0.01$; $0.001$; ...** is the same as multiplying by $10$; $100$; $1000$; ...
:::

Why does this work? Each place is $10$ times the place to its right. Multiplying by $10$ moves every digit up one place, which means the decimal point moves one place to the right. And $0.1=\dfrac{1}{10}$, so multiplying by $0.1$ means taking one tenth, that is, dividing by $10$. Dividing by $0.1$ asks how many tenths there are, so it equals multiplying by $10$.

**Connection.** You use the multiplication and division of decimals from the last two lessons, and changing units from the lesson on measurements as decimals.

## Examples

We start with multiplication.

:::example Multiply
a) $3.57\times10$; b) $0.048\times100$; c) $6.2\times1000$.

**Solution.** a) Move the decimal point right by one place: $3.57\times10=35.7$.

b) Move the decimal point right by two places: $0.048\times100=4.8$.

c) Move the decimal point right by three places, but $6.2$ has only one decimal place, so write two digits $0$: $6.2\times1000=6200$.

So a) $35.7$; b) $4.8$; c) $6200$.
:::

Next, division.

:::example Divide
a) $45.6\div10$; b) $7.5\div100$; c) $38\div1000$.

**Solution.** a) Move the decimal point left by one place: $45.6\div10=4.56$.

b) Move the decimal point left by two places; there is one digit missing, so write the digit $0$ on the left: $7.5\div100=0.075$.

c) $38=38.0$; move the decimal point left by three places, writing the digit $0$ on the left: $38\div1000=0.038$.

So a) $4.56$; b) $0.075$; c) $0.038$.
:::

Now $0.1$ and $0.01$.

:::example Work out
a) $56\times0.1$; b) $4.5\div0.01$; c) $0.82\div0.1$.

**Solution.** a) Multiplying by $0.1$ is dividing by $10$: $56\times0.1=56\div10=5.6$.

b) Dividing by $0.01$ is multiplying by $100$: $4.5\div0.01=4.5\times100=450$.

c) Dividing by $0.1$ is multiplying by $10$: $0.82\div0.1=0.82\times10=8.2$.

So a) $5.6$; b) $450$; c) $8.2$.
:::

Moving the decimal point helps you change units of measurement.

:::example Changing units
a) $2.35$ m $=?$ cm; b) $45$ cm $=?$ m; c) $3.2$ tonnes $=?$ kg; d) $750$ g $=?$ kg.

**Solution.** a) $1$ m $=100$ cm, so multiply by $100$: $2.35\times100=235$ cm.

b) $1$ m $=100$ cm, so divide by $100$: $45\div100=0.45$ m.

c) $1$ tonne $=1000$ kg, so multiply by $1000$: $3.2\times1000=3200$ kg.

d) $1$ kg $=1000$ g, so divide by $1000$: $750\div1000=0.75$ kg.

So a) $235$ cm; b) $0.45$ m; c) $3200$ kg; d) $0.75$ kg.
:::

Now a real-life problem, solved in four steps.

:::example Boxes of biscuits
Suppose a factory packs $1000$ boxes of biscuits each day, and each box has mass $0.35$ kg. How many kilograms of biscuits does the factory pack each day, and how many tonnes is that?

**Understand.** We know the number of boxes and the mass of one box. We need the total mass, in kilograms and in tonnes.

**Plan.** Multiply the mass of one box by the number of boxes, then change the result into tonnes.

**Solve.** The mass of biscuits packed each day is:
$$0.35\times1000=350\ \text{(kg)}.$$
Since $1$ tonne $=1000$ kg, we have $350\ \text{kg}=350\div1000=0.35$ tonnes.

So the factory packs $350$ kg, which is $0.35$ tonnes.

**Look back.** Is there another way? Yes: each box is $350$ g, and $1000$ boxes are $350\,000$ g $=350$ kg. Is it reasonable? Yes, because $1000$ boxes each lighter than $1$ kg have a total mass of less than $1000$ kg.
:::

Finally, finding the missing number.

:::example Find the missing number
a) $4.5\times?=450$; b) $4.5\div?=0.045$.

**Solution.** a) From $4.5$ to $450$ the decimal point moves two places to the right, so we multiply by $100$.

b) From $4.5$ to $0.045$ the decimal point moves two places to the left, so we divide by $100$.

So a) $100$; b) $100$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Moving the decimal point the wrong way.** Wrong: $45.6\div10=456$. Why it is wrong: dividing makes a number smaller, so the decimal point moves to the left. Right: $4.56$.
- **Forgetting to write extra digits $0$.** Wrong: $6.2\times1000=62$. Why it is wrong: the decimal point must move three places but $6.2$ has only one decimal place, so two digits $0$ are needed. Right: $6200$.
- **Treating multiplying by $0.1$ as multiplying by $10$.** Wrong: $56\times0.1=560$. Why it is wrong: $0.1=\dfrac{1}{10}$, so multiplying by $0.1$ is dividing by $10$. Right: $5.6$.
:::

## Summary

- Multiplying by $10$; $100$; $1000$; ...: move the decimal point **right** by one, two, three, ... places.
- Dividing by $10$; $100$; $1000$; ...: move the decimal point **left** by one, two, three, ... places.
- Multiplying by $0.1$; $0.01$; ... is dividing by $10$; $100$; ...
- Dividing by $0.1$; $0.01$; ... is multiplying by $10$; $100$; ...
- Write the digit $0$ if digits are missing; change units of measurement by multiplying or dividing by $10$; $100$; $1000$.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out in your head: a) $4.26\times10$; b) $0.035\times100$; c) $7.8\times1000$; d) $0.5\times1000$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiplying by $10$; $100$; $1000$ moves the decimal point right by one, two, three places.

**Steps:**
1. Count the digits $0$ in the multiplier.
2. Move the decimal point right by that many places, writing the digit $0$ if there are not enough digits.

::solution
a) $4.26\times10=42.6$.

b) $0.035\times100=3.5$.

c) $7.8\times1000=7800$.

d) $0.5\times1000=500$.

**Answer.** a) $42.6$; b) $3.5$; c) $7800$; d) $500$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out in your head: a) $62.5\div10$; b) $9.4\div100$; c) $4\div1000$; d) $2300\div100$.

::hint
**What you need:** dividing by $10$; $100$; $1000$ moves the decimal point left by one, two, three places.

**Steps:**
1. Write a whole number with a decimal point, for example $4=4.0$.
2. Move the decimal point left, writing the digit $0$ on the left if there are not enough digits.

::solution
a) $62.5\div10=6.25$.

b) $9.4\div100=0.094$.

c) $4\div1000=0.004$.

d) $2300\div100=23$.

**Answer.** a) $6.25$; b) $0.094$; c) $0.004$; d) $23$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out in your head: a) $35\times0.1$; b) $7.2\times0.01$; c) $6.3\div0.1$; d) $0.45\div0.01$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiplying by $0.1$; $0.01$ is dividing by $10$; $100$. Dividing by $0.1$; $0.01$ is multiplying by $10$; $100$.

**Steps:**
1. Change each calculation into multiplying or dividing by $10$ or $100$.
2. Move the decimal point.

::solution
a) $35\times0.1=35\div10=3.5$.

b) $7.2\times0.01=7.2\div100=0.072$.

c) $6.3\div0.1=6.3\times10=63$.

d) $0.45\div0.01=0.45\times100=45$.

**Answer.** a) $3.5$; b) $0.072$; c) $63$; d) $45$.
:::

:::exercise
Change units: a) $7.5$ m $=?$ cm; b) $63$ cm $=?$ m; c) $0.8$ kg $=?$ g; d) $1250$ g $=?$ kg.

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ m $=100$ cm; $1$ kg $=1000$ g.

**Steps:**
1. Larger unit to smaller unit: multiply. Smaller unit to larger unit: divide.
2. Move the decimal point for $100$ or $1000$.

::solution
a) $7.5\times100=750$ cm.

b) $63\div100=0.63$ m.

c) $0.8\times1000=800$ g.

d) $1250\div1000=1.25$ kg.

**Answer.** a) $750$ cm; b) $0.63$ m; c) $800$ g; d) $1.25$ kg.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose each packet of biscuits has mass $0.12$ kg. What is the mass in kilograms of $1000$ packets like this?

::hint
**What you need:** several equal packets put together: use multiplication.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Multiply $0.12$ by $1000$ by moving the decimal point.

::solution
The mass of $1000$ packets of biscuits is:
$$0.12\times1000=120\ \text{(kg)}.$$

**Answer.** $120$ kg.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a sheet of paper is $0.1$ mm thick. How many millimetres and how many centimetres thick is a pile of $1000$ sheets like this?

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ cm $=10$ mm; multiply by $1000$, then change units.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the thickness in millimetres.
2. Change it to centimetres by dividing by $10$.

::solution
The thickness of the pile is:
$$0.1\times1000=100\ \text{(mm)}.$$
Since $1$ cm $=10$ mm, we have $100\ \text{mm}=100\div10=10$ cm.

**Answer.** $100$ mm, which is $10$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose $100$ identical bottles of water hold $150$ litres altogether. How many litres does each bottle hold?

::hint
**What you need:** sharing equally: use division; dividing by $100$ moves the decimal point two places left.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Divide $150$ by $100$. Check by multiplying.

::solution
Each bottle holds:
$$150\div100=1.5\ \text{(litres)}.$$

**Check.** $1.5\times100=150$. Correct.

**Answer.** $1.5$ litres.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on multiplying decimals. Work out in the easy way: $2.5\times3.7\times4\times100$.

::hint
**What you need:** swap and group the factors; $2.5\times4=10$.

**Steps:**
1. Group $2.5$ with $4$.
2. Multiply the result by $3.7$ and by $100$ by moving the decimal point.

::solution
$$\begin{aligned} 2.5\times3.7\times4\times100 &= (2.5\times4)\times3.7\times100 \\ &= 10\times3.7\times100 \\ &= 37\times100=3700. \end{aligned}$$

**Answer.** $3700$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the missing numbers:
a) $4.5\times?=4500$; b) $?\div100=0.36$; c) $0.07\times?=7$; d) $85\div?=0.085$.

::hint
**What you need:** see how many places the decimal point moves, and in which direction.

**Steps:**
1. Compare the position of the decimal point in the two numbers.
2. If it moves right, multiply; if it moves left, divide.

::solution
a) From $4.5$ to $4500$ the decimal point moves three places right, so multiply by $1000$.

b) $?=0.36\times100=36$.

c) From $0.07$ to $7$ the decimal point moves two places right, so multiply by $100$.

d) From $85$ to $0.085$ the decimal point moves three places left, so divide by $1000$.

**Answer.** a) $1000$; b) $36$; c) $100$; d) $1000$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
When the decimal point of a decimal is moved one place to the right, the number increases by $31.5$. Find the decimal.

::hint
**What you need:** moving the decimal point one place to the right means multiplying the number by $10$.

**Steps:**
1. If the old number is one part, how many parts is the new number?
2. By how many parts is the new number greater? That amount is $31.5$.

::solution
Take the old number as $1$ part. Then the new number is $10$ parts, so it is $10-1=9$ parts greater than the old number. So $9$ parts equal $31.5$, and the old number is $31.5\div9=3.5$.

**Check.** $3.5\times10=35$ and $35-3.5=31.5$. Correct.

**Answer.** $3.5$.
:::
