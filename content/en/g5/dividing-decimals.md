:::goals
- Divide a decimal by a whole number and carry on dividing when there is a remainder.
- Divide a number by a decimal by moving the decimal point.
- Round the quotient of a division.
- Solve word problems using the division of decimals.
:::

A ribbon $8.4$ m long is cut into $4$ equal pieces. How many metres long is each piece?

To find out, you have to divide $8.4$ by $4$. This lesson shows you how to divide decimals and where to put the decimal point in the quotient.

## Let's explore

Change $8.4$ m $=84$ dm. Dividing $84$ dm into $4$ equal parts gives $84\div4=21$ dm each, which is $2.1$ m. So $8.4\div4=2.1$.

We can also divide straight away without changing units: divide the whole-number part $8\div4=2$, write the decimal point in the quotient, then divide the tenths digit $4\div4=1$. The quotient is $2.1$.

:::theorem Dividing a decimal by a whole number
1. Divide the whole-number part of the dividend by the divisor.
2. Write the decimal point to the right of the quotient, before bringing down the first digit of the decimal part to carry on dividing.
3. Carry on dividing with each digit of the decimal part.

If there is a remainder, write a decimal point in the quotient (if it is not there yet), write a digit $0$ to the right of the remainder, and carry on dividing.
:::

Why does this work? A decimal is made of tenths, hundredths, ... so dividing place by place from left to right is just like dividing whole numbers; the decimal point in the quotient sits right after the ones place. Writing a digit $0$ to the right of the remainder means changing to a smaller unit: $1$ unit is $10$ tenths.

:::theorem Dividing a number by a decimal
1. Count the digits in the decimal part of the divisor. Move the decimal point in the dividend to the right by that many places (write the digit $0$ if there are not enough).
2. Remove the decimal point in the divisor.
3. Divide as you would by a whole number.
:::

Why does this work? When you multiply both the dividend and the divisor by the same number other than $0$, the quotient does not change. For example, $4.5$ is $45$ tenths and $0.9$ is $9$ tenths, so $4.5\div0.9=45\div9=5$.

**Connection.** Division is the inverse of the multiplication you have just learnt, so you can check your answers by multiplying.

## Examples

We start with dividing a decimal by a whole number.

:::example Work out
a) $17.5\div5$; b) $0.36\div6$.

**Solution.** a) Divide the whole-number part: $17\div5=3$ (remainder $2$). Write the decimal point in the quotient, bring down $5$ to make $25$, then $25\div5=5$. The quotient is $3.5$.

b) Divide the whole-number part: $0\div6=0$, write $0$ and then the decimal point. Bring down $3$: $3\div6=0$, write the digit $0$. Bring down $6$ to make $36$, then $36\div6=6$. The quotient is $0.06$.

So a) $17.5\div5=3.5$; b) $0.36\div6=0.06$.
:::

When there is a remainder, write more digits $0$ and keep dividing, and the quotient becomes a decimal.

:::example Work out
a) $9\div4$; b) $6\div8$.

**Solution.** a) $9\div4=2$ (remainder $1$). Write the decimal point in the quotient, write a digit $0$ after the remainder to make $10$, then $10\div4=2$ (remainder $2$). Write another digit $0$ to make $20$, then $20\div4=5$. The quotient is $2.25$.

b) $6\div8=0$ (remainder $6$). Write $0$ and then the decimal point, write a digit $0$ to make $60$, then $60\div8=7$ (remainder $4$). Write another digit $0$ to make $40$, then $40\div8=5$. The quotient is $0.75$.

So a) $9\div4=2.25$; b) $6\div8=0.75$.
:::

Now dividing by a decimal by moving the decimal point.

:::example Work out
a) $4.5\div0.9$; b) $7.35\div1.5$; c) $12\div0.25$.

**Solution.** a) The divisor $0.9$ has one decimal place, so move the decimal point in the dividend one place to the right: $4.5\div0.9=45\div9=5$.

b) The divisor $1.5$ has one decimal place: $7.35\div1.5=73.5\div15$. We have $73\div15=4$ (remainder $13$), write the decimal point, bring down $5$ to make $135$, then $135\div15=9$. The quotient is $4.9$.

c) The divisor $0.25$ has two decimal places, so write two digits $0$ after the dividend: $12\div0.25=1200\div25=48$.

So a) $5$; b) $4.9$; c) $48$.
:::

Now a real-life problem, solved in four steps.

:::example The length of a plot
Suppose a rectangular plot of land has area $24.3\ \text{m}^2$ and width $2.7$ m. What is the length of the plot in metres?

**Understand.** We know the area and the width. We need the length.

**Plan.** The area is the length times the width, so the length is the area divided by the width.

**Solve.** The length of the plot is:
$$24.3\div2.7=243\div27=9\ \text{(m)}.$$
So the length is $9$ m.

**Look back.** Check by multiplying: $9\times2.7=24.3$. Correct. Is it reasonable? Yes: a width of $2.7$ m times $9$ is about $27$ m², close to $24.3\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

Next, a problem about finding the price of one unit.

:::example The price of meat
Suppose $2.5$ kg of meat costs $187\,500$ dong. How much does $1$ kg of meat cost?

**Solution.** The price of $1$ kg of meat is:
$$187\,500\div2.5=1\,875\,000\div25=75\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$
So the price is $75\,000$ dong per kilogram.
:::

Some divisions never end, so we round the quotient.

:::example Divide $10\div3$ and give the quotient to two decimal places
**Solution.** We have $10\div3=3$ (remainder $1$). Write a digit $0$ after the remainder to make $10$, and again $10\div3=3$ (remainder $1$), and so on for ever, so $10\div3=3.333\ldots$ The thousandths digit is $3<5$, so rounding to two decimal places gives $3.33$.

So $10\div3\approx3.33$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting the decimal point in the quotient.** Wrong: $8.4\div4=21$. Why it is wrong: after dividing the whole-number part $8$, you must write the decimal point before dividing the digit $4$; checking, $21\times4=84\ne8.4$. Right: $2.1$.
- **Leaving out a digit $0$ in the quotient.** Wrong: $0.36\div6=0.6$. Why it is wrong: $3<6$, so the tenths digit of the quotient is $0$; checking, $0.6\times6=3.6\ne0.36$. Right: $0.06$.
- **Moving the decimal point in only one number.** Wrong: $4.5\div0.9=4.5\div9=0.5$. Why it is wrong: the decimal point must be moved in both the dividend and the divisor, by the same number of places. Right: $45\div9=5$.
- **Thinking division always makes a number smaller.** Wrong: $6\div0.5=3$. Why it is wrong: dividing by a number less than $1$ gives a quotient greater than the dividend. Right: $6\div0.5=60\div5=12$.
:::

## Summary

- Dividing a decimal by a whole number: divide the whole-number part, write the decimal point in the quotient, then carry on dividing the decimal digits.
- If there is a remainder, write a digit $0$ to the right of the remainder and carry on; the quotient may be a decimal.
- Dividing by a decimal: move the decimal point in both the dividend and the divisor to the right by the same number of places, then divide as by a whole number.
- You can round the quotient when the division does not end, and check by multiplying.
- Dividing by a number less than $1$ gives a quotient greater than the dividend.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Set out in columns and work out: a) $15.6\div4$; b) $4.05\div5$.

::hint
**What you need:** divide the whole-number part, write the decimal point in the quotient, then carry on with the decimal part.

**Steps:**
1. Divide the whole-number part of the dividend by $4$ and by $5$.
2. Write the decimal point, then bring down each digit of the decimal part to carry on dividing.

::solution
a) $15\div4=3$ (remainder $3$), write the decimal point, bring down $6$ to make $36$, then $36\div4=9$. So $15.6\div4=3.9$.

b) $4\div5=0$ (remainder $4$), write $0$ and the decimal point, bring down $0$ to make $40$, then $40\div5=8$, bring down $5$ to make $5$, then $5\div5=1$. So $4.05\div5=0.81$.

**Answer.** a) $3.9$; b) $0.81$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out: a) $7\div4$; b) $18\div8$; c) $5\div8$.

::hint
**What you need:** when there is a remainder, write the decimal point in the quotient and write a digit $0$ after the remainder to carry on dividing.

**Steps:**
1. Divide the whole-number part and find the remainder.
2. Write a digit $0$ after the remainder and carry on until there is no remainder.

::solution
a) $7\div4=1$ (remainder $3$); $30\div4=7$ (remainder $2$); $20\div4=5$. So $7\div4=1.75$.

b) $18\div8=2$ (remainder $2$); $20\div8=2$ (remainder $4$); $40\div8=5$. So $18\div8=2.25$.

c) $5\div8=0$ (remainder $5$); $50\div8=6$ (remainder $2$); $20\div8=2$ (remainder $4$); $40\div8=5$. So $5\div8=0.625$.

**Answer.** a) $1.75$; b) $2.25$; c) $0.625$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out: a) $6.3\div0.7$; b) $9.6\div0.16$; c) $15\div0.6$.

::hint
**What you need:** move the decimal point in both the dividend and the divisor to the right by the same number of places, then divide.

**Steps:**
1. Count the decimal places in the divisor.
2. Move the decimal point in the dividend (write the digit $0$ if there are not enough), then divide.

::solution
a) $6.3\div0.7=63\div7=9$.

b) The divisor $0.16$ has two decimal places: $9.6\div0.16=960\div16=60$.

c) $15\div0.6=150\div6=25$.

**Answer.** a) $9$; b) $60$; c) $25$.
:::

:::exercise
A piece of electric cable $12.6$ m long is cut into $6$ equal pieces. How many metres long is each piece?

::hint
**What you need:** sharing equally: use division.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Divide $12.6$ by $6$. Check by multiplying.

::solution
Each piece is:
$$12.6\div6=2.1\ \text{(m)}.$$

**Check.** $2.1\times6=12.6$. Correct.

**Answer.** $2.1$ m.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose each bottle holds $0.75$ litres of water and there are $9$ litres of water to pour into bottles like this, filling each one. How many bottles can be filled?

::hint
**What you need:** to find how many equal parts an amount contains, divide the amount by the part.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Divide $9$ by $0.75$ by moving the decimal point.

::solution
The number of bottles that can be filled is:
$$9\div0.75=900\div75=12\ \text{(bottles)}.$$

**Check.** $12\times0.75=9$. Correct.

**Answer.** $12$ bottles.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose $3.5$ kg of apples costs $87\,500$ dong. How much does $1$ kg of apples cost?

::hint
**What you need:** the price of $1$ kg is the total cost divided by the number of kilograms.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Divide $87\,500$ by $3.5$. Remember the unit.

::solution
The price of $1$ kg of apples is:
$$87\,500\div3.5=875\,000\div35=25\,000\ \text{(dong)}.$$

**Answer.** $25\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on multiplying decimals. Find the number $x$ such that $x\times2.5=12$, then check by multiplying.

::hint
**What you need:** to find a factor, divide the product by the other factor.

**Steps:**
1. Write $x=12\div2.5$.
2. Move the decimal point to divide, then multiply $x$ by $2.5$ to check.

::solution
We have $x=12\div2.5=120\div25=4.8$.

**Check.** $4.8\times2.5=12$. Correct.

**Answer.** $x=4.8$.
:::

:::exercise
Divide $17\div6$, then round the quotient to one decimal place and to two decimal places.

::hint
**What you need:** to round to a place, look at the digit immediately to the right of that place.

**Steps:**
1. Divide $17\div6$ to a few decimal places.
2. Round from the full quotient, not from an already rounded result.

::solution
We have $17\div6=2.8333\ldots$

To one decimal place: the hundredths digit is $3<5$, so $2.8$.

To two decimal places: the thousandths digit is $3<5$, so $2.83$.

**Answer.** $2.8$ and $2.83$.
:::

:::exercise
A piece of string $5$ m long is cut into pieces $0.4$ m long. What is the greatest number of pieces $0.4$ m long that can be cut?

::hint
**What you need:** the number of pieces is a whole number, so we use only the whole-number part of the quotient.

**Steps:**
1. Divide $5$ by $0.4$.
2. Think about what the whole-number part of the quotient tells you.

::solution
We have $5\div0.4=50\div4=12.5$. The quotient $12.5$ means there are $12$ full pieces of length $0.4$ m and a small part that is not enough for another piece.

**Answer.** $12$ pieces.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Work out in the easy way: $12.5\div0.25\div4$.

::hint
**What you need:** dividing by two numbers in turn is the same as dividing by their product: $a\div b\div c=a\div(b\times c)$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $0.25\times4$.
2. Divide $12.5$ by that result.

::solution
Since $0.25\times4=1$, we have:
$$12.5\div0.25\div4=12.5\div(0.25\times4)=12.5\div1=12.5.$$

**Answer.** $12.5$.
:::
