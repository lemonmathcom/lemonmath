:::goals
- Draw a bar model that shows the ratio of two numbers.

- Find two numbers when you know their difference and their ratio.
- Solve word problems of these two types and check the answers.

An and Binh have $24$ marbles altogether. An has $\dfrac{1}{3}$ as many marbles as Binh. How many marbles does each friend have?

The question gives the sum and the ratio of two numbers. This lesson teaches you to use a bar model to find each number, then to do the same when you know the difference and the ratio.

## Finding two numbers from their sum and ratio

### Let's explore

An has $\dfrac{1}{3}$ as many marbles as Binh, so if Binh's marbles are divided into $3$ equal parts, An's marbles are $1$ part of the same size. Draw a model:

From the model, the two friends have $1+3=4$ equal parts altogether, which stand for $24$ marbles. One part is $24\div4=6$ marbles. So An has $6$ marbles and Binh has $6\times3=18$ marbles.

:::theorem Finding two numbers from their sum and ratio
1. Draw a bar model that shows the two numbers in the given ratio.
2. Find the total number of equal parts (add the numbers of parts of the two numbers).
3. Find the value of one part: divide the sum by the total number of equal parts.
4. Find each number: multiply the value of one part by the number of parts of that number.
:::

Why does this work? Both numbers are made of equal parts, so when you add them, the sum is the total number of parts times the value of one part. If you know the sum, you can divide to find the value of one part.

**Connection.** This lesson uses the ratio you learnt in the last lesson: if two numbers have ratio $\dfrac{a}{b}$, then the first number has $a$ parts and the second has $b$ parts.

### Examples

:::example How many marbles do An and Binh have?
An and Binh have $24$ marbles altogether. An has $\dfrac{1}{3}$ as many marbles as Binh. How many marbles does each friend have?

**Understand.** The total is $24$ marbles and the ratio of An's marbles to Binh's is $\dfrac{1}{3}$. We need the number of marbles for each friend.

**Plan.** Draw a model, find the total number of parts, then find the value of one part.

**Solution.** From the model, the total number of equal parts is:
$$1+3=4\ \text{(parts)}.$$
The number of An's marbles is:
$$24\div4\times1=6\ \text{(marbles)}.$$
The number of Binh's marbles is:
$$24-6=18\ \text{(marbles)}.$$
So An has $6$ marbles and Binh has $18$ marbles.

**Look back.** Check: $6+18=24$ and $\dfrac{6}{18}=\dfrac{1}{3}$. Correct. Is there another way? Yes, work out Binh's marbles first: $24\div4\times3=18$, then $24-18=6$.
:::

Next, a problem with a different ratio.

:::example Find the two numbers
The sum of two numbers is $80$. The ratio of the two numbers is $\dfrac{3}{5}$. Find the two numbers.

**Solution.** The smaller number has $3$ parts and the larger number has $5$ parts. The total number of equal parts is:
$$3+5=8\ \text{(parts)}.$$
The value of one part is $80\div8=10$. The smaller number is $10\times3=30$. The larger number is $10\times5=50$.

So the numbers are $30$ and $50$.

**Check.** $30+50=80$ and $\dfrac{30}{50}=\dfrac{3}{5}$. Correct.
:::

Word problems often describe the ratio in words.

:::example The students in a class
A class has $36$ students. The number of boys is $\dfrac{4}{5}$ of the number of girls. How many boys and how many girls are in the class?

**Solution.** The number of boys has $4$ parts and the number of girls has $5$ parts. The total number of equal parts is $4+5=9$ (parts).

The value of one part is $36\div9=4$ (students). The number of boys is $4\times4=16$ (students). The number of girls is $4\times5=20$ (students).

So there are $16$ boys and $20$ girls.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Dividing the sum by the number of parts of one number.** Wrong: the sum is $24$, the ratio is $\dfrac{1}{3}$, and one part is $24\div3=8$. Why it is wrong: the total number of equal parts is $1+3=4$, not $3$. Right: $24\div4=6$.
- **Forgetting to multiply by the number of parts.** Wrong: the sum is $80$, the ratio is $\dfrac{3}{5}$, and the smaller number is $10$. Why it is wrong: the smaller number has $3$ parts, each of value $10$. Right: the smaller number is $10\times3=30$.
:::

## Finding two numbers from their difference and ratio

### Let's explore

A rectangular garden is $12$ m longer than it is wide, and its width is $\dfrac{3}{5}$ of its length. How long and how wide is the garden, in metres?

From the model, the length is $5-3=2$ equal parts more than the width, and that difference is $12$ m. One part is $12\div2=6$ m. The width is $6\times3=18$ m and the length is $6\times5=30$ m.

:::theorem Finding two numbers from their difference and ratio
1. Draw a bar model that shows the two numbers in the given ratio.
2. Find the difference in the number of equal parts (subtract the parts of the smaller number from the parts of the larger number).
3. Find the value of one part: divide the difference by the difference in the number of parts.
4. Find each number: multiply the value of one part by the number of parts of that number.
:::

Why does this work? The difference of the two numbers is the extra length in the model, which is the difference in the number of equal parts. If you know the difference, you can divide to find the value of one part.

### Examples

:::example A rectangular garden
A rectangular garden is $12$ m longer than it is wide. Its width is $\dfrac{3}{5}$ of its length. How long and how wide is the garden, in metres?

**Understand.** The difference of the two sides is $12$ m and the width is $\dfrac{3}{5}$ of the length. We need the two sides.

**Plan.** Draw a model, find the difference in the number of parts, then find the value of one part.

**Solution.** From the model, the difference in the number of equal parts is:
$$5-3=2\ \text{(parts)}.$$
The value of one part is $12\div2=6$ (m). The width is $6\times3=18$ (m). The length is $6\times5=30$ (m).

So the width is $18$ m and the length is $30$ m.

**Look back.** Check: $30-18=12$ and $\dfrac{18}{30}=\dfrac{3}{5}$. Correct. Is it reasonable? Yes, because a length of $30$ m is exactly $12$ m more than a width of $18$ m.
:::

:::example Find the two numbers
The difference of two numbers is $36$. The ratio of the smaller number to the larger number is $\dfrac{4}{7}$. Find the two numbers.

**Solution.** The smaller number has $4$ parts and the larger number has $7$ parts. The difference in the number of equal parts is:
$$7-4=3\ \text{(parts)}.$$
The value of one part is $36\div3=12$. The smaller number is $12\times4=48$. The larger number is $12\times7=84$.

So the numbers are $48$ and $84$.

**Check.** $84-48=36$ and $\dfrac{48}{84}=\dfrac{4}{7}$. Correct.
:::

:::example The ages of a father and his child
A father is $28$ years older than his child. The child's age is $\dfrac{1}{5}$ of the father's age. How old are the father and the child?

**Solution.** The child's age has $1$ part and the father's age has $5$ parts. The difference in the number of equal parts is $5-1=4$ (parts).

The value of one part is $28\div4=7$. The child's age is $7\times1=7$ (years). The father's age is $7\times5=35$ (years).

So the child is $7$ years old and the father is $35$ years old.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Using the total number of parts in a difference problem.** Wrong: the difference is $12$, the ratio is $\dfrac{3}{5}$, and one part is $12\div(3+5)=1.5$. Why it is wrong: the difference matches the difference in the number of parts, $5-3=2$, not the total number of parts. Right: $12\div2=6$.
:::

## Summary

- When you know the sum and the ratio: total number of parts $=$ the parts of the two numbers added; one part $=$ sum $\div$ total number of parts.
- When you know the difference and the ratio: difference in parts $=$ parts of the larger number $-$ parts of the smaller number; one part $=$ difference $\div$ difference in parts.
- Each number $=$ value of one part $\times$ number of parts of that number.
- Always draw the bar model first, and check using the sum or difference and using the ratio.
- Write the unit in the answer when the problem has a unit.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
The sum of two numbers is $56$. The ratio of the two numbers is $\dfrac{3}{4}$. Find the two numbers.

::hint
**What you need:** the smaller number has $3$ parts and the larger number has $4$ parts.

**Steps:**
1. Find the total number of equal parts, then the value of one part.
2. Multiply the value of one part by the number of parts of each number. Check using the sum.

::solution
The total number of equal parts is $3+4=7$ (parts). The value of one part is $56\div7=8$. The smaller number is $8\times3=24$. The larger number is $8\times4=32$.

**Check.** $24+32=56$ and $\dfrac{24}{32}=\dfrac{3}{4}$. Correct.

**Answer.** $24$ and $32$.
:::

:::exercise
The sum of two numbers is $90$. The smaller number is $\dfrac{2}{7}$ of the larger number. Find the two numbers.

::hint
**What you need:** "the smaller number is $\dfrac{2}{7}$ of the larger number" means the smaller number has $2$ parts and the larger number has $7$ parts.

**Steps:**
1. Draw a bar model.
2. Find the total number of parts and the value of one part.

::solution
The total number of equal parts is $2+7=9$ (parts). The value of one part is $90\div9=10$. The smaller number is $10\times2=20$. The larger number is $10\times7=70$.

**Answer.** $20$ and $70$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose two tanks hold $150$ litres of water altogether. The amount of water in the first tank is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of the amount in the second tank. How many litres of water does each tank hold?

::hint
**What you need:** the first tank has $2$ parts and the second tank has $3$ parts.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked? Draw a bar model.
2. Find the total number of parts, the value of one part, then the amount of water in each tank. Remember the unit.

::solution
The total number of equal parts is $2+3=5$ (parts). One part is $150\div5=30$ (litres). The first tank holds $30\times2=60$ (litres). The second tank holds $30\times3=90$ (litres).

**Answer.** The first tank holds $60$ litres; the second tank holds $90$ litres.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the review lesson on rectangles. Suppose a school playground is a rectangle with half-perimeter $75$ m, and its width is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of its length. Find the length, the width and the area of the playground.

::hint
**What you need:** half the perimeter is the sum of the length and the width; the area is the length times the width.

**Steps:**
1. Find two numbers from the sum $75$ and the ratio $\dfrac{2}{3}$.
2. Multiply the length by the width.

::solution
The width has $2$ parts and the length has $3$ parts. The total number of equal parts is $2+3=5$ (parts). One part is $75\div5=15$ (m). The width is $15\times2=30$ (m). The length is $15\times3=45$ (m).

The area of the playground is $45\times30=1350\ \text{(m}^2\text{)}$.

**Answer.** Length $45$ m; width $30$ m; area $1350\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
The difference of two numbers is $15$. The ratio of the smaller number to the larger number is $\dfrac{2}{5}$. Find the two numbers.

::hint
**What you need:** the smaller number has $2$ parts and the larger number has $5$ parts; the difference in parts is $5-2$.

**Steps:**
1. Find the difference in the number of equal parts, then the value of one part.
2. Find each number, then check using the difference.

::solution
The difference in the number of equal parts is $5-2=3$ (parts). The value of one part is $15\div3=5$. The smaller number is $5\times2=10$. The larger number is $5\times5=25$.

**Check.** $25-10=15$ and $\dfrac{10}{25}=\dfrac{2}{5}$. Correct.

**Answer.** $10$ and $25$.
:::

:::exercise
The difference of two numbers is $48$. The ratio of the larger number to the smaller number is $\dfrac{7}{3}$. Find the two numbers.

::hint
**What you need:** the larger number has $7$ parts and the smaller number has $3$ parts.

**Steps:**
1. Draw a bar model with $7$ parts for the larger number and $3$ parts for the smaller number.
2. Find the difference in parts, one part, then the two numbers.

::solution
The difference in the number of equal parts is $7-3=4$ (parts). The value of one part is $48\div4=12$. The larger number is $12\times7=84$. The smaller number is $12\times3=36$.

**Answer.** The larger number is $84$; the smaller number is $36$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a shop has $120$ kg less sticky rice than ordinary rice. The mass of sticky rice is $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of the mass of ordinary rice. How many kilograms of each kind of rice does the shop have?

::hint
**What you need:** "$120$ kg less" is the difference of the two numbers; sticky rice has $3$ parts and ordinary rice has $8$ parts.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked? Find the difference in the number of parts.
2. Find one part, then the mass of each kind of rice. Remember the unit.

::solution
The difference in the number of equal parts is $8-3=5$ (parts). One part is $120\div5=24$ (kg). The sticky rice is $24\times3=72$ (kg). The ordinary rice is $24\times8=192$ (kg).

**Check.** $192-72=120$. Correct.

**Answer.** Sticky rice $72$ kg; ordinary rice $192$ kg.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a father is $30$ years older than his child and the father's age is $6$ times the child's age. How old are the father and the child?

::hint
**What you need:** "$6$ times" means the child's age has $1$ part and the father's age has $6$ parts.

**Steps:**
1. Find the difference in the number of equal parts.
2. Find one part, then each person's age.

::solution
The difference in the number of equal parts is $6-1=5$ (parts). One part is $30\div5=6$ (years). The child's age is $6$ years. The father's age is $6\times6=36$ (years).

**Answer.** The child is $6$ years old; the father is $36$ years old.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on ratios and percentages. A class has $40$ students, and the girls make up $55\%$ of the class. a) Find the number of girls and the number of boys. b) Write the ratio of the number of boys to the number of girls as a fraction in its simplest form, then solve the problem again by finding two numbers from their sum and ratio, to check.

::hint
**What you need:** $55\%$ of a number is that number times $\dfrac{55}{100}$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the number of girls, then the number of boys.
2. Simplify the ratio of boys to girls, then use the sum $40$ and that ratio to find the two numbers again.

::solution
a) The number of girls is $40\times\dfrac{55}{100}=22$ (students). The number of boys is $40-22=18$ (students).

b) The ratio of boys to girls is $\dfrac{18}{22}=\dfrac{9}{11}$. The total number of equal parts is $9+11=20$ (parts) and one part is $40\div20=2$ (students). The number of boys is $2\times9=18$ and the number of girls is $2\times11=22$, the same as in part a).

**Answer.** a) $22$ girls, $18$ boys; b) the ratio is $\dfrac{9}{11}$ and the results agree.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
The sum of two numbers is $91$. We know that $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of the first number equals $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of the second number. Find the two numbers.

::hint
**What you need:** if $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of the first number and $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of the second number both equal one part, then the first number has $3$ parts and the second number has $4$ parts.

**Steps:**
1. Write the ratio of the first number to the second number.
2. Solve the problem with a known sum and ratio.

::solution
The first number has $3$ parts and the second number has $4$ parts of the same size. The total number of equal parts is $3+4=7$ (parts). One part is $91\div7=13$. The first number is $13\times3=39$. The second number is $13\times4=52$.

**Check.** $39+52=91$; $39\div3=13$ and $52\div4=13$. Correct.

**Answer.** $39$ and $52$.
:::
