:::goals
- Recognise decimal fractions: fractions whose denominator is $10$, $100$, $1\,000$, ...
- Write a fraction as a decimal fraction using the basic property of fractions.
- Change measurements of length and mass into decimal fractions.
- Add and subtract decimal fractions and solve word problems.
:::

Nam measures a piece of cloth $35$ cm long and writes its length in metres as $\dfrac{35}{100}$ m. Lan also measures the cloth and writes $\dfrac{7}{20}$ m.

Are both of them right? Why did Nam choose the denominator $100$? This lesson teaches you a special kind of fraction, called a decimal fraction, which is very handy for working with measurements.

## Let's explore

One metre is $100$ centimetres. So $1$ cm is $\dfrac{1}{100}$ m, and $35$ cm is $\dfrac{35}{100}$ m. In the same way, $1$ dm is $\dfrac{1}{10}$ m, and $3$ dm is $\dfrac{3}{10}$ m. One kilogram is $1\,000$ grams, so $125$ g is $\dfrac{125}{1\,000}$ kg.

The fractions $\dfrac{3}{10}$, $\dfrac{35}{100}$, $\dfrac{125}{1\,000}$ have denominators $10$, $100$, $1\,000$, so they are called **decimal fractions**.

:::definition Decimal fraction
A **decimal fraction** is a fraction whose denominator is $10$, $100$, $1\,000$, ...

Examples: $\dfrac{3}{10}$ (three tenths), $\dfrac{35}{100}$ (thirty-five hundredths), $\dfrac{125}{1\,000}$ (one hundred and twenty-five thousandths).
:::

What about $\dfrac{7}{20}$? The denominator $20$ is not $10$, $100$, ... but $20\times5=100$. By the basic property of fractions:
$$\dfrac{7}{20}=\dfrac{7\times5}{20\times5}=\dfrac{35}{100}.$$
So $\dfrac{7}{20}=\dfrac{35}{100}$, which means both Nam and Lan are right.

:::theorem Writing a fraction as a decimal fraction
If the denominator of a fraction is a divisor of $10$, $100$, $1\,000$, ..., multiply the numerator and denominator by the same number so that the denominator becomes $10$, $100$ or $1\,000$, ... We get a decimal fraction equal to the given fraction.
:::

Why does this work? Multiplying the numerator and denominator by the same number other than $0$ does not change the value of a fraction (the basic property of fractions).

**Connection.** This lesson uses the basic property of fractions, common denominators and changing units of measurement that you already know. Decimal fractions are the foundation for learning decimals in the next lesson.

## Examples

We start with recognising decimal fractions.

:::example Recognising decimal fractions
Which of the fractions $\dfrac{3}{10}$, $\dfrac{7}{20}$, $\dfrac{125}{1\,000}$, $\dfrac{5}{100}$ are decimal fractions?

**Solution.** A decimal fraction has denominator $10$, $100$, $1\,000$, ...

The fractions $\dfrac{3}{10}$, $\dfrac{125}{1\,000}$ and $\dfrac{5}{100}$ have denominators $10$, $1\,000$ and $100$, so they are decimal fractions. The fraction $\dfrac{7}{20}$ has denominator $20$, so it is not yet a decimal fraction.

So the decimal fractions are $\dfrac{3}{10}$, $\dfrac{125}{1\,000}$ and $\dfrac{5}{100}$.
:::

Now writing a fraction as a decimal fraction.

:::example Writing as a decimal fraction (1)
Write the fractions $\dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\dfrac{7}{20}$ as decimal fractions.

**Solution.** Since $5\times2=10$, $\dfrac{3}{5}=\dfrac{3\times2}{5\times2}=\dfrac{6}{10}$.

Since $20\times5=100$, $\dfrac{7}{20}=\dfrac{7\times5}{20\times5}=\dfrac{35}{100}$.

So $\dfrac{3}{5}=\dfrac{6}{10}$ and $\dfrac{7}{20}=\dfrac{35}{100}$.
:::

Some fractions need a bigger denominator.

:::example Writing as a decimal fraction (2)
Write the fractions $\dfrac{3}{4}$ and $\dfrac{5}{8}$ as decimal fractions.

**Solution.** Since $4\times25=100$, $\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{3\times25}{4\times25}=\dfrac{75}{100}$.

Since $8\times125=1\,000$, $\dfrac{5}{8}=\dfrac{5\times125}{8\times125}=\dfrac{625}{1\,000}$.

So $\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{75}{100}$ and $\dfrac{5}{8}=\dfrac{625}{1\,000}$.
:::

The other way round, you can simplify decimal fractions.

:::example Simplifying decimal fractions
Simplify the fractions $\dfrac{75}{100}$ and $\dfrac{250}{1\,000}$ to their simplest form.

**Solution.** Both $75$ and $100$ are divisible by $25$: $\dfrac{75}{100}=\dfrac{75\div25}{100\div25}=\dfrac{3}{4}$.

Both $250$ and $1\,000$ are divisible by $250$: $\dfrac{250}{1\,000}=\dfrac{250\div250}{1\,000\div250}=\dfrac{1}{4}$.

So $\dfrac{75}{100}=\dfrac{3}{4}$ and $\dfrac{250}{1\,000}=\dfrac{1}{4}$.
:::

Decimal fractions are very handy for changing measurements.

:::example Changing measurements
a) Write $35$ cm as a fraction of a metre, then simplify.

b) Write $125$ g as a fraction of a kilogram, then simplify.

**Solution.** a) $1$ m $=100$ cm, so $35$ cm $=\dfrac{35}{100}$ m. Simplify: $\dfrac{35}{100}=\dfrac{7}{20}$ m.

b) $1$ kg $=1\,000$ g, so $125$ g $=\dfrac{125}{1\,000}$ kg. Simplify: $\dfrac{125}{1\,000}=\dfrac{1}{8}$ kg.

So $35$ cm $=\dfrac{7}{20}$ m and $125$ g $=\dfrac{1}{8}$ kg.
:::

Decimal fractions with the same denominator are added or subtracted by their numerators; otherwise, write them over a common denominator.

:::example Joining two pieces of string
A blue string is $\dfrac{3}{10}$ m long and a red string is $\dfrac{27}{100}$ m long. The two strings are joined into one. How many metres long is the joined string?

**Understand.** The blue string is $\dfrac{3}{10}$ m, the red string is $\dfrac{27}{100}$ m, and they are joined. We need the length of the joined string.

**Plan.** The length of the joined string is the sum of the two lengths. The fractions have denominators $10$ and $100$, so we write them over the denominator $100$.

**Solve.** We have $\dfrac{3}{10}=\dfrac{30}{100}$. The length of the joined string is:
$$\dfrac{30}{100}+\dfrac{27}{100}=\dfrac{57}{100}\ \text{(m)}.$$

**Answer:** $\dfrac{57}{100}$ m.

**Look back.** We change to centimetres to check: $\dfrac{3}{10}$ m $=30$ cm, $\dfrac{27}{100}$ m $=27$ cm, and $30+27=57$ cm $=\dfrac{57}{100}$ m. Correct.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Thinking a denominator containing a zero makes a decimal fraction.** Wrong: $\dfrac{7}{20}$ is a decimal fraction because the denominator $20$ has a zero. Why it is wrong: the denominator must be $10$, $100$, $1\,000$, ..., not just contain a zero. Right: $\dfrac{7}{20}$ is not yet a decimal fraction; $\dfrac{7}{20}=\dfrac{35}{100}$ is.
- **Changing only the denominator and not the numerator.** Wrong: $\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{3}{100}$. Why it is wrong: both the numerator and the denominator must be multiplied by the same number. Right: $\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{75}{100}$.
- **Using the wrong denominator when changing measurements.** Wrong: $125$ g $=\dfrac{125}{100}$ kg. Why it is wrong: $1$ kg $=1\,000$ g, not $100$ g. Right: $125$ g $=\dfrac{125}{1\,000}$ kg.
:::

## Summary

- A decimal fraction is a fraction whose denominator is $10$, $100$, $1\,000$, ...
- To write a fraction as a decimal fraction, multiply the numerator and denominator by the same number so that the denominator becomes $10$, $100$, $1\,000$, ...
- Decimal fractions can be simplified: $\dfrac{75}{100}=\dfrac{3}{4}$.
- Changing measurements: $1$ dm $=\dfrac{1}{10}$ m, $1$ cm $=\dfrac{1}{100}$ m, $1$ g $=\dfrac{1}{1\,000}$ kg.
- Add and subtract decimal fractions by writing them over the same denominator.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Which of the fractions $\dfrac{4}{10}$, $\dfrac{9}{25}$, $\dfrac{17}{100}$, $\dfrac{3}{8}$, $\dfrac{120}{1\,000}$ are decimal fractions?

::hint
**What you need:** a decimal fraction has denominator $10$, $100$, $1\,000$, ...

**Steps:**
1. Look at the denominator of each fraction.
2. Choose the fractions whose denominator is $10$, $100$ or $1\,000$.

::solution
The fractions $\dfrac{4}{10}$, $\dfrac{17}{100}$ and $\dfrac{120}{1\,000}$ have denominators $10$, $100$ and $1\,000$. The other two have denominators $25$ and $8$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{4}{10}$, $\dfrac{17}{100}$, $\dfrac{120}{1\,000}$.
:::

:::exercise
Write the fractions $\dfrac{4}{5}$ and $\dfrac{9}{25}$ as decimal fractions.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply the numerator and denominator by the same number to make the denominator $10$ or $100$.

**Steps:**
1. $5$ times what makes $10$? $25$ times what makes $100$?
2. Multiply the numerator and denominator.

::solution
$\dfrac{4}{5}=\dfrac{4\times2}{5\times2}=\dfrac{8}{10}$ and $\dfrac{9}{25}=\dfrac{9\times4}{25\times4}=\dfrac{36}{100}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{8}{10}$ and $\dfrac{36}{100}$.
:::

:::exercise
Write the fraction $\dfrac{7}{8}$ as a decimal fraction with denominator $1\,000$.

::hint
**What you need:** $8\times125=1\,000$.

**Steps:**
1. Find the number that multiplies $8$ to give $1\,000$.
2. Multiply the numerator and denominator.

::solution
$$\dfrac{7}{8}=\dfrac{7\times125}{8\times125}=\dfrac{875}{1\,000}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{875}{1\,000}$.
:::

:::exercise
Simplify the decimal fractions $\dfrac{60}{100}$ and $\dfrac{125}{1\,000}$ to their simplest form.

::hint
**What you need:** divide the numerator and denominator by a common divisor greater than $1$.

**Steps:**
1. Which number divides both $60$ and $100$?
2. Which number divides both $125$ and $1\,000$?

::solution
$\dfrac{60}{100}=\dfrac{60\div20}{100\div20}=\dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\dfrac{125}{1\,000}=\dfrac{125\div125}{1\,000\div125}=\dfrac{1}{8}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\dfrac{1}{8}$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $450$ g as a fraction of a kilogram, then simplify it to its simplest form.

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ kg $=1\,000$ g.

**Steps:**
1. Write $450$ g as some thousandths of $1$ kg.
2. Simplify the fraction.

::solution
$450$ g $=\dfrac{450}{1\,000}$ kg. Both $450$ and $1\,000$ are divisible by $50$: $\dfrac{450}{1\,000}=\dfrac{9}{20}$ kg.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{9}{20}$ kg.
:::

:::exercise
In the morning Lan walks $\dfrac{2}{10}$ km, and in the afternoon she walks $\dfrac{35}{100}$ km. How many kilometres does Lan walk in the two sessions altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** "altogether" means addition; write over the denominator $100$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Change $\dfrac{2}{10}$ to a fraction with denominator $100$, then add.

::solution
We have $\dfrac{2}{10}=\dfrac{20}{100}$. The number of kilometres Lan walks altogether is:
$$\dfrac{20}{100}+\dfrac{35}{100}=\dfrac{55}{100}=\dfrac{11}{20}\ \text{(km)}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{11}{20}$ km.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on comparing fractions. Compare $\dfrac{7}{10}$ and $\dfrac{65}{100}$.

::hint
**What you need:** write over a common denominator, then compare the numerators.

**Steps:**
1. Change $\dfrac{7}{10}$ to a fraction with denominator $100$.
2. Compare the two numerators.

::solution
$\dfrac{7}{10}=\dfrac{70}{100}$. Since $70>65$, $\dfrac{70}{100}>\dfrac{65}{100}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{7}{10}>\dfrac{65}{100}$.
:::

:::exercise
Binh has $\dfrac{9}{10}$ of a litre of milk and drinks $\dfrac{37}{100}$ of a litre. How many litres of milk does Binh have left?

::hint
**What you need:** "left" means subtraction; write over the denominator $100$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Change $\dfrac{9}{10}$ to a fraction with denominator $100$, then subtract.

::solution
We have $\dfrac{9}{10}=\dfrac{90}{100}$. The number of litres of milk left is:
$$\dfrac{90}{100}-\dfrac{37}{100}=\dfrac{53}{100}\ \text{(litres)}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{53}{100}$ litre.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on finding a fraction of an amount. Write $\dfrac{3}{5}$ as a decimal fraction with denominator $10$, then find $\dfrac{3}{5}$ of $150$ kg.

::hint
**What you need:** $\dfrac{a}{b}$ of $n$ is $n\div b\times a$.

**Steps:**
1. Write $\dfrac{3}{5}=\dfrac{6}{10}$.
2. Divide $150$ by $10$ and multiply by $6$.

::solution
$\dfrac{3}{5}=\dfrac{6}{10}$. So $\dfrac{3}{5}$ of $150$ kg is $150\div10\times6=15\times6=90$ (kg).

**Answer.** $\dfrac{6}{10}$; $90$ kg.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find all the one-digit numbers $n$ (other than $0$) for which the fraction $\dfrac{1}{n}$ can be written as a decimal fraction with denominator $1\,000$.

::hint
**What you need:** $n$ must divide $1\,000$ exactly, otherwise no whole number multiplies $n$ to give $1\,000$.

**Steps:**
1. Try $n=1,2,\ldots,9$ in turn: does $n$ divide $1\,000$?
2. For each $n$ that divides it, write $\dfrac{1}{n}=\dfrac{?}{1\,000}$.

::solution
$1\,000$ is divisible by $1$, $2$, $4$, $5$, $8$, and not by $3$, $6$, $7$, $9$.

For example $\dfrac{1}{8}=\dfrac{125}{1\,000}$, $\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{250}{1\,000}$, $\dfrac{1}{5}=\dfrac{200}{1\,000}$, $\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{500}{1\,000}$, $\dfrac{1}{1}=\dfrac{1\,000}{1\,000}$.

**Answer.** $n=1$, $2$, $4$, $5$ or $8$.
:::

::key A
