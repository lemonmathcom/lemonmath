You have two boxes shaped like cuboids. You pack small cubes with side $1$ cm into each box until it is completely full: the first box takes $24$ cubes and the second box takes $30$ cubes.

Which box holds more, and by how many cubes? Is there a unit you can use to measure without packing every cube?

## Volume of a shape

### Let's explore

Every shape takes up some space. That amount of space is the **volume** of the shape. To compare volumes, pack small equal cubes into the shapes: the shape that takes more cubes has the greater volume.

For the two boxes: the first box has $24$ cubes and the second box has $30$ cubes. Since $30>24$, the second box holds more, by $30-24=6$ cubes.

:::definition Cubic centimetre
A cube with side $1$ cm has volume **one cubic centimetre**, written $1\ \text{cm}^3$. If a shape is exactly filled by $24$ such cubes, then its volume is $24\ \text{cm}^3$.
:::

When the cubes are packed in layers, you do not need to count every cube: count the cubes in one row, multiply by the number of rows to get the cubes in one layer, then multiply by the number of layers.

**Connection.** This lesson is like the lesson on area: there you counted squares of $1\ \text{cm}^2$, and here you count cubes of $1\ \text{cm}^3$.

### Examples

:::example Counting cubes
A cuboid is exactly filled with cubes of volume $1\ \text{cm}^3$ in $2$ layers, each layer having $3$ rows and each row having $4$ cubes. Find the volume of the cuboid.

**Solution.** Each layer has $4\times3=12$ cubes. Two layers have $12\times2=24$ cubes.

So the volume of the cuboid is $24\ \text{cm}^3$.
:::

Now go back to the question at the start, solved in four steps.

:::example Comparing two boxes
The first box is exactly filled by $24$ cubes of volume $1\ \text{cm}^3$ and the second box is exactly filled by $30$ such cubes. Which box has the greater volume, and by how many cubic centimetres?

**Understand.** We know the number of cubes in each box, and the cubes are equal. We need to compare the volumes of the two boxes.

**Plan.** Each cube is $1\ \text{cm}^3$, so the volume equals the number of cubes. Compare the two numbers, then subtract the smaller from the larger.

**Solution.** The volume of the first box is $24\ \text{cm}^3$ and the volume of the second box is $30\ \text{cm}^3$. Since $30>24$, the second box has the greater volume. The second box is greater than the first box by:
$$30-24=6\ \text{(cm}^3\text{)}.$$
So the second box is greater, by $6\ \text{cm}^3$.

**Look back.** Is there another way? Yes: take away $24$ cubes from each box, and the second box has $6$ cubes left while the first box has none. Is it reasonable? Yes, because $24+6=30$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Writing the wrong unit for volume.** Wrong: the volume of the shape is $24\ \text{cm}^2$. Why it is wrong: $\text{cm}^2$ is a unit of area; volume is measured with cubes, so the unit is $\text{cm}^3$. Right: $24\ \text{cm}^3$.
:::

## Cubic centimetres, cubic decimetres, cubic metres

### Let's explore

Besides $1\ \text{cm}^3$, there are two larger units of volume: the **cubic decimetre** ($\text{dm}^3$) is the volume of a cube with side $1$ dm, and the **cubic metre** ($\text{m}^3$) is the volume of a cube with side $1$ m.

A cube with side $1$ dm is also a cube with side $10$ cm. Pack cubes of $1\ \text{cm}^3$ into it until it is full: each row has $10$ cubes, each layer has $10$ rows, and there are $10$ layers. The number of cubes is $10\times10\times10=1000$. So $1\ \text{dm}^3=1000\ \text{cm}^3$.

:::theorem Relationships between units of volume
- $1\ \text{dm}^3=1000\ \text{cm}^3$.
- $1\ \text{m}^3=1000\ \text{dm}^3$ and $1\ \text{m}^3=1\,000\,000\ \text{cm}^3$.
- Two neighbouring units of volume are $1000$ times apart.
- $1\ \text{dm}^3=1$ litre (a unit of capacity), so $1\ \text{m}^3=1000$ litres.
:::

Why is $1\ \text{m}^3=1000\ \text{dm}^3$? Because $1$ m $=10$ dm, a cube with side $1$ m holds $10\times10\times10=1000$ cubes of $1\ \text{dm}^3$. In the same way, $1$ m $=100$ cm, so $1\ \text{m}^3=100\times100\times100=1\,000\,000\ \text{cm}^3$.

To change from a larger unit to a smaller unit, multiply by $1000$; to change from a smaller unit to a larger unit, divide by $1000$.

### Examples

:::example Changing units
a) $5\ \text{dm}^3=?\ \text{cm}^3$; b) $7\,000\,000\ \text{cm}^3=?\ \text{m}^3$; c) $3\ \text{m}^3=?\ \text{dm}^3$; d) $2500\ \text{dm}^3=?\ \text{m}^3$.

**Solution.** a) $5\ \text{dm}^3=5\times1000=5000\ \text{cm}^3$.

b) $7\,000\,000\div1\,000\,000=7$, so $7\,000\,000\ \text{cm}^3=7\ \text{m}^3$.

c) $3\ \text{m}^3=3\times1000=3000\ \text{dm}^3$.

d) $2500\ \text{dm}^3=2000\ \text{dm}^3+500\ \text{dm}^3=2\ \text{m}^3+0.5\ \text{m}^3=2.5\ \text{m}^3$.

So a) $5000\ \text{cm}^3$; b) $7\ \text{m}^3$; c) $3000\ \text{dm}^3$; d) $2.5\ \text{m}^3$.
:::

You can also change a measurement with two units into one unit.

:::example Measurements with two units
a) $4\ \text{m}^3\ 250\ \text{dm}^3=?\ \text{dm}^3$; b) $8\ \text{dm}^3\ 45\ \text{cm}^3=?\ \text{cm}^3$.

**Solution.** a) $4\ \text{m}^3=4000\ \text{dm}^3$, so $4\ \text{m}^3\ 250\ \text{dm}^3=4000+250=4250\ \text{dm}^3$.

b) $8\ \text{dm}^3=8000\ \text{cm}^3$, so $8\ \text{dm}^3\ 45\ \text{cm}^3=8000+45=8045\ \text{cm}^3$.

So a) $4250\ \text{dm}^3$; b) $8045\ \text{cm}^3$.
:::

Cubic decimetres and litres help you work out the amount of water in a tank.

:::example A water tank
A tank holds $1800\ \text{dm}^3$ of water. How many litres of water does the tank hold? How many cubic metres of water does it hold?

**Solution.** Since $1\ \text{dm}^3=1$ litre, $1800\ \text{dm}^3=1800$ litres.

Change to cubic metres: $1800\ \text{dm}^3=1000\ \text{dm}^3+800\ \text{dm}^3=1\ \text{m}^3+0.8\ \text{m}^3=1.8\ \text{m}^3$.

So the tank holds $1800$ litres, which is $1.8\ \text{m}^3$ of water.
:::

Finally, a problem adding two volumes with regrouping.

:::example Two water tanks
Suppose the first tank holds $2\ \text{m}^3\ 500\ \text{dm}^3$ of water and the second tank holds $1\ \text{m}^3\ 700\ \text{dm}^3$ of water. How many cubic metres of water do the two tanks hold altogether?

**Solution.** The two tanks hold altogether:
$$2\ \text{m}^3\ 500\ \text{dm}^3+1\ \text{m}^3\ 700\ \text{dm}^3=3\ \text{m}^3\ 1200\ \text{dm}^3.$$
Since $1200\ \text{dm}^3=1\ \text{m}^3\ 200\ \text{dm}^3$, the total is $4\ \text{m}^3\ 200\ \text{dm}^3$, which is $4.2\ \text{m}^3$.

So they hold $4.2\ \text{m}^3$ of water.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Using $1\ \text{dm}^3=10\ \text{cm}^3$.** Wrong: $5\ \text{dm}^3=50\ \text{cm}^3$. Why it is wrong: a cube with side $1$ dm is a cube with side $10$ cm, which holds $10\times10\times10$ cubes of $1\ \text{cm}^3$. Right: $5\ \text{dm}^3=5000\ \text{cm}^3$.
- **Comparing measurements in different units.** Wrong: $2500\ \text{cm}^3>3\ \text{dm}^3$ because $2500>3$. Why it is wrong: the two measurements are in different units; $3\ \text{dm}^3=3000\ \text{cm}^3$. Right: $2500\ \text{cm}^3<3\ \text{dm}^3$.
:::

## Summary

- The volume of a shape is the amount of space it takes up; you can compare volumes by counting small equal cubes.
- $1\ \text{cm}^3$, $1\ \text{dm}^3$ and $1\ \text{m}^3$ are the volumes of cubes with side $1$ cm, $1$ dm and $1$ m.
- Two neighbouring units of volume are $1000$ times apart: $1\ \text{dm}^3=1000\ \text{cm}^3$ and $1\ \text{m}^3=1000\ \text{dm}^3$.
- $1\ \text{dm}^3=1$ litre.
- To compare or calculate with measurements in different units, write them in the same unit.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Find the missing numbers:
a) $6\ \text{dm}^3=?\ \text{cm}^3$; b) $9000\ \text{cm}^3=?\ \text{dm}^3$; c) $4\ \text{m}^3=?\ \text{dm}^3$; d) $12\,000\ \text{dm}^3=?\ \text{m}^3$.

::hint
**What you need:** two neighbouring units of volume are $1000$ times apart.

**Steps:**
1. Larger unit to smaller unit: multiply. Smaller unit to larger unit: divide.
2. Remember that you multiply or divide by $1000$.

::solution
a) $6\times1000=6000\ \text{cm}^3$.

b) $9000\div1000=9\ \text{dm}^3$.

c) $4\times1000=4000\ \text{dm}^3$.

d) $12\,000\div1000=12\ \text{m}^3$.

**Answer.** a) $6000$; b) $9$; c) $4000$; d) $12$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the missing numbers:
a) $7\ \text{m}^3\ 300\ \text{dm}^3=?\ \text{dm}^3$; b) $5\ \text{dm}^3\ 60\ \text{cm}^3=?\ \text{cm}^3$; c) $6500\ \text{dm}^3=?\ \text{m}^3\ ?\ \text{dm}^3$; d) $3450\ \text{cm}^3=?\ \text{dm}^3\ ?\ \text{cm}^3$.

::hint
**What you need:** $1\ \text{m}^3=1000\ \text{dm}^3$ and $1\ \text{dm}^3=1000\ \text{cm}^3$.

**Steps:**
1. Change the larger unit into the smaller unit, then add the rest.
2. For parts c and d, split the measurement into two units.

::solution
a) $7\ \text{m}^3=7000\ \text{dm}^3$, so we get $7000+300=7300\ \text{dm}^3$.

b) $5\ \text{dm}^3=5000\ \text{cm}^3$, so we get $5000+60=5060\ \text{cm}^3$.

c) $6500\ \text{dm}^3=6000\ \text{dm}^3+500\ \text{dm}^3=6\ \text{m}^3\ 500\ \text{dm}^3$.

d) $3450\ \text{cm}^3=3000\ \text{cm}^3+450\ \text{cm}^3=3\ \text{dm}^3\ 450\ \text{cm}^3$.

**Answer.** a) $7300$; b) $5060$; c) $6\ \text{m}^3\ 500\ \text{dm}^3$; d) $3\ \text{dm}^3\ 450\ \text{cm}^3$.
:::

:::exercise
A cuboid is exactly filled with cubes of volume $1\ \text{cm}^3$ in $3$ layers, each layer having $5$ rows and each row having $6$ cubes. Find the volume of the cuboid.

::hint
**What you need:** the volume equals the number of cubes of $1\ \text{cm}^3$ that exactly fill the shape.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the number of cubes in one layer.
2. Multiply by the number of layers.

::solution
Each layer has $6\times5=30$ cubes. Three layers have $30\times3=90$ cubes.

So the volume of the cuboid is $90\ \text{cm}^3$.

**Answer.** $90\ \text{cm}^3$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a bottle holds $1.5$ litres of water. How many cubic decimetres and how many cubic centimetres of water does the bottle hold?

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ litre $=1\ \text{dm}^3$ and $1\ \text{dm}^3=1000\ \text{cm}^3$.

**Steps:**
1. Change litres into cubic decimetres.
2. Change cubic decimetres into cubic centimetres.

::solution
Since $1$ litre $=1\ \text{dm}^3$, $1.5$ litres $=1.5\ \text{dm}^3$.

Change to cubic centimetres: $1.5\ \text{dm}^3=1\ \text{dm}^3+0.5\ \text{dm}^3=1000+500=1500\ \text{cm}^3$.

**Answer.** $1.5\ \text{dm}^3$, which is $1500\ \text{cm}^3$.
:::

:::exercise
A tank holds $2400$ litres of water. How many cubic decimetres and how many cubic metres of water does it hold?

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ litre $=1\ \text{dm}^3$ and $1\ \text{m}^3=1000\ \text{dm}^3$.

**Steps:**
1. Change litres into cubic decimetres.
2. Change cubic decimetres into cubic metres. Remember the unit.

::solution
Since $1$ litre $=1\ \text{dm}^3$, $2400$ litres $=2400\ \text{dm}^3$.

Change to cubic metres: $2400\ \text{dm}^3=2000\ \text{dm}^3+400\ \text{dm}^3=2\ \text{m}^3+0.4\ \text{m}^3=2.4\ \text{m}^3$.

**Answer.** $2400\ \text{dm}^3$, which is $2.4\ \text{m}^3$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the gap: a) $2500\ \text{cm}^3\ \ldots\ 3\ \text{dm}^3$; b) $0.5\ \text{m}^3\ \ldots\ 500\ \text{dm}^3$; c) $4\ \text{dm}^3\ 20\ \text{cm}^3\ \ldots\ 4200\ \text{cm}^3$.

::hint
**What you need:** write both measurements in the same unit, then compare.

**Steps:**
1. Choose a common unit for each part.
2. Compare the two numbers.

::solution
a) $3\ \text{dm}^3=3000\ \text{cm}^3$. Since $2500<3000$, write $<$.

b) $0.5\ \text{m}^3=500\ \text{dm}^3$, so write $=$.

c) $4\ \text{dm}^3\ 20\ \text{cm}^3=4020\ \text{cm}^3$. Since $4020<4200$, write $<$.

**Answer.** a) $<$; b) $=$; c) $<$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a barrel holds $3\ \text{m}^3\ 600\ \text{dm}^3$ of water, and $1\ \text{m}^3\ 700\ \text{dm}^3$ more water is poured in. How many cubic metres of water does the barrel hold after that?

::hint
**What you need:** "poured in more" means putting together: use addition; $1000\ \text{dm}^3=1\ \text{m}^3$.

**Steps:**
1. Add the measurements in the same unit.
2. If the number of $\text{dm}^3$ is $1000$ or more, change it into $\text{m}^3$.

::solution
The total is $3\ \text{m}^3\ 600\ \text{dm}^3+1\ \text{m}^3\ 700\ \text{dm}^3=4\ \text{m}^3\ 1300\ \text{dm}^3=5\ \text{m}^3\ 300\ \text{dm}^3$, which is $5.3\ \text{m}^3$.

**Answer.** $5.3\ \text{m}^3$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on units of area, in which $1\ \text{m}^2=100\ \text{dm}^2$. Why is $1\ \text{m}^3$ equal to $1000\ \text{dm}^3$ and not to $100\ \text{dm}^3$?

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ m $=10$ dm; area has two dimensions, volume has three dimensions.

**Steps:**
1. Pack cubes of $1\ \text{dm}^3$ into a cube with side $1$ m: how many cubes fit in one row?
2. How many rows are there in each layer and how many layers?

::solution
A cube with side $1$ m is a cube with side $10$ dm. Each row holds $10$ cubes of $1\ \text{dm}^3$, each layer has $10$ rows, and there are $10$ layers. The number of cubes is $10\times10\times10=1000$.

With area there are only rows and columns: $10\times10=100$ squares, so $1\ \text{m}^2=100\ \text{dm}^2$.

**Answer.** $1\ \text{m}^3=1000\ \text{dm}^3$ because there is one more dimension: $10\times10\times10=1000$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose one fish tank holds $60$ litres of water and a second fish tank holds $0.08\ \text{m}^3$ of water. Which tank holds more water, and by how many litres?

::hint
**What you need:** $1\ \text{m}^3=1000$ litres; write both measurements in litres.

**Steps:**
1. Change $0.08\ \text{m}^3$ into litres.
2. Compare, then subtract the smaller from the larger.

::solution
$0.08\ \text{m}^3=\dfrac{8}{100}\times1000=80$ litres.

Since $80>60$, the second tank holds more. The second tank holds $80-60=20$ litres more than the first.

**Answer.** The second tank holds more, by $20$ litres.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A cube with side $1$ m is cut into small cubes with side $1$ cm. If all the small cubes are placed end to end in one straight line, how many kilometres long is the line?

::hint
**What you need:** $1\ \text{m}^3=1\,000\,000\ \text{cm}^3$; each small cube is $1$ cm long.

**Steps:**
1. How many small cubes are there?
2. Change the length of the line into metres, then into kilometres.

::solution
There are $1\,000\,000$ small cubes of $1\ \text{cm}^3$. The line is $1\,000\,000$ cm $=10\,000$ m $=10$ km long.

**Answer.** $10$ km.
:::
