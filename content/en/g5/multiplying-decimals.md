:::goals
- Multiply a decimal by a whole number and multiply two decimals.
- Place the decimal point correctly in the product by counting decimal places.
- Use the commutative and associative properties to calculate quickly.
- Solve word problems using the multiplication of decimals.
:::

Mum buys $3$ bags of rice, each with mass $2.4$ kg. How many kilograms of rice does she buy altogether?

To find out, you have to multiply $2.4$ by $3$. This lesson shows you how to multiply decimals and place the decimal point in the product correctly.

## Let's explore

Write $2.4$ kg as $\dfrac{24}{10}$ kg. Three bags of rice have mass:
$$\dfrac{24}{10}\times3=\dfrac{24\times3}{10}=\dfrac{72}{10}=7.2\ \text{(kg)}.$$

Notice that $24\times3=72$, and $72$ has one decimal place because $2.4$ has one decimal place. Set it out in columns:
$$\begin{array}{r} 2.4 \\ \times\ \ \ 3 \\ \hline 7.2 \end{array}$$

Now let us multiply two decimals. A rectangular piece of card $1.2$ m long and $0.5$ m wide has area:
$$1.2\times0.5=\dfrac{12}{10}\times\dfrac{5}{10}=\dfrac{12\times5}{100}=\dfrac{60}{100}=0.6\ \text{(m}^2\text{)}.$$

This time $12\times5=60$, and the product has two decimal places, the total number of decimal places in the two factors ($1+1$).

:::theorem Multiplying decimals
To multiply two numbers (decimals or whole numbers):
1. Ignore the decimal points in the factors and multiply as with whole numbers.
2. Count the total number of decimal places in the factors (a whole number has $0$ decimal places).
3. Put the decimal point in the product so that it has that many decimal places, counting from the right; write the digit $0$ on the left if there are not enough digits; remove any digits $0$ at the end of the decimal part.
:::

This works because $\dfrac{a}{10^m}\times\dfrac{b}{10^n}=\dfrac{a\times b}{10^{m+n}}$: the denominators are multiplied, so the numbers of decimal places add up.

**Connection.** Multiplication uses the decimal fractions and the way of writing decimals from the earlier lessons. It also prepares you for dividing decimals and for multiplying by $10$, $100$ and $1000$.

## Examples

We start with multiplying a decimal by a whole number.

:::example Work out
a) $4.5\times6$; b) $0.28\times7$.

**Solution.** a) We have $45\times6=270$. The factor $4.5$ has one decimal place, so the product is $27.0=27$.

b) We have $28\times7=196$. The factor $0.28$ has two decimal places, so the product is $1.96$.

So a) $4.5\times6=27$; b) $0.28\times7=1.96$.
:::

Next, multiplying two decimals, with a case where the product has too few digits.

:::example Work out
a) $4.2\times1.3$; b) $0.3\times0.07$.

**Solution.** a) We have $42\times13=546$. The two factors have $1+1=2$ decimal places in total, so the product is $5.46$.

b) We have $3\times7=21$. The two factors have $1+2=3$ decimal places in total, but $21$ has only two digits, so we write one digit $0$ on the left: $0.021$.

So a) $4.2\times1.3=5.46$; b) $0.3\times0.07=0.021$.
:::

The commutative and associative properties also work for decimals.

:::example Work out in the easy way: $0.25\times6.5\times4$
**Solution.** Since $0.25\times4=1$ is a round number, use the commutative and associative properties to group these two together:
$$\begin{aligned} 0.25\times6.5\times4 &= (0.25\times4)\times6.5 \\ &= 1\times6.5 \\ &= 6.5. \end{aligned}$$
So $0.25\times6.5\times4=6.5$.
:::

Now a real-life problem about area, solved in four steps.

:::example A garden
Suppose a garden is a rectangle $12.5$ m long and $8.4$ m wide. What is the area of the garden in square metres?

**Understand.** We know the length and the width. We need the area.

**Plan.** The area of a rectangle is the length times the width.

**Solve.** The area of the garden is:
$$12.5\times8.4=105\ \text{(m}^2\text{)}.$$
Since $125\times84=10\,500$ and the two factors have $2$ decimal places, the product is $105.00=105$.

So the area is $105\ \text{m}^2$.

**Look back.** Is the answer reasonable? Yes: $12\times8=96$ and $13\times9=117$, and $105$ lies between them. Is there another way? Yes: $12.5\times8=100$ and $12.5\times0.4=5$, and adding gives $105$.
:::

Finally, a problem about money.

:::example Buying cloth
Suppose $1$ m of cloth costs $45\,000$ dong. How much does $2.5$ m of this cloth cost?

**Solution.** The cost of $2.5$ m of cloth is:
$$45\,000\times2.5=112\,500\ \text{(dong)}.$$
Since $45\,000\times25=1\,125\,000$ and there is one decimal place, the product is $112\,500.0=112\,500$.

So it costs $112\,500$ dong.
:::

Multiplying by a number less than $1$ makes the product smaller, and you can tell without calculating.

:::example Without calculating, say which product is greater than $12$: a) $12\times0.8$; b) $12\times1.05$
**Solution.** a) Since $0.8<1$, we have $12\times0.8<12$.

b) Since $1.05>1$, we have $12\times1.05>12$.

So the product in part b) is greater than $12$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Putting the decimal point in the wrong place.** Wrong: $0.3\times0.07=0.21$. Why it is wrong: the two factors have $1+2=3$ decimal places, so the product must have three decimal places. Right: $0.021$.
- **Multiplying the whole-number and decimal parts separately.** Wrong: $4.2\times1.3=4.6$ (taking $4\times1$ and $2\times3$). Why it is wrong: you must multiply the whole numbers, $42\times13=546$. Right: $5.46$.
- **Thinking multiplying always makes a number bigger.** Wrong: $8\times0.5=40$. Why it is wrong: $0.5=\dfrac{1}{2}$, and multiplying by $0.5$ means taking half. Right: $8\times0.5=4$.
:::

## Summary

- To multiply decimals, ignore the decimal points, multiply as with whole numbers, then place the decimal point in the product.
- The product has as many decimal places as the **total** number of decimal places in the factors; if there are too few digits, write the digit $0$ on the left.
- Remove any digits $0$ at the end of the decimal part of the product.
- The commutative and associative properties help you calculate quickly: $0.25\times4=1$.
- Multiplying by a number less than $1$ gives a product smaller than the other factor; multiplying by a number greater than $1$ gives a greater product.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Set out in columns and work out: a) $3.4\times2$; b) $0.45\times6$.

::hint
**What you need:** ignore the decimal point, multiply as with whole numbers, then place the decimal point in the product.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $34\times2$ and $45\times6$.
2. Count the decimal places of the decimal factor, place the decimal point, and remove any extra digits $0$.

::solution
a) $34\times2=68$, with one decimal place, so $3.4\times2=6.8$.

b) $45\times6=270$, with two decimal places, so $0.45\times6=2.70=2.7$.

**Answer.** a) $6.8$; b) $2.7$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out: a) $2.5\times1.6$; b) $0.8\times0.35$; c) $12.3\times0.04$; d) $0.05\times0.06$.

::hint
**What you need:** the number of decimal places in the product is the total number of decimal places in the two factors.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply the two numbers, ignoring the decimal points.
2. Count the total decimal places; write the digit $0$ on the left if there are not enough.

::solution
a) $25\times16=400$, with $2$ decimal places in total, so $2.5\times1.6=4.00=4$.

b) $8\times35=280$, with $3$ decimal places in total, so $0.8\times0.35=0.280=0.28$.

c) $123\times4=492$, with $3$ decimal places in total, so $12.3\times0.04=0.492$.

d) $5\times6=30$, with $4$ decimal places in total, so $0.05\times0.06=0.0030=0.003$.

**Answer.** a) $4$; b) $0.28$; c) $0.492$; d) $0.003$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out in the easy way: $0.4\times3.7\times2.5$.

::hint
**What you need:** swapping and grouping the factors does not change the product.

**Steps:**
1. Find two factors whose product is a round number.
2. Group those two, then multiply by the remaining number.

::solution
Since $0.4\times2.5=1$, we have:
$$0.4\times3.7\times2.5=(0.4\times2.5)\times3.7=1\times3.7=3.7.$$

**Answer.** $3.7$.
:::

:::exercise
Each bottle holds $1.5$ litres of water. How many litres do $8$ bottles like this hold?

::hint
**What you need:** several equal bottles put together: use multiplication.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Multiply $1.5$ by $8$. Check: the result must be greater than $1.5$.

::solution
The number of litres of water in $8$ bottles is:
$$1.5\times8=12\ \text{(litres)}.$$

**Answer.** $12$ litres.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose each metre of electric cable costs $12\,500$ dong. How much does $3.4$ m of cable cost?

::hint
**What you need:** cost $=$ price per metre times the number of metres.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Multiply $12\,500$ by $3.4$. Remember the unit.

::solution
The cost of the cable is:
$$12\,500\times3.4=42\,500\ \text{(dong)}.$$
Since $12\,500\times34=425\,000$ and there is one decimal place, the product is $42\,500.0=42\,500$.

**Answer.** $42\,500$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
A rectangular sheet of glass is $6.5$ m long and $3.2$ m wide. Find the area of the sheet of glass.

::hint
**What you need:** the area of a rectangle is the length times the width.

**Steps:**
1. Are the two measurements in the same unit, metres?
2. Multiply the two numbers, then write the unit as square metres.

::solution
The area of the sheet of glass is:
$$6.5\times3.2=20.8\ \text{(m}^2\text{)}.$$
Since $65\times32=2080$ and there are two decimal places, the product is $20.80=20.8$.

**Answer.** $20.8\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
Without calculating, write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the gap: a) $15\times0.9\ \ldots\ 15$; b) $0.7\times1.1\ \ldots\ 0.7$; c) $2.5\times1\ \ldots\ 2.5$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiplying by a number less than $1$ makes the product smaller, by a number greater than $1$ makes it greater, and by $1$ leaves it unchanged.

**Steps:**
1. Compare the second factor with $1$.
2. Decide whether the product is greater than, less than or equal to the other factor.

::solution
a) Since $0.9<1$, we have $15\times0.9<15$.

b) Since $1.1>1$, we have $0.7\times1.1>0.7$.

c) Multiplying by $1$ changes nothing, so $2.5\times1=2.5$.

**Answer.** a) $<$; b) $>$; c) $=$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on square kilometres and hectares. A field is a rectangle $1.25$ km long and $0.8$ km wide. Find the area of the field in square kilometres and in hectares.

::hint
**What you need:** $1\ \text{km}^2=100$ ha.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply the length by the width (both in kilometres).
2. Change the result into hectares.

::solution
The area of the field is $1.25\times0.8=1\ \text{km}^2$, because $125\times8=1000$ and there are three decimal places, so the product is $1.000=1$.

Change to hectares: $1\ \text{km}^2=100$ ha.

**Answer.** $1\ \text{km}^2$, which is $100$ ha.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Work out quickly: $0.125\times0.25\times32$.

::hint
**What you need:** $0.125\times8=1$ and $0.25\times4=1$.

**Steps:**
1. Write $32=8\times4$.
2. Group the factors to make round products.

::solution
Since $32=8\times4$, we have:
$$\begin{aligned} 0.125\times0.25\times32 &= (0.125\times8)\times(0.25\times4) \\ &= 1\times1 \\ &= 1. \end{aligned}$$

**Answer.** $1$.
:::
