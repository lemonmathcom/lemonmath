In the first group, $3$ cakes are shared equally among $4$ friends. In the second group, $5$ identical cakes are shared equally among $6$ friends.

In which group does each friend get more cake? To find out, you need to know what fraction of a cake each friend gets, and then compare two fractions. This lesson reviews what you have learned about fractions so that you can do this.

## Let's explore

Share $3$ cakes equally among $4$ friends: each friend gets $3\div4$ of a cake. You can cut each cake into $4$ equal pieces, and each friend takes $1$ piece from each cake, so each friend has $3$ pieces, which is $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a cake. So:
$$3\div4=\dfrac{3}{4}.$$

In the same way, in the second group each friend gets $5\div6=\dfrac{5}{6}$ of a cake.

:::theorem What to remember about fractions
- **Fractions and division:** the quotient of a natural number $a$ divided by a natural number $b$ other than $0$ can be written as a fraction: $a\div b=\dfrac{a}{b}$. Every natural number $n$ can be written as $n=\dfrac{n}{1}$.
- **Basic property:** multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same natural number other than $0$ gives an equivalent fraction.
- **Simplifying** is dividing the numerator and denominator by a common divisor greater than $1$ until the fraction is in its simplest form; **writing over a common denominator** is changing fractions to have the same denominator.
- **Comparing:** same denominator: the greater numerator gives the greater fraction; same numerator: the smaller denominator gives the greater fraction; different denominators: write over a common denominator and then compare; compare with $1$ by comparing the numerator with the denominator.
:::

Why is $a\div b=\dfrac{a}{b}$? Sharing $a$ things equally into $b$ parts gives each part $\dfrac{a}{b}$ of a thing, just like the cakes above.

**Connection.** This lesson gathers the lessons on simplifying, common denominators and comparing fractions from Year $4$. It prepares you for the lesson on decimal fractions and the review of calculations with fractions.

## Examples

We start with fractions and division.

:::example Writing a quotient as a fraction
Write the quotient of each division as a fraction (simplified if possible):
a) $7\div5$
b) $12\div8$

**Solution.** a) $7\div5=\dfrac{7}{5}$.

b) $12\div8=\dfrac{12}{8}$. Both $12$ and $8$ are divisible by $4$, so $\dfrac{12}{8}=\dfrac{3}{2}$.

So a) $\dfrac{7}{5}$; b) $\dfrac{3}{2}$.
:::

Next, equivalent fractions.

:::example Equivalent fractions
Find the number $x$ such that $\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{x}{20}$.

**Solution.** The denominator $20=4\times5$, so we multiply the numerator and denominator of $\dfrac{3}{4}$ by $5$:
$$\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{3\times5}{4\times5}=\dfrac{15}{20}.$$
So $x=15$.
:::

Simplifying a fraction to its simplest form.

:::example Simplifying a fraction
Simplify the fraction $\dfrac{42}{63}$ to its simplest form.

**Solution.** Both $42$ and $63$ are divisible by $21$:
$$\dfrac{42}{63}=\dfrac{42\div21}{63\div21}=\dfrac{2}{3}.$$
The numerator and denominator of $\dfrac{2}{3}$ are not both divisible by any number greater than $1$, so it is in its simplest form.

So $\dfrac{42}{63}=\dfrac{2}{3}$.
:::

Writing two fractions over a common denominator.

:::example Writing over a common denominator
Write the fractions $\dfrac{5}{6}$ and $\dfrac{7}{9}$ over a common denominator.

**Solution.** We look for a common denominator: $18$ is divisible by both $6$ and $9$. Change the two fractions:
$$\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{5\times3}{6\times3}=\dfrac{15}{18},\qquad \dfrac{7}{9}=\dfrac{7\times2}{9\times2}=\dfrac{14}{18}.$$
So the two fractions are $\dfrac{15}{18}$ and $\dfrac{14}{18}$.
:::

Ordering several fractions, including the number $1$.

:::example Ordering fractions
Put the numbers $\dfrac{7}{4}$, $\dfrac{3}{5}$, $1$, $\dfrac{9}{10}$ in order from least to greatest.

**Solution.** We compare with $1$ first: $\dfrac{7}{4}>1$ (numerator greater than denominator), while $\dfrac{3}{5}<1$ and $\dfrac{9}{10}<1$.

We compare $\dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\dfrac{9}{10}$: $\dfrac{3}{5}=\dfrac{6}{10}$ and $6<9$, so $\dfrac{3}{5}<\dfrac{9}{10}$.

So $\dfrac{3}{5}<\dfrac{9}{10}<1<\dfrac{7}{4}$.
:::

Now we answer the question about the two groups sharing cakes.

:::example Sharing cakes
The first group shares $3$ cakes equally among $4$ friends. The second group shares $5$ identical cakes equally among $6$ friends. In which group does each friend get more cake?

**Understand.** First group: $3$ cakes, $4$ friends. Second group: $5$ cakes, $6$ friends. We need to compare the amount of cake each friend gets.

**Plan.** Each friend in the first group gets $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of a cake, and in the second group $\dfrac{5}{6}$ of a cake. Write over a common denominator and compare.

**Solve.** Write over the common denominator $12$:
$$\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{9}{12},\qquad \dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{10}{12}.$$
Since $9<10$, $\dfrac{3}{4}<\dfrac{5}{6}$.

**Answer:** each friend in the second group gets more cake.

**Look back.** Check another way: $\dfrac{3}{4}$ is still $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of a cake short of $1$ whole cake, while $\dfrac{5}{6}$ is only $\dfrac{1}{6}$ short. Since $\dfrac{1}{6}<\dfrac{1}{4}$, $\dfrac{5}{6}$ is greater. The result is the same.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Writing the quotient upside down.** Wrong: $3\div4=\dfrac{4}{3}$. Why it is wrong: the dividend is the numerator and the divisor is the denominator. Right: $3\div4=\dfrac{3}{4}$.
- **Simplifying by subtracting.** Wrong: $\dfrac{4}{6}=\dfrac{2}{4}$ (subtracting $2$ from both numerator and denominator). Why it is wrong: we may only multiply or divide both numbers, not subtract. Right: $\dfrac{4}{6}=\dfrac{2}{3}$.
- **Comparing fractions with different denominators by their numerators.** Wrong: $\dfrac{5}{6}<\dfrac{7}{9}$ because $5<7$. Why it is wrong: numerators can only be compared when the denominators are equal. Right: $\dfrac{15}{18}>\dfrac{14}{18}$, so $\dfrac{5}{6}>\dfrac{7}{9}$.
:::

## Summary

- $a\div b=\dfrac{a}{b}$ (with $b$ not $0$); a natural number $n$ can be written as $\dfrac{n}{1}$.
- Multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same number other than $0$ gives an equivalent fraction.
- Simplify to the simplest form; write over a common denominator to compare.
- Comparing fractions: same denominator, same numerator, different denominators (common denominator), or with $1$.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Write the quotient of each division as a fraction: $5\div8$ and $9\div4$.

::hint
**What you need:** $a\div b=\dfrac{a}{b}$.

**Steps:**
1. The dividend is the numerator.
2. The divisor is the denominator.

::solution
$5\div8=\dfrac{5}{8}$ and $9\div4=\dfrac{9}{4}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{5}{8}$ and $\dfrac{9}{4}$.
:::

:::exercise
Write the natural number $7$ as a fraction with denominator $3$.

::hint
**What you need:** $7=\dfrac{7}{1}$; multiply the numerator and denominator by the same number.

**Steps:**
1. Write $7=\dfrac{7}{1}$.
2. By what number must you multiply the numerator and denominator to make the denominator $3$?

::solution
$7=\dfrac{7}{1}=\dfrac{7\times3}{1\times3}=\dfrac{21}{3}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{21}{3}$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on simplifying fractions. Simplify $\dfrac{36}{60}$ to its simplest form.

::hint
**What you need:** divide the numerator and denominator by a common divisor greater than $1$.

**Steps:**
1. Which number divides both $36$ and $60$?
2. Divide, then check whether the fraction is in its simplest form.

::solution
Both $36$ and $60$ are divisible by $12$: $\dfrac{36}{60}=\dfrac{36\div12}{60\div12}=\dfrac{3}{5}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{3}{5}$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the number $x$ such that $\dfrac{5}{8}=\dfrac{x}{24}$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply the numerator and denominator by the same number.

**Steps:**
1. How many times as big as $8$ is $24$?
2. Multiply the numerator $5$ by that number.

::solution
Since $24=8\times3$, $\dfrac{5}{8}=\dfrac{5\times3}{8\times3}=\dfrac{15}{24}$.

**Answer.** $x=15$.
:::

:::exercise
Write the fractions $\dfrac{3}{10}$ and $\dfrac{4}{15}$ over a common denominator.

::hint
**What you need:** find a number divisible by both $10$ and $15$.

**Steps:**
1. The smaller common denominator is $30$.
2. Change each fraction to denominator $30$.

::solution
$$\dfrac{3}{10}=\dfrac{3\times3}{10\times3}=\dfrac{9}{30},\qquad \dfrac{4}{15}=\dfrac{4\times2}{15\times2}=\dfrac{8}{30}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{9}{30}$ and $\dfrac{8}{30}$.
:::

:::exercise
Compare $\dfrac{7}{12}$ and $\dfrac{5}{8}$.

::hint
**What you need:** write over a common denominator, then compare the numerators.

**Steps:**
1. The smaller common denominator of $12$ and $8$ is $24$.
2. Compare the two numerators.

::solution
$$\dfrac{7}{12}=\dfrac{14}{24},\qquad \dfrac{5}{8}=\dfrac{15}{24}.$$
Since $14<15$, $\dfrac{7}{12}<\dfrac{5}{8}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{7}{12}<\dfrac{5}{8}$.
:::

:::exercise
Put the fractions $\dfrac{3}{4}$, $\dfrac{5}{6}$, $\dfrac{2}{3}$, $\dfrac{7}{12}$ in order from least to greatest.

::hint
**What you need:** write them all over the same denominator.

**Steps:**
1. $12$ is divisible by $4$, $6$ and $3$; write them over the denominator $12$.
2. Put the numerators in order.

::solution
$$\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{9}{12},\qquad \dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{10}{12},\qquad \dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{8}{12},\qquad \dfrac{7}{12}\ \text{(unchanged)}.$$
We have $7<8<9<10$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{7}{12}<\dfrac{2}{3}<\dfrac{3}{4}<\dfrac{5}{6}$.
:::

:::exercise
Nam has read $\dfrac{7}{10}$ of a story book and Ha has read $\dfrac{5}{8}$ of a book of the same kind. Who has read more?

::hint
**What you need:** the two books are the same, so compare the two fractions.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Write over a common denominator, then compare.

::solution
Write over the common denominator $40$:
$$\dfrac{7}{10}=\dfrac{28}{40},\qquad \dfrac{5}{8}=\dfrac{25}{40}.$$
Since $28>25$, $\dfrac{7}{10}>\dfrac{5}{8}$.

**Answer.** Nam has read more.
:::

:::exercise
A shop shares $5$ kg of rice equally into $6$ identical bags. How many kilograms of rice are in each bag?

::hint
**What you need:** dividing $5$ by $6$ gives a fraction.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Write the quotient $5\div6$ as a fraction and write the unit.

::solution
The number of kilograms of rice in each bag is:
$$5\div6=\dfrac{5}{6}\ \text{(kg)}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{5}{6}$ kg.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find a fraction that is greater than $\dfrac{3}{5}$ and less than $\dfrac{2}{3}$.

::hint
**What you need:** write both fractions over a common denominator, then find a fraction between them.

**Steps:**
1. Write $\dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\dfrac{2}{3}$ over the denominator $15$. Is there a fraction with denominator $15$ between them?
2. If not, multiply both by $2$ to get denominator $30$.

::solution
We have $\dfrac{3}{5}=\dfrac{9}{15}$ and $\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{10}{15}$. There is no natural number between the numerators $9$ and $10$.

Multiply the numerator and denominator by $2$: $\dfrac{3}{5}=\dfrac{18}{30}$ and $\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{20}{30}$. The fraction $\dfrac{19}{30}$ has numerator between $18$ and $20$.

**Answer.** One fraction that works is $\dfrac{19}{30}$.
:::
