:::goals
- Know that a net of a solid is a flat shape made of the faces of the solid.
- Recognise the nets of a cube, a cuboid and a cylinder.
- Work out the area of card needed to make these solids by adding the areas of the faces.
- Solve real-life problems with nets.
:::

Lan wants to make a gift box shaped like a cube with side $6$ cm from a flat piece of card. What shapes does she need to cut out of the card, and what is the least area of card, in square centimetres, that she needs (not counting the gluing flaps)?

To make a solid from flat card, you need to know its net. This lesson teaches you that.

## Let's explore

Imagine cutting along some of the edges of a box and flattening it out. The flat shape you get is a **net** of the box. A cube has $6$ faces and each face is a square. Here is one net of a cube: four squares in a row, with one square above and one square below the second square.

Fold the six squares along the shared edges and you get a cube: the row makes the four side faces, and the squares above and below are the top and bottom.

:::theorem Nets of some solids
- **Cube:** $6$ equal squares.
- **Cuboid:** $6$ rectangles, with opposite faces equal, so there are $3$ pairs of equal rectangles.
- **Cylinder:** $2$ equal circles (the two bases) and $1$ rectangle (the curved surface). The length of the rectangle is equal to the circumference of the base, and its width is equal to the height of the cylinder.
:::

Why is the length of the rectangle equal to the circumference of the base? When you roll the rectangle into a tube, the long edge meets itself and runs all the way round the base, so the length is exactly the circumference of the circular base. The width becomes the height of the tube.

**Connection.** The area of a net is the sum of the areas of the faces. This lesson uses the area of rectangles and squares, and the circumference and area of a circle that you already know.

## Examples

We start with the question at the beginning, solved in four steps.

:::example A cube-shaped gift box
Lan wants to make a gift box shaped like a cube with side $6$ cm. What is the least area of card she needs, in square centimetres (not counting the gluing flaps)?

**Understand.** The cube has side $6$ cm. We need the area of the card, which is the area of the net.

**Plan.** The net has $6$ squares with side $6$ cm. Work out the area of one square, then multiply by $6$.

**Solution.** The area of one square is $6\times6=36\ \text{(cm}^2\text{)}$. The area of card needed is:
$$36\times6=216\ \text{(cm}^2\text{)}.$$
So she needs $216\ \text{cm}^2$.

**Look back.** Is there another way? Yes: add $36+36+36+36+36+36=216$. Is it reasonable? Yes, because each face is $36\ \text{cm}^2$ and there are $6$ faces.
:::

Next, a cuboid, which has three pairs of equal faces.

:::example A cuboid
A cuboid has length $5$ cm, width $3$ cm and height $2$ cm. Find the total area of the faces of the cuboid.

**Solution.** The cuboid has three pairs of equal faces:
- two faces of size $5$ cm $\times3$ cm, each of area $15\ \text{cm}^2$, so $30\ \text{cm}^2$ for the two;
- two faces of size $5$ cm $\times2$ cm, each of area $10\ \text{cm}^2$, so $20\ \text{cm}^2$ for the two;
- two faces of size $3$ cm $\times2$ cm, each of area $6\ \text{cm}^2$, so $12\ \text{cm}^2$ for the two.

The total area of the faces is $30+20+12=62\ \text{cm}^2$.

So the total area of the faces is $62\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

Now a cylinder. Find the size of the rectangle in the net.

:::example The net of a cylinder
A cylinder has base radius $5$ cm and height $10$ cm. What are the dimensions of the rectangle in its net? (Take $\pi\approx3.14$.)

**Solution.** The length of the rectangle is the circumference of the base:
$$2\times3.14\times5=31.4\ \text{(cm)}.$$
The width of the rectangle is the height of the cylinder, $10$ cm.

So the rectangle measures $31.4$ cm $\times10$ cm.
:::

Next, work out the area of the whole net.

:::example The area of the net of a cylinder
For the cylinder with base radius $5$ cm and height $10$ cm, find the area of the whole net (two circles and one rectangle).

**Solution.** The area of the rectangle is $31.4\times10=314\ \text{cm}^2$. The area of each circular base is $3.14\times5\times5=78.5\ \text{cm}^2$.

The area of the whole net is:
$$314+2\times78.5=314+157=471\ \text{(cm}^2\text{)}.$$
So the area of the net is $471\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

Finally, recognising which shape is a net of a cube.

:::example Recognising a net
Which of these shapes is a net of a cube? A: four squares in a row, with one square above the first square and one square below the fourth square. B: six squares in two rows, with three squares in each row.

**Solution.** Shape A folds up: the four squares in the row make the four side faces and the other two squares are the two bases, so there are $6$ different faces.

Shape B does not fold up: when folded, two squares lie on top of each other and one face of the cube is missing.

So shape A is a net of a cube and shape B is not.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Thinking that every arrangement of $6$ squares folds into a cube.** Wrong: six squares in two rows of three squares form a net. Why it is wrong: when folded, two squares lie on top of each other and a face is missing. Right: the cross shape with a row of four squares, one square above and one below.
- **Using the diameter of the base as the length of the rectangle.** Wrong: with base radius $5$ cm, the length of the rectangle is $10$ cm. Why it is wrong: the long edge runs all the way round the base, so it equals the circumference of the base. Right: $2\times3.14\times5=31.4$ cm.
- **Adding only three faces of a cuboid.** Wrong: a $5\times3\times2$ cuboid has total area of faces $15+10+6=31\ \text{cm}^2$. Why it is wrong: a cuboid has $6$ faces, with $2$ of each kind. Right: $2\times31=62\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

## Summary

- A net is a flat shape made of the faces of a solid, joined along edges.
- Cube: $6$ equal squares; cuboid: $6$ rectangles, $3$ pairs of equal rectangles.
- Cylinder: $2$ equal circles for the bases and $1$ rectangle whose length is the circumference of the base and whose width is the height.
- The area of a net is the sum of the areas of the faces.
- Not every arrangement of $6$ squares folds into a cube.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
A cube has side $4$ cm. How many squares are in its net? What is the area of each square? Find the area of the whole net.

::hint
**What you need:** a cube has $6$ faces, which are $6$ equal squares.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the area of one square with side $4$ cm.
2. Multiply by the number of squares.

::solution
The net has $6$ squares. The area of each square is $4\times4=16\ \text{cm}^2$. The area of the whole net is $16\times6=96\ \text{cm}^2$.

**Answer.** $6$ squares; $16\ \text{cm}^2$; $96\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
A cuboid has length $8$ cm, width $5$ cm and height $3$ cm. Find the total area of the $6$ faces of the cuboid.

::hint
**What you need:** a cuboid has $3$ pairs of equal faces.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the area of the three different faces.
2. Add the three areas, then multiply by $2$.

::solution
The three different faces have areas $8\times5=40$, $8\times3=24$ and $5\times3=15$ ($\text{cm}^2$). The total area of the $6$ faces is $2\times(40+24+15)=2\times79=158\ \text{cm}^2$.

**Answer.** $158\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
A cylinder has base radius $3$ cm and height $8$ cm. What are the length and the width of the rectangle in its net? (Take $\pi\approx3.14$.)

::hint
**What you need:** the length of the rectangle is the circumference of the base; the width is the height.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the circumference of the circular base.
2. The width is the height of the cylinder.

::solution
The length is the circumference of the base: $2\times3.14\times3=18.84$ (cm). The width is the height: $8$ cm.

**Answer.** $18.84$ cm and $8$ cm.
:::

:::exercise
A cylinder has base radius $2$ cm and height $6$ cm. Find the area of the whole net. (Take $\pi\approx3.14$.)

::hint
**What you need:** the area of the net is the area of the rectangle plus the area of the two circular bases.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the circumference of the base, then the area of the rectangle.
2. Work out the area of one base, multiply by $2$, then add.

::solution
The circumference of the base is $2\times3.14\times2=12.56$ cm. The area of the rectangle is $12.56\times6=75.36\ \text{cm}^2$.

The area of one base is $3.14\times2\times2=12.56\ \text{cm}^2$, so two bases have area $25.12\ \text{cm}^2$.

The area of the whole net is $75.36+25.12=100.48\ \text{cm}^2$.

**Answer.** $100.48\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
Which of these shapes is a net of a cube? A: four squares in a row, with one square above the first square and one square below the fourth square. B: five squares in a row and one square above the second square.

::hint
**What you need:** a net that folds up must give $6$ different faces, with no faces lying on top of each other.

**Steps:**
1. Imagine folding the row into the side faces.
2. Think about the greatest number of squares a row can have.

::solution
Shape A folds up: the row of four squares makes the four side faces and the other two squares are the two bases.

Shape B does not fold up: when the row of five squares is folded round the cube, the fifth square lies on top of the first square.

**Answer.** Shape A.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a cake box is shaped like a cuboid with length $20$ cm, width $10$ cm and height $5$ cm. What is the least area of card, in square centimetres, needed to make the box (not counting the gluing flaps)?

::hint
**What you need:** the area of card is the total area of the $6$ faces of the cuboid.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the area of the three different faces.
2. Add them and multiply by $2$. Remember the unit.

::solution
The three different faces have areas $20\times10=200$, $20\times5=100$ and $10\times5=50$ ($\text{cm}^2$). The area of card needed is $2\times(200+100+50)=2\times350=700\ \text{cm}^2$.

**Answer.** $700\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a drink can is a cylinder with base diameter $6$ cm and height $12$ cm. What is the area of a label that covers the curved surface of the can exactly? (Take $\pi\approx3.14$.)

::hint
**What you need:** the curved surface unrolls into a rectangle whose length is the circumference of the base; the circumference is the diameter times $3.14$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the circumference of the base from the diameter.
2. Multiply the circumference by the height.

::solution
The circumference of the base is $6\times3.14=18.84$ (cm). The area of the label is $18.84\times12=226.08\ \text{(cm}^2\text{)}$.

**Answer.** $226.08\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on units of area and volume. A cube has side $1$ dm. Its net is made of $6$ squares with side $10$ cm. Find the area of the net in $\text{cm}^2$ and in $\text{dm}^2$.

::hint
**What you need:** $1\ \text{dm}=10$ cm and $1\ \text{dm}^2=100\ \text{cm}^2$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the area of each square and of the whole net in $\text{cm}^2$.
2. Change the result into $\text{dm}^2$.

::solution
The area of each square is $10\times10=100\ \text{cm}^2$. The area of the net is $100\times6=600\ \text{cm}^2$.

Change to $\text{dm}^2$: $600\div100=6\ \text{dm}^2$.

**Answer.** $600\ \text{cm}^2$, which is $6\ \text{dm}^2$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A rectangular piece of card $31.4$ cm long and $10$ cm wide is rolled into the curved surface of a cylinder, with the long edges meeting. Find the radius of the base of the cylinder. (Take $\pi\approx3.14$.)

::hint
**What you need:** the length of the card is the circumference of the base, and the circumference is $2\times3.14\times$ the radius.

**Steps:**
1. Write the circumference of the base as the length of the card.
2. Find the radius by dividing.

::solution
The circumference of the base is $31.4$ cm, so $2\times3.14\times r=31.4$, that is, $6.28\times r=31.4$. The radius is $r=31.4\div6.28=5$ (cm).

**Check.** $2\times3.14\times5=31.4$. Correct.

**Answer.** The radius of the base is $5$ cm.
:::

The length is the circumference of the base: $2\times3.14\times5=31.4$ cm. Option B is half the circumference; C is the height; D is the area of one base.
