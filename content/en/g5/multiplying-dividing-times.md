:::goals
- Multiply a time given in two units by a whole number.
- Divide a time given in two units by a whole number, including when there is a remainder.
- Regroup $60$ minutes as $1$ hour (or the other way round) while calculating.
- Solve word problems about time using multiplication and division.
:::

Each football practice of our team lasts $1$ hour $25$ minutes. The team practises $3$ times a week. How long does the team practise altogether each week?

You could add three times, but multiplying is quicker. This lesson shows you how to multiply and divide times without mistakes.

## Multiplying a time by a number

Let's work out $1$ h $25$ min $\times3$ in two ways.

- **Way 1:** multiply each unit. $1\times3=3$ (hours) and $25\times3=75$ (minutes). That gives $3$ h $75$ min, and $75$ min $=1$ h $15$ min, so the answer is $4$ h $15$ min.
- **Way 2:** change to minutes. $1$ h $25$ min $=85$ min, $85\times3=255$ (min), and $255=4\times60+15$, so the answer is $4$ h $15$ min.

Both ways give the same result. So the team practises $4$ h $15$ min each week.

:::theorem Multiplying a time by a number
To multiply a time by a number:
1. Multiply the hours by the number, and multiply the minutes (or seconds) by the number.
2. If the minutes make $60$ or more, change them to hours ($60$ min $=1$ h); change seconds to minutes in the same way.
:::

We multiply each unit because $a$ lots of "hours" plus $a$ lots of "minutes" make $a$ lots of the whole time, just as when multiplying a sum by a number. Then we change $60$ minutes to $1$ hour, like regrouping in whole-number multiplication.

:::example Multiplying with regrouping
Work out $2$ h $40$ min $\times3$.

**Solution.** We multiply each unit and then regroup:
$$2\text{ h }40\text{ min}\times3=6\text{ h }120\text{ min}=8\text{ h}.$$
So $2$ h $40$ min $\times3=8$ h.
:::

:::example Football practice
Each football practice lasts $1$ hour $25$ minutes. The team practises $3$ times a week. How long does the team practise altogether each week?

**Solution.** The time the team practises in one week is:
$$1\text{ h }25\text{ min}\times3=3\text{ h }75\text{ min}=4\text{ h }15\text{ min}.$$
**Answer.** $4$ h $15$ min.
:::

## Dividing a time by a number

Let's work out $7$ h $30$ min $\div3$.

- Divide the hours: $7\div3=2$ (remainder $1$ hour).
- Change the spare hour to minutes: $1$ h $=60$ min. Adding $30$ min gives $90$ min.
- Divide the minutes: $90\div3=30$ (min).

So $7$ h $30$ min $\div3=2$ h $30$ min.

:::theorem Dividing a time by a number
To divide a time by a number:
1. Divide the hours by the number.
2. Change the remainder in hours to minutes, add the minutes you already have, then divide the minutes by the number.
3. Do the same with minutes and seconds.
:::

We check with multiplication: $2$ h $30$ min $\times3=6$ h $90$ min $=7$ h $30$ min. Correct.

:::example Division with a remainder
Work out $13$ min $20$ s $\div4$.

**Solution.** We divide the minutes first: $13\div4=3$ (remainder $1$ min). Change $1$ min $=60$ s and add $20$ s to get $80$ s. Then divide: $80\div4=20$ (s).

So $13$ min $20$ s $\div4=3$ min $20$ s.
:::

When the number of hours is smaller than the divisor, the hours digit of the quotient is $0$. Change everything to minutes and divide.

:::example Sharing out episodes
A cartoon has $5$ episodes of equal length, $3$ hours $45$ minutes in total. How long is each episode?

**Solution.** Since $3$ hours divided by $5$ gives $0$ hours, we change $3$ h $45$ min $=225$ min. Each episode lasts:
$$225\div5=45\ (\text{min}).$$
**Answer.** $45$ minutes.
:::

The next problem has several steps, so we use *Understand, Plan, Solve, Look back*.

:::example Extra lessons
Each extra lesson that Mai attends lasts $1$ hour $30$ minutes. She has $3$ lessons a week. How many hours of extra lessons does she have in $4$ weeks?

**Solution.**

*Understand.* Each lesson lasts $1$ h $30$ min; there are $3$ lessons a week for $4$ weeks. We need the total number of hours.

*Plan.* Find the number of lessons in $4$ weeks, then multiply by the length of one lesson.

*Solve.* The number of lessons is $3\times4=12$ (lessons). The total time is:
$$1\text{ h }30\text{ min}\times12=12\text{ h }360\text{ min}=18\text{ h}.$$
*Look back.* We can change $1$ h $30$ min $=1.5$ h and then $1.5\times12=18$ (h), the same as above. If each lesson lasted only $1$ h $25$ min, then $12$ lessons would take $12$ h $300$ min $=17$ h, exactly $1$ hour less.

**Answer.** $18$ hours.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting to regroup $60$ minutes as $1$ hour.** Wrong: $1$ h $25$ min $\times3=3$ h $75$ min. Why it is wrong: $75$ minutes is more than $1$ hour. Right: $4$ h $15$ min.
- **Dropping the spare hour when dividing.** Wrong: $7$ h $30$ min $\div3=2$ h $10$ min (working out $30\div3$ and dropping the spare $1$ hour). Why it is wrong: the spare hour still has to be divided. Right: $2$ h $30$ min.
- **Writing the minutes after the decimal point of the hours.** Wrong: $1$ h $25$ min $\times3=4.15$ h. Why it is wrong: $15$ minutes is $0.25$ hour, not $0.15$ hour. Right: $4$ h $15$ min, which is $4.25$ h.
:::

## Summary

- To multiply a time by a number, multiply each unit, then change $60$ minutes to $1$ hour ($60$ seconds to $1$ minute).
- To divide a time by a number, divide the hours, change the spare hours to minutes, add the minutes and divide again.
- Always check with the inverse operation: multiply to check a division.
- Problems with "each... altogether" usually need multiplication; "share equally" usually needs division.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $3$ h $15$ min $\times2$.

b) $4$ min $30$ s $\times4$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply each unit, then regroup if you reach $60$.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply the hours (minutes) and the minutes (seconds).
2. Regroup if needed.

::solution
a) $3$ h $15$ min $\times2=6$ h $30$ min.

b) $4$ min $30$ s $\times4=16$ min $120$ s $=18$ min.

**Answer.** a) $6$ h $30$ min; b) $18$ min.
:::

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $1$ h $50$ min $\times3$.

b) $2$ min $45$ s $\times5$.

::hint
**What you need:** $60$ min $=1$ h; $60$ s $=1$ min.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply each unit.
2. Change to the larger unit.

::solution
a) $1$ h $50$ min $\times3=3$ h $150$ min $=5$ h $30$ min.

b) $2$ min $45$ s $\times5=10$ min $225$ s $=13$ min $45$ s.

**Answer.** a) $5$ h $30$ min; b) $13$ min $45$ s.
:::

:::exercise
Work out:

a) $9$ h $30$ min $\div2$.

b) $10$ min $40$ s $\div4$.

::hint
**What you need:** change the spare hours to minutes (spare minutes to seconds), then divide again.

**Steps:**
1. Divide the hours (minutes) first.
2. Change the remainder, add the smaller unit and divide again.

::solution
a) $9\div2=4$ (remainder $1$ h). $1$ h $=60$ min, $60+30=90$ min, $90\div2=45$ min. So $4$ h $45$ min.

b) $10\div4=2$ (remainder $2$ min). $2$ min $=120$ s, $120+40=160$ s, $160\div4=40$ s. So $2$ min $40$ s.

**Answer.** a) $4$ h $45$ min; b) $2$ min $40$ s.
:::

:::exercise
A girl swims one length of the pool in $2$ min $15$ s. She swims $6$ lengths in a row at the same speed. How long does she take?

::hint
**What you need:** time for $6$ lengths $=$ time for one length $\times6$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. Multiply, then change $60$ seconds to $1$ minute.
3. Check: does $13$ min $30$ s $\div6$ equal $2$ min $15$ s?

::solution
The time for $6$ lengths is:
$$2\text{ min }15\text{ s}\times6=12\text{ min }90\text{ s}=13\text{ min }30\text{ s}.$$
**Answer.** $13$ min $30$ s.
:::

:::exercise
A film in $4$ parts of equal length lasts $2$ hours $20$ minutes in total. How many minutes does each part last?

::hint
**What you need:** sharing equally needs division.

**Steps:**
1. Change $2$ h $20$ min to minutes.
2. Divide by $4$.

::solution
$2$ h $20$ min $=140$ min. Each part lasts:
$$140\div4=35\ (\text{min}).$$
**Answer.** $35$ minutes.
:::

:::exercise
Each of Nam's exercise sessions lasts $45$ minutes. In one week Nam exercises $6$ times. How many hours and minutes does Nam exercise altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** multiply, then change the minutes to hours.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. Work out $45\times6$ minutes.
3. Change to hours and minutes.

::solution
The time Nam exercises is:
$$45\times6=270\ (\text{min})=4\text{ h }30\text{ min}.$$
**Answer.** $4$ h $30$ min.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on adding and subtracting times. A journey has $3$ equal stages, each lasting $1$ h $35$ min. The journey starts at $8{:}20$. At what time does it end?

::hint
**What you need:** time of the whole journey $=$ time of one stage $\times3$; end time $=$ start time $+$ time of the journey.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply to find the time of the whole journey.
2. Add it to the start time and regroup.

::solution
The whole journey takes $1$ h $35$ min $\times3=3$ h $105$ min $=4$ h $45$ min. The journey ends at:
$$8\text{ h }20\text{ min}+4\text{ h }45\text{ min}=12\text{ h }65\text{ min}=13\text{ h }5\text{ min}.$$
**Answer.** $13{:}05$.
:::

:::exercise
A clock loses $2$ min $30$ s every day. After $6$ days, how many minutes slow is it?

::hint
**What you need:** multiply the time lost each day by the number of days.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. Multiply, then change seconds to minutes.

::solution
After $6$ days the clock is slow by:
$$2\text{ min }30\text{ s}\times6=12\text{ min }180\text{ s}=15\text{ min}.$$
**Answer.** $15$ minutes.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A time multiplied by $4$ gives $3$ h $40$ min. Find that time.

::hint
**What you need:** to find an unknown factor, divide the product by the known factor.

**Steps:**
1. Change $3$ h $40$ min to minutes.
2. Divide by $4$, then check by multiplying.

::solution
$3$ h $40$ min $=220$ min, and $220\div4=55$ (min).

**Check.** $55\times4=220$ min $=3$ h $40$ min. Correct.

**Answer.** $55$ minutes.
:::

::key C

::key D
$5\div2=2$ (remainder $1$ h), $60+30=90$ min, $90\div2=45$ min. Option A drops the spare hour, B forgets to divide the minutes and C rounds $5\div2$ up to $3$.
