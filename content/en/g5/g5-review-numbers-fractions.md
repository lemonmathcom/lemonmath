A school garden has an area of $240\ \text{m}^2$. Class $5$A uses $\dfrac38$ of the area for vegetables and $\dfrac14$ of the area for flowers. The rest is a path. What is the area of the path in square metres?

To answer, you need both whole numbers and fractions. This lesson revises what you learnt in primary school.

## Whole numbers and operations

The value of each digit depends on its place: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten thousands, hundred thousands, millions. To compare two whole numbers, first compare the number of digits. If they are equal, compare place by place from left to right.

:::example Writing and comparing numbers
Write the number "three hundred and twenty thousand, seven hundred and eight", then compare it with $320\,780$.

**Solution.** The number is $320$ thousand and $708$ ones, so it is $320\,708$. Both numbers have $6$ digits and agree in the first $4$ places. In the tens place, $0<8$.

So $320\,708<320\,780$.
:::

To work out an expression, do the brackets first, then multiply and divide from left to right, and finally add and subtract.

:::example The value of an expression
Work out $(48+36)\div4\times3-5$.

**Solution.** We follow the order of operations:
$$\begin{aligned} (48+36)\div4\times3-5&=84\div4\times3-5\\ &=21\times3-5\\ &=63-5=58. \end{aligned}$$
So the value of the expression is $58$.
:::

## Fractions

To simplify a fraction, divide both the numerator and the denominator by the same number greater than $1$. To compare two fractions with different denominators, write them over a common denominator and compare the numerators.

:::example Simplifying and comparing
a) Simplify $\dfrac{36}{48}$. b) Compare $\dfrac56$ and $\dfrac79$.

**Solution.** a) Divide the numerator and the denominator by $12$: $\dfrac{36}{48}=\dfrac{3}{4}$.

b) The common denominator is $18$: $\dfrac56=\dfrac{15}{18}$ and $\dfrac79=\dfrac{14}{18}$. Since $15>14$, $\dfrac56>\dfrac79$.
:::

:::theorem The four operations with fractions
- Add, subtract: write over a common denominator, then add (subtract) the numerators and keep the denominator.
- Multiply: $\dfrac ab\times\dfrac cd=\dfrac{a\times c}{b\times d}$.
- Divide: $\dfrac ab\div\dfrac cd=\dfrac ab\times\dfrac dc$ (turn the second fraction upside down, then multiply).
:::

We divide by a fraction by multiplying by its reciprocal because dividing by $\dfrac cd$ asks "how many lots of $\dfrac cd$ fit?", and one whole contains $\dfrac dc$ such lots.

:::example Adding and subtracting fractions
Work out a) $\dfrac38+\dfrac14$; b) $\dfrac7{10}-\dfrac25$.

**Solution.** a) $\dfrac38+\dfrac14=\dfrac38+\dfrac28=\dfrac58$.

b) $\dfrac7{10}-\dfrac25=\dfrac7{10}-\dfrac4{10}=\dfrac3{10}$.
:::

:::example Multiplying and dividing fractions
Work out a) $\dfrac34\times\dfrac29$; b) $\dfrac56\div\dfrac{10}9$.

**Solution.** a) $\dfrac34\times\dfrac29=\dfrac{6}{36}=\dfrac16$.

b) $\dfrac56\div\dfrac{10}9=\dfrac56\times\dfrac9{10}=\dfrac{45}{60}=\dfrac34$.
:::

Now we answer the problem at the start, using *Understand, Plan, Solve, Look back*.

:::example The school garden
A garden has an area of $240\ \text{m}^2$. Class $5$A uses $\dfrac38$ of the area for vegetables and $\dfrac14$ for flowers; the rest is a path. Find the area of the path.

**Solution.**

*Understand.* The whole garden is $240\ \text{m}^2$, vegetables take $\dfrac38$ and flowers take $\dfrac14$. We need the area of the rest.

*Plan.* Find the fraction for the path, then take that fraction of $240$.

*Solve.* Vegetables and flowers take $\dfrac38+\dfrac14=\dfrac58$ of the area. The path takes
$$1-\frac58=\frac38\ \text{(of the area)}.$$
The area of the path is $240\times\dfrac38=90\ (\text{m}^2)$.

*Look back.* Vegetables: $240\times\dfrac38=90\ (\text{m}^2)$; flowers: $240\times\dfrac14=60\ (\text{m}^2)$; path: $90\ \text{m}^2$. We have $90+60+90=240$. Correct. The path and the vegetable bed have the same area because both are $\dfrac38$ of the garden.

**Answer.** $90\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Adding numerators and denominators.** Wrong: $\dfrac12+\dfrac13=\dfrac25$. Why it is wrong: fractions with different denominators cannot be added directly. Right: $\dfrac36+\dfrac26=\dfrac56$.
- **Dividing by a fraction without turning it over.** Wrong: $\dfrac56\div\dfrac{10}9=\dfrac56\times\dfrac{10}9$. Why it is wrong: the second fraction must be turned upside down. Right: $\dfrac56\times\dfrac9{10}=\dfrac34$.
- **Adding before multiplying.** Wrong: $6+4\times5=50$. Why it is wrong: multiplication comes before addition. Right: $6+20=26$.
:::

## Summary

- To compare whole numbers, compare the number of digits, then compare place by place from left to right.
- Order of operations: brackets, multiplication and division, addition and subtraction.
- Adding and subtracting fractions needs a common denominator; to multiply, multiply numerators and denominators; to divide, multiply by the reciprocal.
- To find a fraction of an amount, multiply the amount by the fraction.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Write the number made of $5$ million, $3$ hundred thousand and $8$ tens.

::hint
**What you need:** the value of each digit depends on its place.

**Steps:**
1. Write each part: $5\,000\,000$, $300\,000$, $80$.
2. Add them.

::solution
$5\,000\,000+300\,000+80=5\,300\,080$.

**Answer.** $5\,300\,080$.
:::

:::exercise
Put the numbers $4507$, $4570$, $4057$, $4750$ in order from smallest to largest.

::hint
**What you need:** for numbers with the same number of digits, compare place by place from left to right.

**Steps:**
1. Compare the thousands, then the hundreds.
2. Order them from smallest to largest.

::solution
The thousands digit is $4$ in all of them. Hundreds: $4057$ has $0$, $4507$ and $4570$ have $5$, $4750$ has $7$. The numbers $4507$ and $4570$ have the same hundreds digit, so we compare the tens: $0<7$.

So $4057<4507<4570<4750$.

**Answer.** $4057$; $4507$; $4570$; $4750$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out the value of $3\times(45-27)+56\div8$.

::hint
**What you need:** do the brackets first; multiply and divide before adding and subtracting.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the brackets.
2. Work out $3\times18$ and $56\div8$.
3. Add the two results.

::solution
$$3\times(45-27)+56\div8=3\times18+7=54+7=61.$$
**Answer.** $61$.
:::

:::exercise
a) Simplify $\dfrac{42}{56}$. b) Compare $\dfrac7{12}$ and $\dfrac58$.

::hint
**What you need:** simplify by dividing by the same number; compare by writing over a common denominator.

**Steps:**
1. Part a: divide by $14$.
2. Part b: the lowest common denominator of $12$ and $8$ is $24$.

::solution
a) $\dfrac{42}{56}=\dfrac34$.

b) $\dfrac7{12}=\dfrac{14}{24}$ and $\dfrac58=\dfrac{15}{24}$. Since $14<15$, $\dfrac7{12}<\dfrac58$.

**Answer.** a) $\dfrac34$; b) $\dfrac7{12}<\dfrac58$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out a) $\dfrac23+\dfrac5{12}$; b) $\dfrac9{10}-\dfrac34$.

::hint
**What you need:** write over a common denominator, then add (subtract) the numerators.

**Steps:**
1. Part a: the common denominator is $12$.
2. Part b: the common denominator is $20$.

::solution
a) $\dfrac23+\dfrac5{12}=\dfrac8{12}+\dfrac5{12}=\dfrac{13}{12}$.

b) $\dfrac9{10}-\dfrac34=\dfrac{18}{20}-\dfrac{15}{20}=\dfrac3{20}$.

**Answer.** a) $\dfrac{13}{12}$; b) $\dfrac3{20}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out a) $\dfrac45\times\dfrac{15}8$; b) $\dfrac79\div\dfrac{14}3$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply numerators and denominators; to divide, multiply by the reciprocal.

**Steps:**
1. Part a: multiply, then simplify.
2. Part b: turn $\dfrac{14}3$ upside down to get $\dfrac3{14}$.

::solution
a) $\dfrac45\times\dfrac{15}8=\dfrac{60}{40}=\dfrac32$.

b) $\dfrac79\div\dfrac{14}3=\dfrac79\times\dfrac3{14}=\dfrac{21}{126}=\dfrac16$.

**Answer.** a) $\dfrac32$; b) $\dfrac16$.
:::

:::exercise
A tank holds $600$ litres of water. In the morning $\dfrac25$ of the water is used and in the afternoon $\dfrac14$ of the water is used. How many litres of water are left in the tank?

::hint
**What you need:** a fraction of an amount: multiply the amount by the fraction.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What are we asked?
2. Find the fraction of water used, then the fraction left.
3. Check by working out the amount used.

::solution
The fraction used is $\dfrac25+\dfrac14=\dfrac{13}{20}$. The fraction left is $1-\dfrac{13}{20}=\dfrac7{20}$. The number of litres left is $600\times\dfrac7{20}=210$ (litres).

**Check.** The amount used is $600\times\dfrac{13}{20}=390$ litres, and $390+210=600$.

**Answer.** $210$ litres.
:::

:::exercise
Mum receives $6\,000\,000$ dong. She spends $\dfrac12$ of it on school fees, $\dfrac15$ on food and saves the rest. How much does she save, and what percentage of the money is that?

::hint
**What you need:** $\dfrac3{10}=30\%$; finding a fraction of an amount.

**Steps:**
1. Find the fraction that is saved.
2. Multiply by $6\,000\,000$, then change it to a percentage.

::solution
The money spent is $\dfrac12+\dfrac15=\dfrac7{10}$ of the total. The savings are $1-\dfrac7{10}=\dfrac3{10}$, which is $30\%$. The amount saved is $6\,000\,000\times\dfrac3{10}=1\,800\,000$ (dong).

**Answer.** $1\,800\,000$ dong; $30\%$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on decimals. Write $\dfrac38$ as a decimal and as a percentage.

::hint
**What you need:** a fraction is a division of the numerator by the denominator.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $3\div8$.
2. Change the result to a percentage.

::solution
$3\div8=0.375=37.5\%$.

**Answer.** $0.375$; $37.5\%$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
The difference between the denominator and the numerator of a fraction is $20$, and the fraction equals $\dfrac38$. Find the fraction.

::hint
**What you need:** a fraction equal to $\dfrac38$ has the form $\dfrac{3\times k}{8\times k}$.

**Steps:**
1. Write the difference of the denominator and the numerator in terms of $k$.
2. Find $k$, then the fraction, and check.

::solution
The fraction has the form $\dfrac{3\times k}{8\times k}$, so the difference of the denominator and the numerator is $8\times k-3\times k=5\times k$. Since $5\times k=20$, $k=4$ and the fraction is $\dfrac{12}{32}$.

**Check.** $32-12=20$ and $\dfrac{12}{32}=\dfrac38$. Correct.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{12}{32}$.
:::

D. $28$.
::key A
We multiply first: $6+20-2=24$. Option B works out $(6+4)\times5-2$, C works out $6+4\times(5-2)$ and D adds $2$ instead of subtracting.

$\dfrac12+\dfrac13$ equals
A. $\dfrac25$.
B. $\dfrac56$.
C. $\dfrac16$.
D. $\dfrac26$.
::key B
$\dfrac12+\dfrac13=\dfrac36+\dfrac26=\dfrac56$. Option A adds numerators and denominators, D adds the numerators but keeps the denominator $6$ and C is the difference $\dfrac12-\dfrac13$.

::mc
$\dfrac23\div\dfrac45$ equals
A. $\dfrac8{15}$.
B. $\dfrac65$.
C. $\dfrac56$.
D. $\dfrac{22}{15}$.
::key C
$\dfrac23\div\dfrac45=\dfrac23\times\dfrac54=\dfrac{10}{12}=\dfrac56$. Option A forgets to turn the fraction upside down, B turns the first fraction upside down by mistake and D is the addition.

::tf
Consider the fractions $\dfrac7{12}$ and $\dfrac58$.
a) Over the common denominator $24$ they become $\dfrac{14}{24}$ and $\dfrac{15}{24}$.
b) $\dfrac7{12}>\dfrac58$.
c) $\dfrac58-\dfrac7{12}=\dfrac24$.
d) $\dfrac7{12}+\dfrac58=\dfrac{29}{24}$.
::key T F F T
a) True: $\dfrac7{12}=\dfrac{14}{24}$ and $\dfrac58=\dfrac{15}{24}$. b) False: $14<15$, so $\dfrac7{12}<\dfrac58$. c) False: $\dfrac{15}{24}-\dfrac{14}{24}=\dfrac1{24}$; the denominators are not subtracted. d) True: $\dfrac{14+15}{24}=\dfrac{29}{24}$.
