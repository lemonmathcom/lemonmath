:::goals
- Understand that a percentage tells you how many hundredths the first number is of the second.
- Express one number as a percentage of another: divide, multiply by $100$, add the $\%$ sign.
- Solve word problems of the type "what percentage of ... is ...?".
:::

There are $40$ children in Class 5A, and $30$ of them can swim. The teacher asks: what percentage of the class can swim?

Saying "per cent" means comparing with $100$ equal parts. This lesson shows you how to answer questions like this one.

## Let's explore

We compare the $30$ swimmers with all $40$ children in the class. The ratio of the two numbers is $30:40$, or $\dfrac{30}{40}$.

Remember that $1\%=\dfrac{1}{100}$, so $75\%=\dfrac{75}{100}$. So we need to write the ratio as a fraction with denominator $100$. There are two ways.

- **Method 1.** Simplify $\dfrac{30}{40}=\dfrac{3}{4}$, then multiply the numerator and the denominator by $25$: $\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{75}{100}=75\%$.
- **Method 2.** Divide: $30\div40=0.75$. And $0.75=\dfrac{75}{100}=75\%$.

Method 2 works for any pair of numbers, so this is the one we use.

:::theorem Expressing one number as a percentage of another
To express the first number as a percentage of the second:
1. Divide the first number by the second and write the quotient as a decimal.
2. Multiply the quotient by $100$ (move the decimal point two places to the right).
3. Write the $\%$ sign after the result.
:::

This works because $0.75=\dfrac{75}{100}$, and $\dfrac{75}{100}$ is exactly $75\%$. Multiplying by $100$ and writing $\%$ turns a decimal into "how many per cent".

## Examples

We start with two small numbers.

:::example Express $9$ as a percentage of $20$
**Solution.** Divide $9$ by $20$, then multiply the quotient by $100$:
$$9\div20=0.45;\qquad 0.45\times100=45.$$
So $9$ is $45\%$ of $20$.
:::

Some divisions give a quotient with three decimal places. You still follow the same three steps.

:::example Express $7$ as a percentage of $8$
**Solution.** Dividing $7$ by $8$ gives $0.875$. Multiplying by $100$ moves the decimal point two places to the right:
$$7\div8=0.875;\qquad 0.875\times100=87.5.$$
So $7$ is $87.5\%$ of $8$.
:::

In a word problem, "what percentage of B is A?" means comparing A with B. We divide A by B, so the "whole" is always the number we divide by.

:::example Reading a book
Nam's book has $90$ pages. He has read $36$ pages. What percentage of the book has Nam read?

**Solution.** We compare the pages read with the pages in the whole book, so we divide $36$ by $90$:
$$36\div90=0.4;\qquad 0.4\times100=40.$$
So Nam has read $40\%$ of the book.
:::

When the two numbers have different units, you must convert them to the same unit first.

:::example Cutting string
A piece of string is $2$ m long. $50$ cm is cut off. What percentage of the string has been cut off?

**Solution.** The two numbers must have the same unit. Convert $2\ \text{m}=200\ \text{cm}$, then divide $50$ by $200$:
$$50\div200=0.25;\qquad 0.25\times100=25.$$
So $25\%$ of the string has been cut off.

**Check.** $50$ cm is a quarter of $200$ cm, and $\dfrac{1}{4}=25\%$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Forgetting to multiply by $100$.** Wrong: $9\div20=0.45$, answer $0.45\%$. Why it is wrong: $0.45\%$ is less than one per cent. Right: $0.45\times100=45$, answer $45\%$.
- **Dividing the wrong way round.** Wrong: $5$ as a percentage of $20$ is $20\div5=4$, so $400\%$. Why it is wrong: $5$ is smaller than $20$, so the result must be less than $100\%$. Right: $5\div20=0.25$, so $25\%$.
- **Forgetting to convert units.** Wrong: $50$ cm and $2$ m give $50\div2=25$, so $2500\%$. Why it is wrong: the two numbers have different units. Right: $50\div200=0.25$, so $25\%$.
:::

## Summary

- A percentage tells you how many hundredths the first number is of the second. Remember that $1\%=\dfrac{1}{100}$.
- Method: divide, multiply by $100$, write $\%$. Example: $30\div40=0.75$, then $0.75\times100=75$, giving $75\%$.
- The "whole" (the number you compare with) comes second and is the one you divide by. Both numbers need the same unit.
- If the first number is smaller than the second, the result is less than $100\%$; if it is larger, the result is more than $100\%$.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Express $3$ as a percentage of $5$.

::hint
**What you need:** divide the first number by the second, multiply the quotient by $100$, then write $\%$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $3\div5$.
2. Multiply the quotient by $100$ and add $\%$.

::solution
$$3\div5=0.6;\qquad 0.6\times100=60.$$

**Answer.** $60\%$.
:::

:::exercise
Express $18$ as a percentage of $24$.

::hint
**What you need:** the first number is divided by the second.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $18\div24$.
2. Multiply the quotient by $100$ and add $\%$.

::solution
$$18\div24=0.75;\qquad 0.75\times100=75.$$

**Answer.** $75\%$.
:::

:::exercise
Express $5$ as a percentage of $8$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiplying by $100$ moves the decimal point two places to the right.

**Steps:**
1. Divide $5$ by $8$; the quotient has three decimal places.
2. Move the decimal point two places to the right and add $\%$.

::solution
$$5\div8=0.625;\qquad 0.625\times100=62.5.$$

**Answer.** $62.5\%$.
:::

:::exercise
Write the fraction $\dfrac{13}{20}$ as a percentage.

::hint
**What you need:** the fraction $\dfrac{13}{20}$ is the ratio $13:20$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $13\div20$.
2. Multiply the quotient by $100$ and add $\%$.

::solution
$$13\div20=0.65;\qquad 0.65\times100=65.$$

**Another way.** Multiply the numerator and the denominator by $5$: $\dfrac{13}{20}=\dfrac{65}{100}=65\%$.

**Answer.** $65\%$.
:::

:::exercise
A garden has $40$ trees, and $14$ of them are orange trees. What percentage of the trees in the garden are orange trees?

::hint
**What you need:** "what percentage of the trees in the garden" means dividing the number of orange trees by the number of trees in the whole garden.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? $14$ orange trees and $40$ trees in all. What is asked? The percentage that are orange trees.
2. Work out $14\div40$, multiply by $100$ and add $\%$.

::solution
$$14\div40=0.35;\qquad 0.35\times100=35.$$
So $35\%$ of the trees in the garden are orange trees.

**Answer.** $35\%$.
:::

:::exercise
Binh's home is $1.5$ km from school. This morning he has gone $600$ m. What percentage of the way has he gone?

::hint
**What you need:** both numbers must have the same unit before you divide.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? The whole way is $1.5$ km and he has gone $600$ m. What is asked? The percentage of the way he has gone.
2. Convert $1.5$ km to metres.
3. Work out $600\div1500$, multiply by $100$ and add $\%$.

::solution
Convert $1.5\ \text{km}=1500\ \text{m}$. Then:
$$600\div1500=0.4;\qquad 0.4\times100=40.$$
So Binh has gone $40\%$ of the way.

**Answer.** $40\%$.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson. The difference between two numbers is $24$, and the ratio of the smaller number to the larger number is $\dfrac{3}{5}$.

a) Find the two numbers.

b) What percentage of the larger number is the smaller number?

::hint
**What you need:** the smaller number has $3$ parts, the larger has $5$ parts, and the difference is $5-3$ parts.

**Steps:**
1. Find the difference in parts, then divide $24$ by it to get one part.
2. Find the smaller and the larger number.
3. For part b, divide the smaller number by the larger one and multiply by $100$.

::solution
a) The difference is $5-3=2$ parts. One part is $24\div2=12$. The smaller number is $12\times3=36$ and the larger number is $12\times5=60$.

**Check.** $60-36=24$. Correct.

b) We divide the smaller number by the larger one:
$$36\div60=0.6;\qquad 0.6\times100=60.$$

**Answer.** a) The numbers are $36$ and $60$. b) The smaller number is $60\%$ of the larger number.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Yesterday a shop sold $80$ exercise books. Today it sold $100$ exercise books. The number sold today is what percentage of the number sold yesterday?

::hint
**What you need:** the number after "what percentage of" is the one you compare with, so you divide by it. A larger number gives a result above $100\%$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? $80$ books yesterday and $100$ today. What is asked? Today's number as a percentage of yesterday's.
2. Divide today's number by yesterday's number.
3. Multiply by $100$ and add $\%$.

::solution
We compare today with yesterday, so we divide $100$ by $80$:
$$100\div80=1.25;\qquad 1.25\times100=125.$$

**Check.** $100>80$, so the result must be more than $100\%$. Correct.

**Answer.** $125\%$.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
$5$ as a percentage of $20$ is
A. $0.25\%$.
B. $15\%$.
C. $25\%$.
D. $400\%$.
::key C
$5\div20=0.25$, and $0.25\times100=25$. Option A forgets to multiply by $100$, option B uses the difference $20-5$, and option D divides the wrong way round ($20\div5$).

::mc
Class 5B has $40$ pupils, and $12$ of them belong to the chess club. What percentage of the class belong to the chess club?
A. $0.3\%$.
B. $12\%$.
C. $28\%$.
D. $30\%$.
::key D
$12\div40=0.3$, and $0.3\times100=30$. Option A forgets to multiply by $100$, and option C uses the difference $40-12$.

::mc
A piece of cloth is $4$ m long. $80$ cm of it has been used. What percentage of the cloth has been used?
A. $20\%$.
B. $2000\%$.
C. $80\%$.
D. $0.2\%$.
::key A
Convert $4\ \text{m}=400\ \text{cm}$; then $80\div400=0.2$, which is $20\%$. Option B forgets to convert units ($80\div4=20$, then $\times100$), and option C is the cloth that is left.

::mc
$3$ as a percentage of $8$ is
A. $375\%$.
B. $37.5\%$.
C. $0.375\%$.
D. $5\%$.
::key B
$3\div8=0.375$, and $0.375\times100=37.5$. Option A multiplies by $1000$, option C forgets to multiply by $100$, and option D uses the difference $8-3$.

::tf
Class 5C has $25$ pupils, and $10$ of them are boys. Look at these statements.
a) The boys make up $40\%$ of the class.
b) The girls make up $60\%$ of the class.
c) The boys make up $10\%$ of the class.
d) The boys and the girls together make up $100\%$ of the class.
::key T T F T
a) True: $10\div25=0.4=40\%$. b) True: there are $25-10=15$ girls and $15\div25=0.6=60\%$. c) False: $10\%$ mixes up the number $10$ with a percentage; the correct value is $40\%$. d) True: $40\%+60\%=100\%$.

::sa
Express $21$ as a percentage of $40$. Give the number only, without the $\%$ sign.
::key 52.5
$21\div40=0.525$, and $0.525\times100=52.5$.

::sa
A sack holds $25$ kg of rice. $4$ kg has been sold. What percentage of the rice has been sold? Give the number only, without the $\%$ sign.
::key 16
$4\div25=0.16$, and $0.16\times100=16$, so $16\%$.
:::
