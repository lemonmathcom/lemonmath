:::goals
- Know the square kilometre and the hectare and how they relate to the square metre.
- Know the table of units of area, in which neighbouring units are $100$ times apart.
- Change between units of area, including measurements written as decimals.
- Solve real-life problems about area with several units.

Suppose a forest is a rectangle $5$ km long and $3$ km wide. If you work out its area in square metres, you get a very large number.

How many square metres is the area of the forest? And is there a more convenient unit for talking about large pieces of land like this?

## Square kilometres

### Let's explore

:::diagram A rectangular forest, 5 km long and 3 km wide
alt: A rectangle ABCD with length 5 km and width 3 km
point A 0,0 below-left
point B 5,0 below-right
point C 5,3 above-right
point D 0,3 above-left
polygon A B C D
label 2.5,0.5 "length 5 km"
label 4,1.5 "width 3 km"
:::

Change to metres: $5$ km $=5000$ m and $3$ km $=3000$ m. The area of the forest is $5000\times3000=15\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2$.

This number has eight digits. To make it shorter, people use the **square kilometre**, with symbol $\text{km}^2$. A square kilometre is the area of a square whose side is $1$ km long.

:::definition Square kilometre
$1\ \text{km}^2$ is the area of a square with side $1$ km. Since $1$ km $=1000$ m,
$$1\ \text{km}^2=1000\times1000=1\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2.$$
:::

Why are units of area so many times apart? If the side is $1000$ times longer, the area is $1000\times1000$ times greater. The forest has area $5\times3=15\ \text{km}^2$, exactly $15\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2$.

**Connection.** You already know $1\ \text{m}^2=100\ \text{dm}^2$ because $1$ m $=10$ dm. The reasoning here is exactly the same.

### Examples

:::example Changing units
a) $3\ \text{km}^2=?\ \text{m}^2$; b) $7\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2=?\ \text{km}^2$; c) $5\ \text{km}^2\ 200\,000\ \text{m}^2=?\ \text{m}^2$.

**Solution.** a) $3\ \text{km}^2=3\times1\,000\,000=3\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2$.

b) $7\,000\,000\div1\,000\,000=7$, so $7\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2=7\ \text{km}^2$.

c) $5\ \text{km}^2=5\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2$, so $5\ \text{km}^2\ 200\,000\ \text{m}^2=5\,200\,000\ \text{m}^2$.

So a) $3\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2$; b) $7\ \text{km}^2$; c) $5\,200\,000\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

Next, the area of a rectangle whose sides are measured in kilometres.

:::example A nature reserve
Suppose a nature reserve is a rectangle $6$ km long and $4$ km wide. Find the area of the reserve.

**Solution.** Both measurements are in kilometres, so we multiply straight away:
$$6\times4=24\ \text{(km}^2\text{)}.$$
So the area of the reserve is $24\ \text{km}^2$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Using $1\ \text{km}^2=1000\ \text{m}^2$.** Wrong: $3\ \text{km}^2=3000\ \text{m}^2$. Why it is wrong: $1\ \text{km}^2$ is a square with side $1000$ m, with area $1000\times1000$. Right: $3\ \text{km}^2=3\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

## Hectares

### Let's explore

For fields and gardens, people often use the **hectare**, with symbol ha. A hectare is the area of a square whose side is $100$ m long, that is, $1$ hectometre.

:::definition Hectare
$1$ ha is the area of a square with side $100$ m, so
$$1\ \text{ha}=100\times100=10\,000\ \text{m}^2.$$
Since $1\ \text{km}^2=1\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2$, we have $1\ \text{km}^2=100$ ha.
:::

Why is $1\ \text{km}^2=100$ ha? A square with side $1$ km can be cut into $10\times10=100$ squares with side $100$ m, and each of those squares is $1$ ha.

### Examples

:::example Changing units
a) $4$ ha $=?\ \text{m}^2$; b) $25\,000\ \text{m}^2=?$ ha; c) $3\ \text{km}^2=?$ ha; d) $450$ ha $=?\ \text{km}^2$.

**Solution.** a) $4$ ha $=4\times10\,000=40\,000\ \text{m}^2$.

b) $25\,000\ \text{m}^2=20\,000\ \text{m}^2+5000\ \text{m}^2=2$ ha $+0.5$ ha $=2.5$ ha.

c) $3\ \text{km}^2=3\times100=300$ ha.

d) $450$ ha $=400$ ha $+50$ ha $=4\ \text{km}^2+0.5\ \text{km}^2=4.5\ \text{km}^2$.

So a) $40\,000\ \text{m}^2$; b) $2.5$ ha; c) $300$ ha; d) $4.5\ \text{km}^2$.
:::

Now a rice-growing problem.

:::example A field
Suppose a field is a rectangle $250$ m long and $120$ m wide. How many hectares is the field?

**Solution.** The area of the field is:
$$250\times120=30\,000\ \text{(m}^2\text{)}.$$
Since $1$ ha $=10\,000\ \text{m}^2$, we have $30\,000\ \text{m}^2=3$ ha.

So the field is $3$ ha.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Using $1$ ha $=1000\ \text{m}^2$.** Wrong: $4$ ha $=4000\ \text{m}^2$. Why it is wrong: $1$ ha is a square with side $100$ m, with area $100\times100$. Right: $4$ ha $=40\,000\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

## Units of area

You know $\text{m}^2$, $\text{dm}^2$, $\text{cm}^2$, $\text{km}^2$ and ha. We put them in a table together with $\text{dam}^2$, $\text{hm}^2$ and $\text{mm}^2$.

| Square kilometre | Square hectometre | Square decametre | Square metre | Square decimetre | Square centimetre | Square millimetre |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\text{km}^2$ | $\text{hm}^2$ (ha) | $\text{dam}^2$ | $\text{m}^2$ | $\text{dm}^2$ | $\text{cm}^2$ | $\text{mm}^2$ |

:::theorem Relationships between units of area
- Two neighbouring units of area are $100$ times apart: a unit is $100$ times the next smaller unit, and a unit is $\dfrac{1}{100}$ of the next larger unit.
- $1\ \text{hm}^2=1$ ha $=10\,000\ \text{m}^2$ and $1\ \text{km}^2=100$ ha $=1\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2$.
- To change from a larger unit to a smaller unit, multiply; to change from a smaller unit to a larger unit, divide; each step is $100$.
:::

This works because neighbouring units of length are $10$ times apart, so the matching units of area are $10\times10=100$ times apart.

### Examples

:::example Changing using the table
a) $2\ \text{dam}^2=?\ \text{m}^2$; b) $5\ \text{hm}^2=?$ ha $=?\ \text{m}^2$; c) $30\,000\ \text{dm}^2=?\ \text{m}^2$.

**Solution.** a) $2\ \text{dam}^2=2\times100=200\ \text{m}^2$.

b) $5\ \text{hm}^2=5$ ha, and $5$ ha $=5\times10\,000=50\,000\ \text{m}^2$.

c) $30\,000\div100=300$, so $30\,000\ \text{dm}^2=300\ \text{m}^2$.

So a) $200\ \text{m}^2$; b) $5$ ha $=50\,000\ \text{m}^2$; c) $300\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

When two measurements are in different units, write them in the same unit before comparing or calculating.

:::example Two plots of land
Suppose the first plot of land is $3.5$ ha and the second plot is $20\,000\ \text{m}^2$. Which plot is larger, and by how many square metres?

**Understand.** We know the areas of two plots, in different units. We need to compare the areas and find the difference.

**Plan.** Change both measurements to square metres, compare, then subtract the smaller from the larger.

**Solve.** We have $3.5$ ha $=3$ ha $+0.5$ ha $=30\,000+5000=35\,000\ \text{m}^2$. Since $35\,000>20\,000$, the first plot is larger. The difference between the areas is:
$$35\,000-20\,000=15\,000\ \text{(m}^2\text{)}.$$
So the first plot is larger, by $15\,000\ \text{m}^2$.

**Look back.** Is there another way? Yes, change to hectares: $20\,000\ \text{m}^2=2$ ha, and $3.5-2=1.5$ ha, which is $15\,000\ \text{m}^2$. Is it reasonable? Yes, because the first plot is $1.5$ ha larger, and $1$ ha is $10\,000\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Using $1\ \text{km}^2=10$ ha.** Wrong: $1\ \text{km}^2=10$ ha because $1$ km $=10$ hm. Why it is wrong: units of area are $10\times10$ times apart. Right: $1\ \text{km}^2=100$ ha.
:::

## Summary

- $1\ \text{km}^2=1\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2$.
- $1$ ha $=1\ \text{hm}^2=10\,000\ \text{m}^2$ and $1\ \text{km}^2=100$ ha.
- Two neighbouring units of area are $100$ times apart.
- To change from a larger to a smaller unit, multiply; from a smaller to a larger unit, divide; each step is $100$.
- To compare or calculate with measurements in different units, write them in the same unit.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Find the missing numbers:
a) $7\ \text{km}^2=?\ \text{m}^2$; b) $12\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2=?\ \text{km}^2$; c) $4\ \text{km}^2=?$ ha; d) $900$ ha $=?\ \text{km}^2$.

::hint
**What you need:** $1\ \text{km}^2=1\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2=100$ ha.

**Steps:**
1. Larger unit to smaller unit: multiply. Smaller unit to larger unit: divide.
2. Check whether you multiply or divide by $1\,000\,000$ or by $100$.

::solution
a) $7\times1\,000\,000=7\,000\,000\ \text{m}^2$.

b) $12\,000\,000\div1\,000\,000=12\ \text{km}^2$.

c) $4\times100=400$ ha.

d) $900\div100=9\ \text{km}^2$.

**Answer.** a) $7\,000\,000$; b) $12$; c) $400$; d) $9$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the missing numbers:
a) $6$ ha $=?\ \text{m}^2$; b) $80\,000\ \text{m}^2=?$ ha; c) $3.5$ ha $=?\ \text{m}^2$; d) $0.25$ ha $=?\ \text{m}^2$.

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ ha $=10\,000\ \text{m}^2$. For decimals, separate the whole-number part and the decimal part.

**Steps:**
1. Change the whole-number part to square metres.
2. Change the decimal part using a fraction, for example $0.5$ ha $=\dfrac{5}{10}$ ha.

::solution
a) $6\times10\,000=60\,000\ \text{m}^2$.

b) $80\,000\div10\,000=8$ ha.

c) $3.5$ ha $=3$ ha $+0.5$ ha $=30\,000+5000=35\,000\ \text{m}^2$.

d) $0.25$ ha $=\dfrac{25}{100}$ ha $=\dfrac{25}{100}\times10\,000=2500\ \text{m}^2$.

**Answer.** a) $60\,000$; b) $8$; c) $35\,000$; d) $2500$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the missing numbers:
a) $3\ \text{dam}^2=?\ \text{m}^2$; b) $5\ \text{hm}^2=?\ \text{dam}^2$; c) $40\,000\ \text{dm}^2=?\ \text{m}^2$; d) $70\,000\ \text{mm}^2=?\ \text{dm}^2$.

::hint
**What you need:** two neighbouring units of area are $100$ times apart.

**Steps:**
1. Count how many steps there are between the two units.
2. Multiply or divide by $100$ for each step.

::solution
a) $3\times100=300\ \text{m}^2$.

b) $5\times100=500\ \text{dam}^2$.

c) $40\,000\div100=400\ \text{m}^2$.

d) From $\text{mm}^2$ up to $\text{dm}^2$ there are two steps, each dividing by $100$, so we divide by $10\,000$: $70\,000\div10\,000=7\ \text{dm}^2$.

**Answer.** a) $300$; b) $500$; c) $400$; d) $7$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a nature reserve is a rectangle $8$ km long and $5$ km wide. Find the area of the reserve in square kilometres and in hectares.

::hint
**What you need:** both measurements are in kilometres; $1\ \text{km}^2=100$ ha.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked? Work out the area in $\text{km}^2$.
2. Change the result into hectares.

::solution
The area of the reserve is $8\times5=40\ \text{km}^2$.

Change to hectares: $40\times100=4000$ ha.

**Answer.** $40\ \text{km}^2$, which is $4000$ ha.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a field is a rectangle $400$ m long and $150$ m wide. Each hectare of the field produces $7$ tonnes of rice. How many tonnes of rice does the whole field produce?

::hint
**What you need:** change the area to hectares, then multiply by the number of tonnes produced per hectare.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the area of the field in square metres, then change it to hectares.
2. Multiply the number of hectares by $7$. Check: is the result reasonable?

::solution
The area of the field is $400\times150=60\,000\ \text{m}^2=6$ ha.

The number of tonnes of rice produced is $6\times7=42$ (tonnes).

**Answer.** $42$ tonnes of rice.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the gap: a) $3$ ha $\ldots\ 30\,000\ \text{m}^2$; b) $5\ \text{km}^2\ \ldots\ 450$ ha; c) $2500\ \text{m}^2\ \ldots\ 0.3$ ha.

::hint
**What you need:** write both measurements in the same unit, then compare.

**Steps:**
1. Choose a common unit for each part.
2. Compare the two numbers.

::solution
a) $3$ ha $=30\,000\ \text{m}^2$, so write $=$.

b) $5\ \text{km}^2=500$ ha. Since $500>450$, write $>$.

c) $0.3$ ha $=3000\ \text{m}^2$. Since $2500<3000$, write $<$.

**Answer.** a) $=$; b) $>$; c) $<$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a village has three plots of land of areas $2.5$ ha, $15\,000\ \text{m}^2$ and $0.4$ ha. Put the areas of the three plots in order from least to greatest.

::hint
**What you need:** change all three measurements to square metres to compare them.

**Steps:**
1. Change $2.5$ ha and $0.4$ ha to square metres.
2. Compare the three numbers you have found.

::solution
$2.5$ ha $=25\,000\ \text{m}^2$ and $0.4$ ha $=4000\ \text{m}^2$. The three areas are $25\,000$; $15\,000$; $4000$ (square metres).

Since $4000<15\,000<25\,000$, the order from least to greatest is $0.4$ ha; $15\,000\ \text{m}^2$; $2.5$ ha.

**Answer.** $0.4$ ha $<15\,000\ \text{m}^2<2.5$ ha.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the review lesson on the area of rectangles and squares. A square plot of land has side $200$ m. Find the area of the plot in square metres and in hectares.

::hint
**What you need:** the area of a square is side times side; $1$ ha $=10\,000\ \text{m}^2$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the area in square metres.
2. Change the result into hectares.

::solution
The area of the plot is $200\times200=40\,000\ \text{m}^2$.

Change to hectares: $40\,000\div10\,000=4$ ha.

**Answer.** $40\,000\ \text{m}^2$, which is $4$ ha.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a district has $1200$ ha of rice fields and $8\ \text{km}^2$ of forest. How many hectares are the rice fields and the forest altogether?

::hint
**What you need:** $1\ \text{km}^2=100$ ha; measurements must be in the same unit before you add them.

**Steps:**
1. Change the area of the forest into hectares.
2. Add the two areas.

::solution
The forest has area $8\ \text{km}^2=8\times100=800$ ha.

The total area is $1200+800=2000$ (ha).

**Answer.** $2000$ ha.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A rectangle has area $1$ ha and its length is $4$ times its width. Find the perimeter of the rectangle in metres.

::hint
**What you need:** $1$ ha $=10\,000\ \text{m}^2$. This rectangle can be cut into $4$ equal squares.

**Steps:**
1. What is the area of each square in square metres?
2. Find the side of a square (which number multiplied by itself gives that area?), then find the perimeter.

::solution
The rectangle can be cut into $4$ equal squares, each with area $10\,000\div4=2500\ \text{m}^2$. Since $50\times50=2500$, the side of a square is $50$ m.

The width is $50$ m and the length is $50\times4=200$ m. The perimeter is $(50+200)\times2=500$ (m).

**Answer.** $500$ m.
:::
