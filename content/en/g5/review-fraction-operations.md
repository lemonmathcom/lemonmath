:::goals
- Add, subtract, multiply and divide fractions and simplify the answers.
- Carry out the operations in expressions with fractions in the correct order.
- Find an unknown number in an addition or multiplication with fractions.
- Solve multi-step word problems using calculations with fractions.
:::

Ha has a roll of string $\dfrac{3}{4}$ m long. Ha cuts off $\dfrac{1}{3}$ m to tie a present, and then cuts the rest of the string into $5$ equal pieces.

How long is the string left after the first cut? How long is each of the $5$ pieces? To find out, you need both subtraction and division of fractions. This lesson reviews all four operations with fractions.

## Let's explore

Let's recall how to do each operation. To add or subtract two fractions with different denominators, first write them over a common denominator:
$$\dfrac{3}{4}-\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{9}{12}-\dfrac{4}{12}=\dfrac{5}{12}.$$
The string left is $\dfrac{5}{12}$ m long. Then share it equally among $5$ pieces, which is dividing a fraction by a natural number:
$$\dfrac{5}{12}\div5=\dfrac{5}{12}\times\dfrac{1}{5}=\dfrac{5}{60}=\dfrac{1}{12}.$$
Each piece is $\dfrac{1}{12}$ m long.

:::theorem Calculations with fractions
- **Adding and subtracting:** with the same denominator, add or subtract the numerators and keep the denominator; with different denominators, write over a common denominator and then calculate.
- **Multiplying:** multiply the numerators together and the denominators together: $\dfrac{a}{b}\times\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{a\times c}{b\times d}$.
- **Dividing:** multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor: $\dfrac{a}{b}\div\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{a}{b}\times\dfrac{d}{c}$.
- **Order of operations:** brackets first; multiplication and division before addition and subtraction; operations of the same level from left to right.
- Simplify the answer to its simplest form.
:::

Why is the order of operations the same as for natural numbers? Because fractions are numbers too, so the rules about brackets and doing multiplication and division before addition and subtraction still apply.

**Connection.** This lesson gathers the lessons on adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing fractions and finding a fraction of an amount. It prepares you for later lessons on decimals and percentages.

## Examples

We start with addition.

:::example Addition
Work out $\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{3}{4}$.

**Solution.** The common denominator is $12$:
$$\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{8}{12}+\dfrac{9}{12}=\dfrac{17}{12}.$$
The fraction $\dfrac{17}{12}$ cannot be simplified. So $\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{17}{12}$.
:::

Next, subtraction.

:::example Subtraction
Work out $\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{3}{8}$.

**Solution.** The common denominator is $24$:
$$\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{3}{8}=\dfrac{20}{24}-\dfrac{9}{24}=\dfrac{11}{24}.$$
So $\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{3}{8}=\dfrac{11}{24}$.
:::

Now multiplication and division.

:::example Multiplication and division
Work out:
a) $\dfrac{3}{4}\times\dfrac{8}{9}$
b) $\dfrac{5}{6}\div\dfrac{5}{12}$

**Solution.** a) $\dfrac{3}{4}\times\dfrac{8}{9}=\dfrac{3\times8}{4\times9}=\dfrac{24}{36}=\dfrac{2}{3}$.

b) The reciprocal of $\dfrac{5}{12}$ is $\dfrac{12}{5}$:
$$\dfrac{5}{6}\div\dfrac{5}{12}=\dfrac{5}{6}\times\dfrac{12}{5}=\dfrac{60}{30}=2.$$
So a) $\dfrac{2}{3}$; b) $2$.
:::

The order of operations helps you work out expressions with several operations.

:::example Order of operations
Work out the values of these expressions:
a) $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}\times\dfrac{3}{4}$
b) $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}\right)\times\dfrac{3}{4}$

**Solution.** a) Multiply first, then add:
$$\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}\times\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{3}{12}=\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{3}{4}.$$
b) Brackets first:
$$\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}\right)\times\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{5}{6}\times\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{15}{24}=\dfrac{5}{8}.$$
So a) $\dfrac{3}{4}$; b) $\dfrac{5}{8}$.
:::

You find unknown numbers with the inverse operation.

:::example Finding an unknown number
Find $x$, given:
a) $x+\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{7}{10}$
b) $x\times\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{9}{10}$

**Solution.** a) An unknown addend is the sum minus the known addend:
$$x=\dfrac{7}{10}-\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{7}{10}-\dfrac{4}{10}=\dfrac{3}{10}.$$
b) An unknown factor is the product divided by the known factor:
$$x=\dfrac{9}{10}\div\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{9}{10}\times\dfrac{4}{3}=\dfrac{36}{30}=\dfrac{6}{5}.$$
So a) $x=\dfrac{3}{10}$; b) $x=\dfrac{6}{5}$.
:::

Finally, the problem about the roll of string.

:::example Cutting the string
Ha has a roll of string $\dfrac{3}{4}$ m long. Ha cuts off $\dfrac{1}{3}$ m, and then cuts the rest of the string into $5$ equal pieces. How long is each piece in metres?

**Understand.** The string is $\dfrac{3}{4}$ m long; $\dfrac{1}{3}$ m is cut off; the rest is cut into $5$ equal pieces. We need the length of each piece.

**Plan.** Find the string left by subtracting, then divide by $5$.

**Solve.** The metres of string left are $\dfrac{3}{4}-\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{9}{12}-\dfrac{4}{12}=\dfrac{5}{12}$ (m).

Each piece is $\dfrac{5}{12}\div5=\dfrac{5}{12}\times\dfrac{1}{5}=\dfrac{1}{12}$ (m) long.

**Answer:** $\dfrac{1}{12}$ m.

**Look back.** Check by multiplying: $5\times\dfrac{1}{12}=\dfrac{5}{12}$ and $\dfrac{5}{12}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{5}{12}+\dfrac{4}{12}=\dfrac{9}{12}=\dfrac{3}{4}$, exactly the length of the roll.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Adding the numerators and the denominators.** Wrong: $\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{5}{7}$. Why it is wrong: $\dfrac{5}{7}$ is less than $1$, but $\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{3}{4}$ is greater than $1$. Right: $\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{17}{12}$.
- **Dividing without inverting.** Wrong: $\dfrac{5}{6}\div\dfrac{5}{12}=\dfrac{5}{6}\times\dfrac{5}{12}=\dfrac{25}{72}$. Why it is wrong: dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal. Right: $\dfrac{5}{6}\times\dfrac{12}{5}=2$.
- **Using the wrong order of operations.** Wrong: $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}\times\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{5}{6}\times\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{5}{8}$. Why it is wrong: without brackets, multiplication must be done before addition. Right: $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{3}{4}$.
:::

## Summary

- Adding and subtracting fractions: same denominator, work on the numerators; different denominators, write over a common denominator first.
- Multiplying fractions: numerator times numerator, denominator times denominator.
- Dividing fractions: multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor.
- Order of operations: brackets, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction.
- Find unknown numbers with the inverse operation; always simplify and check your answer.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out $\dfrac{3}{10}+\dfrac{4}{15}$.

::hint
**What you need:** with different denominators, first write over a common denominator.

**Steps:**
1. The smaller common denominator of $10$ and $15$ is $30$.
2. Rewrite, then add the numerators.

::solution
$$\dfrac{3}{10}+\dfrac{4}{15}=\dfrac{9}{30}+\dfrac{8}{30}=\dfrac{17}{30}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{17}{30}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $\dfrac{7}{8}-\dfrac{1}{6}$.

::hint
**What you need:** write over a common denominator and then subtract.

**Steps:**
1. The smaller common denominator of $8$ and $6$ is $24$.
2. Rewrite, then subtract the numerators.

::solution
$$\dfrac{7}{8}-\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{21}{24}-\dfrac{4}{24}=\dfrac{17}{24}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{17}{24}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $\dfrac{5}{6}\times\dfrac{9}{10}$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply numerator by numerator and denominator by denominator, then simplify.

**Steps:**
1. Multiply the two numerators and the two denominators.
2. Simplify the answer.

::solution
$$\dfrac{5}{6}\times\dfrac{9}{10}=\dfrac{45}{60}=\dfrac{3}{4}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{3}{4}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out $\dfrac{4}{9}\div\dfrac{2}{3}$.

::hint
**What you need:** dividing by a fraction is multiplying by its reciprocal.

**Steps:**
1. What is the reciprocal of $\dfrac{2}{3}$?
2. Multiply, then simplify.

::solution
$$\dfrac{4}{9}\div\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{4}{9}\times\dfrac{3}{2}=\dfrac{12}{18}=\dfrac{2}{3}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{2}{3}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out the value of the expression $\dfrac{3}{4}-\dfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{1}{3}$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply first, then subtract.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $\dfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{1}{3}$.
2. Then subtract.

::solution
$$\dfrac{3}{4}-\dfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{3}{4}-\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{9}{12}-\dfrac{2}{12}=\dfrac{7}{12}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{7}{12}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out the value of the expression $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{1}{6}\right)\div\dfrac{5}{12}$.

::hint
**What you need:** do the brackets first.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the sum in the brackets.
2. Divide the result by $\dfrac{5}{12}$.

::solution
$$\left(\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{1}{6}\right)\div\dfrac{5}{12}=\dfrac{5}{6}\div\dfrac{5}{12}=\dfrac{5}{6}\times\dfrac{12}{5}=2.$$

**Answer.** $2$.
:::

:::exercise
A water tank is $\dfrac{3}{5}$ full. $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of a tank of water is used, and then $\dfrac{1}{10}$ of a tank of water is poured in. What fraction of the tank is now full?

::hint
**What you need:** "used" means subtract, "poured in" means add; write over a common denominator when needed.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Subtract $\dfrac{1}{4}$, then add $\dfrac{1}{10}$.

::solution
After the water is used, the water left is $\dfrac{3}{5}-\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{12}{20}-\dfrac{5}{20}=\dfrac{7}{20}$ (of the tank).

After more water is poured in, the water in the tank is $\dfrac{7}{20}+\dfrac{1}{10}=\dfrac{7}{20}+\dfrac{2}{20}=\dfrac{9}{20}$ (of the tank).

**Answer.** $\dfrac{9}{20}$ of the tank.
:::

:::exercise
A length of cloth is $\dfrac{5}{6}$ m long. Each scarf needs $\dfrac{1}{12}$ m of cloth. What is the greatest number of scarves that can be made?

::hint
**What you need:** the number of scarves is the metres of cloth divided by the metres needed for each.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Work out $\dfrac{5}{6}\div\dfrac{1}{12}$.

::solution
The number of scarves that can be made is:
$$\dfrac{5}{6}\div\dfrac{1}{12}=\dfrac{5}{6}\times12=\dfrac{60}{6}=10\ \text{(scarves)}.$$

**Check.** $10\times\dfrac{1}{12}=\dfrac{10}{12}=\dfrac{5}{6}$. Correct.

**Answer.** $10$ scarves.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on finding a fraction of an amount. A class has $32$ students, of whom $\dfrac{3}{8}$ like swimming and $\dfrac{1}{4}$ like badminton. How many more students like swimming than like badminton?

::hint
**What you need:** $\dfrac{a}{b}$ of $n$ is $n\div b\times a$.

**Steps:**
1. Find the number who like swimming and the number who like badminton.
2. Subtract the smaller number from the larger.

::solution
The number who like swimming is $32\div8\times3=12$ (students).

The number who like badminton is $32\div4=8$ (students).

The number who like swimming exceeds the number who like badminton by $12-8=4$ (students).

**Answer.** $4$ students.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Calculate quickly: $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{6}+\dfrac{1}{12}+\dfrac{1}{20}$.

::hint
**What you need:** $\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{1}{1\times2}$, $\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{1}{2\times3}$, ... and $\dfrac{1}{n\times(n+1)}=\dfrac{1}{n}-\dfrac{1}{n+1}$.

**Steps:**
1. Write each fraction as a difference of two fractions.
2. Add them up; the middle terms will cancel.

::solution
We have $\dfrac{1}{2}=1-\dfrac{1}{2}$, $\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{3}$, $\dfrac{1}{12}=\dfrac{1}{3}-\dfrac{1}{4}$, $\dfrac{1}{20}=\dfrac{1}{4}-\dfrac{1}{5}$.

Adding: $1-\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{3}-\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{1}{4}-\dfrac{1}{5}=1-\dfrac{1}{5}=\dfrac{4}{5}$.

**Check.** $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{2}{3}$; $\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{1}{12}=\dfrac{3}{4}$; $\dfrac{3}{4}+\dfrac{1}{20}=\dfrac{4}{5}$. Correct.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{4}{5}$.
:::
