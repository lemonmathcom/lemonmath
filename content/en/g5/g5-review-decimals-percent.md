:::goals
- Remember how to compare and round decimals and how to use the four operations with them.
- Switch between fractions, decimals and percentages.
- Solve the three basic percentage problems, including discounts and price changes.
:::

A bookshop gives a $15\%$ discount on a storybook that costs $80\,000$ dong. Lan has $70\,000$ dong. Does she have enough money to buy the book?

To decide, you need both decimals and percentages. This lesson reviews them and shows how closely they are connected.

## Let's explore

Lan's class has $40$ pupils, and $6$ of them wear glasses. We can write the part of the class that wears glasses in three ways:

- as a fraction: $\dfrac{6}{40}=\dfrac{3}{20}=\dfrac{15}{100}$;
- as a decimal: $\dfrac{15}{100}=0.15$;
- as a percentage: $\dfrac{15}{100}=15\%$.

So $15\%$ means $15$ parts out of $100$ equal parts. All three ways of writing it have the same value. To change a fraction into a percentage, divide the numerator by the denominator and multiply by $100$: $\dfrac{3}{4}=0.75=75\%$.

:::theorem The three basic percentage problems
1. **Find what percentage $a$ is of $b$:** work out $a\div b$, multiply by $100$ and write $\%$.
2. **Find $a\%$ of $b$:** work out $b\times a\div100$.
3. **Find the whole when $a\%$ of it is $c$:** work out $c\div a\times100$.
:::

Method 2 works because $a\%=\dfrac{a}{100}$, so $a\%$ of $b$ is $b\times\dfrac{a}{100}$. Method 3 goes backwards: $a$ parts are worth $c$, so $1$ part is worth $c\div a$ and $100$ parts are worth $c\div a\times100$.

:::note Reminder: decimals
- **Comparing:** compare the whole-number parts first; if they are equal, compare the tenths, then the hundredths, then the thousandths. Writing a $0$ at the end of the decimal part does not change the value: $4.6=4.60$.
- **Rounding:** look at the digit just after the place you are rounding to. If it is $5$ or more, add $1$ to the digit in that place; if it is less than $5$, leave it.
- **Multiplying or dividing by $10$, $100$, $1\,000$:** move the decimal point right (multiply) or left (divide) by $1$, $2$, $3$ places.
- **Dividing by a decimal:** multiply both numbers by $10$, $100$, $\dots$ until the divisor is a whole number. The quotient does not change.
:::

## Examples

We start with decimals: comparing and rounding.

:::example Sorting and rounding
Put the numbers $5.08$, $5.8$, $5.075$ and $4.99$ in order, from smallest to largest. Then round $5.075$ to the nearest tenth.

**Solution.** Compare the whole-number parts first, then each place in turn. Write the numbers with the same three decimal places:
$$4.990,\quad 5.080,\quad 5.800,\quad 5.075.$$
The number $4.990$ has the smallest whole-number part. Among the other three, the tenths digits are $0$, $8$, $0$, so $5.800$ is the largest. The other two have hundredths digits $8$ and $7$, so $5.075<5.080$. Therefore
$$4.99<5.075<5.08<5.8.$$
To round $5.075$ to the nearest tenth, look at the hundredths digit: $7$ is more than $5$, so add $1$ to the tenths digit. So $5.075\approx5.1$.
:::

Next, the four operations with decimals in a real situation.

:::example Cutting cloth
A roll of cloth is $12.6$ m long. A shopkeeper cuts $4.75$ m, then $3.8$ m. The rest is cut into pieces $0.45$ m long. How many pieces are there?

**Solution.** First find the cloth that is left, by subtraction (in metres):
$$12.6-4.75-3.8=7.85-3.8=4.05.$$
To divide $4.05$ by $0.45$, multiply both numbers by $100$ so that the divisor is a whole number:
$$4.05\div0.45=405\div45=9.$$
So there are $9$ pieces of cloth.

**Check.** $0.45\times9=4.05$. Correct.
:::

Now to percentages. We start with problem type 1: what percentage one number is of another.

:::example Trees that survived
Mr Hung planted $250$ trees and $215$ of them survived. What percentage of the trees survived? What percentage did not survive?

**Solution.** Divide the number of surviving trees by the number planted, then multiply by $100$:
$$215\div250=0.86=86\%.$$
All the trees planted make $100\%$, so the percentage that did not survive is
$$100\%-86\%=14\%.$$
So $86\%$ of the trees survived and $14\%$ did not.
:::

Back to the opening question, which is type 2: finding a percentage of a number.

:::example A discounted storybook
A storybook costs $80\,000$ dong and has a $15\%$ discount. Lan has $70\,000$ dong. Does she have enough money?

**Solution.** First find the discount, which is $15\%$ of $80\,000$ dong:
$$80\,000\times15\div100=12\,000.$$
The price after the discount is:
$$80\,000-12\,000=68\,000.$$
Since $68\,000<70\,000$, Lan has enough money, with $2\,000$ dong left over.
:::

Finally, type 3: knowing a percentage of a number, find the number.

:::example Rice in a warehouse
A warehouse has sold $360$ kg of rice, which is $45\%$ of the rice it had at first. How many kilograms of rice did it have at first?

**Solution.** $45\%$ of the rice is $360$ kg, so $1\%$ is $360\div45=8$ (kg). The whole stock is $100\%$:
$$360\div45\times100=8\times100=800.$$
So the warehouse had $800$ kg of rice at first.

**Check.** $800\times45\div100=360$. Correct.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Comparing decimals like whole numbers.** Wrong: $2.35>2.4$ because $35>4$. Why it is wrong: the tenths digit of $2.35$ is $3$, which is less than $4$. Right: $2.35<2.4$.
- **Forgetting to divide by $100$.** Wrong: $15\%$ of $80\,000$ is $80\,000\times15=1\,200\,000$. Why it is wrong: $15\%$ is only $\dfrac{15}{100}$. Right: $80\,000\times15\div100=12\,000$.
- **Using the wrong whole.** Wrong: the price after a $20\%$ discount is $240\,000$ dong, so the original price is $240\,000+20\%\times240\,000=288\,000$ dong. Why it is wrong: $20\%$ is of the original price, not of the reduced price. Right: $240\,000$ dong is $80\%$ of the original price, so the original price is $240\,000\div80\times100=300\,000$ dong.
:::

## Summary

- To compare decimals, compare the whole-number parts first, then each place from left to right. To round, look at the digit just after the place you round to.
- To divide by a decimal, multiply both numbers by $10$, $100$, $\dots$ until the divisor is a whole number.
- $\dfrac{15}{100}=0.15=15\%$: switch between them by dividing or multiplying by $100$.
- Type 1: $(a\div b)\times100\%$. Type 2: $a\%$ of $b$ is $b\times a\div100$. Type 3: the whole with $a\%$ equal to $c$ is $c\div a\times100$.
- After an $a\%$ discount the price is $(100-a)\%$ of the old price; after an $a\%$ increase it is $(100+a)\%$ of the old price. Each change is a percentage of the price just before it.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Write each fraction as a decimal and as a percentage: a) $\dfrac{3}{4}$; b) $\dfrac{7}{20}$.

::hint
**What you need:** dividing the numerator by the denominator gives a decimal; multiplying a decimal by $100$ gives a percentage.

**Steps:**
1. a) Work out $3\div4$. b) Write $\dfrac{7}{20}$ as a fraction with denominator $100$.
2. Write the result as a percentage.

::solution
a) $3\div4=0.75=75\%$. b) $\dfrac{7}{20}=\dfrac{35}{100}=0.35=35\%$.

**Answer.** a) $0.75=75\%$; b) $0.35=35\%$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out: a) $25.4-8.75$; b) $4.8\times0.25$; c) $9.6\div0.8$.

::hint
**What you need:** when subtracting, line up the decimal points. When multiplying, multiply as whole numbers, then count the decimal places. When dividing, multiply both numbers by $10$ so the divisor is a whole number.

**Steps:**
1. a) Write $25.4=25.40$, then subtract.
2. b) Work out $48\times25$, then put in $3$ decimal places.
3. c) Change it to $96\div8$.

::solution
a) $25.40-8.75=16.65$.
b) $48\times25=1\,200$, and there are $1+2=3$ decimal places, so $4.8\times0.25=1.2$.
c) $9.6\div0.8=96\div8=12$.

**Answer.** a) $16.65$; b) $1.2$; c) $12$.
:::

:::exercise
Write $>$, $<$ or $=$ in the box: a) $7.305\;\square\;7.35$; b) $0.6\;\square\;65\%$; c) $\dfrac{2}{5}\;\square\;40\%$.

::hint
**What you need:** write the numbers in the same form (as decimals), then compare place by place.

**Steps:**
1. a) Compare the tenths, then the hundredths.
2. b), c) Change the percentage and the fraction into decimals.

::solution
a) Both have tenths digit $3$; the hundredths digits are $0$ and $5$, so $7.305<7.35$.
b) $65\%=0.65$ and $0.6=0.60$, so $0.6<65\%$.
c) $\dfrac{2}{5}=2\div5=0.4=40\%$, so the two are equal.

**Answer.** a) $<$; b) $<$; c) $=$.
:::

:::exercise
Lan runs $1.2$ km and Mai runs $1.5$ km. What percentage of Mai's distance is Lan's distance?

::hint
**What you need:** $a$ as a percentage of $b$ is $a\div b\times100\%$.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? Two distances. What is asked? Lan's distance compared with Mai's.
2. Divide Lan's distance by Mai's distance, then change to a percentage.

::solution
$1.2\div1.5=12\div15=0.8=80\%$.

**Answer.** $80\%$.
:::

:::exercise
A school has $1\,250$ pupils, and $48\%$ of them are girls. How many boys are there?

::hint
**What you need:** $a\%$ of $b$ is $b\times a\div100$.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the number of girls.
2. Subtract the number of girls from the number of pupils in the school.

::solution
The number of girls is $1\,250\times48\div100=600$.
The number of boys is $1\,250-600=650$.

**Answer.** $650$ boys.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the last lesson on fractions. A water tank is used like this: in the morning $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of the original water is used, and in the afternoon $35\%$ of the original water is used. Now $100$ litres are left. How many litres were in the tank at first?

::hint
**What you need:** $\dfrac{2}{5}=0.4=40\%$; the whole tank is $100\%$.

**Steps:**
1. Change $\dfrac{2}{5}$ into a percentage.
2. Work out the percentage used, then the percentage left.
3. $100$ litres is the percentage left: use problem type 3.

::solution
Since $\dfrac{2}{5}=40\%$, the amount used is $40\%+35\%=75\%$, so $100\%-75\%=25\%$ is left.
$25\%$ of the water is $100$ litres, so at first there were $100\div25\times100=400$ litres.

**Check.** $\dfrac{2}{5}\times400=160$ and $35\%\times400=140$; $400-160-140=100$. Correct.

**Answer.** $400$ litres.
:::

:::exercise
A pair of shoes has a $25\%$ discount, and the price after the discount is $150\,000$ dong. a) What was the original price, in dong? b) How much was the discount, in dong?

::hint
**What you need:** the price after a $25\%$ discount is $(100-25)\%=75\%$ of the original price.

**Steps:**
1. $150\,000$ dong is what percentage of the original price?
2. Use problem type 3 to find the original price.
3. Subtract the reduced price from the original price.

::solution
a) $150\,000$ dong is $75\%$ of the original price, so the original price is $150\,000\div75\times100=200\,000$ dong.
b) The discount is $200\,000-150\,000=50\,000$ dong. This is also $25\%$ of $200\,000$.

**Answer.** a) $200\,000$ dong; b) $50\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
A shirt costs $500\,000$ dong. Last month the shop raised the price by $10\%$. This month the shop lowers the price by $10\%$ of the raised price. What is the price now, in dong? Is it higher or lower than the original price, and by what percentage of the original price?

::hint
**What you need:** each time the price goes up or down, the percentage is of the price just before that change.

**Steps:**
1. The price after a $10\%$ increase is $110\%$ of the old price.
2. The price after the decrease is $90\%$ of the raised price, not of the original price.
3. Compare with $500\,000$ dong, then write the difference as a percentage of the original price.

::solution
The price after the increase is $500\,000\times110\div100=550\,000$ dong.
The price now is $550\,000\times90\div100=495\,000$ dong.
It is lower than the original price by $500\,000-495\,000=5\,000$ dong, and $5\,000\div500\,000=0.01=1\%$.

**Answer.** The price now is $495\,000$ dong, which is $1\%$ lower than the original price.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
The largest of the numbers $4.62$, $4.6$, $4.602$ and $4.26$ is
A. $4.26$.
B. $4.6$.
C. $4.602$.
D. $4.62$.
::key D
Write them with three decimal places: $4.620$, $4.600$, $4.602$, $4.260$. Option C is the mistake of treating $602$ as bigger than $62$, as with whole numbers.

::mc
$25\%$ of $360$ is
A. $90$.
B. $14.4$.
C. $9\,000$.
D. $1\,440$.
::key A
$360\times25\div100=90$. Option B is $360\div25$, option C forgets to divide by $100$, and option D treats $360$ as $25\%$ of a number.

::mc
$6.4\div0.08$ equals
A. $0.8$.
B. $80$.
C. $8$.
D. $800$.
::key B
Multiply both numbers by $100$: $6.4\div0.08=640\div8=80$. The other options come from moving the decimal points by different amounts in the two numbers.

::mc
$30\%$ of a number is $45$. The number is
A. $13.5$.
B. $1\,350$.
C. $150$.
D. $75$.
::key C
$45\div30\times100=150$. Option A is the mistake of finding $30\%$ of $45$, option B is $45\times30$, and option D is $45+30$.

::tf
Class 5A has $40$ pupils, and $18$ of them are girls.
a) Girls make up $45\%$ of the class.
b) Boys make up $22\%$ of the class.
c) There are more boys than girls, by an amount equal to $10\%$ of the class.
d) The number of girls equals $0.45$ of the number of boys.
::key T F T F
a) True: $18\div40=0.45=45\%$. b) False: there are $40-18=22$ boys and $22\div40=0.55=55\%$. c) True: $22-18=4$ and $10\%\times40=4$. d) False: $0.45$ compares with the whole class; compared with the number of boys we need $18\div22$.

::sa
Write $\dfrac{3}{8}$ as a percentage. Fill in the box: $\square\%$.
::key 37.5
$\dfrac{3}{8}=3\div8=0.375=37.5\%$.

::sa
A shirt costs $250$ thousand dong and has a $12\%$ discount. How many thousand dong does it cost after the discount?
::key 220
The discount is $250\times12\div100=30$ (thousand dong), so the new price is $250-30=220$ (thousand dong).
:::
