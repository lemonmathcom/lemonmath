- Write two fractions with different denominators over a common denominator.
- Add and subtract two fractions with different denominators, then simplify the answer.
- Work out expressions with three fractions.
- Solve word problems using the addition and subtraction of fractions.
:::

A cake is shared between two friends. Mai eats $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of the cake and Nam eats $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of the cake. What fraction of the cake do they eat altogether?

The two pieces are different sizes, so you cannot put them together yet. This lesson shows you how to turn them into pieces of equal size.

## Let's explore

Cut the cake into $12$ equal pieces, because $12$ is divisible by both $3$ and $4$.

- $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of the cake is $4$ pieces out of $12$, which is $\dfrac{4}{12}$.
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of the cake is $3$ pieces out of $12$, which is $\dfrac{3}{12}$.
- Together they eat $4+3=7$ pieces, which is $\dfrac{7}{12}$ of the cake.

So $\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{4}{12}+\dfrac{3}{12}=\dfrac{7}{12}$.

:::theorem Adding and subtracting fractions with different denominators
To add (or subtract) two fractions with different denominators:
1. Write both fractions over a common denominator.
2. Add (or subtract) the numerators and keep the common denominator.
3. Simplify the answer if possible.
:::

Why does this work? If you cut each part of a fraction into $k$ smaller equal parts, the numerator and the denominator are both multiplied by $k$, and the value of the fraction does not change. Once the denominators match, all the parts are the same size, so we add and subtract the number of parts just as we do with whole numbers.

**Connection.** This method uses the common denominators and simplifying you already know. Later it will help you multiply and divide fractions and work with decimals, because $\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{25}{100}$.

## Examples

We start with two fractions that have small denominators.

:::example Work out $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}$
**Solution.** A common denominator is $6$, because $6$ is divisible by $2$ and by $3$:
$$\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{3}{6}+\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{5}{6}.$$
So $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{5}{6}$.
:::

Now a subtraction, with common denominator $12$.

:::example Work out $\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{1}{4}$
**Solution.** A common denominator is $12$. We rewrite both fractions, then subtract the numerators:
$$\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{10}{12}-\dfrac{3}{12}=\dfrac{7}{12}.$$
So $\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{7}{12}$.
:::

When one denominator is divisible by the other, we only need to change one fraction, and we must remember to simplify.

:::example Work out $\dfrac{3}{4}-\dfrac{5}{12}$
**Solution.** Since $12$ is divisible by $4$, we take $12$ as the common denominator and change only $\dfrac{3}{4}$:
$$\dfrac{3}{4}-\dfrac{5}{12}=\dfrac{9}{12}-\dfrac{5}{12}=\dfrac{4}{12}=\dfrac{1}{3}.$$
So $\dfrac{3}{4}-\dfrac{5}{12}=\dfrac{1}{3}$.
:::

With three fractions, work from left to right, or write all three over a common denominator at once.

:::example Work out $\dfrac{7}{10}+\dfrac{1}{4}-\dfrac{2}{5}$
**Solution.** A common denominator of $10$, $4$ and $5$ is $20$:
$$\dfrac{7}{10}+\dfrac{1}{4}-\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{14}{20}+\dfrac{5}{20}-\dfrac{8}{20}=\dfrac{11}{20}.$$
So the result is $\dfrac{11}{20}$.
:::

Finally, a real-life problem, solved in four steps.

:::example Mrs Hoa's garden
Suppose $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of Mrs Hoa's garden is used for vegetables, $\dfrac{1}{3}$ is used for flowers, and the rest is a path. What fraction of the garden is the path?

**Understand.** We know the fractions for vegetables and flowers. We need the path, and the whole garden is $1$.

**Plan.** Find the fraction that is planted (addition), then subtract it from $1$.

**Solve.** The fraction planted with vegetables and flowers is:
$$\dfrac{2}{5}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{6}{15}+\dfrac{5}{15}=\dfrac{11}{15}\ \text{(of the garden)}.$$
The fraction that is the path is:
$$1-\dfrac{11}{15}=\dfrac{15}{15}-\dfrac{11}{15}=\dfrac{4}{15}\ \text{(of the garden)}.$$
So the path is $\dfrac{4}{15}$ of the garden.

**Look back.** Is there another way? Yes: $1-\dfrac{2}{5}-\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{15}{15}-\dfrac{6}{15}-\dfrac{5}{15}=\dfrac{4}{15}$, the same answer. Is it reasonable? Yes, because the planted part and the path must make the whole garden: $\dfrac{11}{15}+\dfrac{4}{15}=1$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Adding numerators and adding denominators.** Wrong: $\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{2}{7}$. Why it is wrong: $\dfrac{2}{7}$ is smaller than $\dfrac{1}{3}$, but a sum cannot be smaller than one of its addends. Right: $\dfrac{4}{12}+\dfrac{3}{12}=\dfrac{7}{12}$.
- **Changing only the denominator.** Wrong: $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{1}{6}+\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{2}{6}$. Why it is wrong: changing the denominator to $6$ and keeping the numerator changes the value of the fraction. Right: $\dfrac{3}{6}+\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{5}{6}$.
- **Forgetting to simplify.** Wrong: $\dfrac{3}{4}-\dfrac{5}{12}=\dfrac{4}{12}$ (not in its simplest form). Right: $\dfrac{4}{12}=\dfrac{1}{3}$.
:::

## Summary

- Fractions with different denominators must be **written over a common denominator** before you add or subtract.
- After that, add (subtract) the two numerators and **keep the common denominator**.
- Finally, simplify the answer to its simplest form.
- The part that is left of something equals $1$ minus the part that was used.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Work out: $\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{2}{3}$.

::hint
**What you need:** write both fractions over a common denominator, then add the numerators.

**Steps:**
1. Find a common denominator of $4$ and $3$.
2. Rewrite both fractions, then add.

::solution
$$\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{3}{12}+\dfrac{8}{12}=\dfrac{11}{12}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{11}{12}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out: $\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{3}{8}$.

::hint
**What you need:** a common denominator is a number divisible by both denominators.

**Steps:**
1. What is the smallest common denominator of $6$ and $8$?
2. Rewrite both fractions, then subtract.

::solution
A common denominator is $24$:
$$\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{3}{8}=\dfrac{20}{24}-\dfrac{9}{24}=\dfrac{11}{24}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{11}{24}$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out and simplify the answer: $\dfrac{3}{4}+\dfrac{1}{12}$.

::hint
**What you need:** $12$ is divisible by $4$, so you only need to change one fraction.

**Steps:**
1. Rewrite $\dfrac{3}{4}$ with denominator $12$.
2. Add, then simplify.

::solution
$$\dfrac{3}{4}+\dfrac{1}{12}=\dfrac{9}{12}+\dfrac{1}{12}=\dfrac{10}{12}=\dfrac{5}{6}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{5}{6}$.
:::

:::exercise
Find the missing number: $\dfrac{1}{2}+\ ?=\dfrac{5}{6}$.

::hint
**What you need:** to find a missing addend, subtract the known addend from the sum.

**Steps:**
1. Write the calculation $\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{1}{2}$.
2. Write over a common denominator, subtract, then simplify.

::solution
$$\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{3}{6}=\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{1}{3}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{1}{3}$.
:::

:::exercise
In the morning Lan drinks $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of a bottle of water, and at lunchtime she drinks $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of the bottle. What fraction of the bottle has she drunk? What fraction is left?

::hint
**What you need:** "drunk in both parts of the day" means putting together, and the part left is $1$ minus the part drunk.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked? Add the two parts she drank.
2. Subtract the result from $1$. Check: the part drunk plus the part left must make $1$.

::solution
The fraction of the bottle Lan has drunk is:
$$\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{5}{20}+\dfrac{8}{20}=\dfrac{13}{20}\ \text{(of the bottle)}.$$
The fraction of the bottle left is:
$$1-\dfrac{13}{20}=\dfrac{7}{20}\ \text{(of the bottle)}.$$

**Answer.** She has drunk $\dfrac{13}{20}$ of the bottle, and $\dfrac{7}{20}$ is left.
:::

:::exercise
A piece of string is $\dfrac{7}{8}$ m long. The teacher cuts off $\dfrac{1}{3}$ m to make a skipping rope. How many metres of string are left?

::hint
**What you need:** "cuts off" means taking away: use subtraction.

**Steps:**
1. What do we know? What is asked?
2. Write $\dfrac{7}{8}$ and $\dfrac{1}{3}$ over a common denominator, then subtract. Remember the unit.

::solution
The length of string left is:
$$\dfrac{7}{8}-\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{21}{24}-\dfrac{8}{24}=\dfrac{13}{24}\ \text{(m)}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{13}{24}$ m.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the lesson on comparing fractions. Compare $\dfrac{5}{8}$ and $\dfrac{7}{12}$, then work out by how much the larger fraction is greater than the smaller one.

::hint
**What you need:** with a common denominator you compare the numerators; the difference of two fractions is a subtraction.

**Steps:**
1. Write both fractions over the same denominator.
2. Compare the numerators, then subtract the smaller fraction from the larger one.

::solution
A common denominator is $24$: $\dfrac{5}{8}=\dfrac{15}{24}$ and $\dfrac{7}{12}=\dfrac{14}{24}$.

Since $15>14$, we have $\dfrac{5}{8}>\dfrac{7}{12}$. The difference is $\dfrac{15}{24}-\dfrac{14}{24}=\dfrac{1}{24}$.

**Answer.** $\dfrac{5}{8}>\dfrac{7}{12}$, and the difference is $\dfrac{1}{24}$.
:::

:::exercise
Suppose a plot of land uses $\dfrac{3}{10}$ of its area for a house and $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of its area for a pond. The rest is planted with trees. What fraction of the plot is planted with trees?

::hint
**What you need:** the whole plot is $1$; the part with trees is what is left.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the fraction used for the house and the pond.
2. Subtract that fraction from $1$.

::solution
The fraction used for the house and the pond is:
$$\dfrac{3}{10}+\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{6}{20}+\dfrac{5}{20}=\dfrac{11}{20}\ \text{(of the plot)}.$$
The fraction planted with trees is:
$$1-\dfrac{11}{20}=\dfrac{9}{20}\ \text{(of the plot)}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{9}{20}$ of the plot.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Work out quickly: $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{6}+\dfrac{1}{12}+\dfrac{1}{20}+\dfrac{1}{30}$.

::hint
**What you need:** $\dfrac{1}{2}=1-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{3}$.

**Steps:**
1. Write $\dfrac{1}{12}$, $\dfrac{1}{20}$ and $\dfrac{1}{30}$ as a difference of two fractions in the same way.
2. Look at which fractions cancel each other.

::solution
Since $2=1\times2$, $6=2\times3$, $12=3\times4$, $20=4\times5$ and $30=5\times6$, we have:
$$\left(1-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)+\left(\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{3}\right)+\left(\dfrac{1}{3}-\dfrac{1}{4}\right)+\left(\dfrac{1}{4}-\dfrac{1}{5}\right)+\left(\dfrac{1}{5}-\dfrac{1}{6}\right)=1-\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{5}{6}.$$

**Answer.** $\dfrac{5}{6}$.
:::
