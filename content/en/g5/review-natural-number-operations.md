:::goals
- Add, subtract, multiply and divide natural numbers with many digits, and check the answer.
- Follow the correct order of operations, including brackets.
- Use the commutative, associative and distributive laws to calculate quickly.
- Solve multi-step word problems and write them as one expression.
:::

Class 5A collects old books for the school library. In the first week the class collects $235$ books, and in the second week $179$ books. The librarian shares all the books equally between $9$ boxes. How many books are in each box?

This problem needs both addition and division. You will review the four operations and learn to join the steps into one expression.

## Let's explore

Solve it one step at a time:

- Books in the two weeks: $235+179=414$.
- Books in each box: $414\div9=46$.

When you join the two steps, you must put in **brackets** so that the addition is done first:
$$(235+179)\div9=414\div9=46.$$

Without brackets, $235+179\div9$ would work out $179\div9$ first, and the answer would be wrong. You see: the order of the operations decides the answer.

:::theorem The order of operations
1. Only addition and subtraction (or only multiplication and division): work from left to right.
2. All four operations and no brackets: **multiply and divide first; add and subtract after**.
3. Brackets: work out **the brackets first**.
:::

:::theorem Three laws of arithmetic
- Commutative: $a+b=b+a$ and $a\times b=b\times a$.
- Associative: $(a+b)+c=a+(b+c)$ and $(a\times b)\times c=a\times(b\times c)$.
- Distributive: $a\times(b+c)=a\times b+a\times c$.
:::

## Examples

We start with adding and subtracting.

:::example Adding and subtracting large numbers
Work out a) $4\,586+3\,729$; b) $7\,002-3\,458$.

**Solution.** Set out the sum in columns and work from the ones; carry when adding, exchange when subtracting:
$$\begin{array}{r}4\,586\\+\ 3\,729\\\hline 8\,315\end{array}\qquad\qquad\begin{array}{r}7\,002\\-\ 3\,458\\\hline 3\,544\end{array}$$
In part b, the tens and hundreds digits are both $0$, so you exchange from the thousands.

So $4\,586+3\,729=8\,315$ and $7\,002-3\,458=3\,544$.

**Check.** $3\,544+3\,458=7\,002$. Correct.
:::

Next come multiplication and division.

:::example Multiplying and dividing
Work out a) $537\times48$; b) $2\,184\div7$.

**Solution.** Part a: multiply $537$ by $8$ and then by $40$, and add the two partial products:
$$537\times48=537\times8+537\times40=4\,296+21\,480=25\,776.$$
Part b: divide from the left: $21\div7=3$; bring down $8$, $8\div7=1$ remainder $1$; bring down $4$ to make $14$, $14\div7=2$. So $2\,184\div7=312$.

**Check.** $312\times7=2\,184$. Correct.
:::

With many operations, you must watch the order.

:::example Working out an expression
Work out a) $84-12\times5+36\div4$; b) $120\div(18-6)\times4$.

**Solution.** Part a has no brackets, so multiply and divide first:
$$\begin{aligned}84-12\times5+36\div4&=84-60+9\\&=24+9\\&=33.\end{aligned}$$
Part b: brackets first, then divide and multiply from left to right:
$$\begin{aligned}120\div(18-6)\times4&=120\div12\times4\\&=10\times4\\&=40.\end{aligned}$$
So the two expressions have values $33$ and $40$.
:::

When the numbers are "friendly", use the laws to calculate quickly.

:::example Quick calculation
Work out a) $25\times37\times4$; b) $6\times43+6\times57$.

**Solution.** Part a: swap $37$ and $4$, then group $25\times4=100$:
$$25\times37\times4=(25\times4)\times37=100\times37=3\,700.$$
Part b: the two products share the factor $6$, so take $6$ out as a common factor:
$$6\times43+6\times57=6\times(43+57)=6\times100=600.$$
So the answers are $3\,700$ and $600$.
:::

:::warning Mistakes to watch out for
- **Going left to right when there is multiplication.** Wrong: $18+6\times4=24\times4=96$. Why it is wrong: multiply before you add. Right: $18+6\times4=18+24=42$.
- **Forgetting to exchange when there is a $0$ digit.** Wrong: $7\,002-3\,458=4\,456$. Why it is wrong: you subtracted the smaller digit from the larger in each column. Right: $7\,002-3\,458=3\,544$.
- **Forgetting a $0$ in the quotient.** Wrong: $1\,260\div6=21$. Why it is wrong: $6\times21=126$, not $1\,260$. Right: $1\,260\div6=210$.
:::

## Summary

- Set out sums in columns, work from the ones, and check with the inverse operation.
- Without brackets: multiply and divide first, then add and subtract. With brackets: brackets first.
- The commutative, associative and distributive laws make calculating quicker, for example $25\times37\times4=(25\times4)\times37$.
- In a multi-step problem, work one step at a time and give the unit. If a division leaves a remainder but everyone still needs a place, you need one more group.

## Practice

Each exercise comes with what you need and how to start. Try it yourself first, then open the solution.

:::exercise
Set out in columns and work out: a) $5\,274+3\,968$; b) $6\,005-2\,847$.

::hint
**What you need:** line up the digits; carry when adding, exchange when subtracting.

**Steps:**
1. Set out the sum and work from the ones.
2. Check with the inverse operation.

::solution
a) $5\,274+3\,968=9\,242$.

b) $6\,005-2\,847=3\,158$ (check: $3\,158+2\,847=6\,005$).

**Answer.** a) $9\,242$; b) $3\,158$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out: a) $406\times25$; b) $3\,150\div15$.

::hint
**What you need:** $25=20+5$; if a digit cannot be divided, write $0$ in the quotient.

**Steps:**
1. Part a: work out $406\times20$ and $406\times5$, then add.
2. Part b: divide one digit at a time and watch the $0$.

::solution
a) $406\times25=8\,120+2\,030=10\,150$.

b) $3\,150\div15=210$, because $15\times210=3\,150$.

**Answer.** a) $10\,150$; b) $210$.
:::

:::exercise
Work out the value of: a) $56+24\div6\times5$; b) $90-(18+12)\times2$.

::hint
**What you need:** multiply and divide before adding and subtracting; brackets come first.

**Steps:**
1. Part a: work out $24\div6\times5$ from left to right first.
2. Part b: work out the brackets first.

::solution
a) $56+24\div6\times5=56+4\times5=56+20=76$.

b) $90-(18+12)\times2=90-30\times2=90-60=30$.

**Answer.** a) $76$; b) $30$.
:::

:::exercise
Calculate quickly: a) $4\times18\times25$; b) $75\times34+75\times66$.

::hint
**What you need:** group $4\times25=100$; take the common factor out.

**Steps:**
1. Part a: swap the numbers so that $4$ and $25$ are side by side.
2. Part b: write $75\times(34+66)$.

::solution
a) $4\times18\times25=(4\times25)\times18=100\times18=1\,800$.

b) $75\times34+75\times66=75\times(34+66)=75\times100=7\,500$.

**Answer.** a) $1\,800$; b) $7\,500$.
:::

:::exercise
Mum buys $3$ cartons of milk at $38\,000$ dong each and $2$ bags of biscuits at $25\,000$ dong each. She gives the cashier $200\,000$ dong. How much change does she get?

::hint
**What you need:** change is the money given minus the total cost.

**Steps:**
1. Work out the cost of the milk and of the biscuits.
2. Subtract the total from $200\,000$.

::solution
Cost of the milk: $3\times38\,000=114\,000$ dong.

Cost of the biscuits: $2\times25\,000=50\,000$ dong.

Change: $200\,000-(114\,000+50\,000)=36\,000$ dong.

**Answer.** $36\,000$ dong.
:::

:::exercise
$250$ students go on a trip. Each coach carries at most $45$ students. What is the smallest number of coaches needed?

::hint
**What you need:** if the division leaves a remainder, one more coach is needed.

**Steps:**
1. Work out $250\div45$ and find the quotient and the remainder.
2. A remainder that is not $0$ means some students have no seat yet.

::solution
$250\div45=5$ remainder $25$. Five coaches carry $225$ students, so $25$ are left and $1$ more coach is needed: $5+1=6$.

**Answer.** $6$ coaches.
:::

:::exercise
Remember the previous lesson. Use the four digits $3,\ 0,\ 8,\ 5$, each exactly once, to make the largest and the smallest four-digit number. Work out the difference between them.

::hint
**What you need:** largest number: put the digits in order from biggest to smallest. Smallest number: the first digit cannot be $0$.

**Steps:**
1. Make the largest and the smallest number.
2. Subtract the smaller from the larger.

::solution
The largest number is $8\,530$ and the smallest is $3\,058$. The difference is $8\,530-3\,058=5\,472$.

**Answer.** $5\,472$.
:::

:::exercise Challenge
Find the natural number $x$ such that $5\times(x+12)-30=120$.

::hint
**What you need:** find an unknown by using the inverse operation.

**Steps:**
1. Find $5\times(x+12)$: it is the number we subtract from.
2. Find $x+12$, then find $x$.

::solution
$5\times(x+12)=120+30=150$, so $x+12=150\div5=30$ and $x=30-12=18$.

**Check.** $5\times(18+12)-30=150-30=120$. Correct.

**Answer.** $18$.
:::

## Mini test

:::quiz
::mc
The value of $18+6\times4$ is
A. $96$.
B. $24$.
C. $28$.
D. $42$.
::key D
Multiply first: $18+24=42$. Option A is the mistake of adding first: $(18+6)\times4=96$.

::mc
$7\,000-2\,345$ equals
A. $4\,655$.
B. $5\,765$.
C. $5\,345$.
D. $9\,345$.
::key A
$4\,655+2\,345=7\,000$. Option C subtracts the smaller digit from the larger in each column; option D adds instead of subtracting.

::mc
The value of $12\times35+12\times65$ is
A. $485$.
B. $1\,200$.
C. $100$.
D. $112$.
::key B
$12\times(35+65)=12\times100=1\,200$. Option A multiplies only $35$ by $12$; option C forgets to multiply by $12$.

::mc
A box holds $12$ pens. A teacher buys $9$ boxes and gives away $25$ pens. How many pens does she have left?
A. $123$.
B. $133$.
C. $83$.
D. $183$.
::key C
$9\times12=108$, then $108-25=83$. Option B is $108+25$, adding by mistake.

::tf
Look at the expression $A=60-12\times3+24\div4$.
a) The multiplication $12\times3$ and the division $24\div4$ are done before the subtraction and the addition.
b) $A=48\times3+6$.
c) $A=30$.
d) $A$ is greater than $35$.
::key T F T F
a) True: with no brackets, multiply and divide first. b) False: $48\times3+6=150$, because $60-12$ was done first. c) True: $60-36+6=30$. d) False: $30<35$.

::sa
Work out the value of $(36+14)\times8-250$.
::key 150
$50\times8=400$, then $400-250=150$.

::sa
A store has $12$ sacks of rice, each of mass $45$ kg. $8$ sacks are sold. How many kilograms of rice are left in the store?
::key 180
$12-8=4$ sacks are left, so $4\times45=180$ kg.
:::
